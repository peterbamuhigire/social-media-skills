# Swahili Language Books — Copywriting Extraction (2026)

**Purpose**: Distilled, copy-relevant material from a set of Kiswahili reference books, to upgrade `content-copy/swahili-native-copy` (website engine) and the mirrored `language/swahili-native-copy` (social engine) so Kiswahili copy reads as written by an educated **East African** native speaker (Tanzania/Kenya), register **Kiswahili sanifu** — formal-but-warm, respectful, relationship-first.

**Source books**
1. Peter M. Wilson, *Simplified Swahili* (Longman) — core grammar, noun classes, verb system (OCR; see §1, appended).
2. *Rough Guide Phrasebook — Swahili* (Lexus) — practical phrases, the Swahili clock, numbers, service vocabulary.
3. John M. Mugane, *The Story of Swahili* — sociolinguistics, register, proverbs, prestige geography.
4. Derek Nurse & Thomas Spear, *The Swahili* — culture, values, trust ethos.
5. Miriam R. Malaquias, *Authentic East African Swahili Cuisine* — food/hospitality vocabulary.
6. Johannes Fabian, *Language and Colonial Power* — cautions: pidginised/command registers to avoid.
7. Andrew Aames, *Trilingual Story Book* (English + Swahili columns only) — natural sentence patterns.

Conventions: **(K)** = more common in Kenya, **(T)** = more common in Tanzania. Glosses follow the sources.

## 1. Core grammar (Simplified Swahili — Wilson, OCR)

Extracted via OCR (swa) and distilled. The higher-value points beyond a basic noun-class + tense table (which the existing references already cover). Standard coastal/Zanzibar Kiswahili; stress on the penultimate syllable (shifts with suffixes like `-eni`, relative `-ye`).

### 1.1 Relative clauses (highest-value)
Relative particle by class: M-/WA- `-ye-`/`-o-`; M-/MI- `-o-`/`-yo-`; KI-/VI- `-cho-`/`-vyo-`; N- `-yo-`/`-zo-`; JI-/MA- `-lo-`/`-yo-`; U- `-o-`/`-zo-`; place `-po-`/`-ko-`/`-mo-`.
- **`amba-` relative** (any tense): `Huduma ambayo inakusaidia`; `Bidhaa ambazo uliziagiza` (object infix `-zi-`).
- **Tensed relative infix** (only `-na-`/`-li-`/`-ta-`): `Bidhaa zinazouzwa hapa`; `Wateja waliofika jana`. **`-ta-` needs extra `-ka-`**: `Wateja watakaofika kesho` (omitting `-ka-` is a classic error).
- **General relative** (suffix, no tense) = timeless: `Wateja wanunuao bidhaa zetu`; idiom for "next": `mwezi ujao`, `wiki ijayo`, `mwaka ujao`.
- **Negative** `-si-` (affirmative subject prefix): `bidhaa zisizopatikana`. **Manner** `-vyo-`: `kama tunavyofanya`. **Time** `-po-`: `atakapofika…`, with `kila` = "whenever".

### 1.2 The -ka- narrative tense ("…and then…")
Built-in "and" for sequences (story copy): `Tulifika dukani, tukaona bidhaa nzuri, tukanunua, tukarudi.` Do not add `na`.

### 1.3 Conditionals
- `-ki-` real "if/when" (+ future/imperative): `Ukiagiza leo, utapata punguzo.` Negative `-sipo-` (affirmative prefix): `Usipolipa, huduma itasimama.`
- `-nge-` present hypothetical; `-ngali-` past counterfactual; negatives `-singe-`/`-singali-` (affirmative prefixes — a trap).

### 1.4 "Already" / "finish": `-mekwisha`/`kwisha` + bare stem (`Tumekwisha kamilisha`); `kumaliza ku-` (`Amemaliza kusoma`).

### 1.5 "To be" / "to have"
- Present "to be" = invariable `ni`/`si`: `Bidhaa zetu ni bora.` Other tenses use `kuwa`.
- Locative copula `-po`(definite)/`-ko`(general)/`-mo`(inside), 3sg human `yu-`; questions use `-ko` (`Bidhaa ziko wapi?`); don't mix P/K.
- "There is" = `pana`/`kuna`/`mna`; neg `hapana`/`hakuna`/`hamna`.
- "To have" = `kuwa na`; present merges (`Tuna…`, neg `hatuna/sina/hana`); **all other tenses keep `na` separate** (`Tutakuwa na ofa`); never dangle `na` (use relative: `Umekuja nacho?`).

### 1.6 Object infix: optional with things (adds definiteness/emphasis), strongly preferred with definite humans/animals (`Nilimwona mganga` = the doctor), obligatory with `ku-ambia`/`ku-ita`/`ku-pa` (`Nipe…`, `Mwambie…`). One infix per verb; human wins.

### 1.7 Qualifiers: `-enye` (having/with — `kampuni yenye uzoefu`), `-enyewe` (-self; `mwenyewe` = owner), `-ote` (all), `-o -ote` (any at all), `-ingine` (other — not for "some" indefinite).

### 1.8 Noun-phrase order (reverse of English): noun → possessive → adjective(s) → number/quantity → demonstrative (`Kitabu changu kizuri kimoja hiki`). `kila` ("every") precedes the noun + singular.

### 1.9 Reference demonstrative for cohesion: `-o`-reference forms (`huyo, hilo, hicho, hizo…`) precede the noun and refer back: `…duka jipya. Hilo duka lauza…`.

### 1.10 Comparatives: surpass-word `kuliko`/`kupita`/`kushinda` (`bora kuliko zao`); bare "more" = `zaidi`; superlative = surpass-word + `-ote` plural (`nafuu kushinda zote`); `kama` = like/as/about.

### 1.11 Three present tenses: `-na-` (now, default), `-a-` (habitual/general), `hu-` (habitual, no subject prefix — slogans/proverbs). Negative infinitive `ku-to-` (`Ni bora kutochelewa`). Emphatic `ndi-` ("the very": `Hii ndiyo bidhaa unayoitaka`).

### 1.12 Frequent non-native errors (Wilson)
`-na-` is present only (future = `-ta-`); statives (`kuvunjika`, `kupotea`, `kuchoka`) take `-me-` for present state (`Nimechoka` = I'm tired) and never `-na-`; `-sipo-/-singe-/-singali-` use affirmative prefixes; `-ta-` relative needs `-ka-`; don't dangle `na`; don't mix locative P/K/M; keep the `ng'` apostrophe; possessive agrees with the thing owned, not the owner.

## 2. ⚠️ The Swahili clock & time — 6-hour offset (Rough Guide)

**Highest-risk item for copy: opening hours.** Swahili time runs from sunrise/sunset, **6 hours offset** from the international clock. **Swahili hour = international hour − 6 (mod 12).**

| International | Swahili | | International | Swahili |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 am | **saa moja** | | 1:00 | saa saba |
| 8 am | saa mbili | | 2:00 | saa nane |
| 9 am | **saa tatu** | | 3:00 | saa tisa |
| 10 am | saa nne | | 4:00 | saa kumi |
| 11 am | saa tano | | 5:00 | saa kumi na moja |
| 12 noon | saa sita (mchana) | | 6:00 | saa kumi na mbili |

- Day-part qualifiers disambiguate AM/PM: **asubuhi** (morning), **mchana** (midday), **alasiri** (afternoon), **jioni** (evening), **usiku** (night).
- Fractions: *…na robo* (quarter past), *…u nusu / na nusu* (half past), *…kasoro robo* (quarter to), *…na dakika tano* (five past), *…kasoro dakika ishirini* (twenty to).
- Worked: 17:30 → *saa kumi na moja u nusu jioni*; noon → *saa sita mchana*; midnight → *saa sita usiku*.
- **COPY RULE**: "9 am–5 pm" → **Saa tatu asubuhi hadi saa kumi na moja jioni** — NOT *saa tisa hadi saa tano*. Always subtract 6 and add the day-part word. ("What time do you open/close?" → *Unafungua/Inafungwa saa ngapi?*)

## 3. Practical reference (Rough Guide)

### 3.1 Greetings, politeness, register
- All-purpose/warm: **Habari!** · **Habari za asubuhi / jioni** (good morning/evening) · **Habari gani?** (how are things) · **Hujambo?** → *Sijambo, asante.*
- **Plural-of-respect** (use to address customers/audience — warm AND respectful): **Hamjambo?** → *Hatujambo*; **Karibuni** (welcome, pl.); **Asanteni** (thanks, pl.).
- Cornerstone hospitality: **Karibu** / **Karibu kwa [brand]** / **Unakaribishwa** (you're welcome).
- **Tafadhali** (please) · **Asante (sana)** thanks · **Samahani** sorry/excuse me · **Sawa** OK · **Ndiyo / Hapana** yes/no.
- Honorifics: **Bwana** (Mr/Sir), **Bibi/Bi** (Mrs/Ms), **Mzee** (elder — high respect), **Mwalimu** (teacher — deep respect), **mgeni/wageni** (guest/guests).
- High-respect exchange (authentic, junior→senior): **Shikamoo** → **Marahaba**. (Not in the Rough Guide; standard and expected — use when addressing elders/seniors.)

### 3.2 Days, months, dates
- Days: Sunday **Jumapili** · Monday **Jumatatu** · Tuesday **Jumanne** · Wednesday **Jumatano** · Thursday **Alhamisi** · Friday **Ijumaa** · Saturday **Jumamosi**.
- Months (English-derived, standard): **Januari, Februari, Machi, Aprili, Mei, Juni, Julai, Agosti, Septemba, Oktoba, Novemba, Desemba** (T also: *mwezi wa kwanza…*).
- Dates: *Leo ni tarehe gani?* (what's the date); *ni Aprili kumi na tatu*; **leo/kesho/jana** today/tomorrow/yesterday; *wiki ijayo / iliyopita* next/last week; *…ijayo / …iliyopita* next/last (Jumanne ijayo).

### 3.3 Numbers, money, quantities (with concord)
- 1 moja · 2 mbili · 3 tatu · 4 nne · 5 tano · 6 sita · 7 saba · 8 nane · 9 tisa · 10 kumi · 20 ishirini · 30 thelathini · 40 arobaini · 50 hamsini · 100 mia · 1,000 elfu · 100,000 laki · 1,000,000 milioni.
- **Concord matters**: *biya mbili* (two beers) but *vikombe **viwili*** (two coffees, vi- class), *watoto **wawili*** (two children, wa- class). Ordinals carry -a concord: *ghorofa **ya** tatu* (third floor), *mara **ya** kwanza* (first time).
- Money: **shilingi** (number follows: *shilingi elfu hamsini* = 50,000); **bei** price (*ni bei gani? / ni kiasi gani?* how much); **ghali/rahisi** expensive/cheap; **chenji** change; **risiti** receipt; **bili** bill; **jumla** total; **bure** free; **upungufu/kipunguzo cha bei** discount; **kadi ya malipo** payment card; **akaunti ya benki** bank account; **kubadilisha fedha** to change money; **asilimia** per cent.

### 3.4 Service / business / booking / shopping / directions
- Booking: *Nataka kuweka miadi* (make an appointment); **miadi** appointment, **mkutano** meeting, **mapokezi** reception; *Naweza kuwekesha meza/chumba?* (book a table/room); *Je, mnavyo vyumba?* (any rooms?); *Hiyo ni pamoja na kifungua kinywa?* (incl. breakfast?); *Saa ngapi inabidi kutoka chumbani?* (check-out time).
- Shopping: *Nikusaidie?* (can I help?); *Ninaangalia tu* (just looking); *Ninatafuta…* (looking for); *Hii ni bei gani?*; *Naweza kulipa kwa kadi?*; *Nitaichukua* (I'll take it); *ni hiyo tu, asante*.
- Directions: *iko wapi?* (where is it); **moja kwa moja** straight ahead; **kushoto/kulia** left/right; **karibu (na)/mbali** near/far; **katikati ya mji** city centre.
- Signs/labels (UI): **WAZI** open · **IMEFUNGWA** closed · **SELI** sale · **DESKI LA MALIPO** cash desk.

### 3.5 Loanword handling — native term vs bare English
Use native coinages for polished/sanifu copy; bare English is casual.

| Concept | Native (preferred) | Casual |
|---|---|---|
| email | **barua pepe** | — |
| website | **tovuti** | — |
| internet/web | **mtandao / wavuti** | "Internet" |
| online | **mkondoni** | — |
| mobile phone | **simu ya mkono(ni)** | "mobile" |
| computer | **kompyuta** | — |
| download | **kupakua** | — |
| password | **neno la siri** | "password" |
| Wi-Fi | — | **wai fai** |

### 3.6 Set phrases & warmth (copy-ready)
**Safari njema!** (have a good trip) · **Karibu / Karibuni!** · **Hakuna matata / Hakuna tatizo / Bila ya wasiwasi** (no problem / no worries) · **Hongera!** (congratulations) · **Asante kwa ukarimu wako** (thank you for your hospitality) · **Ni furaha yangu** (my pleasure) · **Nakutakia mema / siku njema** (best wishes / good day) · **Inategemea** (it depends) · **safi / nzuri sana** (excellent).

### 3.7 Tanzania vs Kenya usage (from (K)/(T) tags)
beer: biya (K) / bia (T) · toilet: choo (K) / **msalani** (T) · school: skuli (K) / **shule** (T) · ticket: tikiti / **tiketi** · tip: tipu / **bahashishi** · brochure: brosha / **kabrasha** · old town: mji wa kale / **mji mkongwe**. Keep standard forms in copy (*ghali, dhamani, thamani, rangi*); avoid regional sound-shifts (l↔r, gh→g, dh→z, th→s) and mainland *-ga* verb endings.

## 4. Variety, register & prestige geography (Mugane + Fabian)

- **Default to Kiswahili sanifu** (based on **Kiunguja/Zanzibar**) for professional/national copy — the undisputed "good Swahili" of media, government, education across TZ and KE. Fabian's speakers call the standard **kiswahili bora** / **kiswahili ya Dar** (the Dar es Salaam newscast standard) — write to that.
- **Calibrate by country**: **Tanzania** — Swahili is the language of all official life; go rich and eloquent, minimise English (English-heavy copy can read as alienating). **Kenya** — English outranks Swahili in prestige for upmarket/corporate audiences; polished bilingual or English-led copy with Swahili warmth touches is normal; pure deep Swahili can read as "schoolteacher-ish".
- **Coast vs inland**: coastal dialects (Kimvita/Mombasa, Kiamu/Lamu, Kiunguja) carry heritage prestige and more Arabic flavour; don't fake a dialect — use sanifu unless you genuinely command it.
- **Sheng** (Nairobi street code) signals urban-youth, hip, in-group — powerful for KE youth/lifestyle/telecom/entertainment; **reads as unprofessional** for finance/health/government/pan-regional copy and is opaque to Tanzanians. Deliberate, never default.
- **Trust rule**: the language's self-image is *"in its brokenness it is whole"* — communication over pedantry. *"Si kitu sisi kuelewana? Ni kuelewana tu."* Copy that connects beats copy that is merely correct — but for premium/formal copy, correct concord signals respect.

## 5. Tone, values & trust language (Mugane + Nurse & Spear)

- **Relationship before the sale.** The *duka* is a social institution; jumping straight to a hard sell breaks a norm. Open with greeting/welcome/acknowledgment.
- **Hospitality as ethos**: **Karibu / Karibuni** ("come near"); proverb **Mgeni njoo mwenyeji apone** ("Guest, come, so the host may prosper") — perfect for service/tourism/retail.
- **Indirectness = sophistication.** Allusive, suggestive copy outperforms loud and literal (the whole *kanga* tradition is deniable, allusive one-liners).
- **Inclusive "we" (tu-)**: *Tujenge pamoja* (let's build together), *taifa letu / nchi yetu* — collective framing is deeply resonant. Avoid barked commands (see §6).

### 5.1 Load-bearing value words (anchor brand values authentically)

| Term | Meaning | Copy use |
|---|---|---|
| **heshima** | respect, dignity | premium/finance/service: *tunakuhudumia kwa heshima* |
| **utu** | humanity, decency | values/CSR/health/community |
| **undugu** | kinship, solidarity | community, fintech, cooperatives, insurance |
| **ujamaa** | familyhood, communalism | cooperative/savings/community products |
| **umoja** | unity (*umoja ni nguvu*) | team, togetherness |
| **harambee** | all pull together (KE motto) | fundraising, group effort (strongly Kenyan) |
| **ukarimu** | hospitality | the warm open |
| **pole / pole pole** | sympathy / gently | service recovery; patience/reassurance |
| **subira / saburi** | patience (*subira huvuta heri*) | savings, long-term goals |
| **baraka / riziki** | blessing / provision | gratitude, prosperity, daily-bread warmth |
| **hekima** | wisdom | advisory/financial/educational |
| **amani / uhuru / maendeleo** | peace / freedom / progress | stability / empowerment (gravity) / growth |

- **Faith register** (sincere, light, non-sectarian): *Mungu* (God — Bantu word, kept; not *Allah* for general use), *Inshallah*, *Mola ndiye mpaji* (God is the provider). Reads as authentic and warm in pan-regional copy.

### 5.2 Proverbs (methali) — highest-prestige device (vet meaning!)
- **Haba na haba hujaza kibaba** — little by little fills the measure → savings, incremental progress (flagship).
- **Pole pole ndio mwendo** — slowly is the way → patience, reliability, "at your pace".
- **Haraka haraka haina baraka** — careless haste has no blessing → quality, don't cut corners.
- **Umoja ni nguvu (utengano ni udhaifu)** — unity is strength → teamwork.
- **Mkono mmoja haupigi kofi** — one hand can't clap → partnership.
- **Usipoziba ufa utajenga ukuta** — seal the crack or rebuild the wall → insurance, maintenance, prevention.
- **Akiba haiozi** — savings never rot → banking/savings.
- **Mtaka cha mvunguni sharti ainame** — who wants what's under the bed must bend → effort/reward.
- **Cautions**: many methali are warnings/cynical (*Asante ya punda ni mateke* = ingratitude; *Pesa fitina ya binadamu* = money breeds discord). **Reduplication often = careless/shoddy**, not "very" (*haraka haraka, ovyo ovyo*). A misquoted proverb flags an outsider — quote exactly.

### 5.3 Kanga-style one-liners (model for short Swahili taglines)
*Palipo na upendo Mungu yupo* (where there's love, God is there) · *Hisani haiozi* (kindness never rots) · *Tenda wema nenda zako* (do good and move on) · *Kuuliza si ujinga* (asking is not stupidity — great for support/FAQ) · *Tuishi kwa wema tupate neema* (live kindly to be blessed).

## 6. Cautions — what to avoid (Fabian + Mugane)

- **Never bark commands / drop tense markers.** Bare imperatives + uninflected verbs (*lete, tia, fania*) and the frozen *mi ana-… / wei ana-…* construction are the fingerprint of colonial command-pidgin (KiSetla/KiHindi/Kingwana) — they carry a master-servant stain. Always use **full, properly inflected verbs with full noun-class concord** and inclusive *tu-* forms (*tafadhali…, karibu u-…, tujenge…*).
- **Avoid the regional/work labels & grammar** (Kingwana, Congo/Katanga Swahili) for East African coastal/standard audiences — write *kiswahili bora / sanifu*.
- **Don't deploy tourist/Hollywood Swahili** to locals: *Jambo / Jambo bwana / Hakuna matata / Simba / Rafiki* signal an outsider marketing to tourists. Use real greetings (*Habari?, Hujambo?, Mambo?* casual, *Shikamoo→Marahaba* respect).
- **Avoid slur/hierarchy words**: *mtumwa* (slave), *mjakazi* (female servant), *shenzi / mshenzi / ushenzi* (uncultured/savage — offensive; the opposite of prized *uungwana*, civility/refinement), colonial *boi*.
- **Don't mock vernacular accents**; celebrate mutual understanding.
- **Code-switching has a hierarchy**: Swahili+English mixing is authentic for KE/TZ youth/digital/casual; clean standard Swahili signals respect for formal/premium/governmental/older/rural audiences. Match the mix to the audience.

## 7. Cuisine & hospitality vocabulary (Malaquias)

- **Hospitality core**: *Karibu(ni)* welcome; *sinia* the shared serving plate (communal warmth); the "always something ready to share" ethos — gold for hospitality/F&B/tourism/agriculture copy.
- **Signature dishes**: *ugali* (maize staple), *pilau* (spiced rice; *pilau la sikukuu* never missing at any celebration), *kachumbari* (tomato-onion-chili relish), *chapati*, *nyama choma* (barbecued meat — conviviality), *maandazi*/*vitumbua* (fried doughnut / rice pancakes), *samosa/sambusa*, *chai (ya viungo)* (spiced tea ritual), *futari/uji* (Ramadan).
- **Glossary (F&B / agri)**: spices — *iliki* cardamom, *karafuu* cloves, *mdalasini* cinnamon, *tangawizi* ginger, *pilipili* chili, *tui la nazi* coconut milk, *ukwaju* tamarind. Staples — *mchele/wali* rice, *muhogo* cassava, *ndizi* banana, *maharage* beans, *mtama* millet. Fruits — *embe* mango, *nanasi* pineapple, *parachichi* avocado, *nazi* coconut, *papai* papaya. Meat/sea — *nyama ya ng'ombe* beef, *nyama ya mbuzi* goat, *kuku* chicken, *samaki* fish, *pweza* octopus, *kamba* prawns, *dagaa* anchovy, *sato* tilapia. Greens — *mchicha* amaranth, *kisamvu* cassava leaves, *mboga* vegetables, *nyanya* tomato, *vitunguu* onion.
- **Productive cooking patterns**: *wa/za kukaanga* fried, *za kuchoma* roasted/grilled, *wa kuchemsha* boiled, *wa nazi* in coconut sauce, *Mchuzi wa…* stew/curry of…, *Supu/Mchemsho wa…* soup of…

## 8. Natural sentence patterns (Trilingual Story Book — EN+SW)
- Openers/flow: **Hapo zamani za kale kulikuwa na…** (once upon a time there was…), **Siku moja…** (one day), **Wakati huo huo…** (meanwhile), **Kuanzia wakati huo…** (from that moment on), **Hatimaye…** (finally), **Baada ya…** (after), **Kwa bahati nzuri/mbaya…** (luckily/unfortunately), **Punde si punde…** (before long).
- Warmth/reassurance: **Usijali / Usiogope** (don't worry / don't be afraid), **Nimefurahi sana kwamba…** (I'm so glad that…), **Ninashukuru sana kwa…** (I'm grateful for…), **Karibu nyumbani** (welcome home).
- Connectors for flowing copy: **Kwa upande mwingine…** (on the other hand), **Kwa hivyo…** (so/therefore), **badala yake…** (instead), **haijalishi jinsi alivyo-…** (no matter how…), **huku aki-…** (while …-ing — fluid simultaneous action), **kwa upole / kwa nguvu** (gently / forcefully).
- Style note: the Swahili column uses full concord and proper -li-/-na-/-me- tenses + these connectors — exactly the fluent *kiswahili bora* to aim for, opposite of the §6 pidgin.
