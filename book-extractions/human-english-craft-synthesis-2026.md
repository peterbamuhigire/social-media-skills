# Human English and Reader-Centred Craft — Social Content

Status: operational reference, 2026-08-27

Original synthesis only; no raw book text is stored.

## Source limits and usable lessons

Bolitho, Mann, and *Natural English Reading & Writing Skills* are title-only stubs and cannot support substantive claims. David’s ceramic manual is useful for observation, controlled description, consistent categories, and evidence before interpretation, not as a social-copy model. Gupta’s vocabulary list supports selective word choice and confusable-word checks, not rare-word display. Pinnacle’s error practice supports agreement, articles, tense, prepositions, pronouns, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling, and cohesion, with caution about exam simplification. The CAE supplement supports genre, audience, register, task coverage, balanced judgement, and recommendation; its model answers are not captions.

## Social writing standard

Write for a real person in a real feed, with one useful idea and one honest reason to respond. Human social copy is channel-native and recognisable because it notices something specific, takes a position, or offers a clear invitation. It is not noisy, slang-heavy, or artificially imperfect.

### Five passes

1. Name audience, channel, purpose, emotional temperature, and desired action.
2. Choose the post form: observation, explanation, proof, story, offer, response, or conversation prompt.
3. Check facts, rights, claims, names, links, and local context; mark inference and avoid invented lived experience.
4. Revise for spoken clarity, exact verbs, natural rhythm, correct grammar, accessibility, and one strong idea.
5. Proof the first line, line breaks, punctuation, alt text, CTA, tags, links, and platform constraints; read it aloud.

## Craft rules

- British English and East African defaults apply unless the audience says otherwise.
- Use local detail only when true and relevant. Do not manufacture intimacy, dialect, humour, or community voice.
- Prefer precise ordinary words; check any advanced word for meaning, connotation, collocation, and register.
- Check agreement, articles, tense, pronouns, prepositions, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling, and sentence boundaries.
- Avoid automatic hooks, three-item adjective piles, “in today’s world”, empty inspiration, and hashtag clouds.
- Vary length and format when the idea needs it. Do not make every post sound like the same copywriter.
- Distinguish a conversational tone from careless writing. Contractions may be natural; ambiguity and errors are not.

## Anti-slop questions

Would this post still exist if the brand name were removed? If yes, add the actual product, person, place, customer tension, observation, proof, or point of view. Does the CTA ask for a natural next action? Is humour earned? Is the claim supported? Do not imitate humanity with typos or forced slang.

## Evidence record

Record audience/channel/purpose, source and rights status, claim checks, language review, read-aloud/proof status, unresolved issues, reviewer, and date. A lively caption cannot rescue an untrue claim or borrowed identity.
