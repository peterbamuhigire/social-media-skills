## Durable ideas retained

### Garner: a real-time social operation is a human handoff system

The useful idea is not a permanent list of networks. It is an outside-in loop:
people discover, question, discuss, compare, click, ask for help, and report
back across several surfaces. Search, social, content, community, and customer
response should share a purpose, evidence boundary, and handoff model.

Retain these practices:

- define audience, purpose, voice, destination, approval boundary, and business
  outcome before choosing a post or platform;
- keep an evergreen/timely portfolio, with source, owner, expiry or review date,
  rights status, moderation route, and canonical destination;
- adapt one verified idea natively to each channel instead of cross-posting
  mechanically or turning communities into link-distribution lists;
- treat comments, questions, and community norms as research and service inputs,
  not merely engagement counts; and
- route social, SEO, content, paid, PR, sales, support, legal/compliance, and
  analytics through explicit handoffs.

### Woolley: customer language is a discovery and content-planning asset

Use a dated language bank and intent map rather than a pile of supposedly high-
volume terms. Capture the words people use, what they mean in context, the
question or decision behind them, and the destination that can help.

The social workflow should:

1. collect language from owned questions, customer conversations, search data,
   live observations, and authorised platform research;
2. cross-check sources and record locale, date, confidence, ambiguity, and
   privacy/consent boundaries;
3. group terms and questions into useful themes that can support a post, reply,
   guide, service page, or escalation;
4. map each asset to an intent and a next action; and
5. review qualified conversations, referrals, and actions—not just reach,
   follower counts, or ranking positions.

### Bishop/Starkey: protect a recognisable human voice

The creative-writing lens adds a needed quality test: accessible language should
still have a point of view. Voice is expressed through diction, syntax, rhythm,
mood, tone, and the values made visible by what is said and omitted.

For social content and replies:

- write a short voice contract with examples of warmth, directness, humour,
  local specificity, and prohibited claims;
- preserve contributors’ distinct perspective while editing for clarity,
  factuality, inclusion, accessibility, and channel fit;
- use workshops and peer review to expose blind spots, not to make every post
  sound like a template or an AI-generated average; and
- leave room for honest uncertainty, correction, disagreement, and escalation.

## Social engine application: the verified real-time bridge

Use this operating sequence for a timely content opportunity:

`listen → frame the human job → verify → draft the native asset → approve →
publish/respond → route to the canonical destination → moderate → measure →
learn`

The operator records: audience and market, source and locator, claim status,
voice intention, rights/consent, accessibility, platform-specific adaptation,
CTA and failure path, owner, review time, and rollback or correction procedure.

| Signal | What it can show | What it cannot prove by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Question or comment language | Customer vocabulary, confusion, unmet need, or service risk | Consent to reuse a person’s words or a market-wide conclusion |
| Reach, views, reactions, replies | Distribution and interaction on a named surface | Trust, revenue, search rank, or AI citation |
| Referral or tagged visit | A measurable path to an owned destination | Incremental causation without a defined attribution design |
| Qualified conversation or action | Progress toward the stated business job | Universal performance for another audience or platform |
| Correction, moderation, or opt-out event | Safety and trust failure requiring response | That the original claim was acceptable |

Current platform rules, features, and metrics must come from the social source
register or a current primary source. Google’s current [Search
Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) supports
people-first content, natural reader language, crawlable links, and genuine
community participation; it does not make this book’s old social-signal claims
current. Google’s current [AI Search
guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
also rejects artificial chunking, special AI files as a default, and
inauthentic mentions as general requirements.

## Claims quarantined as historical or unassessed

Do not carry forward without a new primary-source check:

- named network rosters, interface paths, platform capabilities, and analytics
  instructions from the older books;
- direct ranking or AI-citation claims attributed to likes, shares, velocity,
  hashtags, or a particular social network;
- fixed keyword counts, density rules, volume/difficulty assumptions, or
  guaranteed reach, engagement, leads, or revenue;
- the books’ algorithm, acronym, analytics-product, and search-feature claims
  as current operating rules; and
- any statistic, platform rule, legal assertion, local fact, quote, image, UGC,
  or testimonial without current evidence and rights status.

## Kaizen experiment

Hypothesis: a real-time bridge that joins customer language, verified facts,
distinctive voice, native adaptation, approval/rights gates, and separate
outcome measures will reduce generic posts and unsafe reactive publishing.

Pilot: run the bridge on one approved content slice. Record baseline, source map,
draft and approval state, native variants, canonical destination, moderation and
correction path, and result. Guardrails are no fabricated proof, no privacy or
rights breach, no seeded or manufactured mentions, no spam/link-dropping, and no
accessibility or cultural regression. Stop and roll back when evidence is
contradictory, the audience job is unclear, or a material claim is unverified.
Standardise only after owner review and a dated re-audit.
