# Human English Craft Standard — Social and Commercial Writing

Use this reference for every caption, post, script, email, comment, campaign, calendar, report, training asset and client message written in English. Read it before drafting and again at the proof pass. It works alongside [`english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md`](english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md), the channel skill, the native-language skill, the rights review and the `anti-ai-slop` gate.

## What "human" means here

Write for a real person in a real feed, with one useful idea and one honest reason to respond. Human copy is channel-native and recognisable because it notices something specific, takes a position, or offers a clear invitation. It is not noisy, slang-heavy or artificially imperfect. A conversational tone is allowed; careless writing is not. Contractions may be natural; ambiguity and errors are not.

## The five passes

1. **Frame.** Name the audience, channel, purpose, emotional temperature and desired action.
2. **Choose the form.** Observation, explanation, proof, story, offer, response or conversation prompt. One form per unit.
3. **Check the substance.** Facts, rights, claims, names, links and local context. Mark inference and never invent lived experience.
4. **Revise for the ear.** Spoken clarity, exact verbs, natural rhythm, correct grammar, accessibility and one strong idea.
5. **Proof the surface.** First line, line breaks, punctuation, alt text, CTA, tags, links and platform constraints. Read it aloud.

## Craft rules

- Apply British English and East African defaults unless the audience says otherwise.
- Use local detail only when true and relevant. Do not manufacture intimacy, dialect, humour or community voice.
- Prefer precise ordinary words. Check any advanced word for meaning, connotation, collocation and register.
- Check agreement, articles, tense, pronouns, prepositions, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling, confusable words and sentence boundaries.
- Avoid automatic hooks, three-adjective piles, "in today's world", empty inspiration and hashtag clouds.
- Vary sentence length and format when the idea needs it. Do not make every post sound like the same copywriter.
- For descriptive work, observe before interpreting and keep categories consistent (a lesson from technical description manuals).
- Treat exam-style grammar rules as checks, not as a voice; exam simplifications can sound stiff in social copy.
- Match genre, audience, register and task coverage; give balanced judgement and a clear recommendation where the form needs one.

## Anti-slop questions

- Would this post still exist if the brand name were removed? If yes, add the real product, person, place, customer tension, observation, proof or point of view.
- Does the CTA ask for a natural next action?
- Is the humour earned?
- Is every claim supported?
- Is any "humanity" faked through typos, forced slang or phrase-book language? Remove it.
