# Swahili Noun Classes and Concord

**Purpose**: Get agreement right. Concord (`upatanisho`) across noun classes (`ngeli`) is the grammatical backbone of Kiswahili and the single feature machine translation breaks most. Correct concord is non-negotiable for native-quality copy.

**Read this when**: You are writing or proofreading any Kiswahili phrase that contains a noun plus an adjective, possessive, demonstrative, number, or verb — that is, almost every phrase.

## The principle

Every Swahili noun belongs to a class. Each class imposes its own prefix on the words that depend on the noun: adjectives, possessives, demonstratives, numbers (the agreeing ones), the `-a` of association, and the verb's subject and object markers. Agreement follows the **class of the noun**, never the meaning and never the English word order. Adjectives and possessives **follow** the noun.

## The class table (singular/plural pairs)

| Class | Example sing. | Example plur. | Typical members | Adjective conc. | Subject conc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M-/WA- (1/2) | mtu (person) | watu | people | m- / wa- | a- / wa- |
| M-/MI- (3/4) | mti (tree) | miti | trees, plants, body parts, some objects | m- / mi- | u- / i- |
| JI-/MA- (5/6) | duka (shop) | maduka | many objects, fruits, augmentatives | Ø/ji- / ma- | li- / ya- |
| KI-/VI- (7/8) | kitu (thing) | vitu | objects, tools, languages | ki- / vi- | ki- / vi- |
| N- (9/10) | nyumba (house) | nyumba | many borrowed nouns, animals, abstracts, family terms | (nasal/Ø) | i- / zi- |
| U- (11/14) | ufunguo (key) | funguo | abstracts, mass nouns, some objects | m-/mw- / (n-) | u- / zi- |
| KU- (15) | kusoma (to read) | — | verbal nouns / infinitives | ku- | ku- |
| PA-/KU-/MU- (16/17/18) | mahali (place) | — | locatives | pa-/ku-/m- | pa-/ku-/m- |

Languages and most abstractions sit in KI-/VI- or N-: `Kiswahili`, `Kiingereza`.

## Adjective concord — worked examples

- M-/WA-: `mtu mzuri` (a good person) → `watu wazuri`.
- M-/MI-: `mti mkubwa` (a big tree) → `miti mikubwa`.
- JI-/MA-: `duka kubwa` (a big shop) → `maduka makubwa`. (Class 5 adjective often takes no prefix; class 6 takes `ma-`.)
- KI-/VI-: `kitabu kizuri` (a good book) → `vitabu vizuri`; `kiti kidogo` → `viti vidogo`.
- N-: `nyumba nzuri` (a good house) → `nyumba nzuri` (singular and plural look alike in N-class). The adjective stem changes its initial consonant after the nasal: `-zuri` → `nzuri`, `-dogo` → `ndogo`, `-baya` → `mbaya`, `-pya` → `mpya`.
- U-: `ukuta mrefu` (a long wall) → `kuta ndefu`.

Many high-value adjectives are **invariable** (mostly Arabic loans) and never change: `bora` (excellent), `safi` (clean/fine), `rahisi` (easy/cheap), `ghali` (expensive), `tayari` (ready), `maalum` (special), `kamili` (complete), `imara` (firm/solid), `hodari` (skilled). So `huduma bora`, `bidhaa safi`, `bei rahisi`, `timu hodari` need no prefix — convenient and very common in commercial copy.

## Possessives — agree with the thing owned, not the owner

Stems: `-angu` (my), `-ako` (your sg), `-ake` (his/her), `-etu` (our), `-enu` (your pl), `-ao` (their). The prefix matches the possessed noun's class:

- M-/WA-: `mtoto wangu` (my child) → `watoto wangu`.
- M-/MI-: `mradi wangu` (my project) → `miradi yangu`.
- JI-/MA-: `duka langu` (my shop) → `maduka yangu`.
- KI-/VI-: `kitabu changu` (my book) → `vitabu vyangu`.
- N-: `nyumba yangu` (my house) → `nyumba zangu`; `huduma yetu` → `huduma zetu`; `bidhaa zetu` (our products).
- U-: `ufunguo wangu` → `funguo zangu`.

**Family/relationship terms sit in N-class**, so they take N-class possessives, not human concord: `dada yangu` (my sister), `kaka zangu` (my brothers), `rafiki yangu` / `rafiki zangu` (my friend/s), `baba yangu`. Writing `dada wangu` is a classic error.

## The -a of association (genitive)

"X of Y" uses the connector `-a`, prefixed for the class of the **first** noun: `wa` (M-/WA-, M-/MI- where 3), `ya` (M-/MI- pl, N-, MA-), `la` (JI- sing.), `cha`/`vya` (KI-/VI-), `za` (N- pl, U- pl), `pa`/`kwa`/`mwa` (locatives), `kwa` (KU-).

- `kiti cha mteja` (the customer's chair), `viti vya wateja` (the customers' chairs).
- `huduma ya kampuni` (the company's service) → `huduma za kampuni`.
- `duka la nguo` (a clothes shop) → `maduka ya nguo`.
- `mradi wa ujenzi` (a construction project) → `miradi ya ujenzi`.
- `lengo la kampuni yetu` (the goal of our company).

## Demonstratives

Three positions, all class-marked. "This" = `h-` + class vowel; "that (near you)" = noun + `-o` reference; "that (yonder)" = `-le` form.

- M-/WA-: `mtu huyu` (this person), `watu hawa` (these), `mtu yule` (that person), `watu wale`.
- KI-/VI-: `kitu hiki` / `vitu hivi`; `kitu kile` / `vitu vile`.
- N-: `nyumba hii` / `nyumba hizi`; `nyumba ile` / `nyumba zile`.
- JI-/MA-: `duka hili` / `maduka haya`.
- The `-o` of reference ("that aforementioned") is idiomatic for cohesion: `huduma hiyo` (that service [we mentioned]), `bidhaa hizo`.

## Agreeing numbers

Only `-moja, -wili, -tatu, -nne, -tano, -nane` agree; the rest (`sita, saba, tisa, kumi`, tens, hundreds, and borrowed numerals) are invariable.

- `mtu mmoja` (one person), `watu wawili` (two people), `vitabu vinne` (four books), `miaka mitano` (five years).
- Invariable: `watu sita` (six people), `vitabu kumi` (ten books), `miaka ishirini` (twenty years).

## Verb subject/object agreement

The verb carries a subject prefix (and optionally an object infix) for the noun's class. See `references/verb-system-and-politeness.md` for the full verb. Concord example: `Bidhaa zetu zinapatikana mtandaoni.` (Our products are available online.) — `zi-` agrees with `bidhaa` (N-class plural). `Mradi huu utakamilika mwezi ujao.` (This project will be completed next month.) — `u-` agrees with `mradi` (M-/MI- class 3).

## The audit habit

For every noun you write, ask: which class? Then make the adjective, possessive, demonstrative, number, and verb all carry that class's marker. Run this as a dedicated final pass over every caption before it ships — concord errors are the clearest tell of machine translation.

## Quantifiers and qualifiers that take concord

- **`-enye` = "having / with"** (class-marked: mwenye, wenye, lenye, chenye, yenye, zenye…): `kampuni yenye uzoefu` (a company with experience), `chai yenye sukari` (tea with sugar). Its opposite is `bila` (without).
- **`-enyewe` = "-self / itself"**, and as a noun **`mwenyewe`** = "owner": `bidhaa zenyewe` (the goods themselves), `mwenyewe wa duka` (the shop's owner).
- **`-ote` = "all / whole"** (wote, yote, vyote, zote…; comes last): `bidhaa zote` (all the goods), `mji wote` (the whole town). Irregular: `sisi sote` (all of us), `ninyi nyote` (all of you).
- **`-o -ote` = "any … at all"** (wo wote, cho chote, yo yote…): `wakati wo wote` (any time at all), `kitu cho chote` (anything at all).
- **`-ingine` = "other / another"** (nyingine, wengine, vingine…; comes last): `bidhaa nyingine` (other products). Trap: for "give me some bread" (indefinite article), do **not** use `-ingine` — just `nipe mkate`.

## Word order inside the noun phrase

The order is the reverse of English: **noun → possessive → adjective(s) → number/quantity → demonstrative**. `Kitabu changu kizuri kimoja hiki` = "this one good book of mine." Quantity words (`-chache`, `-ingi`, `-ingine`, numbers, `-ote`) come last among the qualifiers; the demonstrative follows them. The exception is **`kila`** ("every"), which **precedes** the noun and takes the singular: `kila mteja` (every customer).

## The demonstrative of reference (cohesion)

To refer back to something already mentioned, use the `-o`-reference demonstrative (`huyo, hilo, hicho, hizo, huko…` — built on the relative vowel, and it **precedes** the noun). Native copy chains it: `…duka jipya. Hilo duka lauza…` (…a new shop. This shop sells…). This reads more native than repeating `huyu/hili`.

## Comparatives and superlatives

No inflection — use a surpass-word after the statement: **`kuliko`** (most common), `kupita`, `kushinda` (+ the compared item): `Huduma yetu ni bora kuliko zao.` (Our service is better than theirs.) Bare "more/-er" = **`zaidi`**: `bei nafuu zaidi` (cheaper); `zaidi ya` = "more than". **Superlative** = surpass-word + **`-ote`** (always plural): `Bei yetu ni nafuu kushinda zote.` (Our price is the lowest of all.) `kama` = "like / as / about": `kama bei hii` (like this price), `kama watu kumi` (about ten people).
