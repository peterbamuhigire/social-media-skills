# French Register and Address

**Purpose**: Decide and hold the right level of formality in French commercial copy — the single largest driver of whether French reads as native and professional or as careless translation.

**Read this when**: You are about to write or review any French copy and need to fix `tu`/`vous`, soften requests, or strike a formal-but-warm commercial tone.

## The rule first: choose vous, then hold it

French has two second persons. Commerce and services default to `vous` (the formal, also-plural address). `tu` is for people you are close to, children, and a deliberate peer/youth brand voice. Pick one for the brand and keep it across every page, button, form, error message, and email. A page that opens with `vous` and later slips to `tu` reads as machine-assembled.

- `vous` default — banking, legal, health, B2B, professional services, premium retail, public-facing institutions.
- `tu` deliberate — youth fashion, gaming, casual lifestyle, peer communities, some startups targeting under-30s. Choosing `tu` is a brand decision, not a default; record it.

When the audience is mixed or unknown, use `vous`. It is never rude; `tu` to a stranger can be.

## Politeness is built into grammar, not added as words

Native French softens requests and statements through verb mood, not through stacking polite words. The conditional (`conditionnel`) is the main tool.

- `Je voudrais…` (I would like) over `Je veux…` (I want). `Je veux un rendez-vous` sounds blunt; `Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous` is normal.
- `Pourriez-vous…` (could you) over `Pouvez-vous…` (can you) for a softer ask: `Pourriez-vous nous indiquer…`.
- `Nous aimerions…` / `Nous souhaiterions…` (we would like) for brand-side offers and invitations.
- `Il serait préférable de…` (it would be preferable to) over a flat imperative when guiding the user.

Conditional endings on the future stem: `-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient` → `j'aimerais, vous aimeriez, nous voudrions, il faudrait`.

## Disagreement and negative messages stay courteous

French commercial register avoids blunt contradiction. For "that is wrong" or "no", prefer:

- `Je ne suis pas certain que…` / `Il semble qu'il y ait une erreur…` over `C'est faux`.
- `Vous avez peut-être raison, mais…` to concede before correcting.
- `Je suis désolé, mais…` / `Malheureusement, …` to open a refusal, then state the fact plainly: `Malheureusement, ce créneau n'est plus disponible.`

For service recovery, name the human feeling, not the corporate process: `Je comprends que cette situation soit pénible` reads better than a literal rendering of "we understand your frustration".

## Formal commercial register markers (use)

These phrases signal a professional native voice in `vous`:

- Greeting / help: `Bonjour`, `Bonsoir` (after ~18h), `En quoi pouvons-nous vous aider ?`, `Comment pouvons-nous vous être utiles ?`
- Invitation: `N'hésitez pas à nous contacter`, `Nous vous invitons à découvrir…`, `Nous serions ravis de vous accompagner`.
- Holding / process: `Un instant, je vous prie`, `Nous revenons vers vous dans les meilleurs délais`.
- Reassurance: `Vous pouvez compter sur nous`, `Nous restons à votre disposition`, `Sans engagement de votre part`.
- Closings (email): `Cordialement` (standard professional), `Bien cordialement` (a touch warmer), `Bien à vous` (warm professional). `Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, mes salutations distinguées` is reserved for formal letters, not web copy.

## Forms of address in the interface

- Sign-up / account: `Votre nom`, `Votre adresse e-mail`, `Créer votre compte` (not `Créez un compte` as a label; label nouns and infinitives over imperatives in form fields).
- Buttons in `vous`: `Demander un devis`, `Prendre rendez-vous`, `Découvrir nos offres`, `Nous contacter`. Infinitive button labels are idiomatic French UI convention — French buttons use the infinitive (`Valider`, `Envoyer`, `Continuer`), where English uses the bare verb.
- Honorifics: `Madame`, `Monsieur`, `Madame, Monsieur` (when gender unknown, this order). Avoid `Cher client` as an opener — it reads as a mass mailing; greet with `Bonjour` and move to the point.

## tu register, when chosen

If the brand voice is `tu`, keep it consistently idiomatic: `Crée ton compte`, `Découvre nos offres`, `On t'accompagne`, `Tu peux compter sur nous`. Note `on` (informal "we"/"one") pairs naturally with `tu` brand voice and with spoken-feel copy: `On te rappelle sous 24 h`. Keep `on` out of strict formal `vous` copy except where it means "people in general" (`En France, on…`).

## Quick decision aid

- Don't know the audience age or context → `vous`.
- Regulated, financial, legal, health, B2B → `vous`, always.
- Youth/lifestyle brand that has explicitly chosen an informal voice → `tu`, consistently.
- Brand-side statements about the company → `nous` (`Nous concevons…`) or, in `tu` voice, `on`.

## For social / short-form

- **Pick the register per channel and hold it across posts AND replies.** A brand that posts in `vous` but answers comments in `tu` reads as two people. Decide once: institutional/B2B/finance/health social → `vous`; youth/lifestyle/entertainment → `tu` + `on` (`On t'explique tout en story`).
- **Bios and handles**: a bio is a promise, not a sentence dump. `vous` bio: `Nous accompagnons les entreprises d'Afrique francophone. Devis sous 24 h.` `tu` bio: `On t'aide à faire grandir ta marque.`
- **Comment and DM replies stay in the brand register and stay courteous**, even to criticism. Concede then correct: `Merci pour votre retour. Il semble qu'il y ait eu un souci de votre côté — écrivez-nous en message privé, nous réglons cela.` Never match a hostile tone.
- **CTAs in captions** use the infinitive or the chosen-register imperative: `Découvrir en bio`, `Réservez votre place`, `Écrivez-nous en DM`. Avoid the calque `Cliquez le lien dans la bio` → `Lien en bio` / `Le lien est dans la bio`.
- **`on` is your friend for warm `tu`-voice social** (`On vous régale`, `On revient très vite`) but keep it out of strict `vous` institutional copy except for "people in general".
