# Outreach plays, cadence and scripts

Read when drafting outreach messages, sequences, speed-to-lead responses and partner approaches. Parent skill: [biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach](../SKILL.md). The three plays come from Nelson, J. (2019) *The Seven Figure Agency Roadmap*, Seven Figure Agency LLC, rewritten here with a compliance layer. Warm-network language draws on Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press. Nelson's own volumes and close rates are one practitioner's US experience from 2019, not benchmarks.

## 1. List building, compliant version

1. **Sources (in order of preference):** your own opted-in contacts and referrals; association member directories where their terms allow business contact; public business registries; event attendee lists shared with consent; partner introductions. Purchased or scraped personal data is not used unless a documented lawful basis exists for each record (see the checks reference).
2. **Clean through at least five fit filters:** geography, business type, size proxy (e.g. has a full-time receptionist, number of branches or vehicles), still trading, valid business address or number. Expect a large list to shrink sharply; that is the point.
3. **Split roles:** a researcher builds and cleans; a caller books short discovery calls. Do not give every step to one person.
4. **Domain protection:** if email is used, send from a secondary domain that points to the main site, respect bulk-sender rules (check current mailbox-provider requirements), and keep volumes small.
5. **Business, not personal:** prefer role and business addresses. Every record carries source, date and basis.

## 2. The three plays (correct names)

Rotate in this order: start with play 2; if there is no response, play 1 (only where the policy is real); then content offers on an ongoing, consented basis.

| Play | Mechanism | Plain-text opener (slot template) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 — "Can you handle an additional X this month?" | Name the niche's highest-value job; replies self-qualify | "Quick question, [Name]: could [Business] take on [2–3] more [highest-value job] in [month]?" |
| 1 — "One company in your area" | Territorial exclusivity: you work with one [niche business] per [town] | "We work with one [niche business] per [town or district]. [Town] is open right now. Worth a 15-minute call to see if it's you?" — use only if exclusivity is enforced in writing |
| 3 — Content offer | A useful guide delivered by a call, not just a PDF | "I've written a short guide on how [niche] firms in [city] are winning [outcome] in [year]. Would you like me to walk you through it?" |

**Follow-ups:** "Did this reach you? Happy to explain in two minutes." Then proof, only if verified and consented: "For context, here's what we did for a [niche] firm in [other town]: [verified result]."

**East African versions of the high-value-job question (scenarios):** "3 more IVF consultations" for a private clinic; "4 more mid-week conference groups" for a lodge; "10 more A-level boarding admissions for Term 1" for a school.

## 3. Channels and cadence

- Use at least three of four channels where each is lawful for that market: email, phone, LinkedIn message, WhatsApp (policy check first), website contact form.
- **Maximum three value-adding touches**, spaced over about two weeks, then stop. Each touch must give something: a finding, a guide, a relevant example.
- Every message identifies the sender and offers a simple opt-out ("Reply 'stop' and I won't contact you again").
- Never use daily probes, guilt ("I guess you don't care about growth"), fabricated third-party prompts ("our director asked about you") or one-word chasers.

## 4. Speed to lead

- Call a responder within minutes during working hours; name the owner and backup.
- If unreachable, send a 60–90-second personalised screen video: "Hi [Name], you replied about [outcome]. I couldn't reach you by phone, so here's a quick look at your site. [Strength]. [Gap and its cost]. Here's how we'd fix it: [mechanism]. The link to book 15 minutes is below."
- Ask two qualifying questions before sending material: buying timeframe and what they are trying to achieve.

## 5. Joint-venture and referral partners

People who already sell to your niche (trainers, software vendors, associations, accountants) can introduce you. Choose partners by audience fit and incentives, start with a proof-first trial, and put terms in writing: introduction method, revenue share or referral fee (Nelson reports a recurring share for referred clients — agree your own figure and check tax treatment with the finance engine), consent for any data shared, and exit terms. The partner contacts its own opted-in audience; you do not receive their list.

## 6. Warm network and gatekeepers

- **Permission script for people who know you socially:** acknowledge the relationship; explain in one line whom you help and with what result; say you held back so they would not think you joined [group] to sell; leave the choice to them: "Would you have any objection to us meeting on a professional basis?"
- **Gatekeeper phrase:** "I wonder if you could help me with something. I'm trying to find out who looks after [online bookings / marketing] here."
- **Connector habit:** each week make one useful introduction between two people and share one useful piece of information that is not about you.

## 7. Weekly tracking

Sends · replies · booked calls · held calls · proposals · wins · opt-outs · complaints · average response time. A rising complaint or opt-out rate stops the programme for review.

## 8. Before and after

- "Following up on my previous email." → "Last week I asked whether you could handle three more corporate cover clients in October. Two insurers we work with added five each last quarter [verified, with consent]. Want to see how?"
- "Please find attached our company profile." → "Before I send anything, can I ask two quick questions so I send only what's useful?"
