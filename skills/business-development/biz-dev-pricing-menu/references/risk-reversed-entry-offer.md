# Risk-Reversed Entry Offer ("First Date" Engagement)

Merged from skills/business-development/biz-dev-beyond-agency-offer on 2026-09-29 at 69cca10 (S06 tree); preservation map: [biz-dev-beyond-agency-offer.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/biz-dev-beyond-agency-offer.md)

## When to use this reference

The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) builds the standing services and pricing menu. Use this reference when a new prospect will not yet commit to a monthly retainer and the consultant wants a **low-risk first engagement**: a short test campaign (default 7–10 days) with a result guarantee, pay-per-appointment, qualified-lead, bundled-value or revenue-share fee, a one-page offer document, a two-touch written pitch, and an expectations sign-off before work starts.

The principle: traditional agencies ask clients to commit to a monthly retainer while hoping for results. The "beyond agency" model inverts this: results come first and payment follows. The consultant grows the client's revenue first, then takes a share. The client carries little or no financial risk on the test; the consultant demonstrates capability before asking for a long-term commitment. This answers the biggest objection in any agency sales conversation: "How do I know you can deliver?" (Fihn, 2025).

Evidence status. The five structures, profit-share range and timeframes are one practitioner's method (Fihn, 2025), not market benchmarks. Fees, late-payment terms and exchange rates have no register record: verify before stating. Any use of the client's customer list is personal-data processing (registers UG-DPPA-2019, UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026, KE-ODPC-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026); WhatsApp campaigns follow WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY. This is not legal or tax advice.

## Inputs

Ask for these before producing the offer:

1. **Prospect business name and industry** — trading name and sector.
2. **Country and city** — defaults to Uganda/Kampala if not specified.
3. **Primary pain point** — the specific problem the prospect has described, in their exact words (not what you assume).
4. **What they have already tried** — previous agency, ads, referrals or organic efforts that underperformed.
5. **Their customer base** — a dormant customer list, an active WhatsApp database, a Facebook Group or a physical address file. The answer decides which test campaign type fits.
6. **The offer or service** the test campaign will promote.
7. **Revenue metric** — how they define success: new bookings, leads generated, WhatsApp enquiries or sales revenue.
8. **Preferred fee structure** — profit-share (30–50% of revenue generated above baseline), flat test fee or deferred payment.
9. **Timeframe** — days for the test campaign (default 7–10).
10. **Outreach method** — how the prospect was approached: video audit (`biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach`), referral, white-label partner (`playbook-agency-operations`) or inbound. It changes the tone and framing of the offer.
11. **Baseline figure** (for any revenue-share or result guarantee) — the prospect's normal revenue or enquiry level for a comparable period, from their records.

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Clear, measurable conversion action (bookings, sign-ups, WhatsApp enquiries) and a campaign type you trust | Structure 1 — result guarantee | Guaranteeing an outcome you cannot measure |
| Service business where a booked appointment drives revenue (salons, clinics, coaches, consultants) | Structure 2 — guaranteed appointment booking; charge only for appointments that show up | Paying-for-no-shows disputes |
| B2B or high-value service where lead quality matters more than volume | Structure 3 — specific desirable lead types, with written qualification criteria | Volume of unqualified leads |
| Client is sceptical of the campaign alone | Structure 4 — combine value with one bundled add-on | Price sensitivity on a bare campaign |
| Client genuinely cannot afford a fee but has a strong customer base | Structure 5 — revenue share, with a written agreement naming the baseline figure and a legal disclaimer | Disputes over what counts as "excess" revenue |
| Any guarantee is offered | Frame it as fee at risk ("if we miss the target, you pay nothing"), never as a promise of the outcome; keep the parent's walk-away rule for clients who want guaranteed follower counts or sales numbers | Unethical outcome promises and misleading claims |
| Profit-share agreement worth more than UGX 500,000 | Recommend legal review before signature; check tax and invoicing treatment with the finance engine (added at merge) | Unenforceable or mis-taxed terms |
| The test uses the client's customer list or WhatsApp contacts | Confirm the client's lawful basis and opt-out handling before any send; the agency acts as processor under written instructions (added at merge); honour objections (Uganda: 14 days, UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026) | Data-protection breach on the client's own customers |

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Choose one of the five offer structures

Scripts and templates below are engine house wording from the retired skill, not quotations from Fihn (2025); adapt before use. Select the structure that best fits the client's situation and appetite for risk.

**Structure 1 — Result guarantee**

*"We will generate [specific number] of [leads / bookings / enquiries] in the next [7/10/14] days. If we do not reach that number, you pay nothing."*

Best for: businesses with a clear, measurable conversion action (bookings, sign-ups, WhatsApp enquiries). Requires the consultant to be confident in the campaign type.

**Structure 2 — Guaranteed appointment booking**

*"We will fill [X] appointment slots in the next [X] days using your existing customer list / our targeting. You only pay for the appointments that show up."*

Best for: service businesses (salons, clinics, coaches, consultants) where a booked appointment is the main revenue driver.

**Structure 3 — Specific desirable lead types**

*"We will deliver [X] qualified enquiries from [specific audience description] in [X] days. Qualified means: [define criteria]."*

Best for: B2B and high-value services where lead quality matters more than volume.

**Structure 4 — Combine value**

*"We are not just running a campaign — we will also provide [add-on: a WhatsApp welcome sequence / a referral programme / a customer reactivation campaign / a social media month plan] as part of the test. Our goal is to make this the best [X] days of marketing your business has ever had."*

Best for: clients who are sceptical of the specific campaign alone; bundled value raises perceived worth and reduces price sensitivity.

**Structure 5 — Revenue sharing**

*"We invest our time in the test campaign at no upfront cost to you. If the campaign generates revenue above your normal baseline, we take [30–50]% of the excess. You keep the other [50–70]%. If it does not generate excess revenue, you owe us nothing."*

Best for: clients who genuinely cannot afford a fee but have a strong customer base. It is the highest-trust and highest-reward model. It needs a written agreement stating the baseline revenue figure; Fihn recommends including a legal disclaimer.

### Step 2 — Write the one-page offer document

Scripts and templates below are engine house wording from the retired skill, not quotations from Fihn (2025); adapt before use. Replace every bracket with client-specific information from the inputs.

> **[CLIENT BUSINESS NAME]**
> **[CONSULTANT NAME / AGENCY NAME]**
> **7-Day Marketing Test — No Upfront Commitment**
> Proposed start date: [DD Month YYYY]
>
> **What we are proposing**
> A focused 7-day test campaign targeting [specific audience: past customers / Facebook followers / WhatsApp contacts / paid audience]. The campaign will promote [specific offer/service] with the goal of generating [specific result: X bookings / X enquiries / UGX X in revenue] by [end date].
>
> This is not a proposal for a monthly retainer. It is an invitation to see what we can do together before either party makes a larger commitment. You will have results — or you will have a clear answer — within 7 days.
>
> **What we will do**
> - [Campaign activity 1 — e.g. write and send a 4-part reactivation sequence to your existing customer list]
> - [Campaign activity 2 — e.g. set up a WhatsApp click-to-chat campaign to your warm Facebook audience]
> - [Campaign activity 3 — e.g. optimise your Instagram profile and pin a campaign-specific Story]
>
> **What we will not do**
> - We will not ask for access to your bank account, payment processors or business systems beyond what the campaign requires.
> - We will not make any public-facing change to your brand without your approval.
> - We will not commit you to any ongoing obligation at the end of the 7 days.
>
> **What we need from you**
> - [Requirement 1 — e.g. access to your customer email list or WhatsApp contacts (names and numbers), with confirmation that you may use them for marketing]
> - [Requirement 2 — e.g. one hour of your time at the start to brief us on your best offer]
> - [Requirement 3 — e.g. prompt responses to our questions during the campaign window]
>
> **What success looks like**
> We define the test as successful if: [specific measurable result — e.g. "a minimum of 5 confirmed bookings" or "UGX 500,000 in new enquiries"].
>
> **What happens at the end of the 7 days**
> If the campaign achieves or exceeds the success target, we will present a proposal for an ongoing engagement. You are under no obligation to accept it. If the campaign does not achieve the target, you owe us [nothing / the agreed flat test fee only].
>
> **The fee** [select one]
> - Option A (risk-free): no upfront cost. If the campaign generates results above your baseline, we retain [35]% of the excess revenue for [30] days. After that, we agree new terms or part as friends.
> - Option B (flat test fee): UGX [amount] covers the 7-day test in full. No profit-share. If results exceed expectations, we discuss ongoing terms.
> - Option C (deferred): no payment during the test. If you proceed to an ongoing engagement after the test, the test fee of UGX [amount] is included in Month 1.
>
> Agreed by: ______________ Date: ________ [Client name, title]
> Proposed by: ______________ Date: ________ [Consultant name, agency name]

The "access to your customer list" wording is an addition to the source template so the data-protection rule above is visible to the client.

### Step 3 — Written pitch (phoneless close)

Scripts and templates below are engine house wording from the retired skill, not quotations from Fihn (2025); adapt before use. Use for written outreach (email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp) or as the basis for a short video audit message. The goal is a response, not a close on first contact. The full video audit method (audit checklist, script, message versions, follow-ups and lawful-contact checks) lives in `biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach` (personalised video outreach reference).

**Opening hook (written or video)**

> "I was looking at your [website / Facebook Page / Instagram] and noticed [specific, genuine observation: 'your customer reviews are excellent but there's no way for new visitors to contact you quickly' / 'you have 4,000 Facebook followers but I couldn't see a WhatsApp link anywhere']. I filmed a 3-minute walkthrough of what I noticed — would it be useful if I sent it to you?"

Do not pitch services in the opening message. The goal is a "yes, send it".

**The video audit (Loom or WhatsApp voice note)**

- 3–5 minutes maximum.
- Screen-record their digital presence while narrating what you see.
- Identify 2–3 specific, genuine improvements; do not manufacture problems.
- End with: *"I have an idea for how to test this quickly — would you be open to a short message or a quick chat to see if it makes sense for you?"*

**Close message (sent after the audit is viewed)**

> "[Name], thanks for watching. Based on what I saw, I think a focused 7-day test on [specific tactic] could generate [estimated result]. I've put together a one-page outline — no commitment needed to read it. Happy to send it over?"

By the time the one-page offer document lands, the prospect has engaged twice and seen the consultant's thinking. The "yes" to the offer document is the natural next step, not a hard sell.

### Step 4 — Expectations sign-off document

Produce this one-page document at the start of every engagement to prevent misalignment, scope creep and disappointing client experiences. Have it signed before any campaign begins. It covers Fihn's (2025) four key expectation areas: results, time, cost and communication.

> **[CLIENT BUSINESS NAME] — Engagement Expectations**
> Agreed on: [DD Month YYYY]
>
> **Results**
> - What we are working towards: [specific goal agreed in the offer document]
> - Realistic timeframe for early results: [e.g. 3–5 days for reactivation enquiries; 14–21 days for organic social growth]
> - What results depend on: [factors the client controls — e.g. "prompt responses to WhatsApp enquiries received from the campaign; approval of copy within 24 hours"]
> - What is not guaranteed: no marketing campaign can guarantee a specific revenue outcome. We guarantee our effort, our process and our professional standard — not a specific number. (If a guarantee was agreed in the offer, it is stated in the offer document.)
>
> **Time**
> - Our typical turnaround for campaign materials: [e.g. 48 hours for copy; 5 business days for full strategy documents]
> - Your expected turnaround for approvals: [e.g. 24 hours on weekdays]
> - Campaign launch date: [DD Month YYYY]
> - Campaign end date: [DD Month YYYY or "ongoing — reviewed monthly"]
>
> **Cost**
> - Agreed fee structure: [from the offer document]
> - Payment schedule: [e.g. "35% profit-share collected monthly on the 10th" or "UGX X invoiced on campaign completion"]
> - Late payment terms: [e.g. "Invoices unpaid after 14 days incur a UGX X late fee"]
>
> **Communication**
> - Our primary contact point: [name, WhatsApp number, email]
> - Your primary contact point: [client name, number, email]
> - Response time commitment (us): [e.g. "WhatsApp replies within 4 hours during business hours EAT; emails within 24 hours"]
> - Response time expectation (client): [e.g. "copy approvals within 24 hours; campaign queries within 2 hours when the campaign is live"]
> - Scheduled check-in: [e.g. "brief WhatsApp update every Monday morning; full written report on Day 7 or Day 30"]
>
> Signed: ______________ Date: ________ [Client name, title]
> Signed: ______________ Date: ________ [Consultant name]

After a successful test, move the client onto a programme from the parent's services menu; the Starter-to-Growth guidance in the parent's Document 2 applies from there.

## Release checklist

- [ ] The offer document is complete and ready to send, with no unfilled placeholders: every bracket is replaced with client-specific information.
- [ ] One of the five offer structures is selected, and its terms state the measurable success target, the timeframe and the fee structure in UGX (or local currency).
- [ ] The written pitch produces two touchpoints before the offer document is sent: the opening hook, then the video audit response request.
- [ ] The expectations document covers all four areas (Results, Time, Cost, Communication) with dates and names filled in, not generic.
- [ ] Revenue-share or flat-fee terms are unambiguous, the baseline figure is written down, and a note recommends legal review for any profit-share agreement over UGX 500,000.
- [ ] Any guarantee is fee-at-risk only, and the expectations document's "what is not guaranteed" line is kept.
- [ ] Use of the client's customer data has a confirmed lawful basis and opt-out route.
- [ ] British English throughout; no American spelling variants.
