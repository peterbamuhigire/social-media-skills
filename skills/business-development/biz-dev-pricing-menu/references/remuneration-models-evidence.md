# Remuneration models and the evidence on each

Read when choosing how the agency is paid (not just how much), when a client proposes payment by results or a commission, or when a retainer's pay model is up for review.

Added 29 Sep 2026 (Social Kaizen S10-T10, benchmark row SL18-C1). The model definitions and decision rules are the engine's own; the evidence lines cite the register. UK industry guidance is a reference model for Ugandan and Kenyan agencies, not a binding standard.

## 1. The industry guide

The Agency Remuneration Best Practice Guide from the IPA, ISBA, MCCA and PRCA (updated 2013 from a 2005 original; free PDF) covers fixed fees, time-based and retainer arrangements, performance-related pay, commission and hybrid value-based models. It includes a ten-point remuneration checklist, separate approaches for new and existing relationships, and worked examples (register `IPA-REMUNERATION-GUIDE`). It is old; use it for structure, not for current market rates.

## 2. The models

| Model | How the fee is set | Best fit | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-based retainer (input) | Agreed staff time or FTE share per month, priced from cost-to-serve plus margin | Ongoing social management, community work, variable workloads | Clients buy hours, not results; needs monthly reconciliation of hours planned against used |
| Fixed fee per scope (output bundle) | A monthly or project price for a defined list of deliverables | The engine's standard tiered menu; SMEs who want a known cost | Scope creep; revisit the scope sheet at every renewal |
| Deliverable or unit pricing | A price per post, video, campaign build or report | Project work; add-ons | Encourages volume over value |
| Commission on media | A percentage of media spend placed | Traditional media buying | Rewards spending more; disclose rebates and principal trading (see [commercial governance and contracts](../../../playbooks/playbook-agency-operations/references/commercial-governance-and-contracts.md#3-transparency-and-audit-rights-media-buying)) |
| Payment by results (PBR) | A base fee plus a bonus (and sometimes a reduction) tied to agreed measures | Mature relationships with reliable measurement | See the evidence and rules below |
| Value-based | Price set against the value of the outcome to the client | Strategic or high-stakes work with a quantifiable outcome | Needs trust and data; hard for a new agency to defend |
| Hybrid | A base retainer plus a PBR element or unit fees | Most growing agencies | Keep it simple enough to invoice |
| Risk-reversed entry offer | Fee partly or wholly at risk for a short test | First engagement with a wary prospect | Uses [risk-reversed entry offer](risk-reversed-entry-offer.md); not a standing model |

## 3. What the evidence says about payment by results

- ISBA's *Future of Agency Remuneration* report (27 Nov 2024, with RightSpend) found that only 27% of respondents said payment by results had changed agency performance for the better, and 48% felt they got value for money from their agencies (register `ISBA-REMUNERATION-2024`). These are advertiser survey figures from the public highlights; the full report is member-only (`NOT_ASSESSED`), so the sample size and method are unknown. Read them as a caution, not a benchmark.
- No independent East African evidence on PBR outcomes was found: `NOT_ASSESSED`.

**Engine rules for PBR:**

1. Offer PBR only as a bonus on top of a base fee that covers cost-to-serve; never put the agency's costs at risk in a standing retainer.
2. Tie the bonus to measures the agency influences and both sides can see (for example cost per qualified lead from the client's own CRM), plus at most one business outcome. Mix business, brand and relationship measures if the client insists on a scorecard.
3. Cap the bonus, agree the baseline before the period starts, and write down what happens when measurement breaks (tracking outage, platform change, internet shutdown).
4. Exclude factors the agency does not control: stock-outs, price changes, sales-team follow-up, platform blocks.
5. Review the scheme after two quarters; drop it if it changes nothing, which is what most ISBA respondents reported.

## 4. Choosing and reviewing the model

| Condition | Model | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| New client, uncertain workload | Fixed fee per scope with a three-month review | Known cost for the client; data for the agency |
| Workload varies month to month | Time-based retainer with monthly reconciliation | Pays for the real effort |
| Client demands "results only" | Decline, or offer a short risk-reversed test then a base fee plus capped PBR | Protects cash flow |
| Agency places media on the client's behalf | Disclosed fee on actual media cost; no hidden commission | Transparency and audit rights |
| Retainer renewal | Re-price from cost-to-serve, value delivered and the market; check whether the model still fits | Stops a model outliving its purpose |

Tax on fees (VAT, withholding) goes to `chwezi-accounting-doctrine` (https://github.com/peterbamuhigire/chwezi-accounting-doctrine) and current URA or KRA guidance.
