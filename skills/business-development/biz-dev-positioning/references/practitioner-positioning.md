# Practitioner Positioning: the Consultant's Own Practice

Merged from skills/business-development/biz-dev-practitioner-positioning on 2026-09-29 at 69cca10 (S06 tree); preservation map: [biz-dev-practitioner-positioning.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/biz-dev-practitioner-positioning.md)

## When to use this reference

The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) positions a business or firm (USP, spoken pitch, niche, mission, vision, preeminence). Use this reference when the subject is the social media consultant's **own practice**: an internal business-development piece for the consultant, not a client-facing deliverable. Typical moments:

- starting out and defining a market position;
- rebranding or repositioning after some experience in the market;
- business development has stalled and the consultant cannot say clearly why a client should hire them;
- competing for clients against other agencies or freelancers.

Why it matters in the region. In Uganda and East Africa, professional services are won through relationships and reputation. A consultant who cannot answer "why you?" loses to a better-connected or better-positioned competitor. Positioning is not a website exercise: it is how the consultant describes themselves in a WhatsApp message, on a discovery call and in a proposal introduction. This reference builds that position step by step, and the output must be actionable in Week 1 without extra budget, tools or resources.

Evidence status. The method draws on Sobia Publication (2022), Johnson (2023) and, for the market viability test, Nelson (2019). The claim that LinkedIn is the main professional networking platform in East African urban centres has no register record: verify before stating it to a client. Posting frequencies and engagement habits below are working disciplines, not platform rules. Outreach to anyone who is not already a client follows [biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach](../../biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach/SKILL.md) and registers UG-DPPA-2019, UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026 and KE-ODPC-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026.

## Inputs

Ask for all six before generating any output; do not proceed until all are provided.

1. Consultant's name and years of experience in social media or digital marketing.
2. Current services offered: every service currently provided or intended.
3. Industries with the most experience and the best results: name specific sectors.
4. Current client base: approximate number of clients, their size and type.
5. Biggest business-development challenge: too few leads / wrong clients / price competition / unclear positioning / other (describe).
6. LinkedIn presence: active (posting regularly) / inactive (profile exists, no posts) / no account.

## Decision rules

| # | Condition | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The three niche-test answers (experience, results, best-paying and most respectful clients) diverge | Prioritise the third answer: payment quality and client respect decide whether the work is sustainable | A niche the consultant cannot live on |
| 2 | A candidate niche fails a market viability question | Narrow, widen to regional or diaspora-facing, or accept fewer, larger clients at a higher fee before committing | Committing to a market that cannot fill the plan |
| 3 | Tempted to change niche after a few months | Hold the niche for 12 months; own the local market with two or three proof clients before widening | Resetting credibility every few months |
| 4 | Fewer than three of five prospects say "yes, that is exactly what I need" to the USP | Revise the USP and test again | Carrying an unclear position into proposals |
| 5 | A results post, screenshot or case study would name or identify a client | Get the client's permission first | Breaching confidentiality and trust |
| 6 | LinkedIn is inactive or absent | Still prioritise the recommendation request; set up the profile before the content plan | Losing the cheapest credibility signal |
| 7 | A referral partner asks about commission | Do not promise commission unless separately agreed and documented | Undisclosed or disputed referral payments |
| 8 | A prospecting source or association member list is proposed | Route contact sourcing to lawful prospecting; check the body's current data-sharing and contact rules first | Unlawful direct marketing |

## Procedure

### 1. Niche selection

The most common mistake is trying to serve everyone. The most effective East African consultants specialise: a narrow niche builds credibility faster, commands higher fees and produces stronger referrals. Choose **one primary niche** and at most one secondary. A niche can be defined by sector, service type or audience size.

| Dimension | Options |
|---|---|
| By sector | Financial services (SACCOs, MFIs, banks, mobile money); healthcare and wellness; NGO and donor-funded organisations; hospitality (hotels, restaurants, lodges); retail and FMCG; education (schools, universities, training institutions); real estate and construction; professional services (law firms, accounting firms); government and public sector |
| By service type | Social media management (content and community); strategy and training only (no execution); Instagram growth specialist; WhatsApp marketing and automation; paid advertising; creator and influencer management; content production (video, photography, writing) |
| By audience size | Solo founders and personal brands; SMEs (5–50 staff); corporate (50+ staff); NGOs and INGOs |

**Three-question niche test.** Using the consultant's inputs, ask:

1. In which sector, service type or audience size do you have the most experience?
2. In which do you produce the best results?
3. Which clients pay the best and respect your expertise the most?

The right niche is the overlap of all three (decision rule 1 where they diverge). The Part 3 selection grid and narrowing layers in [positioning-method.md § Part 3 — Defining the Niche](positioning-method.md) can then sharpen it.

**Market viability test** (adapted from Nelson, 2019). Test the candidate niche against four questions:

| Question | Pass condition | East African adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Does the group self-identify? | A member reads a one-line headline naming them and says "this is for me" ("plumbers" worked; "contractors" did not, in Nelson's experience) | Name the trade as the market names itself, for example "private midwifery clinics", not "healthcare" |
| Do they already invest in marketing? | Visible spend on search, directories, radio, billboards or social ads | Also check FM radio, outdoor, press and the Meta Ad Library (register META-SIEP-AD-LIBRARY-2026) |
| Are there enough of them? | Enough firms of the right size within reach to fill the plan | Few single-country niches are large; go regional or diaspora-facing, or accept fewer, larger clients at a higher fee |
| Is there an association? | A body with members, events and partner listings | Use it for trust and speaking; check its current data-sharing and contact rules before using any member list |

Then narrow once more: a **specialty within the specialty** (dentistry → orthodontics; hospitality → conference lodges), with high transaction value for the client and a fit with the consultant's own background. Own the local market with two or three proof clients before widening, and commit for 12 months.

**Output:** a one-paragraph niche statement naming the primary niche, explaining why it is right on the consultant's inputs and the viability test, and naming the secondary niche if any. List prospecting sources for the niche only through [biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach](../../biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach/SKILL.md). For niche, programme and pricing decisions about the agency as a business, see the [agency growth roadmap](../../../playbooks/playbook-agency-operations/references/agency-growth-roadmap.md).

### 2. USP articulation

The USP is the one thing that makes this consultant different from every other social media consultant in their market. It must be specific enough to test and narrow enough to remember. For a solo practitioner use this first-person formula (the parent's Part 1 formula serves firms):

> "I help [specific client type] achieve [specific result] through [specific approach], faster/better/cheaper than [alternative]."

East African examples (illustrative):

- "I help Ugandan SACCOs grow their member base using WhatsApp marketing campaigns that cost less than a Facebook boost."
- "I help EA hospitality brands produce hotel-quality content using smartphones — no photography budget required."
- "I train NGO communications teams to produce donor-quality social media content in-house, so they don't need an agency."

Using the niche, experience and challenge inputs, draft **three** USP candidates, explain the strengths of each and recommend one as the primary USP. Run the parent's competitor test on the recommended one.

**USP testing instruction** (include in the output):

> Say the USP to five prospective clients. If three or more respond with "yes, that is exactly what I need", it is working. If they look confused or say "interesting", revise. Report back and iterate.

### 3. Social proof strategy

In East African professional markets, social proof is the strongest sales tool: no amount of portfolio polishing beats a referral from a trusted contact. Build proof in this order:

1. **Client testimonials.** Ask every client at Month 3 and at contract renewal, by WhatsApp (house template, not a quotation): "We've been working together for 3 months. Would you be willing to share a sentence or two about what's been most valuable? I'd like to share it on LinkedIn."
2. **Case studies.** For every significant result (follower growth, a lead-generation campaign, a viral post), produce a one-page case study with the [case study method](../../biz-dev-credentials/references/case-study-method.md) in `biz-dev-credentials`.
3. **Results screenshots.** Engagement spikes, follower milestones, positive mentions: screenshot them and, with client permission, share them on the consultant's own channels.
4. **LinkedIn recommendations.** Ask every satisfied client for one. The request is low-friction; most will agree if asked directly after a win.
5. **Speaking and training.** An invitation to speak at a business event, a media training session or a university is the highest-credibility signal in East African professional markets. Pursue it actively (see the parent's Part 6, Speak route).

**Social proof collection schedule** (standard operating procedure):

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Request one results screenshot for the portfolio |
| Month 3 | Request a written testimonial via WhatsApp |
| Contract end | Request a LinkedIn recommendation and case study consent |
| Any major win | Document immediately; request permission to share within 48 hours |

Personalise the schedule to the consultant's current client base and LinkedIn status. If LinkedIn is inactive or absent, add a note on why the recommendation request is still worth prioritising and what to do first (decision rule 6).

### 4. LinkedIn thought leadership

A consistent LinkedIn presence produces referrals and inbound enquiries for consultants, and a consultant who wants corporate or NGO clients to take them seriously needs one (verify the platform's regional standing before stating it; see Evidence status).

**Posting frequency:** at least 2 posts a week to maintain presence; 4 a week for active growth.

**Content types** (rotate across all four):

| Type | Pattern | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Insight posts | "What I have learned managing social media for [industry] clients in Uganda" | Specific, credible and shareable among peers; ground every insight in a real client situation, anonymised if needed |
| Results posts | "We grew [client type]'s WhatsApp list from 200 to 1,200 subscribers in 90 days — here is what worked." (illustrative) | Client permission before naming or identifying any client |
| Contrarian takes | Challenge a received wisdom, for example "Why posting every day on Instagram is damaging Ugandan SME brands." | Screen the idea with the [bold-idea risk screen](../../../playbooks/playbook-viral-content-design/references/bold-idea-risk-screen.md) |
| East African market observations | Commentary on a local business or marketing trend: platform shifts, brand launches, campaign failures, emerging client behaviour | Specific to Uganda or East Africa |

**Engagement discipline:**

- Comment meaningfully on 5 posts a day by potential clients, peers and sector influencers.
- Reply to every comment on your own posts within 24 hours.
- Send every new connection a one-line human message: not a pitch, not a link, not a service offer.

**Output:** a 4-week LinkedIn content plan for the consultant's niche, with one post idea per content type per week and a suggested hook for each. If the consultant has no LinkedIn account, put a step-by-step profile set-up checklist before the plan. For platform-specific optimisation use [platform-linkedin](../../../platforms/platform-linkedin/SKILL.md).

### 5. Referral system design

Referrals are the main business-development channel for East African consultants. The goal is a system, not a hope: most consultants wait for referrals; effective ones engineer them.

**Step 1 — Identify referral partners.** Professionals who work with the consultant's ideal clients and are not competitors:

- accountants and bookkeepers (they work with almost every SME);
- lawyers (corporate and commercial practices);
- business coaches and trainers;
- printers and graphic designers;
- event organisers and venues;
- web developers;
- bank relationship managers.

Approach script: "I refer clients who need [their service] to you. Would you be comfortable referring clients who need social media help to me?"

Produce a shortlist of 5–8 referral partner categories matched to the consultant's niche, each with a short rationale.

**Step 2 — Make referrals easy.** Produce two assets:

1. **WhatsApp referral message** that partners can forward on the consultant's behalf to people they know:
   > "I work with [Name], a social media consultant who specialises in [niche]. She/He has helped brands like [example] get real results. Happy to connect you."

   Name an example brand only with that client's permission.
2. **Referral acknowledgement approach.** A public LinkedIn shout-out or a thank-you message goes a long way in East African relationship culture; offer one to every partner who sends a referral. Do not promise commission unless separately agreed and documented.

**Step 3 — Track and reciprocate.**

- Log every referral received and made in a spreadsheet with the columns Date / Source / Client Name / Outcome / Thank-you Sent.
- Refer clients to your referral partners as a priority: reciprocity drives referrals in East African professional networks.
- Thank the referrer by WhatsApp within 24 hours of every referral, whether or not the lead converts.

**Output:** a referral tracking template:

| Date | Source | Client Name | Outcome | Thank-you Sent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

## Output structure

Produce these sections in order:

1. **Niche statement** — one paragraph.
2. **USP** — three candidates and the recommended primary.
3. **USP testing instruction.**
4. **Social proof collection schedule** — personalised table.
5. **LinkedIn content plan** — 4 weeks, by content type.
6. **Referral partner shortlist** — 5–8 categories with a brief rationale.
7. **WhatsApp referral message** — ready to send.
8. **Referral tracking template** — simple table.

## Release checklist

- [ ] Niche selection follows the three-question test (experience / results / payment quality) and names a specific niche, not a broad category.
- [ ] The market viability test has been run on the chosen niche.
- [ ] The USP is one specific, testable sentence naming a client type, a result and an approach.
- [ ] The social proof system is a named schedule with explicit triggers (Month 1 / Month 3 / contract end / major win).
- [ ] The LinkedIn strategy states content types and weekly frequency, with concrete post ideas from the consultant's actual niche.
- [ ] The referral system names at least five referral partner categories, with rationale relevant to the niche.
- [ ] All advice is specific to East African professional services, not generic business development.
- [ ] The output is actionable in Week 1 without extra budget, tools or resources.
- [ ] The WhatsApp referral message and the tracking template are delivered as ready-to-use assets, not descriptions.
- [ ] No client is named or identified without permission.
