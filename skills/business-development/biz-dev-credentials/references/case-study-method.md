# Client Case Study Method

Merged from skills/business-development/biz-dev-case-study on 2026-09-29 at 69cca10 (S06 tree); preservation map: [biz-dev-case-study.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/biz-dev-case-study.md)

## When to use this reference

The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) builds the whole credentials pack, with three short success stories. Use this reference when one client result needs its own case study: a standalone, shareable piece for a credentials pack, a proposal, the agency website or a pitch. It produces two outputs from one set of inputs:

1. a one-page written case study; and
2. a three-slide deck outline.

Both must be ready to share or present without significant editing. Use specific metrics throughout, never vague claims. Write in East African English, in a third-person professional register.

Evidence and permission status. Naming the client, quoting them and showing screenshots of their accounts all need the client's permission; keep the consent record with the case study. Handle any personal data in screenshots or results (customer names, phone numbers, messages) under Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 (register UG-DPPA-2019), or the law of the named market. Where the case study will be used in a market with its own endorsement and testimonial rules, such as the United States, check them before release (register FTC-ENDORSEMENTS-REVIEWS-2026). The example figures below are illustrative registers, not benchmarks; do not quote them as typical results.

## Inputs

Ask for all of these before generating:

| Input | Detail | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Client industry or anonymised descriptor | For example "Kampala-based retail brand", or the client's actual name if approved | Ask |
| Whether the client name can be used | If yes, use it; if no, create one consistent anonymised descriptor for both outputs | Treat as "no" and anonymise |
| Challenge faced | The specific problem or situation the client had before engaging the consultant | Ask |
| What was done | The work in detail: what was implemented, in what sequence, and why each step mattered | Ask |
| Results achieved | Specific metrics: follower growth (numbers or percentages), engagement rate before and after, leads generated, revenue attributed, website traffic change or any other measurable outcome | Ask for numbers; do not proceed on vague results |
| Client testimonial | A direct quote, if available | Note the gap and insert the placeholder (see Procedure, step 1) |

If results are vague ("things improved"), ask the consultant for specific numbers before proceeding. The case study is only credible with real metrics.

## Decision rules

| # | Condition | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Results are described only in words ("things improved") | Stop and ask for specific numbers | A case study with no evidence |
| 2 | Client name not approved | Use one anonymised descriptor, worded identically in both outputs | Identifying a client without consent; inconsistent anonymisation |
| 3 | No testimonial supplied | Insert the exact placeholder; never write a quote | A fabricated testimonial |
| 4 | Several past results could be used for a pitch | Choose the one that most closely mirrors the prospect's sector, challenge type and scale (Sant: evidence placement) | Proof that does not persuade this buyer |
| 5 | A result has only one data point | State it clearly as an absolute outcome, not as a change | Implying a baseline that does not exist |
| 6 | A draft result reads "social media presence" or uses an intensifier ("significantly", "dramatically") | Replace it with a named metric, or remove it | Weasel language standing in for evidence |

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Write the one-page case study

Generate a standalone, shareable document. Use exactly this structure; do not reorder or add sections. Aim for one printed page, about 350–450 words.

**Document header**

```
**[Client Name or Anonymised Descriptor]**
*[Industry / Sector] | [City, Country]*
*A case study by [Agency Name]*
```

**Problem** — 2–3 sentences. Describe the specific challenge before the engagement. Be concrete: name the platform, the gap and the business impact. Do not editorialise or offer analysis here; describe the situation as it was.

> Example register (illustrative): "A Kampala-based hospitality group with three properties had an active Facebook page but was generating fewer than 50 engagements per month on a following of 4,200. Their posting was inconsistent — sometimes daily, sometimes silent for two weeks — and their content was almost entirely promotional. Enquiries via social media had dropped by 30% over the previous six months."

**Approach** — 3–5 sentences. Describe what was done, in what sequence, and the reasoning behind the key decisions. Be specific about tactics and tools without heavy jargon. Name a framework only where it genuinely applies, for example content pillars, the Hero/Hub/Hygiene model (YouTube/Google) or the 10-4-1 rule (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012). Show the thinking, not just the activity.

> Example register (illustrative): "The engagement began with a full content audit to understand what had performed historically and identify audience preferences. A three-pillar content strategy was developed — one pillar focused on behind-the-scenes hotel life, one on local Kampala travel inspiration, and one on direct promotional offers — following the Hero/Hub/Hygiene framework. A consistent posting schedule of five times per week across Facebook and Instagram was implemented, with each post individually captioned to match the pillar's tone. Community management was introduced with a four-hour response target during business hours."

**Results** — a short introductory sentence, then a bullet list of specific metrics taken from the input. Where a metric improved, give the before and after; where only one data point exists, state it clearly. Never use "significantly", "dramatically" or other vague intensifiers.

- Intro sentence: "Over [timeframe], the engagement delivered the following results:"
- Bullet formats:
  - `[Metric]: [Before figure or baseline] → [After figure] ([percentage change if applicable])`
  - `[Metric]: [Absolute outcome]`

> Example (illustrative):
> - Facebook engagement rate: 1.1% → 4.7% over 90 days
> - Monthly post reach: 3,200 → 18,600 (average across final 30 days of engagement)
> - Enquiries via Facebook Messenger: 12 per month → 41 per month
> - Instagram followers: grew from 820 to 2,340 over 6 months

**What the Client Said** — if a testimonial was supplied, format it as:

```
> "[Direct quote from client.]"
> — [Client Name or Title, e.g. "Marketing Manager, [Company Name]"]
```

If no testimonial was supplied, insert exactly:

```
> [Client quote to be added — request testimonial from [client name/descriptor].]
```

Do not fabricate a quote under any circumstances.

### Step 2 — Write the three-slide deck outline

Generate it immediately after the written case study. Every slide uses the engine's four-field deck format:

```
**Slide N — [Slide Title]**
**Headline:** [The one thing the audience must remember]
**Bullets:**
- [3–5 bullets maximum]
**Speaker Notes:** [What the presenter says — context not shown on slide]
**Visual Direction:** [Layout, imagery, chart type, colour guidance]
```

| Slide | Headline | Bullets | Speaker notes | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Challenge | Names the client (or anonymised descriptor) and captures the core problem in one phrase | 3–4 facts about the client's situation before the engagement | How the consultant first heard about the client; what the first discovery conversation revealed | Clean and minimal. Left column: client descriptor and an industry icon or sector image. Right column: the 3–4 bullets. Muted colours (navy, grey). A single "before" metric may be shown as a large number for impact |
| 2 — The Approach | Conveys the strategic logic: why it worked, not just what was done | 3–5 steps or decisions in sequence, each a short action phrase | The reasoning behind 1–2 key decisions, connected to the challenge on Slide 1 | Timeline or numbered-flow layout; a simple icon per step; brand colours or a neutral palette; no dense text |
| 3 — The Results | Leads with the strongest metric | Every key metric from the written Results section, most impressive and most credible first; below the bullets, the client quote if available (italicised, attributed) | What the numbers mean in business terms, for example "41 enquiries a month from a single channel, at no additional ad spend" | Data-forward. The headline metric as a large typographic element (for example "4.7% engagement rate"); secondary metrics as a compact list; the client quote at the bottom in a subtle callout box |

### Step 3 — Apply the persuasion principles

Use the frameworks in [proposal-frameworks.md](proposal-frameworks.md) (the NOSE structure and evidence standards). For case studies specifically:

- Structure the story with NOSE: the client's **N**eed first, then the **O**utcome they achieved, then the **S**olution (the agency's approach), then the **E**vidence (specific numbers) (Sant: NOSE).
- Write from the client's perspective: the client is the hero; the agency is the enabler (Hatton: Empathy Model).
- The most persuasive case study mirrors the prospect's own situation: match sector, challenge type and scale (Sant: evidence placement).
- Every case study contains at least one specific number: enquiries generated, revenue attributed, percentage growth, cost per lead. General outcomes are not evidence (Sant: Quality Maxim).
- Remove weasel language. "We helped them improve performance" is not evidence; "enquiries increased from 12 to 47 per month in 90 days" is (Sant: Fluff/Guff/Geek/Weasel Test).

## Formatting rules

- Written case study: full prose for Problem and Approach; a bullet list for Results; a block quote for the testimonial.
- Deck outline: strict slide-by-slide format, with no deviation from the four-field structure.
- The anonymised descriptor is identical throughout both outputs; do not vary the phrasing.
- Dates and timeframes in British format, for example "over 90 days", "January–March 2025".
- Do not editorialise in the Problem section; keep analysis for Approach and the speaker notes.
- Do not use "social media presence" as a stand-alone result; it is not a metric.

## Release checklist

- [ ] The Problem section is specific and factual, with no vague observations.
- [ ] The Approach section explains the reasoning behind decisions, not just what was done.
- [ ] The Results section contains only specific, named metrics, with no vague intensifiers.
- [ ] The written case study fits on one printed page (about 350–450 words).
- [ ] Every deck slide has all four fields.
- [ ] The Slide 3 headline leads with the strongest metric, not a generic phrase.
- [ ] The client quote is genuine or clearly marked as a placeholder, never fabricated.
- [ ] Anonymisation is consistent and plausible throughout both outputs.
- [ ] Permission to name the client, quote them and show their data is on record.
