# Pitch conduct and generative-AI clauses

Read when a prospect runs a competitive pitch or tender, asks for free speculative work, or when the proposal's terms must say how the agency uses generative AI on the client's work and data.

Added 29 Sep 2026 (Social Kaizen S10-T10, benchmark rows SL18-C3 and SL17-C7). Commercial screening, not legal advice: the final terms go to counsel.

## 1. Pitch conduct: the Pitch Positive Pledge

The IPA's Pitch Positive Pledge (launched 11 May 2022) asks advertisers, agencies and intermediaries to commit to three things (register `IPA-PITCH-POSITIVE`):

1. **Be positive a pitch is required (before):** the advertiser confirms in writing why a pitch is needed.
2. **Run a positive pitch (during):** both sides consider the effect of requirements and timelines on staff wellbeing.
3. **Provide a positive resolution (after):** winners and losers hear directly and get feedback on their performance.

Signatories at launch included UK agencies, advertisers and pitch intermediaries; current signatories and any later revision are `NOT_ASSESSED`. The pledge is UK-based and voluntary. For Ugandan and Kenyan pitches and tenders, use it as the agency's own standard of conduct, and follow the procuring entity's rules where it is a public or donor tender (route tenders to `proposal-skills`: https://github.com/peterbamuhigire/proposal-skills).

**Engine rules for pitches:**

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Invitation to pitch with no stated reason, budget or decision date | Ask for all three in writing before committing staff time |
| More than four agencies invited, or the incumbent is re-pitching for show | Ask the prospect; decline if the answer is vague |
| Free speculative creative requested | Offer a paid discovery sprint or a credentials-plus-approach response instead; if the agency does pitch creative, state in writing that ideas not paid for remain the agency's property |
| Pitch timeline forces weekend or overnight work | Propose a realistic date; record the wellbeing cost in the go/no-go decision |
| Pitch lost | Ask for feedback against the brief; log it in the pipeline record |

## 2. Why AI terms now belong in the proposal

- The 2025 ISBA/IPA Creative Services Framework Agreement added clauses on generative AI (register `ISBA-IPA-CSFA-2025`; clause text member-only, `NOT_ASSESSED`).
- ANA's 2023 media buying template requires the agency to get the advertiser's prior consent before using AI applications (register `ANA-MEDIA-CONTRACT-2023`).
- ISBA's 2024 survey found 8% of advertisers had added AI terms to agency contracts and a further 42% were doing so (register `ISBA-REMUNERATION-2024`, public highlights).
- IAB's 2026 framework makes the advertiser accountable for AI disclosure and says agency and client may share it when the agency creates the content (register `IAB-AI-DISCLOSURE-V2-2026`).

## 3. Generative-AI contract clauses

Offer these as the agency's standard AI schedule. The wording is a starting point for counsel, not final legal text.

| Clause | What the agency commits to | Client decision needed |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure of AI use | The agency tells the client which AI tools it uses and for which deliverables, and keeps a per-asset provenance log | None; standard |
| Consent | The agency uses AI for client work only within the categories the client approves (for example drafting copy yes, synthetic people no) | Approved and prohibited categories |
| Client data in AI tools | No client personal data or confidential information goes into public or consumer AI tools; only approved business or enterprise tools whose terms exclude training on inputs; personal data handled under the Uganda DPPA 2019 (register `UG-DPPA-2019`) or the applicable law | Approved tool list |
| Training-data use | The agency does not use client materials, data or outputs to train or fine-tune AI models, its own or a vendor's, without written consent | Whether any brand-voice fine-tuning is allowed |
| Ownership and licences | Final deliverables are assigned or licensed as the main agreement says; the agency states where copyright protection may be limited because of AI generation and documents the human contribution (see [AI IP and copyright policy](../../../policies/policy-ai-content-ethics/references/ai-ip-and-copyright-policy.md)) | Accept the stated limits |
| Warranties and indemnity | The agency warrants it followed the tool's terms and the agreed review process; it does not give an unlimited warranty of non-infringement for AI output. Whether a given AI vendor indemnifies users is `NOT_ASSESSED` per tool: check its current terms | Allocation of residual IP risk |
| Likeness and voice | No synthetic replica of a real person without that person's separate written consent for the specific use | Talent consents |
| Labelling | The agency applies the risk-based disclosure decision and labels in the creative where required ([AI transparency and provenance](../../../policies/policy-ai-content-ethics/references/ai-transparency-and-provenance.md)) | Who signs off the disclosure decision |
| Human review | A named agency reviewer approves every AI-assisted deliverable before it reaches the client | Client approver name |
| Changes and exit | The approved tool list is reviewed quarterly; at exit, the agency deletes client data from AI tools where the tool allows and confirms it in writing | None |

## 4. Where these sit in the proposal

Put a one-line summary under **Terms** ("We use approved AI tools under the AI schedule attached; no client data trains any AI model") and attach the schedule. For a retainer, repeat the approved tool list in the scope sheet so changes go through the change-request log.
