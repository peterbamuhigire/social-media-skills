# Agency Client Acquisition System

**When to read:** when a proposal depends on how the prospect was found, qualified and will be followed up, or when building the agency's own pipeline. Parent skill: [biz-dev-proposal](../SKILL.md). Contact data and outreach must also pass [biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach](../../biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach/SKILL.md). The system is the engine's own synthesis of agency practice, drawing on Nelson, J. (2019) *The Seven Figure Agency Roadmap*, Seven Figure Agency LLC (note the 2019 date; older engine citations said 2018), Kennedy's consultative-sales principles and Maltz et al. (1998) on introductions. Practitioners' reported conversion figures are single-agency anecdotes, never targets.

## Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Chosen niche and core programme | Agency owner | Stop; run the niche test in [agency growth roadmap](../../../playbooks/playbook-agency-operations/references/agency-growth-roadmap.md) |
| Monthly new-client target | Growth plan | Back-solve from the MRR gap |
| Proof assets available (case studies, testimonials with consent) | Delivery records | Plan proof-building before scaling outreach |
| Lawful basis for any contact list | Data owner | Do not contact; see lawful prospecting skill |

## 1. Build the minimum pipeline first

Before any outreach, set up four linked pieces and no more: a clearly named niche; one useful free offer for that niche (a one-page guide or checklist people want and can use at once); a booking page where an interested prospect schedules a one-to-one diagnostic; and a written follow-up plan for people who book. Add complexity only when these four work.

## 2. Authority assets, in build order

Build from one source asset so each takes days, not months:
1. **A core talk** for the niche ("Five things [niche] firms in [city] should fix to win more [outcome]") — every other asset is cut from it.
2. A one-page niche guide derived from the talk.
3. A niche landing page or micro-site.
4. Verified case studies and consented testimonials.
5. A recorded webinar version of the talk.
6. An email and WhatsApp follow-up sequence (opt-in only).
7. Longer-term: a short book or report for the niche, a podcast interviewing respected operators in the niche with a fixed set of questions, a printed newsletter to top prospects.

Ask each month which two or three can be finished that week.

## 3. Choose lead sources by leverage

| Source | Leverage | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Joint ventures and referral partners who already sell to the niche (revenue share agreed in writing) | Highest | Once one proof case exists |
| Associations, events and speaking | High — borrowed trust | From the start, via membership and committee roles |
| Inbound content and webinars | Medium, compounding | Continuously |
| Paid lead generation | Controllable if funded | When the offer and follow-up convert |
| Cold outreach | Lowest, but controllable | To start the pipeline; never the whole engine |

## 4. Outreach plays (named correctly)

Use at most three plays, rotated. The names below are Nelson's (2019); the scripts are the engine's.

| Play | When | Engine script |
|---|---|---|
| "Can you handle an additional X this month?" | Default opener | "Quick question, [Name]: could [Business] take on [two or three] more [highest-value job — e.g. specialist consultations, mid-week conference groups] in [month]?" |
| "One company in your area" | Only if the agency genuinely limits itself to one client per town or district in that niche, in writing | "We work with one [niche business] per [town]. [Town] is open now. Worth fifteen minutes to see if it's you?" |
| "Content offer" | Nurture and warm leads | "I've written a short guide on how [niche] firms in [city] are winning [outcome] this year. Shall I send it and walk you through it?" |

Write plainly, as a personal note. Use at least three lawful channels. Call anyone who replies within minutes; if you cannot reach them, send a 60–90-second personalised screen recording of their website with one strength, one gap and one fix. Cold-sourced prospects typically close less often than inbound ones — a reason to invest in authority assets, not a rate to promise.

## 5. Monthly webinar routine

1. Put the date in the calendar first, then prepare.
2. Pick a topic from a bank of 10–12 evergreen niche topics; re-date titles each year ("The [Year] [Niche] Growth Plan").
3. Promote with three legitimate messages: invitation, reminder, replay.
4. Put a short video on the registration confirmation page and email offering a one-to-one diagnostic booking — many ideal prospects register but never attend.
5. After the session, cut the recording into a full video, slides, several short clips, an audio episode and a transcript article.
6. Count enquiries for about a month after each webinar; call registrants if capacity allows.

## 6. The consultative sale

| Stage | What to do | Template |
|---|---|---|
| First call (short) | Ask a few questions, then postpone the pitch in favour of research | "Rather than pitch you now, let me study your [website, listings, ads] properly. On [day] I'll show you where you're strong, where you're losing enquiries, and what we'd do." |
| Research pack | Website basics (contact visibility, call to action, real photos, a page per service, mobile); what buyers search for by service and town; current visibility; business-profile completeness and reviews; a one-page sheet of three strengths, three gaps and the cost of the gaps | — |
| Review meeting | Genuine rapport; their background and marketing; a future-state question; findings with strengths first; solution as a cause-and-effect chain to enquiries and revenue; two options | "If everything went to plan, where would the business be in twelve months?" |
| Close | Pre-close, ask for a decision, or agree a decide-by date and book the walk-through | "How does this compare with what you had in mind?" · "On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you? What would make it a 10?" |

**Follow-up over about two weeks (value only):** programme overview and agreement → short founder video on what is included → two or three consented client videos from the same niche → "your first 90 days with us" → the pre-booked decision call.

## 7. Presenting the offer and price

- Order: the problem → its cost to their profit → the solution → the price.
- If size signals suggest the fee is out of reach, say the range early: "We sit at the upper end of the market because of the results we deliver, so our programmes run from [UGX X] to [UGX Y] a month. Is that within range for you right now?"
- Hold a conversation, not a slide-by-slide presentation.
- When asked for "something in writing", agree the decision date and book the walk-through before hanging up.
- Close with a choice between two options; keep to three core programmes at most ([biz-dev-pricing-menu](../../biz-dev-pricing-menu/SKILL.md)).

## 8. Qualification and focus

Prefer prospects who already spend on marketing and are established. Keep focus narrow for a year: one market, one lead source mastered, one conversion route, one programme (the "five ones" discipline Nelson attributes to Taki Moore and Clay Collins). Every added niche or programme multiplies complexity. Choose a lead source that can scale beyond one networking group.

## 9. Language checks

Replace tool-first pitches ("We do SEO, PPC and social") with outcome statements; never write "let me send you a proposal" without a booked walk-through; drop "just checking in"; avoid heavy HTML emails; never claim exclusivity or capacity limits that are not real; never send automated reports without a conversation.

**Rewrites (original examples):**
- "We are a full-service digital agency serving businesses of all sizes." → "We help private schools in Wakiso fill Term 1 places. Your website, search listing and ads work as one system, and we report confirmed admissions, not likes."
- "Please find attached our proposal. Let us know your thoughts." → "Attached are the two options we discussed, side by side. We're booked to go through them together on Thursday at 10:00 — bring any questions from your bursar."
