# Consulting authority from LinkedIn to the website and pipeline

Parent: [LinkedIn Presence Plan](../SKILL.md).

## Build a credible buying path

Use this sequence for a consultant or professional practice. It is an operating design, not a platform growth guarantee.

1. Select a specific buyer and costly decision the practice is qualified to help with. Record the alternatives the buyer can choose, including doing the work internally or with AI.
2. Align personal profile, company presence and website service page around the same competence, scope and proof. A personal profile conveys practitioner judgement; the website provides inspectable service details, cases, sample work and contact paths.
3. Build a permissioned evidence library: approved case facts, original analysis, process demonstrations, frequently misunderstood decisions, and useful tools. Record rights, confidentiality, dates, limitations and who can approve each item.
4. Turn a genuine buyer question into one useful piece. State the observation, explain the judgement, show the supporting artefact and make a proportionate next step available. A post may simply teach; a CTA is not mandatory on every item.
5. Link to a relevant website destination where it helps the reader. Choose link placement for usability and observed results; do not hide every link in comments based on an unverified ranking theory.
6. Respond to relevant conversations with substance. Fulfil requested resources without treating the request as permission for unrelated marketing. Move to a meeting only when there is a credible mutual reason.
7. Record accepted opportunities in CRM with evidence of need, fit, decision process, next step and consent boundary. Handoff to discovery, then proposal. Measure delivered and retained value after the sale.

## Content workbench

For each planned item retain: buyer question; originating evidence; intended reader decision; distinct argument; format reason; source/rights status; destination; review owner; and expected learning. A month plan is a capacity plan, not a quota of interchangeable posts.

Useful professional-service units include:

- A redacted diagnostic showing how a decision was reached, with the client's permission.
- A comparison of two credible options with conditions under which each wins.
- A failed approach and the correction, only when the underlying experience is real.
- A short demonstration of a deliverable and its acceptance criteria.
- A sourced local issue with a clearly marked implication for the buyer.
- A worked educational example labelled hypothetical, with no implied client result.

Synthetic example: a website consultant explains how enquiry routing can fail after a form displays success. Show a test-labelled form-to-inbox check and a recovery design. The website destination describes discovery, build acceptance and support. The post must not claim that a particular client's revenue was lost or recovered without evidence.

## Profile and proof review

Use a recognisable, truthful identity and accurate credentials. Write a headline that makes the specialism legible without an outcome guarantee. Make the opening of the profile useful to the intended buyer. Feature inspectable work and a relevant next step when the account supports it. Verify current field lengths, image dimensions, account features and visibility controls before producing specifications. Numbers in a profile URL and lack of a particular optional feature are not evidence of incompetence.

## Relationship and message design

Record why contact is relevant, what is known from an authorised source, the legitimate purpose, and when to stop. Never scrape profiles, buy personal contact lists or automate interactions without an authorised, policy-compliant route. Connection acceptance, profile views and likes do not establish purchase intent or broad marketing consent.

A message draft should state the real shared context, offer one relevant contribution and leave a low-pressure choice. Do not invent familiarity, compliments, referrals or results. Honour refusal immediately. Do not prescribe universal daily connection quotas or repeated unsolicited follow-ups.

[LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies), accessed 2026-09-22, prohibit spam and artificial engagement, including prearranged liking or resharing. They also require truthful identity and prohibit misleading credentials. Recheck current rules before execution. Drafting under this skill never authorises sending.

## Measure the buying path

| Stage | Observable evidence | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Audience relevance | Roles/organisations represented where lawful data is available | Impressions do not establish buyer fit |
| Useful attention | Substantive replies, relevant questions, source clicks | Engagement does not qualify a lead |
| Website decision | Service/case interaction and completed enquiry | Tracking may miss private sharing and offline activity |
| Qualified conversation | Need, fit and agreed next action | A friendly reply is not pipeline |
| Accepted opportunity | Sales owner acceptance and decision process | Pipeline value is not revenue |
| Won and delivered | Contract, collected revenue, delivery cost, acceptance | Correlation does not establish channel incrementality |

Keep self-reported source alongside campaign links and CRM history; record disagreement rather than forcing a single attribution. Establish targets from capacity, baseline and the sales cycle. No universal engagement, reply-rate, follower-growth or SSI target establishes commercial success.

## Pilot and rejection cases

Choose a small set of evidence-bearing content units and an effort budget the practitioner can sustain. Record a baseline, review date, quality criteria and stop rule. Continue when relevant conversations and delivery capacity justify it; revise the offer or audience if attention has no qualified progression. Avoid declaring failure merely because a long sales cycle has not closed within a posting month.

Reject fabricated cases; engagement pods; first-hour ranking formulas; guaranteed conversion claims; copy-pasted thought leadership; coerced employee advocacy; and designs that send people to an unready website. Employee participation is voluntary, original, accurate and appropriately disclosed.
