# LinkedIn Company Pages, Showcase Pages and Events

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the work is the organisation's LinkedIn asset rather than a practitioner's personal profile: Company Page setup, admin roles, page content mix, follower invitations, Showcase, Product or Subsidiary Pages, LinkedIn Events and Live, a thirty-day page content plan or page KPIs. The personal profile, practitioner authority and relationship workflow stay in the parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) and [consulting authority to pipeline](consulting-authority-to-pipeline.md); do not duplicate them here.

Produce a complete, client-specific Company Page strategy with every section populated and no generic filler, in British English, defaulting to Uganda/East Africa unless the client says otherwise.

Evidence boundary. Completion, reach, cadence, invitation-credit, sub-page limit, event and algorithm figures below are carried from legacy guidance (mainly Raymond and Johnston 2021). Verify each against current official LinkedIn help before presenting it as fact, or remove it (verify before stating; no register record). For AI-search visibility, the parent skill's link to the LinkedIn citation section applies; treat the Meltwater profile/page split as an observation from its named sample (see register MELTWATER-LINKEDIN-REPORT-2026), not a platform law, and record study scope and test outcomes separately from engagement, referral and conversion. Community conduct follows LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies (register PREMIUM-LI-POLICY-2026). Company Pages are one institutional surface alongside credible individual voices: when an update needs an accountable human voice, publish through the relevant employee and amplify from the page.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Page not yet set up or incomplete | Finish the 13-step checklist before publishing | Low-credibility, hard-to-find page |
| Leadership willing to post personally | Make personal amplification part of the plan, not optional | Relying on the page's low legacy organic reach (about 3% of followers vs about 10% for personal posts; verify) |
| Agency manages the client page | Client keeps Super Admin; agency holds Content Admin | Agency lock-in and lost page control |
| Follower count 0–500 | Brand Awareness growth strategy | Premature narrow targeting |
| Follower count 500–2,000 | Niche Community strategy | Unqualified audience |
| 2,000+ followers or a defined Ideal Client Profile | Account-Based Marketing strategy | Broad content that misses named accounts |
| Follower count below 1,000 | Do not create Showcase Pages | Fragmented presence and split follower growth |
| Distinct audience with little overlap and capacity for a separate feed | Consider a Showcase Page | Diluting the main page's feed |
| Discrete named products with distinct buyers | Consider Product Pages | Missing product-level social proof |
| Bespoke or project-based services | Do not use Product Pages | Misrepresenting the offer as a product |
| Event is for lead generation | Use a registered event | No attendee data |
| Plan is heavy on Trust content without Know or Like content | Rebalance across the three categories | No top-of-funnel pull, so no growth |

## Procedure

### 1. Page setup: 13-step checklist

Legacy claims to verify before stating: a complete page receives 30% more weekly views than an incomplete one (Raymond and Johnston 2021); pages reach about 3% of followers organically, less than half the reach of personal posts (about 10%). Specifications and limits below are also legacy; verify current values.

1. **Logo**: 300×300px; transparent or white background; consistent with other platforms.
2. **Cover image**: 1128×191px; tagline, website URL and one call to action (for example "Book a demo").
3. **Tagline**: 120 characters maximum; exactly what the business does and for whom; prime search space.
4. **About / Overview**: 2,000 characters maximum; open with the value proposition; include the keywords the audience searches; state location, services and a closing call to action; treat it as a search-optimised landing page, not a mission statement.
5. **Specialties**: up to 20; specific service and product terms, not generic industry words; described as indexed by LinkedIn search.
6. **Page hashtags**: follow 3 relevant hashtags so the page can comment and engage in those communities (verify the feature still exists).
7. **Custom URL**: `linkedin.com/company/[brand-name]`, using the brand name, not a number.
8. **CTA button**: "Visit website" for brand credibility; "Contact us" for lead generation; "Learn more" for audience education.
9. **Featured content**: pin the most important post, resource or announcement; replace when something better exists.
10. **Location**: street address or city, for local search visibility.
11. **Company size**: the correct band; affects appearance in recruiter and procurement searches.
12. **Page type and industry**: the correct industry category; said to feed content recommendations.
13. **Admin profile linking**: every active admin links their personal profile to the page through their Experience section, for credibility and reach.

Legacy cadence note (verify; the parent skill forbids universal posting quotas): weekly posting doubles engagement compared with less frequent posting; five or more page posts a week suppresses each post's reach; three a week is the source's suggested level for most SMEs. Set the actual cadence from capacity and the account baseline.

### 2. Admin roles

Assign roles before the first post. A wrong structure causes inconsistent tone, missed comments and content gaps. Verify current role names and permissions.

| Internal role | Publish | Edit page | Manage admins | View analytics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Super Admin | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Content Admin | Yes | No | No | Yes |
| Curator | Suggest only | No | No | No |
| Analyst | No | No | No | Yes |

| Paid media role (only with a paid budget) | Function |
|---|---|
| Sponsored Content Poster | Boost posts and run sponsored content |
| Lead Gen Forms Manager | Create and manage lead-gen forms for paid campaigns |
| Pipeline Builder | Sales Navigator integration for account targeting |

Minimum viable structure: solo operator, one Super Admin (founder or consultant); small team, Super Admin (brand owner) plus Content Admin (social media manager); agency, Super Admin kept by the client and Content Admin for the agency.

### 3. Content strategy

Use Know, Like and Trust (Raymond and Johnston 2021) as the organising principle: the audience must meet the brand often enough to know it exists, like what it stands for and trust it enough to act.

| Category | Job | Examples | Hero / Hub / Hygiene tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Picture | Awareness; positions the brand as informed | Industry trends, sector news, market observations, thought leadership on business problems | Usually Hub: regular, expected, community-building |
| Human Interest | Affinity; the Like stage | Team stories, behind the scenes, culture, values posts, employee spotlights | Usually Hygiene: always-on, humanising |
| Products & Services | Trust and conversion | Service explainers, client outcomes, demonstrations, case studies, pricing guides | Hero for major announcements; Hygiene for FAQ-style explainers |

Use all three categories in every monthly plan. Apply the 10-4-1 rule (Bodnar and Cohen 2012): Products & Services at no more than 10–15% of volume, with Big Picture and Human Interest carrying most posts. The Hero/Hub/Hygiene tiering is the YouTube/Google content model.

Techniques:
- **Series**: recurring formats on a fixed schedule ("Monday market insight", "Friday team spotlight"), which build return visits and follows.
- **Repurposing**: a blog post becomes a carousel; a webinar becomes three short clips; a research report becomes five insight posts; more output without proportionally more production effort.
- **Themes**: a monthly or seasonal focus tied to the business calendar (tax season for an accounting firm, harvest season for an agri-business) so the plan reads as coherent.

Five posting mistakes:
1. Wall of text: use white space, one idea per paragraph, scannable on a phone.
2. Over-promotion: audiences unfollow pages that feel like catalogues.
3. Cross-posting without tailoring: Instagram or Facebook copy reads off-brand on LinkedIn; rewrite for the professional register.
4. Too many hashtags: legacy guidance of 3–5 at most, with more said to reduce reach (verify; the parent skill rejects compulsory signature hashtags).
5. External links in the post body: legacy guidance says LinkedIn suppresses off-platform links, so the link goes in the first comment with "(link in first comment)" in the post (verify before stating).

### 4. Follower growth: invitations and strategies

Legacy invitation mechanics (verify all before stating): each page has 100 invitation credits a month pooled across admins; a credit is used when an invitation is sent and returned when it is accepted, so accepted invitations cost nothing net; invitations go only to admins' first-degree connections, so more admins with relevant networks mean a larger pool. Legacy traction threshold: at 500–1,000 followers the page begins to surface in discovery and "Pages you might like"; before that, growth needs active effort.

| Strategy | Approach | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Brand Awareness | Use all credits each month; cross-promote the page URL in email signatures, other platform bios, WhatsApp broadcasts and the website footer | 0–500 followers, building a base |
| Niche Community | Targeted invitations to specific job titles and industries; content tuned to a defined professional audience; engage in the 3 page-hashtag communities | 500–2,000, qualifying the audience |
| Account-Based Marketing | Target follows at named companies; content for decision-makers at priority accounts | 2,000+ or B2B clients with a defined Ideal Client Profile |

Employee amplification (legacy): each employee also has 100 credits a month, so a team of five yields 500 targeted invitations, five times the page's own capacity. Give employees a one-paragraph brief on whom to invite and a suggested message; participation stays voluntary (parent skill). For a full employee programme, use the [employee advocacy programme](../../../playbooks/playbook-social-selling/references/employee-advocacy-programme.md) in `playbook-social-selling`.

### 5. Sub-pages

Sub-pages let one Company Page host distinct assets for different audiences, products or initiatives without a separate unrelated page. Limits and approval steps are legacy; verify.

| Type | Legacy facts | Use when | Do not use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Showcase Page | Up to 25 per page; own follower count, feed and admin team; listed under "Affiliated pages"; for brand units, geographic markets, audience segments or initiatives (a recruitment brand, a CSR programme, a product line for another sector) | The initiative's audience does not overlap much with the main page and there is capacity for a separate feed | Followers are below 1,000 |
| Product Page | Up to 35 per page; needs LinkedIn approval (submitted from the page dashboard); name, description, logo, images and video, customer recommendations, featured customers; works as a product catalogue with recommendations shown as social proof | Discrete named products with distinct buyers and a wish to gather product-level proof | Bespoke or project-based services |
| Subsidiary / Acquisition Page | Enterprise feature linking acquired companies or subsidiaries to a parent; shows "Part of [Parent Company]" | Group structures | Most SMEs (note the feature for future growth) |

### 6. Events

| Type | Legacy follower requirement | Registration | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off-platform event | None | External (Eventbrite, Google Forms, landing page) | Workshops, conferences, product demos, client events |
| LinkedIn Live | 150+ followers | Internal LinkedIn registration | Webinars, panels, launches, Q&A |

LinkedIn Live needs a third-party broadcasting tool (StreamYard, Restream or similar) and cannot go out from a standard device without one (verify current requirements). Registered events let the organiser see attendees and follow up directly; non-registered events capture no attendee data and suit broad awareness only, not lead generation.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| 2 weeks before | Announcement post; start invitations (legacy limit 1,000 per admin per week; verify) |
| 1 week before | Reminder with a key benefit or session preview |
| Day before | Final reminder and any preparation instructions |
| Day of event | Go live or share the link; answer event comments in real time |
| Within 48 hours after | Recap with key takeaways; personalised connection requests to registrants not yet connected, referencing the event |

Registrants are warm because they self-selected on the topic. Drafting follow-ups does not authorise sending them (parent skill).

### 7. KPIs

Review monthly from LinkedIn Page Analytics and report through [meta-reporting](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-reporting/SKILL.md).

Company Page engagement rate (Raymond and Johnston 2021): `(Likes + Comments + Shares + Clicks + Follows) ÷ Impressions × 100`. It is broader than the formula usually used for personal posts (reactions + comments + shares) because page objectives include traffic and follower acquisition, not only discussion.

| KPI | Legacy benchmark (hypothesis; set targets from the baseline) | Review trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate | 2–4% organic | Below 2% for 4 consecutive weeks: more Big Picture and Human Interest, less Products & Services |
| Follower growth | 5–10% monthly in a growth phase | Track net new followers; monitor invitation acceptance separately |
| Post impressions | Rising month on month | Segment by category to see what is distributed |
| Invitation acceptance rate | 20–40% | Below 20%: reassess whom admins invite; quality over volume |
| Event registrations | A target per event | Registered events only; separate from organic posts |
| Click-through rate | 1–3% for posts with links | Links in first comment; use UTM parameters |
| Sub-page follower growth | Positive month on month | If a sub-page is not growing, consider folding it back into the main page |

## Template: thirty-day Company Page content plan

The legacy deliverable is 20 posts over four weeks, each with format, category, hook line, content brief and call to action, all written for the client's industry and audience. The parent skill's pilot rule governs volume: agree an effort budget, review the first complete unit before expanding, and do not invent posts to fill the template. Use these rows as the pattern bank, cutting to capacity. Anonymise client stories where needed and use only approved cases.

| Wk | # | Format | Category | Hook line | Content brief | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Establish | 1 | Text | Human Interest | "We help [target audience] with [outcome]. Here's who we are." | Company, the problem it solves, the team, what makes it different; warm and specific, not a press release | "Follow our page for weekly updates." |
| 1 | 2 | Document/carousel | Products & Services | "What we do — and the problem each service solves." | One slide per service: name / problem / one-line outcome; final slide CTA; 8–12 slides | "Send us a message to find out more." |
| 1 | 3 | Text | Big Picture | "[Industry observation]. Here's what we're seeing." | A specific, credible sector observation; name the pattern and its implication for the audience | "Follow for weekly industry insight." |
| 1 | 4 | Text | Human Interest | "Behind the scenes at [Company]: how we [key process]." | Working reality: preparation, team process, quality control or daily operations | "Comment with a question — we'll answer." |
| 1 | 5 | Poll | Big Picture | "[Provocative, relevant question]?" | 3–4 options covering the range of positions; results follow-up post planned (poll answers are a self-selected sample) | "Vote and tell us your reasoning in the comments." |
| 2 Expertise | 6 | Text | Products & Services | "A client came to us with [problem]. Here's what changed." | Challenge / Approach / Result with specific, approved results; anonymise if needed | "Facing a similar challenge? Send us a message." |
| 2 | 7 | Document/carousel | Big Picture | "The [X]-point checklist every [audience] needs for [outcome]." | Practical framework, one step per slide; hook cover first; CTA last | "Repost if your team needs this." |
| 2 | 8 | Native video | Human Interest | "Here's what a day at [Company] looks like." | 60–90-second walkthrough of team, space and process; subtitles (the source says most LinkedIn video is watched without sound; verify) | "Follow us to see more." |
| 2 | 9 | Text | Big Picture | "Most [audience] don't know about [insight]. Here's why it matters." | A specific insight from client work or sector knowledge | "Save this post for later." |
| 2 | 10 | Text | Products & Services | "Here's what [client type] said after working with us." | Testimonial with context: before, what was done, outcome; attributed with permission or anonymised | "Reach out to discuss your situation." |
| 3 Decision-makers | 11 | Text | Big Picture | "[Common industry practice]. We think it's wrong. Here's why." | A clear, defended position with three arguments; invite respectful disagreement | "Agree or disagree? Tell us in the comments." |
| 3 | 12 | Document/carousel | Big Picture | "[Stat or finding] about [industry]. What it means for [audience]." | 5–8 slides on a sourced trend or finding; practical implication last | "Follow for monthly market insights." |
| 3 | 13 | Text | Human Interest | "What's the hardest part of [challenge]? We'll start." | A genuine question; the company answers first; reply to every comment | "Drop your answer below." |
| 3 | 14 | Event post | Products & Services | "We're hosting [event] on [date]. Here's what you'll get." | Three things attendees will learn or gain; registration link in first comment | "Register via the link in the first comment." |
| 3 | 15 | Text | Human Interest | "We're proud of [team member / achievement]. Here's the story." | Named person (with consent), the achievement and what it means for clients | "Congratulate the team in the comments." |
| 4 Convert | 16 | Text | Products & Services | "If you're a [target client] dealing with [problem], this is for you." | Who the service is for, what they get, the outcome, how to start; no jargon | "Send us a message to start a conversation." |
| 4 | 17 | Document/carousel | Human Interest | "A look back at [month]: what we built, who we helped, what's next." | Client wins, content milestones, team news; final slide previews next month | "Follow to stay updated." |
| 4 | 18 | Text | Big Picture | "One thing we learnt this month that every [audience] should know." | Honest, specific reflection: what happened, what was seen, what changed | "What's a lesson your business learnt recently?" |
| 4 | 19 | Text | Human Interest | "We're growing. Here's who we're looking for — and what it's like to work with us." | If hiring: role, responsibilities, how to apply; if not, a team spotlight | "Tag someone suitable, or apply via DM." |
| 4 | 20 | Text | Big Picture | "That's [month] done. Here's what we focused on — and what's coming." | Honest monthly recap, forward-looking, personal and specific | "Follow along — more coming in [next month]." |
