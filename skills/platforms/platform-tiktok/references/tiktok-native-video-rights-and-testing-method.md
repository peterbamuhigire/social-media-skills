# TikTok native video, rights and testing method

Moved from `SKILL.md` in Social Kaizen S09 (29 Sep 2026, start commit `0e0af8a`); text unchanged. Read when deciding the account, developing native video and series, checking rights and participation, planning production and paid tests, or setting measures.

## Audience, offer and account decision

Confirm audience evidence, buyer situation, commercial objective, original
assets, production capacity and follow-up route. Do not assume an East African
age profile, universal humour preference or optimal video length. Research
language, devices and context for the actual audience.

Verify current account types, profile/link controls, commercial music access,
ads, Shop and creator programmes in the intended country/account. Record what
is available and what remains NOT_ASSESSED. Do not import historical menus,
regional rollouts or eligibility thresholds from a book.

## Develop native video with evidence

Choose a useful demonstration, explanation, process story or expert response.
Open with a real reason to watch and fulfil that promise. Show the product or
method clearly; use a story only when it is true. Match polish to brand and
context. A phone recording is not automatically better than studio production.

Brief opening shot, supporting evidence, sequence, voice/sound, captions,
on-screen text, ending and destination. Use a small number of distinct concepts
before making cosmetic variations. Test length and pacing for comprehension;
do not require a universal three-second rule or forced cuts at fixed intervals.

Develop recurring series from real customer questions, quality distinctions,
process demonstrations and useful comparisons. Each proposed episode needs its
own source and point. Do not invent customer transformations, origin stories,
controversy or local cultural details to make the series feel authentic.

## Rights and participation

Verify commercial rights for music, footage, likenesses and creator material.
Trending audio is not automatic permission or a guaranteed distribution benefit.
Review captions for accuracy and key visuals for readability. Verify Duet/Stitch
availability, settings and reuse rights before suggesting a response format;
avoid repeating unverified claims while attempting to debunk them.

## Production and paid testing

Set a sustainable cadence from quality, staffing and audience evidence. Missing
several days is not treated as a verified distribution reset. Do not lower the
quality bar to meet a posting streak. A thirty-day plan states concepts, evidence,
production tasks, owners, destinations and review dates rather than a fixed quota.

For paid tests, confirm availability, authorisation, offer economics, landing
page, event quality, lead routing, budget cap and stopping rules. Separate
creative response from profitable customer acquisition. Native split/lift tools
require current eligibility and a valid study design; tool availability alone
does not prove incrementality or sufficient sample size.

## Measurement and acceptance

Use native retention/attention data where available to diagnose content, and
trace qualified destination actions, accepted leads, sales, returns and
contribution separately. State windows, denominators, deduplication and tracking
gaps. Do not impose universal completion, share or monthly growth thresholds.

Synthetic case: a trend raises views but attracts buyers outside the service
area. Retain the learning, revise audience/offer clarity and judge qualified
outcomes before scaling. A premium plan passes only when its creative can be
produced truthfully, its rights are clear and its next step can be delivered.
