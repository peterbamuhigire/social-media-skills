# AI-Assisted Recycling Pipeline — Ten Assets From One Long-Form Source

Merged from skills/ai-marketing/ai-content-recycling-pipeline on 2026-09-29 at 7c60138; preservation map: [ai-content-recycling-pipeline.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/ai-content-recycling-pipeline.md)

Parent skill: [`meta-content-repurposing`](../SKILL.md). The parent's Content Factory, repurposing matrix and "never post identical content cross-platform" rule still apply; this reference adds the AI-assisted operating pipeline that turns one written source into ten platform-ready assets in under an hour.

## 1. When to use this reference

- The client has one long-form written or spoken source (blog post, article, interview transcript or video script) and wants a fast, repeatable, AI-assisted way to extract platform-specific assets from it.
- The deliverable is an **operating pipeline specification**: prompts, platform rules, quality gate and time budget that a small team can run each week.
- Use [`ai-readiness-diagnostic`](../../../ai-marketing/ai-readiness-diagnostic/SKILL.md) instead when the real question is whether the team's data, maturity and risk position support AI-assisted production at all.

## 2. Inputs

Ask for:

- Client business name and industry.
- Country/city (default: Uganda).
- Source content — paste the blog post, article or video transcript; **minimum 500 words** (the pipeline is designed for sources of 500–1,500 words).
- Active social platforms (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, X, WhatsApp, YouTube).
- Brand voice description (formal/casual, warm/authoritative, local/international).
- Any topics or phrases to avoid.
- The AI marketing use-case brief: the intended human control point (who approves before anything is published) and the success measure for the pipeline.
- Brand voice, offer facts, constraints and approvals from the client source pack; where absent, state assumptions and do not invent names, prices, results or approvals.
- Performance, platform or research evidence behind any claim, from a traceable export, URL, document or named source; where absent, draft the narrowest reviewable version and flag the missing evidence.

## 3. Core principle

Most East African marketing teams create content once and publish it once. That wastes the research, thinking and writing invested in each piece. AI makes it practical to extract ten distinct, platform-optimised assets from a single source in under 60 minutes without the output feeling repetitive (Roth and neuroflash, 2024).

The constraint is not content creation; it is content *extraction and reformatting*. AI removes that constraint. The human reviewer remains the release gate.

## 4. Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Data readiness, AI maturity and risk support the proposed operating level | Choose the lowest viable automation level and define its human approval gate before running any prompt. | Automating an unsafe or unevaluable marketing process. |
| Source is under 500 words or has no clear insight, statistic or quotable line | Ask for a fuller source or run fewer assets; do not pad. | Thin, repetitive derivatives. |
| A required fact, price or approval is missing | Stop that claim; request it or leave an explicit placeholder. | Fabricated facts, implied consent or unauthorised publication. |
| Platform not in the client's active list | Skip that asset; do not produce it "for completeness". | Wasted effort and off-strategy output. |
| AI output reads as a reworded copy of another asset | Regenerate or rewrite so each asset stands alone for its platform. | Cross-platform duplication (parent skill hard rule). |
| An asset has not had human review and approval | Do not schedule or publish it. | Publishing raw AI output. |

## 5. Procedure — the ten-asset pipeline

**Source:** one long-form piece of 500–1,500 words — a blog post, article, interview transcript or video script. Paste it into the AI tool, then run each prompt below in turn (replace `[industry]` with the client's industry and adjust the market if the client is outside Uganda).

**Asset 1 — Facebook post (100–150 words)**

```
Extract the most surprising or counterintuitive insight from this article. Write a Facebook post that opens with that insight as a hook, explains it in 2–3 sentences, and ends with a discussion question. Warm, conversational tone. No hashtags.
```

**Asset 2 — Instagram caption (50–80 words plus hashtags)**

```
Write an Instagram caption based on this article. Start with an emotional hook (not a question). Keep it under 80 words. Add up to 5 relevant hashtags at the end — a mix of branded, niche, and community tags for [industry] in Uganda.
```

**Asset 3 — LinkedIn post (150–200 words)**

```
Write a LinkedIn post based on this article. Start with a professional insight or data point. Use short paragraphs. End by inviting peers to share their experience. No hashtags. Professional but not stiff.
```

**Asset 4 — TikTok / Reels script (30 seconds, spoken)**

```
Write a 30-second TikTok script based on this article. Hook in the first 3 seconds (bold statement or question). 3 punchy points. Strong CTA at the end. Written to be spoken aloud — conversational and energetic.
```

**Asset 5 — X / Twitter post (under 280 characters)**

```
Extract the single most quotable sentence or statistic from this article. Rewrite it as a standalone X post under 280 characters. Add 2 relevant hashtags.
```

**Asset 6 — Instagram carousel outline (5 slides)**

```
Structure this article as a 5-slide Instagram carousel. Slide 1: bold hook/title. Slides 2–4: one key point each with a 10-word headline and 15-word supporting line. Slide 5: summary + CTA. Output slide-by-slide.
```

**Asset 7 — WhatsApp broadcast message (under 700 characters)**

```
Write a WhatsApp broadcast message based on this article. Under 700 characters. Start with the most useful takeaway. One clear CTA at the end (visit link / reply for more / book now).
```

**Asset 8 — Email newsletter paragraph (80–100 words)**

```
Write an 80-word paragraph for a client email newsletter based on this article. Professional, warm tone. Include a 'Read more' link placeholder. Make the reader curious enough to click.
```

**Asset 9 — Quote card text (one sentence)**

```
Extract or craft the single most shareable, standalone sentence from this article — something that works as a quote card. Under 20 words.
```

**Asset 10 — Podcast / audio outline (3 minutes, spoken)**

```
Structure this article as a 3-minute podcast segment outline: 30-second intro (hook + what we'll cover), 4 x 30-second points, 30-second outro (summary + CTA). Written for spoken delivery.
```

Organise the output by asset type so the client handover is clear.

## 6. Platform adaptation rules

| Platform | Practical limit | Tone | Key rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 150 words | Conversational | End with a question or CTA |
| Instagram | 80 words + hashtags | Emotional, visual | Hook in the first line |
| LinkedIn | 200 words | Professional insight | Invite discussion |
| TikTok / Reels | 30-second script | Energetic, direct | Hook in 3 seconds |
| X / Twitter | 280 characters | Punchy, opinionated | One idea only |
| WhatsApp | 700 characters | Direct, warm | Actionable CTA |
| Email | 80–100 words | Helpful, personal | Drive to one action |

These are practical working limits for the pipeline, not platform maxima; verify any current platform limit before quoting it to a client.

## 7. Quality gate (before any asset is published)

- [ ] Does it make sense as a standalone piece, with no context needed from the other assets?
- [ ] Does the tone match the client's brand voice?
- [ ] Is there a local Uganda/East Africa reference or example where appropriate?
- [ ] Has it been read aloud (for spoken-word assets: TikTok, WhatsApp, podcast)?
- [ ] Are hashtags relevant and not overused (Instagram: at most 5 per post or reel caption, register `INSTAGRAM-HASHTAG-LIMIT-PRIMARY`)?
- [ ] Has a human reviewed and approved it — never published directly from AI output?
- [ ] Has it passed the repository [`anti-ai-slop`](../../../ai-marketing/anti-ai-slop/SKILL.md) ship gate? A blocking factual, cultural, safety or permission defect stops release.

## 8. Time benchmark

- Running all 10 prompts: 15–20 minutes.
- Human review and light editing: 30–40 minutes.
- **Total: under 60 minutes for 10 platform-ready assets.**

## 9. Acceptance checklist for the pipeline output

- All 10 asset types are generated from the source content (or an asset is skipped with a stated reason, such as the platform not being active).
- Each asset is platform-specific, not a copy-paste of the same text with minor edits.
- The platform adaptation rules are applied (character limits, tone, format).
- The quality gate checklist is completed before any asset is published.
- The time benchmark is achieved (under 60 minutes total), or the overrun is recorded with its cause.
- Brand voice is consistent across all 10 assets.
- Output is organised by asset type for clear client handover.
- At least one asset references a local Uganda/East Africa context, event or example.
- A decision and gap note names the automation level chosen, the evidence used, unresolved inputs and any action requiring authority.

## 10. Anti-patterns

- Running the prompts before the objective, audience and approver are known. **Fix:** stop and obtain the missing brief fields.
- Reusing a neighbouring skill's template because the headings look similar. **Fix:** route by the requested operating pipeline specification, not vocabulary overlap.
- Adding a price, result, quotation, platform limit or cultural claim that the AI produced without a traceable source. **Fix:** verify it, or qualify or remove it.
- Treating missing access, evidence or native-language review as approval. **Fix:** mark the check `not assessed` and narrow the result.
- Scheduling assets from drafting authority alone. **Fix:** obtain explicit, action-specific publishing authority and keep the approval record.
