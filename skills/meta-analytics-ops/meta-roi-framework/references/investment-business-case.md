# Investment Business Case

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the deliverable is not an ROI model for an existing programme but a board-ready business case, or the inputs for a board presentation, that persuades a sceptical leadership team to fund social media at all. Frame the argument in the language C-suite executives use: competitive risk, customer acquisition cost, financial return and market position. Many Ugandan clients at banks, NGOs, manufacturers and large SMEs run without a dedicated social media budget; this reference gives the consultant the frameworks, calculations and wording to change that. The ROI calculation inside the case uses the SKILL.md formula, ROI = (TLV − COCA) ÷ COCA.

Typical triggers:

- The client's leadership team or board doubts social media's business value.
- A new proposal includes a social media retainer and is challenged on ROI grounds.
- The client has never allocated a dedicated social media budget.
- An existing budget is up for renewal and the finance team wants justification.
- A competitor has recently launched a strong social media presence and the client needs to respond.
- A donor or parent organisation asks for evidence of digital engagement as part of a grant renewal.

Sources: Funk (2013) *Advanced Social Media Marketing*, Apress — primary source for the "Risk of Ignoring" framing and fan/follower valuation; Bodnar and Cohen (2012) *The B2B Social Media Book*, Wiley; Chaffey (2024) *Digital Marketing: Strategy, Implementation and Practice*, Pearson; Reichheld (2003), cited via Chaffey (2024).

## Inputs

Collect from the client or the brief before drafting:

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name | Exact trading name |
| Industry | For example commercial banking, manufacturing, NGO, hospitality, FMCG |
| Country / city | Defaults to Uganda / Kampala if not stated |
| Primary goal | For example increase sales, defend market share, grow brand awareness, attract donors |
| Decision-maker audience | Who will read or receive the case: board, CEO, finance committee, donor |
| Current social media presence | None, minimal, or existing but unfunded |
| Known competitor social activity | What direct competitors are doing on social media |
| Approximate annual marketing spend | Calibrates the budget recommendation; if the client declines to share, use industry-tier estimates |
| Previous social media attempts | Has the client tried and stopped before? If so, what happened? |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership responds to threat more than opportunity | Lead with the Revenue at Risk figure (Step 1), framed as business risk, not a missed opportunity | A case that reads as optional spend |
| No measured follower data exists yet | Use the fan/follower valuations (Step 2) and label them modelled estimates; recommend an A&U study to validate | Presenting a model as a measured result |
| Client has a research budget (UGX 15,000,000+ for a credible study in Uganda) | Commission a full A&U study through a research agency | Weak evidence of brand equity effect |
| No research budget | Run the lightweight A&U (Step 3) | No brand-equity baseline at all |
| Client has no marketing budget to take a percentage of | Present the three Uganda/EA budget tiers (Step 5) instead of the 5–15% rule | An unanchored budget ask |
| Finance team is risk-averse | Present the 90/10 core versus test-and-learn split and a Starter-tier start with a month-3 review gate | Budget rejected as open-ended |

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Quantify the risk of ignoring (Funk, 2013)

Core argument: every month a competitor runs an active social media programme, it builds an audience the client cannot reach. That audience is revenue at risk.

```
Revenue at Risk = Competitor Audience Size × Estimated Conversion Rate × Average Order Value
```

1. Record the competitor's follower count on its most active platform (publicly visible).
2. Apply a conservative conversion rate: 1–2% for B2C; 0.5–1% for B2B. Cite Funk (2013) as the basis for conservative estimates.
3. Multiply by the client's average order value or annual customer value.
4. Present the result as an annual figure (monthly estimate × 12).

Worked example (Ugandan commercial bank):

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Competitor Facebook page followers | 45,000 |
| Estimated conversion rate | 1% |
| Average new account value (first-year fees + average deposit product) | UGX 480,000 |
| **Revenue at Risk** | 45,000 × 1% × UGX 480,000 = **UGX 216,000,000/year** |

Show every assumption and invite the decision-maker to adjust the conversion rate. A conservative, honest calculation is more credible than an inflated one; the aim is to show the risk is real and quantifiable, not to produce an impressive number.

### Step 2 — Value the fan/follower base

Show that a following has a calculable financial value before any campaign spend.

**Method A — Impression valuation (advertising equivalency)**

```
Monthly Advertising Value = (Followers × Organic Reach Rate × Posts per Month) ÷ 1,000 × CPM
```

| Facebook CPM, Uganda | USD | UGX |
|---|---|---|
| Low estimate | 0.30 | 1,100 |
| Mid estimate (default) | 0.55 | 2,000 |
| High estimate | 0.80 | 3,000 |

Source note: Meta Ads Manager observed rates, Uganda market, 2023–2024 — dated; verify before stating (no register record).

State these assumptions explicitly: Facebook organic reach for a page that posts consistently is 5–8% of followers per post; posting frequency is 12–16 posts per month (3–4 per week). Verify before stating (no register record). Facebook availability in Uganda has changed recently; check register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026 before building a Facebook-based case.

Worked example: 10,000 followers, 6% reach per post, 14 posts per month, CPM UGX 2,000.
Monthly impressions = 10,000 × 6% × 14 = 8,400. Advertising value = (8,400 ÷ 1,000) × UGX 2,000 = **UGX 16,800/month** in organic media value.

**Method B — Loyalty premium valuation**

If followers convert at 2–3× the rate of non-followers (Funk, 2013), the follower base carries a loyalty premium.

1. Establish the client's baseline conversion rate in the general market (from CRM, sales data or an industry benchmark).
2. Apply a 2× multiplier for social followers (the conservative end; cite Funk, 2013).
3. Calculate the incremental revenue from the follower base at the higher rate.
4. Present the difference as the loyalty premium.

State that this is a modelled estimate, not a measured result, and recommend an A&U study (Step 3) to validate it over time.

### Step 3 — Attitude and Usage (A&U) study

An A&U study compares brand perception, purchase intent and loyalty between followers and non-followers. It is the most rigorous way to show social media's effect on brand equity.

- **Full A&U study:** a research agency surveys a representative sample of followers and a matched sample of non-followers, measuring brand awareness (prompted and unprompted), purchase intent, Net Promoter Score and brand attribute ratings. Commission it for clients with research budgets (UGX 15,000,000+ for a credible study in Uganda).
- **Lightweight A&U (no research budget):**
  1. *Follower survey* — a 5-question poll via Instagram Stories or a WhatsApp broadcast to existing followers: likelihood to purchase, brand rating (1–10), whether they have recommended the brand, and top brand associations.
  2. *Non-follower benchmark* — compare follower answers with any available market data: earlier brand health surveys, industry NPS benchmarks, or customer satisfaction scores from the client's CRM.
  3. *Gap analysis* — present the difference between follower and general-market perceptions as evidence of the programme's effect on brand equity.

Use the findings three ways: as a "brand equity delta" metric in the business case; as a baseline for tracking improvement once investment begins; and as evidence for the board that the audience already built has measurable value.

### Step 4 — Integrate Net Promoter Score (NPS)

NPS measures the share of customers who would actively recommend the brand. Social media speeds up word of mouth, which feeds NPS:

- Active social communities generate more advocates (Promoters).
- Responsive community management turns Passives into Promoters and stops Detractors escalating.
- Every 1-point NPS improvement correlates with measurable revenue growth (Reichheld, 2003, cited via Chaffey, 2024).

Simple NPS survey via WhatsApp:

1. Send a broadcast to the client's customer contact list: *"On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend [Brand] to a friend or colleague?"*
2. Follow with one open question: *"What is the main reason for your score?"*
3. Calculate NPS = % Promoters (9–10) − % Detractors (0–6).
4. Record the baseline; repeat quarterly.

Present NPS as a financial outcome: estimate the client's average customer lifetime value (CLV); apply the rule of thumb that a 5-point NPS improvement correlates with 1.5–3% revenue growth (Chaffey, 2024); show the projected revenue effect of improving NPS from the current baseline. Label this a directional estimate and recommend tracking NPS alongside social activity to build the client's own correlation over 12–18 months. Broadcasts to customer lists need a lawful basis and consent records — see register UG-DPPA-2019.

### Step 5 — Allocate the budget

Rule of thumb: social media budget = 5–15% of total marketing spend (Chaffey, 2024). Where the client has no existing marketing budget, present the tiers:

| Tier | Monthly budget | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | UGX 500,000–1,000,000 | First-time social media investment; 1–2 platforms; content only |
| Growth | UGX 2,000,000–5,000,000 | Established brand; 2–3 platforms; content + paid amplification |
| Scale | UGX 10,000,000+ | Market leader or aggressive growth phase; full-platform programme |

These tiers cover consultant/agency fees plus content production. Paid media (boosted posts, ads) is a separate line. Tier figures are undated market estimates — verify before stating (no register record).

Include these line items in every budget proposal:

1. Content production — copywriting, photography direction, graphic design briefs.
2. Community management — monitoring, responding and moderating across platforms.
3. Paid amplification — boosted posts and targeted ads, kept separate from agency fees.
4. Tools and software — scheduling, analytics, social listening (for example Hootsuite, Buffer, Brandwatch).
5. Reporting and strategy — monthly performance reports and quarterly strategy reviews.

90/10 split: put 90% of the budget into proven core activity (content production and community management on the primary platform) and reserve 10% for testing new formats, platforms or audience segments. Present this to sceptical finance teams as disciplined, low-risk budget management.

### Step 6 — Present to the board or C-suite

Language to use:

| Use this | Because |
|---|---|
| "Market share" | Finance and strategy language |
| "Competitive risk" | Triggers risk-management thinking |
| "Customer acquisition cost" | Connects to financial KPIs the board already tracks |
| "Lifetime value" | Frames social media as an investment, not a cost |
| "Share of voice" | Shows market position against competitors |
| "Brand equity" | A recognised metric in marketing finance |

Language to avoid:

| Avoid | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "Going viral" | "Achieving broad organic reach" |
| "Engagement" | "Customer interactions" or "community responses" |
| "Likes" | "Brand affinity signals", or omit entirely |
| "Content is king" | Present data; avoid clichés |
| "Everyone is on social media" | Platform penetration data for Uganda specifically |

Objection handling:

- **"Our customers are not on social media."** Answer with data. Facebook has approximately 3.2 million users in Uganda (Meta, 2024) — dated; verify before stating (no register record; for current platform reach see register DATAREPORTAL-UG-KE-2026). WhatsApp is the dominant messaging channel, but no verified share of smartphone users exists, so state no percentage (Kaizen register WA-01, 2026-09-24; see register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026). Ask for the client's target customer profile and present platform demographics that match it. Offer a lightweight A&U poll on WhatsApp in the first 30 days to verify.
- **"We tried it before and it didn't work."** Ask what was tried, for how long, and how success was defined. Usually the earlier attempt had no budget, no strategy, no dedicated resource and no defined KPIs. Acknowledge the failure honestly, present this case as the difference between an unfunded experiment and a managed investment with defined outcomes and a review cadence, and commit to a 90-day review gate.

## Template — business case document

Produce the case in exactly this order (eight numbered sections, suitable for direct submission to a board or finance committee):

1. **Executive Summary** (one paragraph) — what is proposed, what it costs, the expected return. Write it last; present it first.
2. **Current Situation and Competitive Risk** — the client's current social position (absent or minimal); competitor activity with follower counts and engagement estimates; the Revenue at Risk calculation (Step 1).
3. **Proposed Investment** — recommended budget tier with rationale; platforms to prioritise and why; agency/consultant scope of work in plain language.
4. **Expected Outcomes** — Month 3: follower growth target, content volume, share-of-voice estimate. Month 6: community size, lead generation or enquiry volume, website referral traffic. Month 12: brand awareness lift (from A&U or NPS data), sales influence estimate.
5. **ROI Calculation** — apply (TLV − COCA) ÷ COCA from the SKILL.md. Define TLV (total lifetime value of a customer acquired via social) and COCA (cost of customer acquisition through the social programme). Present two scenarios: conservative and realistic.
6. **Risk of Not Investing** — restate Revenue at Risk; add reputational risk: a brand absent from social media when a crisis or negative conversation starts cannot respond. Frame this as risk management.
7. **Recommended Starting Budget** — tier, monthly cost and 12-month total, phased: start at Starter, review at month 3, scale to Growth if targets are met.
8. **Success Metrics and Review Cadence** — monthly: reach, impressions, follower growth, community response rate; quarterly: NPS, lead volume, an A&U data point; annually: full ROI review using (TLV − COCA) ÷ COCA.

## Release checklist

- [ ] All financial calculations are transparent: each figure shows its formula, assumptions and data source; no black-box numbers.
- [ ] Revenue at Risk is present and specific, using actual competitor follower data or a clearly stated proxy, with the conversion-rate assumption stated.
- [ ] Budget figures are calibrated to Uganda/EA, in UGX with USD equivalents, and tiers are realistic for the Ugandan market.
- [ ] Board language is used throughout; no social media jargon in the executive summary or recommendation sections.
- [ ] Two ROI scenarios (conservative and realistic) use (TLV − COCA) ÷ COCA.
- [ ] Both common objections (customers not on social media; previous failure) are anticipated and answered.
- [ ] The document follows the eight-section template in order.
- [ ] Modelled estimates (follower valuation, loyalty premium, NPS revenue effect) are labelled as estimates, not measured results.
- [ ] British English throughout, in a professional East African register consistent with [east-african-english](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md).
