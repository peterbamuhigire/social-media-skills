# Posting Time and Frequency Tests

Merged from skills/meta-analytics-ops/meta-posting-optimisation on 2026-09-29 at 8eacccb; preservation map: [meta-posting-optimisation.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/meta-posting-optimisation.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when a client asks "when should we post, and how often?" and needs a client-specific weekly posting schedule (day, time in EAT, platform, format, content pillar and direction) rather than the general ranking-signal guidance in the SKILL.md.

"Best time to post" articles report global averages that are wrong for most individual accounts and systematically wrong for Ugandan and East African audiences, who use social media at different times from US or UK users. The answer comes from the client's own analytics, backed by East African market context and a 4-week structured test, not from published studies. The §5 frequency benchmarks and §6.1 peak times (moved from SKILL.md to [platform-ranking-reference.md](platform-ranking-reference.md) in S09) are general comparators; where this reference and those tables differ, the client's own test result decides.

## Inputs

Confirm all seven before producing any output:

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name and industry | Trading name and sector |
| Country and city of the primary audience | Sets the time zone and EA window assumptions |
| Platforms in scope | Every active platform |
| Current posting frequency | Posts per week, per platform |
| Access to native analytics | Direct login by the consultant, or screenshots supplied |
| Team capacity | Hours per week available for content production |
| Primary audience age range and city | Affects which EA time windows apply |

If timestamped post performance cannot be obtained, return only the supported sections with a recovery list; label any EA baseline window as provisional.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Account has 90+ days of native analytics | Derive windows from the platform's own activity data (step 1) | Applying global or regional averages to an account with its own evidence |
| New account or fewer than 90 days of data | Use the EA baseline windows, labelled provisional; replace with native data as soon as it exists | Stalling for data that does not yet exist, or presenting defaults as findings |
| Team cannot sustain a frequency at full quality | Set frequency at the highest level quality can hold, within the platform floor and ceiling | Engagement-rate decline that suppresses every later post |
| Engagement per post has fallen as frequency rose in the past | Reduce frequency before running the 4-week test | Testing on top of an already over-stretched schedule |
| WhatsApp Broadcast frequency above 3 a week proposed | Cap at 3 a week; agree the cap with the client and record the rationale | Opt-outs from a list users treat as a trusted inner circle |
| Week 2 engagement rate lifts 5 percentage points or more over Week 1 | Treat the time shift as working | Attributing noise to timing |
| End of Week 4 | Apply the adopt / maintain / revert-one-variable rule without discretion | Open-ended "evaluate performance" |
| Engagement rate drops more than 10 % in any rolling 2-week period | Trigger an early schedule review | Running a failing schedule to the next quarterly check |

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Read the client's own analytics first

Extract peak activity from native analytics on every platform in scope before recommending any schedule. Menu paths are platform interface claims: verify at use (no register record).

| Platform | Where | Extract | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | Meta Business Suite → Insights → Audience → When Your Fans Are Online | The 3 highest-activity hours for each weekday (Monday–Friday); the 1 highest-activity hour for Saturday and for Sunday separately; any consistent daily pattern (for example a morning spike every weekday) | Shows when fans are online, not when they engage; the 4-week test confirms whether presence becomes engagement |
| Instagram | Professional Dashboard → Insights → Total Followers → Most Active Times | Hourly activity by day of week | Compare directly with Facebook: the same audience is often active at different times on each platform even when demographics overlap |
| LinkedIn | Company Page → Analytics → Followers → Follower Activity | Activity by day of week only (no hourly data) | Use for day-of-week decisions, not time-of-day. EA baseline: Monday–Thursday highest, Friday noticeably lower, weekends minimal; confirm against the client's follower data |
| TikTok | Creator Tools → Analytics → Followers → Follower Activity | Hourly activity chart | Use for TikTok timing and for Instagram Reels cross-posting decisions |
| YouTube | YouTube Studio → Analytics → Audience → When viewers are on YouTube | Hour-by-hour activity across the week | Use to schedule video publication and, where applicable, Premieres |

### Step 2 — Use EA baseline windows only when no prior analytics exist

Apply to new accounts or accounts with fewer than 90 days of data; replace with native data as soon as it is available. All times East Africa Time (EAT, UTC+3). These are unregistered practitioner baselines: label them provisional (verify before stating, no register record).

| Platform | EA peak windows |
|---|---|
| Facebook | 07:00–09:00 (morning commute), 12:00–13:30 (lunch), 20:00–22:00 (evening) |
| Instagram | 12:00–14:00, 19:00–21:00 |
| TikTok | 19:00–23:00 |
| LinkedIn | 07:00–09:00, Monday–Thursday only |
| WhatsApp Broadcast | 07:00–08:30 or 19:00–20:30 |
| X / Twitter | 07:00–10:00 (news cycle), 17:00–19:00 |

Why EA times differ from global benchmarks (explain this to the client):

- Uganda is UTC+3. Global "optimal time" studies usually report US Eastern or UK times, 8–11 hours behind EAT; applying them directly gives the wrong schedule.
- **Boda-boda commuting:** mobile use spikes 07:00–09:00 during the commute across all income segments.
- **Afternoon dip:** 14:00–17:00 is a productivity window for office workers; social engagement is low.
- **Evening spike:** 19:00–22:00 is the highest-engagement window for consumer content: users are home, often on WiFi, with discretionary time.
- **Data-cost sensitivity:** use clusters around WiFi access (office in the morning, home in the evening); mobile data costs shape when people scroll.

### Step 3 — Set frequency per platform

The right frequency is the highest one at which full quality can be kept. Quantity without quality lowers engagement rate, which lowers algorithmic reach, which weakens every later post. Never give one "post X times a week" instruction across all channels.

| Platform | Minimum (viable) | Optimal (EA context) | Maximum (before quality drops) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 3/week | 5/week | 7/week |
| Instagram Feed | 3/week | 5/week | 7/week |
| Instagram Stories | 3/week | 5–7/week | Daily |
| Instagram Reels | 1/week | 3/week | 5/week |
| TikTok | 3/week | 5/week | Daily |
| LinkedIn | 2/week | 3–4/week | 5/week |
| YouTube | 1/week | 1–2/week | 3/week |
| WhatsApp Broadcast | 1/week | 2/week | 3/week |
| X / Twitter | 3/week | 5/week | Multiple daily |

WhatsApp Broadcast: more than 3 broadcasts a week materially increases opt-outs in EA markets, where users treat broadcast lists as a trusted inner circle. Keep frequency conservative and content useful.

Ask and record, per platform in scope:

1. Can the team produce this many posts a week at full quality (brand voice, visual standards, QC passed) within confirmed capacity?
2. Is there enough to say at this frequency, or does it force filler that dilutes the account's editorial value?
3. Has engagement per post dropped as frequency rose in the past? If yes, reduce frequency before the optimisation test.

### Step 4 — Run the 4-week optimisation test

Run it when setting a new schedule or reviewing an underperforming one. Each week isolates one variable so any change in performance can be attributed.

| Week | Variable | What to do | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Baseline | None | Post at the existing schedule (or EA baseline windows for a new account) | Impressions; reach; engagement rate (engagements ÷ reach × 100); best-performing post (format, day, time, topic); worst-performing post (format, day, time, topic). This is the benchmark for Weeks 2–4 |
| 2 — Time shift | Timing | Keep Week 1 frequency; move all posts to the peak windows from native analytics (or EA baseline) | Same five metrics; a lift of 5 percentage points or more on engagement rate confirms the time shift works |
| 3 — Frequency | Frequency | Keep Week 2 timing; add 1 post a week on the primary platform only | Same metrics. Engagement rate per post holds or improves: the increase is sustainable. It declines: the ceiling is reached; revert to Week 2 frequency |
| 4 — Content type | Format | Keep Week 2 timing and Week 3 frequency; replace 2 static image posts with 2 Reels (or the equivalent format test for the platform) | Same metrics plus save rate and shares, which show format preference |

Decision rule at the end of Week 4, applied without discretion:

- Engagement rate improved against Week 1 → adopt the new schedule permanently.
- Engagement rate held but reach increased → keep the change (more reach at the same engagement rate is a net gain).
- Engagement rate declined → identify which variable (timing, frequency or content type) caused the drop and revert only that one.

### Step 5 — Produce the posting schedule document

A weekly table, one row per post slot, with every cell populated (never leave pillar or content direction blank). Use the pillars from [10-content-pillars](../../../pipeline/10-content-pillars/SKILL.md) and schedule into [11-content-calendar](../../../pipeline/11-content-calendar/SKILL.md) once confirmed.

| Day | Platform | Time (EAT) | Format | Pillar | Content direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Instagram | 12:00 | Carousel | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Monday | Facebook | 20:00 | Video | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Tuesday | LinkedIn | 08:00 | Static image | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Wednesday | Instagram | 19:30 | Reel | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Wednesday | WhatsApp | 19:00 | Broadcast message | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Thursday | Facebook | 12:30 | Static image | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Thursday | X / Twitter | 08:00 | Text post | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Friday | Instagram | 13:00 | Story | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |
| Saturday | TikTok | 20:00 | Short video | [Pillar name] | [Topic and angle] |

Below the table, add three notes:

1. **Analytics source:** whether times come from native analytics or the EA baseline, and the analytics export date used.
2. **Review trigger:** when to review next (recommended after 4 weeks of data, or if engagement rate drops more than 10 % in any rolling 2-week period).
3. **WhatsApp frequency cap:** the broadcast frequency agreed with the client and the reason for the cap.

## Release checklist

- [ ] Timing comes from native analytics, not global benchmark articles; any EA baseline use is labelled provisional.
- [ ] EA baseline windows cover all six platforms with UTC+3 times and the reasons they differ from global studies.
- [ ] Frequency is specific per platform; no single cross-channel instruction.
- [ ] The 4-week test isolates one variable per week so any change is attributable.
- [ ] The Week 4 rule is binary (adopt / maintain / revert a specific variable), not open-ended.
- [ ] The schedule is a fully populated weekly table usable in 11-content-calendar without further editing.
- [ ] WhatsApp Broadcast timing and the 3-a-week ceiling are addressed, with the opt-out risk noted.
- [ ] All times are in EAT (UTC+3).
