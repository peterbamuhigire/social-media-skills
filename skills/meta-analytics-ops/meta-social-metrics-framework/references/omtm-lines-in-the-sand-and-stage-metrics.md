# Choosing the Focus Metric, Setting Targets and Matching Metrics to the Business Stage

Read this when choosing KPIs for a strategy, retainer, campaign or dashboard; when a client wants to track "everything"; when setting targets; or when deciding whether a business is ready to scale paid acquisition. Parent skill: [`meta-social-metrics-framework`](../SKILL.md). Derive targets from the client's own model or trend; no external benchmark is held here.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Business model (shop, subscription, app, media or creator, community, marketplace, service) | Client | Ask; the metric set depends on it |
| Current evidence of demand, repeat use, referrals and unit economics | Client data | Place the business at the earliest stage the evidence supports |
| The riskiest assumption right now | Strategy discussion | Stop and name it before choosing a metric |
| Baselines with source and date | Analytics, CRM, sales records | Mark `not assessed`; collect before setting a target |

## 2. Admitting a metric to a report

A metric earns a place only if it:
- can be compared (period, cohort, segment or competitor);
- is simple enough for the team to remember and discuss;
- is a rate or ratio rather than a running total (ratios can also hold two forces in tension);
- changes a decision — write in advance what you will do if it rises, falls or stays flat.

Tag each KPI on five axes: qualitative or quantitative; vanity or actionable; exploratory or reporting; leading or lagging; correlated or proven causal (causation needs a controlled test). Every KPI table carries a "decision rule" column.

**Totals are not growth.** Hits, page views, visits, unique visitors, followers, likes, time on site, emails collected and downloads are vanity when presented as growth — except where the business sells attention (a publisher's page views) or when rating one piece of content. Replace them with rates of people acting: share active, enquiries per 100 reached, activated accounts, engaged time on content pages.

**Quick test for an operating ratio:** it should be simple, immediate, actionable, comparable and fundamental to the business.

## 3. Stages and what to measure at each

| Stage | Question the business must answer | Focus metric | Evidence to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem proof | Do enough people feel this problem and already try to solve it? | Share of interviewees raising the problem unprompted; willingness to pay or introduce others | At least 15 problem and 15 solution interviews with consistent findings |
| Repeat use | Do the right customers come back and get value? | Share active; retention by cohort; churn; time between visits | Use as intended; churn low enough for the model |
| Word of mouth | Do customers bring customers? | Referral multiplier; time to first invite; share of active customers who share | Referrals materially lower acquisition cost |
| Revenue | Can money be made repeatably? | Revenue per customer; lifetime value to acquisition cost; payback | Unit economics meet the model |
| Scale | Can channels, regions and partners grow it without hand-holding? | Payback by channel or region; support cost; channel efficiency | Proven market and a working operating rhythm |

Pass the stages in order: buying growth before repeat use is proven burns cash. Businesses that need both sides of a network present at once may seed both early.

## 4. One focus metric (OMTM — Croll and Yoskovitz)

Use one metric the whole team works on for a period, because it answers the most important current question, forces a target, and focuses effort on experiments.

**Warning signs:** the team cannot name the focus metric, names one that does not fit its stage, names several, or does not know the current value.

**Procedure**
- List the three to five numbers reviewed daily and remove the vanity ones.
- Name the riskiest assumption for the current stage.
- Choose the rate that measures that risk most directly; record its current value, source and date.
- Set a target (section 5).
- Ask "if we worked only on this, what could break?" and name three to five guard-rail metrics.
- Revisit when the target is reached or when improving it exposes the next bottleneck.

Retainer use: agree one focus metric per quarter with its guard-rails ("this quarter we move one number"). Dashboard use: the focus metric is the hero tile with value, target and trend; everything else is drill-down.

## 5. Setting a target (a "line in the sand")

A target is a pre-committed number that decides the next action. Take it from one of three places:
- **The business model** — what the plan needs (for example, the share of users who must upgrade).
- **A dated comparison** from similar businesses, with source, year and market stated.
- **The client's own ceiling** when neither exists — plot daily results of an improvement effort, fit a trend line, read where it flattens and compare with what the model needs.

Move a target only when segment-level evidence shows value at the new level (for example, customers using the service less often than assumed but still paying and renewing). Never lower it just to pass; improve the capability instead.

**Target worksheet:** metric · current value (date, source) · target · where the target came from · review date · action if reached · action if missed · who may change it and on what evidence.

## 6. Metric sets by business model

| Model | Core measures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Online shop | Conversion, purchases per year, basket size, drop-off by checkout step, cost per customer, lifetime value, searches with no result, stock availability | Judge the business mode from repeat purchase in 90-day cohorts: low repeat means spend on acquisition (loyalty schemes rarely pay), high repeat means invest in loyalty. Prefer revenue per visitor to conversion alone. Email contribution = incremental revenue − campaign cost − value of unsubscribes lost |
| Subscription | Sign-ups, activation, upgrades, revenue per customer, cost per customer, upsell, uptime, churn (state the denominator), lifetime value | Average life ≈ 1 ÷ monthly churn; time to recover acquisition cost = cost per customer ÷ monthly revenue or contribution per customer |
| Free app | Installs, first launch, engaged users, daily-to-monthly active ratio, share paying, revenue per user and per payer | Read churn timing: loss after day one points to onboarding, after week one to depth, after month one to updates |
| Media and creators | Audience, audience churn (new first-time visitors − net change in unique visitors), inventory, ad rates, content-to-ad balance | Test how much heavier ad load costs in lost audience |
| Community and user content | Movement through lurking → reacting → commenting → joining → posting → creating, by monthly cohort; returning within the week; notification-to-action rate | |
| Marketplace | Active and engaged buyers, active sellers and listings, searches returning a result, search → listing → purchase → satisfied purchase | Focus on the side with the money; defend against off-platform leakage (escrow, reveal identity after commitment) |
| East African service models (engine extension) | Mobile-money agent network: active agents, float-outs, transactions per agent · WhatsApp shop: enquiry-to-paid-order rate within 7 days · SACCO or savings group: active savers, savings streaks, repayment · NGO programme: reach-to-outcome ratio · Tour operator: enquiry-to-deposit rate by source | Set lines from the client's own data |

## 7. Reading cohorts

Show the same data three ways: by calendar month (often flat), by joining-month cohort (cohorts differ), and aligned by months since joining (the true lifecycle curve and where customers drop). Separate free from paying churn, define "inactive" explicitly, and use cohort or daily churn for fast-growing bases.

## 8. Operating rhythm

- **Monthly:** leaders review three big assumptions, each with a metric and a target.
- **Weekly:** three actions per assumption are reviewed.
- **Daily:** experiments are logged with winning criteria written in advance.
- **Weekly learning canvas:** the aim is to learn; current status and key metrics; lessons; on track — yes or no (answer honestly); top three problems ranked, each with candidate fixes, a metric and a target for the next one to two weeks.

## 9. Reporting language

- "This month's cohort retained [x]% at day 30, up from [y]% for the [previous] cohort; the change coincides with [intervention]."
- "Reach rose, but engaged enquiries per 1,000 reached fell from [a] to [b]; we are treating this as a warning, not a win."
- Do not write "viral", "traction", "engagement" or "data-driven" without a definition, a denominator and the decision it changed.
