# Competitive Matrix and Analysis Procedure

Read this when a positioning, offer, advertising or content decision needs an evidence-based comparison of competitors. Parent skill: [`meta-competitor-analysis`](../SKILL.md). Every cell is dated evidence: record the source and access date. Traffic, audience and funding figures from third-party tools are estimates — label them as such.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| The decision the comparison must inform | Client brief | Stop; a matrix without a decision becomes a directory |
| Named competitors the client already fears | Client and sales team | Build the list from section 2 |
| The customer's own words for the need | Interviews, reviews, WhatsApp enquiries | Mark search-term coverage `not assessed` |

## 2. Decide who counts as a competitor

- **Direct:** the same promise to the same buyers.
- **Indirect:** the same buyers served with a different offer, or the same offer aimed at other buyers. Watch for rivals whose side-line is your client's main line — they can move in cheaply.
- **Substitute behaviour:** what buyers do instead (a relative's help, a market stall, doing nothing).

Find them where buyers look, not only where the client looks: names buyers mention in interviews and reviews; searches written in the customer's words, read past the first page of results; ad libraries (register AD-04, checked 2026-09-23); marketplaces and directories; trade and association lists; staff and supplier gossip. Aim for at least five direct and three indirect rivals, mixing incumbents and newcomers; fewer is acceptable only if the market genuinely is smaller, and the brief must say so.

## 3. Build the matrix (one row per competitor)

Group the columns into five blocks so the review reads as a story rather than a spreadsheet dump:

| Block | Columns to fill |
|---|---|
| Identity | Web and app/profile addresses (separate rows if the mobile and desktop experiences differ greatly); founding year; ownership or funding where public |
| Promise | One- or two-sentence value proposition in your words; the three differentiators they actually demonstrate; price position |
| Business model | How they earn (fees, commission, advertising, subscription, product margin); range size (listings, products, services) as a proxy for scale; traffic or audience estimate with tool and date |
| Experience | Main categories (copy their navigation); content formats and depth; personalisation (saved items, profiles, messaging); community or customer-content features; active (not merely registered) social channels; a quick A–F grade answering four questions — can a visitor finish the main task, is navigation consistent, is finding and searching easy, are errors and feedback handled well |
| Market voice | Recurring customer complaints and praise (reviews, app stores, forums, comments); open questions for the team; the analysis notes you add in section 4 |

Practicalities: use a shared test account created for the purpose (never a personal social login); allow roughly half an hour per competitor for a first pass (an engine working estimate); photograph or save evidence with the date.

**Worked example (illustrative):** a Kira Road physiotherapy clinic compares four clinics and two home-visit services. The Experience block shows only one rival lets patients book on WhatsApp; the Market-voice block shows repeated complaints about waiting times. The finding: lead ads on booking speed, not on equipment.

## 4. Turn data into judgement

1. Mark the strongest and weakest cell in each column so patterns jump out.
2. Cluster rivals that play the same game (for example "cheap and fast", "premium and personal").
3. Score each cluster against the same yardstick — the client's chosen positioning and the buyer's top criteria.
4. Write a findings note: the open gaps, the rivals' exploitable weaknesses, practices worth borrowing (including from businesses outside the category), and a recommendation tied to the decision in section 1, with evidence for each point.

Decision rules: if two rivals already own the client's intended claim, choose another claim or prove superiority with evidence; if every rival shares a weakness customers complain about, that weakness is the opening; if the gap exists only in your own opinion, test it with customers before building a campaign on it.

## 5. Quick capability grid

When a slide or proposal needs one visual, put capabilities in rows and the client plus its main and secondary rivals in columns, marking present / partial / absent. Visual layout routes to `design-system-skills`.

## 6. Ethics

Use only public or legitimately obtained information. No pretexting, no approaching rivals' staff for confidential information, no copying of their creative. Any comparative claim that comes out of this work needs evidence and legal review before publication.
