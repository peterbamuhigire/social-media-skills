# Traction Goal, Milestone Path and Channel Arithmetic

Read when setting the traction goal, deciding what counts as a result worth chasing, or turning a long activity list into a milestone plan. Parent skill: [traction-channel-bullseye](../SKILL.md). Formulas are standard marketing arithmetic; example numbers are scenarios.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| One headline goal and the change it unlocks (break-even, loan eligibility, investor readiness, a second branch) | Owner | Stop; a plan without a consequence cannot rank activities |
| Allowable cost per customer and contribution per sale | Finance lead / `chwezi-accounting-doctrine` | Use a labelled assumption and flag it |
| Current stage of the offer (still proving demand, tuning the message, or scaling) | Evidence of repeat purchase and referrals | Assume the earliest stage the evidence supports |

## 2. Decision rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| An activity does not move the headline goal | Put it on the not-doing list, even if it is attractive |
| Something can be bought or borrowed rather than built | Buy or borrow first |
| The offer is still proving demand | Hand-run, unscalable tactics (personal emails, talks, guest posts) are acceptable |
| The message is still being tuned | Favour channels that give fast, repeatable feedback |
| The model works and must grow | Favour channels that can grow without the founder |
| A channel cannot plausibly produce enough results | Exclude it this year — write the threshold, e.g. "fewer than 40 qualified enquiries a month at ≤ UGX 60,000 each is out of scope" |
| The current channel is still growing | Start the next test before it flattens; growth tends to come in steps as channels open and saturate |

## 3. Building the milestone path

- Start from the headline goal and list everything that might help: hires, features, campaigns, partnerships.
- Strike out anything that is not strictly necessary.
- Order what remains by dependency and work only on the next milestone.
- Give each person or team dated, numbered sub-goals on the same calendar as the product or service plan.
- Hold a short weekly check ("are we still on the path?") and a fuller review whenever a milestone is reached.
- Publish the not-doing list next to the plan.

Slot template: "Our year-one goal is [metric] by [date], because reaching it [changes X]. Everything in this plan is on the path to that goal or excluded."

## 4. Traction runs alongside building

Plan customer acquisition in parallel with building the product or service — roughly as much effort as the build itself — not after launch (Weinberg and Mares). Put waitlists, pre-orders and pilot customers into the plan. Before blaming the channel, check the four reasons a wanted offer still fails: nobody will pay, the market is too small, the market is too expensive to reach, or competitors hold it.

## 5. Channel arithmetic

| Measure | Formula | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate | clicks ÷ impressions | Message pull |
| Cost per click | spend ÷ clicks | Media price |
| Conversion rate | customers ÷ visitors | Page and offer strength |
| Cost per customer | cost per click ÷ conversion rate (UGX 1,500 ÷ 5% = UGX 30,000) | Compare with the allowable cost |
| Value per click | revenue or margin ÷ clicks | A small rise can turn a losing campaign profitable |
| Reach needed | customers wanted ÷ conversion rate | Is the channel big enough? |
| Referral multiplier (K) | invites per customer × share of invitees who join | Word of mouth strength |
| Cost per customer after referrals | paid spend ÷ (paid customers ÷ (1 − K)), for K below 1 | Referral as a cost reducer, not "going viral" |
| Event break-even | total event cost ÷ contribution per sale | Always contribution, never price |

At equal K, a shorter time from joining to inviting grows the base much faster.

## 6. Testing discipline

- Set up tracking first — even a spreadsheet with codes and a "how did you hear" question.
- Keep tests small (two ads, two pages); optimise many variants only once a channel shows promise.
- Expect most tests to lose; look first for large changes (new offer, new onboarding, new follow-up sequence) before fine-tuning.
- Compare channels on cost per customer and lifetime value per channel, not on reach.
- Assume every channel weakens as it gets crowded; keep one small experiment running at all times.

## 7. Free tools as a lead source

A genuinely useful free tool tied to the offer — a calculator, checker or grader — can keep producing leads long after launch. Give instant value, gate the detailed result behind contact details collected with consent, follow with a short email or WhatsApp course that ends in an offer, and verify any tax or statutory rate inside the tool before release.
