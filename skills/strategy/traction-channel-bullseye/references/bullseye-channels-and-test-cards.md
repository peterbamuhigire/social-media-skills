# Channel Inventory, Selection Workshop and Test Cards

Read when running a channel-selection workshop, writing test cards or deciding which channel to fund next. Parent skill: [traction-channel-bullseye](../SKILL.md). All platform, price and legal specifics are checks to run, not facts.

## 1. Inputs to collect before the workshop

| Input | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The traction goal and what reaching it changes | Decides which channels can move the result this year |
| Allowable cost per customer (agreed with finance) | The pass line every test is judged against |
| Customer profile and where they already spend attention | Removes channels the buyer never meets |
| How comparable and neighbouring businesses won customers, including failures | Stops the team repeating dead ends |
| Tracking readiness | No channel is tested until a result can be attributed |

## 2. Decision rules for the workshop

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| A channel is dismissed without evidence | Name the reason; if it is unfamiliarity, dislike or effort rather than evidence, keep one idea for it on the long list |
| Competitors avoid a channel | Treat it as a possible temporary advantage, not proof it fails; test cheaply |
| More than three channels look promising | Fund three in parallel; park the rest with a review date |
| Budget allows only one test at a time | Run the three in sequence, strongest hypothesis first |
| A test fails | Diagnose which part failed — message, audience, offer or tracking — before dropping the channel |
| A focused channel's cost rises or volume flattens | Re-open the long list; channels saturate as others crowd in |

## 3. Workshop procedure (Bullseye — Weinberg and Mares)

1. Write at least one realistic idea for every channel in the inventory (section 4), so no channel is skipped out of habit.
2. Score each idea on a shared sheet: chance it works (1–5), expected cost per customer, how many customers are available at that cost, and how long a test would take.
3. Sort the ideas into three rings: most promising now, possible later, long shot.
4. Take the top three into capped, parallel tests using the test card (section 5).
5. Put resources behind the channel whose numbers pass, keep improving it, and set a date to re-run the workshop.

## 4. Channel inventory, grouped by how the customer is reached

Each row gives a cheap first test, what to measure, and East African forms to consider. Verify availability, prices and rules before use.

### Owned and product-led

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral and sharing built into the offer | Add one referral reward for existing customers for a month | Invites per customer, share of invitees who join, time from join to first invite | WhatsApp forwards and group invites; airtime or mobile-money credits (check promotion rules) |
| Content hub | Publish consistently on one theme for a set period after a small pilot | Enquiries and sign-ups traced to content; links and pick-ups | LinkedIn articles, YouTube explainers, WhatsApp Channels, local data stories |
| Lifecycle messaging | A short sequence for new sign-ups | Activation, replies, first purchase | Email for B2B; WhatsApp Business and SMS for consumers (opt-in and approved templates) |
| Free tool or calculator | Build one small, useful tool with a consented gate for the full result | Users, consented leads, lead-to-customer rate | School-fees, loan-repayment, solar-sizing or website-speed checkers (verify any statutory rate) |
| Community | Start a small group and see whether it grows by referral | Active members, member-to-member replies, referrals | WhatsApp communities, alumni and professional groups, customer clubs |
| Organic search | Buy search ads on candidate terms first; invest in organic pages only for terms that convert | Rankings, organic enquiries | Google Business Profile, local-language pages, first-hand local guides |

### Earned attention

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Press and publicity | Place one genuine story in a niche outlet | Placements, visits and sign-ups per placement | Business desks, talk-radio business shows, trade-association newsletters |
| Attention-getting acts and customer appreciation | One low-cost stunt or visible act of appreciation | Mentions, visit spikes, cost per earned mention | Market-day activations, handwritten notes in deliveries (public-order and cultural checks) |
| Niche publishers and hosts | Sponsor or pitch a handful of niche publishers | Sign-ups per placement, cost per sign-up | Niche YouTubers, podcasters, Facebook group and WhatsApp channel admins (paid placements disclosed) |

### Paid media

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid search | Small budget, a few ads, two landing pages | Click-through, cost per click, conversion, cost per customer | Google Ads with call and WhatsApp options → `advertising/paid-search-advertising` |
| Paid social and display | Small buys on one platform and one niche site | Click-through, landing conversion, cost per customer | Meta, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn; direct buys on niche sites → `playbooks/playbook-paid-social-advertising` |
| Offline media | One radio week or one press insertion carrying a unique code | Responses per code, cost per response and per order | FM and vernacular radio, classifieds, billboards, taxi and matatu branding, bulletins (outdoor permits) |

### Partnerships and platforms

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distribution and business partnerships | A scored list of 50–100 possible partners; approach the top ten | Meetings, signed deals, customers per deal | Telcos, banks, SACCOs, distributors, hotel concierges |
| Commission partners (affiliates and agents) | Recruit the first partners from existing customers | Sales per partner, commission versus allowable cost | Agent networks, referral fees (withholding tax via the finance engine) |
| Existing marketplaces and platforms | List on one platform and work its rankings | Listings, platform-sourced customers, ratings | Marketplaces, booking platforms, WhatsApp Business catalogue |

### Sales-led

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct selling | Founder-led outreach to a short list of ideal buyers | Meetings, close rate, days to close | Relationship selling, WhatsApp follow-up, deposits to confirm intent |

### Event-led

| Channel | Cheap first test | Measure | East African forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade fairs and expos | Visit as an attendee first, or interview past exhibitors | Pre-booked meetings, qualified leads, deals within 90 days | National and regional trade fairs, sector expos |
| Own small events | A breakfast briefing or clinic for a named group | Attendance fit, pipeline created | Breakfast briefings, SME clinics with banks, roundtables |
| Speaking | One free talk to a relevant group | Talks booked, post-talk responses, deals | Chamber and association forums, CPD sessions, LinkedIn Live, radio slots |

## 5. Test card

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Channel and idea | e.g. sponsored post in three parenting WhatsApp channels in Wakiso |
| Assumptions to validate | Cost per customer ≤ UGX [x]; ≥ [n] enquiries a month available; enquirers match the target |
| Cap and duration | UGX [amount] / [n] days |
| Tracking | Unique link, QR, code or WhatsApp keyword; "how did you hear" field |
| Success line | ≥ [n] qualified enquiries at ≤ UGX [x] each |
| Legal and consent check | Lawful basis, disclosure, platform policy — owner and status |
| Result and decision | Focus / retest / drop, with the reason |

## 6. Harvesting versus creating demand

Search and marketplaces catch people already looking; social, display, events and publicity reach people who are not yet looking. Channels that create demand usually convert later, so judge them on audience built and assisted sales as well as immediate orders, and put paid money behind content that already shows organic pull rather than behind sales pages alone.

## 7. Working notes by channel type

| Channel type | Practice |
|---|---|
| Publicity | Start with small, specialist outlets that larger ones watch; pitch only real milestones; bundle minor news; give the journalist a human angle; follow up later with how the result was achieved |
| Partnerships | Start from what the partner gains; choose partners by fit, not fame; keep the first terms to one page; record how each deal was done; move from hand-run to standard integration as volume grows |
| Direct selling | Qualify on buying process, need, authority, budget and timing; grade leads by expected time to close (inside a quarter, within a year, longer — the last group stays with marketing nurture); build the sales path from the buyer's questions |
| Trade fairs | Set goals, check attendee fit against your price, budget by quarter, book meetings before the event, carry a coded offer, follow up within two days |
| Speaking | Ask organisers what their audience needs; keep one or two core talks built in modules; end with one clear next step |
| Community | Seed it with people you already serve, give it a purpose larger than the product, set conduct rules early |

## 8. Ethics and compliance

Never buy contact lists, links or engagement. Disclose paid endorsements (see `pipeline/08-influencer-marketing-strategy`). Contact data for outreach follows `business-development/biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach`.
