# Brand Partnership Guide (Creator Side)

**When to read:** when a creator, artist, athlete or public figure needs to become easy to book, prepare a press kit or partnership page, price a deal, answer an offer, check a contract or keep a partnership going. Parent skill: [strategy-personal-brand](../SKILL.md). Brand-side vetting and budgeting live in [creator due diligence and pricing](../../../pipeline/08-influencer-marketing-strategy/references/creator-due-diligence-and-pricing.md). The guide is the engine's own; it draws on buyer-side casting practice described by Hennessy, B. (2018) *Influencer*, Citadel Press. No US fee figures or dated platform tactics are used.

## Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Profiles, audience insights screenshots (dated) | The creator | Collect before pitching; mark audience fit `not assessed` |
| Past partnerships and results | The creator | Present as a new creator; do not imply history |
| Rate history and production costs | The creator | Build a talent-fee sheet first (section 3) |
| Offer or contract received | Brand or agency | Do not accept in principle without it |

## 1. Readiness checklist (fix before any pitch)

| Area | Ready when |
|---|---|
| Identity | One handle everywhere; website links to the correct live profiles; clear recent profile photo |
| Bio | Says what the person creates, their town, a brand-contact email and their first name |
| Show reel | A short talking-to-camera video, refreshed each quarter |
| Contact | A direct email for brands (never a form only); out-of-office with an urgent route |
| Content balance | Organic content clearly outweighs sponsored content (a common working rule is about 70/30); no repeat promotion of rival products in one category |
| Integrity | No bought followers, likes or comments; no engagement groups or automation services |

## 2. Partnership pack

| Piece | Contents |
|---|---|
| About page | Headshot; a few lines written to the audience; the show reel; three to five posts showing range |
| Partnership page | A second headshot; what the person does in one line and three descriptors; roots and current base; background; searchable specifics (languages, districts reached, causes, skills, faith or community roles if the person is comfortable); collaboration types offered (ambassadorships, sponsored posts and videos, live coverage, events, shoots); the line "Rates depend on scope, usage and exclusivity, and I'm happy to hear about any budget." |
| Contact page | Separate addresses for brands and for readers or press |
| Press kit / one-sheet | The same content as a designed PDF / a single page. **No prices.** No wall of past-client logos (it can deter competitors); link a sponsored-work archive instead |
| East African additions | Languages; dated screenshots of WhatsApp Status or Channel views; radio and TV appearances; community affiliations; verified payment identity |

Layout and design of the pack route to `design-system-skills`.

## 3. Pricing a deal

1. **Talent fee** = production costs (photographer or editor, location, props, wardrobe, make-up, transport, data bundles) + the creator's hours (briefing, research, scouting, mood board, shoot, edit) at an agreed hourly rate.
2. **Distribution fee** = the value of reaching the audience, judged on size, engagement, content quality, recognition and special skills, and how precisely the audience matches the brand.
3. Add explicit amounts for **usage** beyond the brand's own channels, **exclusivity** beyond a short, narrow window, and **peak seasons**.
4. If the fee cannot move, trade on terms: deposit, split payment, faster payment, or non-cash value (a brand feature or reshares).
5. Keep a dated log of your own quotes and outcomes; there is no verified local rate card. Payments to individuals may carry withholding tax — check with `chwezi-accounting-doctrine`.

## 4. Replies and pitches (templates)

| Situation | Template |
|---|---|
| Pitching for coverage | "I noticed [product] at [place], and it fits my [audience or need] because [real reason]. I reach [dated figure] on [platform]. I could write a [length] piece with my own photos and share it on [channel]. A short quote from [founder] would make it personal. Shall I send my one-sheet?" |
| Accepting in principle | "Thank you for thinking of me for [campaign]. I'm free on [date] and the budget suits this scope. Please send the full brief or contract so I can check terms and usage before confirming." |
| Counter-offer | "I'd like to work with [Brand]. For [scope] my fee is nearer [amount], mainly because of [usage, exclusivity or production]." |
| Declining on budget | "Thank you for the offer. At this budget I can't give the campaign the quality it deserves. If the budget changes, I'd be glad to look again." |
| Declining on fit | "Thank you for considering me. I don't think this would land with my audience, and I'd rather you got a strong return elsewhere." |
| Proposing an ambassadorship (within about three days of a good result) | "[Campaign] ended on [date] with [verified result]. With [season or event] coming, I'd like to propose a [duration] partnership: [format]. Outline attached — we can agree the numbers once the idea fits." |

## 5. Contract check before signing

Legal name versus handle and payee details match; who is hiring and for which product; shoot date, place and hours; exact deliverables and formats; go-live window and no deleting or archiving before it ends; exclusivity scope and duration (compare competing offers before signing); usage rights (the brand's own channels with credit as the baseline; everything else priced); fee, terms and deposit; travel and expenses agreed before the fee; disclosure duties; what happens if the brief is missed. Take anything beyond a simple gifted post or single paid post to a lawyer.

## 6. Delivering and keeping the relationship

Share a mood board before shooting; deliver a few extra edited images; post an organic Story with the product only if it genuinely fits; send a thank-you note. Within a week, share native analytics screenshots plus clicks or code redemptions, and record what worked.

## 7. Disclosure and safety

Label every paid or gifted partnership clearly and upfront in plain words **and** with the platform's branded-content tool (FTC and ASA/CMA standard, register AD-09, checked 2026-09-23). No specific Uganda or Kenya influencer-disclosure law was found as of 2026-09-23 (register AD-10), so apply the same standard and check the regulators named there before each campaign. Put talking points in your own words. Children: never show a child's home or school; post location only after leaving; obtain guardian consent; never force a child to pose.
