# Expert bio templates, introductions and elevator pitches

Read when writing a speaker bio, a host's introduction, a profile "About" section, a proposal team bio or an elevator pitch. Parent skill: [strategy-personal-brand](../SKILL.md). Sources: Vaden (2023) "Portrait View" bio principle as already used in the parent skill; Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press (stature and introductions); Brown, R. (2016) *Build Your Reputation*, Capstone/Wiley (elevator pitches, back story, points of difference). Every number and named institution in a bio must be true and verifiable.

## 1. Stature principle

The same claim is dismissed from an unknown person and taken seriously from a credentialed one. A bio and a host's introduction should carry earned stature — credentials, discoveries or methods, body of work, recognisable client types — so that resistance falls before the meeting. Never inflate a credential or imply an endorsement.

## 2. Bio formula

Credential or discovery → body of work (publications, talks, programmes) → known clients or recognition → what they do for this reader.

**50 words:** "[Name] [does what] for [whom]. [One named, numbered proof]. [Publication, talk or programme]. [What the reader gets from them today]."

**150 words:** add the method or framework the person is known for, two proofs with numbers and dates, and one human detail (a cause, a community role).

**300 words:** add the back story (origin, the problem seen repeatedly, the turning point, what was built), and a short list of recent engagements.

Replace landscape phrases with portrait detail: "experienced professional" → "ran paid-search programmes for [n] private hospitals in Uganda and Kenya since [year]" (verify before use). Avoid "passionate", "guru", "ninja", "results-driven", unverified "award-winning".

## 3. Host introduction script

"[Name] leads [firm], which has [distinct achievement with a number]. [He/She/They] wrote [publication] and has advised [recognisable client types]. Today [Name] will show you [specific outcome]."

Send this to every host in advance; do not leave the introduction to chance.

## 4. Elevator pitches

Prepare 10-second, 30-second and two-minute versions, with variants per audience.

- **30 seconds:** "I help [specific audience] [achieve outcome] without [common pain]. Last quarter, for example, [short proof story]. Right now I'm working on [current project], which is why I'm here."
- **Point of difference:** "Yes, we [parity item] like everyone else. The difference is that we also [point of difference], which means you [benefit]."
- **Category of one:** "We're the only [category] in [market] that [distinctive mechanism] for [niche]." (Only if true and checkable.)

## 5. Back story (60–90 words)

"I started in [origin]. I saw [problem] again and again: [concrete scene]. After [struggle or turning point], I built [method or solution]. Today I [what you do] so that [who] can [outcome]. That's why I care about [cause]."

## 6. Three-line profile bio

Line 1: credibility (credential or proof). Line 2: the problem solved. Line 3: a human connection detail.

## 7. Before and after

- "Sarah is a passionate digital marketing expert with years of experience." → "Sarah has run paid-search programmes for [n] private hospitals in Uganda and Kenya and writes a monthly health-marketing column in [publication]. On this project she owns the booking-conversion targets." (verify each fact)
- "Results-driven professional passionate about excellence and innovation." → "I turn Uganda's scattered SME data into decisions. My annual SME digital readiness survey is cited by [named outlets]." (only if true)
