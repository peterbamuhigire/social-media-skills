## When to use this reference

Read this reference when a service business or consultant sells through Instagram direct messages: scripting openers, qualifying prospects, presenting the offer with a UGX price, handling objections, moving serious buyers to WhatsApp, and running the DM inbox as a labelled pipeline. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns catalogue, WhatsApp ordering, Mobile Money, fulfilment and commerce content; this reference owns the conversation that turns an Instagram follower into a WhatsApp close. Drafting scripts does not authorise sending messages; every opener is sent by a person, with approval.

Evidence status. Instagram Business features (Quick Replies, conversation labels, away messages, the shortcut-label length), Instagram's treatment of automated or low-reply outreach, WhatsApp in-app payment with Mobile Money, and the benchmark rates below are legacy figures with no register record: verify before stating, and present benchmarks as planning targets. WhatsApp messaging after the move follows the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY: keep opt-in provenance, honour opt-outs, use approved templates outside the 24-hour customer-service window).

## Context

In East Africa the DM-to-sale path is a primary conversion route for service businesses and consultants. Instagram surfaces warm prospects (people who have followed, commented or reacted to content) and a DM is the natural next step. Most East African buyers expect a personal conversation before committing to a service purchase, particularly at UGX 500,000 and above. Scripting every stage lets the client's team work consistently, qualify efficiently and close on WhatsApp, where trust and transaction tools (MTN MoMo, Airtel Money) are strongest.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| The prospect has not invited a sales conversation | Ask permission once or stop | Harassing private outreach |
| Writing an opener | Reference something specific (profile, a post they commented on, a story they reacted to); never open with a pitch or a price; ask one question | Openers ignored or reported as spam |
| Qualifying | Ask one question at a time and wait for each reply | A wall of questions that reads as an interrogation |
| Prospect not qualified | Do not present the offer | Conversation time spent on non-buyers |
| Interest shown after the offer (follow-up question, timing query, price engaged without hard objection) | Move to WhatsApp | Closing in a channel buyers trust less for transactions |
| 48 hours of silence after the offer | Send one follow-up only | Chasing a polite decline |
| Two consecutive non-replies | Archive; do not chase | Wasting time better spent on new conversations |
| Asked to automate openers | Refuse; use AI only for drafting and analysis | Reach and account restrictions (legacy claim; verify) and loss of the personal reference that makes the method work |

## Procedure: the 5-stage DM sequence

### Stage 1 — Opener

Purpose: start a conversation that feels human, not salesy; a cold-feeling opener is ignored or reported as spam.

Templates to adapt:

- "Thanks for the comment on [post] — great point about [topic]. Are you working in [industry]?"
- "Saw your story about [topic]. We work with businesses like yours — what's your biggest challenge with [relevant problem] right now?"
- "Hey [name], noticed you've been following us for a while. Are you working on [relevant goal]?"

Generate three to five custom openers from the client's actual post topics, story themes and offer; each must reference a real content type the client publishes.

Do not:

- open with "Hi! I saw your profile and wanted to reach out about an exciting opportunity" — it marks the account as spam immediately;
- send mass openers with no personalisation — low reply rates reduce reach for all the account's content (legacy claim; verify before stating).

### Stage 2 — Qualify

Purpose: confirm the prospect is a genuine fit before investing more time. Four-question framework (adapt the wording to the offer):

| # | Dimension | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Need | "What's your current situation with [problem area]?" |
| 2 | Timeline | "Is this something you're trying to solve now, or are you exploring for the future?" |
| 3 | Decision | "Is it just you making this decision, or are there others involved?" |
| 4 | Investment | "When businesses work with us, the investment is typically in the [UGX range] — is that within what you're looking at?" |

Generate adapted versions of all four using the client's offer and price range.

### Stage 3 — Present the offer

Purpose: show what the client does in terms of the prospect's problem, not product features; the prospect should feel heard before hearing the pitch.

1. Restate their problem in their own words (active listening).
2. Describe what the client does as a direct solution.
3. State the specific result, with a timeframe.
4. Give a price or price range in UGX.

> House template (not a quotation): "Based on what you've shared, it sounds like [their problem in their words]. What we do is [solution in plain English]. Most clients in your situation see [specific result] within [timeframe]. Our [service/product name] starts at UGX [X]. Does that make sense for where you are?"

Complete the template with the client's offer, typical outcomes and price range; leave no brackets unfilled in the final deliverable.

### Stage 4 — Handle objections

Objections are requests for reassurance or information, not rejections.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "It's too expensive" | "I understand. What were you hoping to invest? Sometimes we can work with different options." |
| "I need to think about it" | "Of course. What would help you decide — more information, a sample, or speaking to someone who's used this before?" |
| "I need to ask my partner/boss" | "Makes sense. Would it help if I put something together you could share with them?" |
| "I'm not ready yet" | "That's fine. When would be a better time to check back in — next month, next quarter?" |
| "Let me check your website first" | "Sure. While you're doing that, what's most important for you to find out?" |

**East African cultural note — the indirect "no".** In Ugandan professional culture a direct written refusal is uncommon. "I'll think about it", or silence after the offer, is often a polite decline rather than genuine deliberation. Protocol: after 48 hours without a reply send one follow-up only (for example "Hey [name], just checking back on this — still relevant for you?"); after two consecutive non-replies archive the conversation and stop; do not read silence as "needs more time" — spend that time on new conversations.

Generate adapted responses for any objections specific to the client's industry or price point.

### Stage 5 — Move to WhatsApp

Purpose: take serious prospects to WhatsApp, where trust, voice notes and document sharing make closing easier. Move after Stage 3 once genuine interest shows.

> House template (not a quotation): "This is getting into detail that's easier on WhatsApp. Can I drop you a message there? My number is +256[X]. Or send me yours."

Why WhatsApp in East Africa:

- Voice notes build personal connection faster than typed DMs; many buyers are more comfortable speaking than writing.
- Proposals, price lists and contracts send and display cleanly.
- Payment with MTN MoMo and Airtel Money can complete without friction; the legacy claim that the client can pay without leaving the app needs verification before stating (no register record).
- Buyers trust WhatsApp more than Instagram DMs for commercial transactions; it signals seriousness on both sides.

Hand the conversation into the parent skill's WhatsApp order and payment-confirmation workflow; for WhatsApp set-up use [platform-whatsapp](../../../platforms/platform-whatsapp/SKILL.md).

## DM operations

- **Response time:** reply to all incoming DMs within 2 hours during business hours (EAT, UTC+3). Set an away message for evenings and weekends so prospects know when to expect a reply.
- **Quick Replies:** save the most-used messages as Quick Replies in Instagram Business settings (via the inbox; legacy shortcut-label limit of 60 characters — verify). Suggested labels: `opener1`, `qualify1`, `offer`, `wa-move`, `follow-up`.
- **Conversation labels:** track the pipeline with `Lead` / `Qualified` / `Sent Offer` / `Moved to WA` / `Won` / `Lost` / `Follow-up`. Review all labelled conversations weekly and follow up every `Sent Offer` conversation older than 3 days that has not moved to `Moved to WA`, `Won` or `Lost`.

For wider Instagram account strategy use [platform-instagram](../../../platforms/platform-instagram/SKILL.md).

## Benchmarks (planning targets; verify before stating)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Opener reply rate | 30%+ |
| Qualified to offer presented | 50% of conversations reaching Stage 2 |
| Offer to WhatsApp move | 40% |
| WhatsApp to close | 20–30% (services priced UGX 500,000 and above) |

Present these as a 3-month baseline target, not immediate results; new accounts or newly structured processes typically take 4–6 weeks to reach them as messaging is refined.

## AI in DM selling

Use AI (see [prompt-engineering-library](../../../content-writing/prompt-engineering-library/SKILL.md)) to draft opener variations for new campaigns, generate objection-response options for human review and selection, and analyse label data to suggest pipeline improvements. Do not use AI to send automated DM openers: the specific reference to a comment, story reaction or recent post is why the method works, and automation removes it.

## Deliverable structure

1. **Opener templates** — five personalised openers referencing the client's actual content.
2. **Qualification script** — four adapted questions in sequence, with pacing instructions.
3. **Offer presentation** — completed template with the client's service, outcomes and UGX pricing.
4. **Objection responses** — full table adapted to industry and price point, including the cultural note.
5. **WhatsApp move script** — completed with the client's WhatsApp number.
6. **Operations checklist** — Quick Reply labels, conversation-label system, weekly review prompt.
7. **Performance benchmarks** — targets table with the 3-month baseline note.

## Release checklist

- All five stages are scripted with completed templates, not described in the abstract; no brackets left unfilled.
- Openers are personalised to the client's actual content; no mass-blast copy.
- The qualification framework includes the investment question with the client's UGX price range stated.
- The WhatsApp move script is explicit and set in the context of East African buyer behaviour.
- Objection responses are specific and actionable, not "handle objections thoughtfully".
- The indirect-refusal note is included with the 48-hour / two-non-reply protocol.
- Benchmarks are in UGX-relevant service terms with a realistic time horizon and marked for verification.
- British English throughout, in the East African professional register of [east-african-english](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md).
