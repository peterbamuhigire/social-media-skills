# Premium Social Selling Gate

Use this gate for premium, executive, affluent, enterprise, or high-ticket social/digital selling.

## Audience And Positioning

- Define one primary premium buyer and one secondary influencer at most.
- State what the buyer wants to be seen as: successful, safe, modern, exclusive, competent, strategic, generous, discerning, or in control.
- State what the buyer wants to avoid: wasted time, public mistakes, poor quality, low status, implementation risk, weak ROI, or generic service.
- Position with a clear point of view, not only service descriptions.

## Content System

- Authority content: diagnosis, trends, mistakes, case studies, executive lessons, benchmarks, teardown, frameworks.
- Trust content: process, proof, founder expertise, client outcomes, behind-the-scenes quality, risk controls.
- Conversion content: diagnostic offer, private consultation, webinar/briefing, lead magnet, case study, invitation, limited availability.
- Relationship content: comments, direct messages, replies, introductions, event follow-up, community participation.
- Digital PR content: original data, expert commentary, campaignable stories, useful visuals, buyer guides, industry roundups, and credible opinions that journalists, partners, or executives can reference.
- Product/website packaging content: show the premium experience, process, quality checks, before/after decisions, and proof assets without turning the feed into a portfolio dump.

## Lead Capture

- Lead magnet must filter for quality: audit checklist, calculator, executive briefing, benchmark, diagnostic, buying guide, tender readiness score, premium product checklist.
- Avoid giveaways that attract freebie seekers unless they are tied to a qualifying next step.
- Every lead capture must have a follow-up sequence and sales owner.

## Email And Nurture

- Use a sequence that moves from problem awareness to proof to decision: welcome, problem framing, insight, case proof, objection/risk handling, invitation.
- Segment VIPs and high-intent leads separately.
- Use personal replies and human follow-up for high-value prospects; do not rely only on automation.
- Treat unsubscribes from low-fit leads as healthy list quality control.

## Outreach

- Research the account or individual before contact.
- Lead with a relevant trigger, problem, or insight, not a generic introduction.
- Ask for a small, clear next step.
- Follow up with useful context, not "just checking in".
- Use referrals and warm introductions wherever possible.
- Create a reason to meet: benchmark result, diagnostic finding, relevant introduction, event context, strategic risk, premium opportunity, or tailored teardown.
- For elite buyers, keep outreach short, specific, respectful of status, and connected to a decision they already care about.

## Measurement

- Track high-intent actions: replies, meeting requests, diagnostic bookings, proposal requests, stakeholder introductions, qualified opportunities, and revenue.
- Do not judge premium social selling mainly by likes or follower count.
- Review lead quality monthly with sales or the owner handling sales.

## Red Flags

- Luxury/premium product marketed with discount-heavy content.
- LinkedIn content sounds like generic motivation instead of expertise.
- Lead magnets collect emails but do not qualify prospects.
- Outreach asks for time without giving a reason worth attention.
- Sales follow-up is slower than the buyer's urgency.
