# B2B Customer Grading, Contact Economics and Account Growth

Read when defining active customers, grading accounts, planning contact, growing accounts or setting acquisition limits for a B2B client or for the agency's own retainer book. Parent skill: [strategy-b2b-customer-community](../SKILL.md). "Contact" covers email, WhatsApp, newsletters, calls, meetings and visits. All figures in examples are scenario data.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction history by individual buyer | CRM, invoices, mobile-money records | Start from invoices; flag gaps |
| Cost of each contact type (digital touch, call, visit, review meeting) | Finance lead | Use labelled estimates |
| Sales-and-service cost ceiling as a share of revenue | Client finance | Agree before building the plan |
| Reasons customers buy and leave | Calls and interviews (section 3) | Run the calls before grading |

## 2. Define the customer and the record

- Write a testable definition: "An active customer has spent at least [amount] in the last [12] months and ordered at least once every [3] months" (add rules such as a second purchase within six months).
- Label every account active, at risk, lapsed or former.
- Keep records at five levels — person, buyer group (role or use), site, company, group of companies — and tie every transaction to the person; store both job title and functional role.
- Watch individuals, not totals: an unchanged account count can hide people leaving.

## 3. Find what really drives buying

| Method | How | Output |
|---|---|---|
| First-purchase call | Thank the buyer soon after the first order and ask why they chose you over the alternatives | Real reasons in the buyer's words |
| Lost-customer interview | Classify the reason: price, product, service, business closed or moved, switched to a substitute, internal politics | Which losses are winnable; do not spend on the rest |
| Attribute comparison | List every product, service, operational and commercial attribute (delivery time, quote speed, invoice accuracy, payment terms); score competitors on the same list; remove what everyone does; mark where you are clearly better; ask customers to rank the shortlist **and** rate its importance to buying | The few attributes that actually decide purchases — an attribute ranked high but unimportant to buying is not a driver |

Check each driver against a real, repeatable capability. Expect a handful of drivers per segment (a working heuristic) and segment by shared drivers and behaviour, not industry code alone.

## 4. Let customers design the contact plan

- Log every contact for about two months by department, direction and medium.
- Run a short workshop per segment with customers, sales and marketing: each group lists and ranks the content it values; merge into one list.
- Ask customers how often they want each item.
- Build the grid: content item × times per year × channel, marked preferred, acceptable or support only.
- If workshops are too costly, collect the same answers a few questions at a time during routine calls.

Agency version: hold a yearly client council (six to eight retainer clients, lunch and 90 minutes) to decide which reports, insight notes and reviews they value and in which format (WhatsApp summary, PDF, dashboard, meeting).

## 5. Grade accounts and match contact to value

**Decision rules**

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Very large "house" accounts | Give them a bespoke key-account plan (see the key-accounts reference) |
| Tiny occasional buyers | Serve reactively through low-cost channels |
| Everyone else | Grade into five bands (AA, A, B, C, D) by expected value; a starting split by account count of about 5 / 15 / 25 / 25 / 30% is a heuristic credited to Hunter and Tietyen — adjust the cut-offs to your own revenue curve |
| An account shows higher-band traits | Promote it for a trial period |
| Planned contact costs exceed the ceiling for a band | Move content from visits to calls to digital until it fits; lower bands get fewer but fuller contacts |
| Customers ask for more visits than capacity allows | Close the gap with lower-cost contacts that carry real value, not with more staff |

Judge the system by cost-to-revenue per band, retention and account growth — never by the response rate of single mailings.

**Worked scenario — a Kampala print and branding supplier with 400 buyer groups:** digital touch ≈ UGX 2,000; inside-sales call ≈ UGX 15,000; field visit ≈ UGX 250,000. Top band (20 groups, UGX 80m expected each): 48 digital + 24 calls + 12 visits ≈ UGX 3.46m, about 4.3% of expected revenue, inside a 5% ceiling. Lowest band (120 groups, UGX 4m each): 24 digital + 4 calls ≈ UGX 108,000, about 2.7%; one visit would add over 6 points, so the band is served digitally.

**Agency version:** grade retainer clients the same way; set strategy meetings, calls, WhatsApp check-ins and reports per band; flag any client whose senior hours break the ceiling for re-pricing, repackaging or a lighter productised tier.

## 6. Customer value as an asset

Lifetime value = purchases over the relationship − cost to serve (including product cost) − acquisition cost; discount future years for accuracy. Growing existing accounts usually moves value more than trimming acquisition cost. Scenario: a UGX 24m-a-year retainer at 30% contribution over four years with UGX 6m acquisition cost ≈ UGX 22.8m; 20% cheaper acquisition → ≈ UGX 24.0m; a 20% scope increase from year two → ≈ UGX 27.1m. Report the year-on-year change in total customer-base value alongside sales.

## 7. Keep before you chase

| | Keeping customers | Winning customers |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Purchase history plus profile | Profile only |
| Needs | Observed | Assumed |
| Contact | Personal | Can be broader |
| Offer | Relationship-led | Offer-led |

A working retention programme comes before heavy acquisition spend.

## 8. Grow inside the account (account cube — Hunter and Tietyen)

Expand in widening circles: more lines to the same buyer; colleagues who use the same thing; other departments on the same site; the same use at other sites or subsidiaries. Find headroom from purchase gaps, tie each proposal to a named business problem (a wish is not a need) and ask satisfied buyers for internal introductions. Keep a grid per key client: bought / not bought / blocked.

## 9. Complaints and revenue at risk

- Make complaining easy, answer fast, give each complaint an owner, tell the customer when an issue is parked, and close the loop.
- **Revenue at risk:** call customers soon after delivery. Multiply the share with any problem × the share of problems of each type × the share saying they will not reorder (low estimate) or will not or might not (high estimate). Rank problems by customers at risk, multiply by the value of a lost customer, fix the biggest, re-measure, and compare stated intent with actual reorders six months later.
- Scenario: 300 clients; 20% had a problem; late delivery is 10% of problems; 60% of those will not reorder → 1.2% ≈ 3.6 clients × UGX 15m ≈ UGX 54m at risk.

## 10. Measure loyalty by behaviour

Ask two questions — "Will you buy from us again?" and "Would you recommend us to a colleague?" — by phone where possible, within about a week or two of a transaction, naming yourself (never anonymously for current clients). Ask directly about problems, tell customers what changed, and check answers against behaviour. Sort accounts into secure (share rising), complacent (flat), at risk (neutral, flat at best) and leaving (share falling).

## 11. Early warnings

- Customer: no order for two months → a useful reminder; ten more days → a call about problems; spend below threshold over most of a year → review the lines not bought.
- Agency: no content approval for 14 days; a missed monthly review; the client's own ad spend falling; a new marketing manager; an invoice more than 45 days overdue; fewer replies; fewer brief requests.

## 12. The first weeks of a new customer

Call soon after the first delivery: thank them, check for problems, confirm the decision was sound, capture contact preferences and other decision-makers, grade the account and assign its contact plan, hand over without a gap, encourage a second purchase, and decline customers you should not keep (chronic late payment, abuse). Prove the value by splitting new customers at random into onboarded and control groups.

## 13. Behaviours that damage the relationship

| Stage | What the customer needs | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| First contact | A real need and a fitting offer | Wrong target, talking about yourself, overpromising, contact with no value |
| Considering | Less uncertainty; a visible win-win | Indifference; prescribing before diagnosing |
| Newly won | To feel valued | No thanks, no reassurance, winning the wrong customer |
| Growing | Proactive, deeper understanding | Treating customers like prospects |
| Winning back | Recognition of the past relationship | Transaction-only messages |
| After a failure | An easy complaint route, speed, apology, fairness | No urgency, solutions before listening, insincerity |

## 14. How much a lead may cost

Work backwards from value:
- Lifetime profit of a new customer (from similar existing customers) × the share you are willing to invest.
- Less the cost of closing (meetings needed × cost per meeting).
- × the share of qualified leads that become customers = the most you can pay for a qualified lead.
- × the share of raw enquiries that qualify, less the cost of qualifying = the most you can pay per raw enquiry.

Scenario (agency new clients): UGX 20m lifetime contribution × 40% = UGX 8m; less 3 pitch meetings at UGX 0.5m = UGX 6.5m; × 33% ≈ UGX 2.15m per qualified lead; × 25% ≈ UGX 536k; less a UGX 50k qualifying call ≈ UGX 486k per raw enquiry. An expo stand costing UGX 12m that yields ten enquiries (UGX 1.2m each) fails unless far more of them qualify.

## 15. Handling leads end to end

Capture every enquiry with its source → check whether the person already belongs to a client (if so, ask your contact for an introduction) → respond fast, because interest fades within weeks → call first, then send only what the conversation shows is useful → pass ready buyers to sales and nurture the rest → close → onboard (section 12). Measure cost per qualified lead by source and close rate per qualified lead.

## 16. Small, knowable markets

Where the target market is small and nameable (licensed insurers, commercial banks, larger SACCOs, registered manufacturers, private hospitals, international schools), build the list from regulator registers and association rolls and run a named-account programme. Test the best-fitting prospects first; if the offer fails with them, it will fail elsewhere.

## 17. The client desk

One coordinator owning the CRM, the WhatsApp Business inbox and the reporting calendar raises consistency. Hire service and sales roles separately, give call guides with objectives rather than scripts, judge the desk on cost-to-revenue, retention and account growth, and share credit between field and inside staff so neither loses when the other wins.
