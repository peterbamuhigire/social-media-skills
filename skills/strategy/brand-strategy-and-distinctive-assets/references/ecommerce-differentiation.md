## When to use this reference

The job turns an online shop that competes only on price into a brand buyers choose on purpose: a strategic quadrant, an intangible type, a Soleness statement, a positioning map, a name shortlist, packaging moves and a buyer community. Verma (2019) *Checkout* argues that few brands are seen by their own customers as genuinely different (no measured figure is quoted; `NOT_ASSESSED`).

Client triggers:

- The shop sells the same products as dozens of Jumia, Kilimall, Jiji or Instagram sellers and only ever wins on price.
- The owner wants to own a niche or a category (a Blue Ocean move or a Soleness statement) instead of fighting on discounts.
- A shop or product name shortlist needs checking for meaning, pronunciation and domain availability.
- Packaging and unboxing should do marketing work: inserts and presentation that buyers photograph and share.
- A small base of repeat buyers could become a community of true fans who vouch for the shop.

How it sits with the parent skill: the differentiation work gives buyers a *reason* to choose; the parent's [category entry points](cep-and-mental-availability.md) and [distinctive asset audit](distinctive-asset-audit.md) make sure the shop *comes to mind* and is *recognised* when they do. Run both. A shop that is different but never remembered still loses to the cheapest listing.

## Option planning quadrant

Choose the strategic approach from capital and competition (Verma, 2019):

| Capital | High competition | Low competition |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Intangibles (story, purpose, surprise, curation) | Positioning (claim a specific market position) |
| High | Disruption (Blue Ocean Strategy, a new business model) | Category creation (invent a new category) |

Most East African SME shops sit in the low-capital, high-competition cell, so intangibles are the first tool.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the problem is price-led sameness, not checkout or order friction. Route conversion problems to `playbook-post-click-strategy` and stop this procedure for them.
2. Place the client on the quadrant and pick the approach.
3. Choose one or two intangible types that fit the client's true story; record why each fits (catalogue in the method, Section 2).
4. Write the Soleness statement and test it against the five characteristics; build the two-axis positioning map. If no position is empty, run the Blue Ocean four actions or test for category creation.
5. Shortlist names by type and score each against the five naming characteristics; flag domain and trademark checks.
6. Set packaging moves with a commercial reason tied to the position. Colour and typography direction is a brief for the design engine, never final visual work.
7. Plan the buyer community by growth stage (under 100, 100–500, 500+ customers) using the 1,000 True Fans model (Kelly, 2008).
8. Check the brief against the quality checks and the anti-slop gate; correct any failed item and rerun the check before hand-off as a client decision draft.

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| The problem is price-led sameness rather than checkout friction | Define a defensible position and proof before visual or conversion tactics | Cosmetic differentiation without a buyer-relevant reason to choose |
| Low capital and high competition | Lead with intangibles (story, purpose, surprise, curation) | Spending on disruption the business cannot fund |
| The positioning map shows no empty quadrant | Run the Blue Ocean four actions; consider category creation only if the offer is new in model, delivery or design | Fighting in a crowded quadrant on price |
| The founder story or cause cannot be verified | Choose another intangible type; never invent a story, giveback or mission | A brand claim that collapses when buyers check it |
| A name is chosen before domain and trademark checks | Keep it on the shortlist marked "checks pending" | Rebranding after launch or a legal dispute |
| An ambassador asks for cash payment | Offer exclusive perks only and refer paid arrangements to a lawyer | Sliding into influencer-contract territory |
| Food or skincare packaging is specified | Include ingredient information and a batch/expiry date; confirm the current Uganda rule with the client's adviser (no register record, `NOT_ASSESSED`) | Breaching labelling requirements |

## Outputs

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| E-commerce differentiation brief: quadrant, intangible type, Soleness statement, positioning map | Client owner | Quadrant stated; intangible choice explained against the client's story; statement passes all five tests. |
| Blue Ocean grid or category-creation case | Client owner and strategist | Eliminate, Reduce, Raise and Create each carry client-specific answers. |
| Name shortlist with scores | Client owner; trademark adviser | Every name scored on Distinctive, Sound, Stickiness, Expression and Appearance; domain status recorded. |
| Colour, typography and packaging direction | Designer; the design engine | Primary and accent colours named (the historical acceptance condition asked for hex values; visual execution routes to the design engine); at least 3 packaging moves, each with its commercial reason. |
| Community plan by growth stage | Owner and whoever runs WhatsApp | At least one tactic per stage; ambassador perks are non-cash. |

## Evidence

| Evidence | Format | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Competitor claim and positioning-map record | Table with source and date per competitor | Every plotted position traces to an observed claim or is labelled opinion. |
| Name-check log | Table: name, pronunciation check, domain, trademark status | Unchecked names are marked "checks pending", never "clear". |
| Assumption register | Table in the brief | Personas, capital level and customer descriptions not backed by evidence are labelled. |

## Degraded mode

Without competitor claims or customer evidence, mark the affected checks `not assessed`. The quadrant choice, intangible options, a draft Soleness statement and a name shortlist marked "checks pending" can still be delivered.

## Quality checks

- The competitive quadrant (capital × competition) is identified with the matching approach.
- The chosen intangible type is justified against this client's story and market.
- A completed Soleness statement follows the template and passes the five tests.
- The two-axis positioning map identifies at least one underserved position; the Blue Ocean grid has client-specific answers.
- Every proposed name is scored against all five naming characteristics.
- Colour and typography direction carries a rationale tied to the position (for the design engine); at least 3 packaging recommendations explain their commercial reason.
- The community plan names at least one tactic per growth stage (under 100; 100–500; 500+ customers).
- Uganda/East Africa, British English, UGX and WhatsApp-first assumptions are explicit; the full checklist is in the [method](ecommerce-differentiation-method.md#full-quality-checklist).

## Anti-patterns

- Differentiating with a new logo while the offer is identical to every Jumia seller. Fix: settle the position and proof first, then the visuals.
- Inventing a founder story or a giveback that is not real. Fix: use only true, specific stories and quantified, verifiable causes.
- Picking a name that is hard to say in English, Luganda or Swahili. Fix: say every shortlisted name aloud and score its Sound.
- Choosing red for a premium natural brand because it "pops". Fix: check the colour against its EA associations and the position, then brief the design engine.
- Packaging that protects but never sells. Fix: add the visible brand name, a thank-you note and a WhatsApp number or QR code for repeat orders.
- Deceptive or unpermitted customer spotlights. Fix: feature customers only with their permission.
- Presenting a name or identity as final. Fix: deliver a shortlist for the client to decide after trademark, domain and competitor checks.

## Boundaries and hand-offs

- `playbook-post-click-strategy` for checkout, cart and WhatsApp order conversion problems.
- `social-commerce-strategy` for catalogues, WhatsApp ordering, Mobile Money payment and delivery set-up.
- `marketing-foundations-stp-positioning` for segmentation and positioning of a business that is not an online store.
- Stop before presenting a name or visual identity as final without trademark, domain and competitor checks; deliver a shortlist for the client to decide. Registering a name or domain, or ordering packaging, is the client's decision after its own checks.
