# Category entry points and mental availability

Read when mapping the buying situations a brand should own, measuring how often it comes to mind in them, or planning physical availability alongside. Parent: [brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets](../SKILL.md).

## 1. The two availabilities

Ehrenberg-Bass research (Sharp 2010; Romaniuk and Sharp 2022) frames brand growth as making the brand easier to think of and easier to buy:

| Term | Working definition used in this engine | What the client controls |
|---|---|---|
| Mental availability | The chance that a category buyer thinks of the brand in a buying situation, and recognises it quickly | Which situations the brand is linked to; how consistently the brand is branded; how many category buyers are reached |
| Physical availability | The ease of finding and buying the brand: where it is sold, how prominent it is there, and whether the pack size, price point and payment route suit the buyer | Outlets and routes, shelf or listing prominence, pack and price ladder, payment and delivery options |

Category entry points (CEPs) are the building blocks of mental availability: the thoughts buyers have as they move towards buying in the category (register `EBI-CATEGORY-ENTRY-POINTS`). A brand linked to more of the common CEPs, in more buyers' memories, gets thought of more often.

## 2. Eliciting CEPs

Build the list from the buyer's situation, not the brand's features. Use prompts such as:

| Prompt family | Example for a Kampala soft-drink or snack brand | Example for a B2B freight forwarder |
|---|---|---|
| When / time | After a football match; the midday break | End of the quarter; when a shipment is stuck |
| Where | At the taxi stage; at a roadside duka | At the port of Mombasa; at the Malaba border |
| With or for whom | With friends; for a child's school break | For the finance director; for a new client |
| Why / need | Thirsty and hot; need energy before a boda ride | Need customs clearance fast; need a quote in UGX |
| With what | With chapati or rolex | With a letter of credit; with a bonded warehouse |
| Feeling | Wanting a treat; wanting to save | Worried about delays; under pressure from the boss |

Sources for elicitation, cheapest first: sales-staff and shopkeeper interviews (dukas, market stalls, pharmacies, agents); WhatsApp enquiry logs and customer-care transcripts; search queries and marketplace search terms; 10–20 short buyer interviews; open-ended survey questions ("the last time you bought X, what was happening?"). Stop when new interviews add no new situations.

## 3. Prioritising CEPs

Score each candidate CEP, then choose a small priority set (typically 5–10 to track and 2–4 to lead creative):

| Criterion | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence | How many category buyers experience this situation, how often? | Survey incidence, sales timing, enquiry logs |
| Brand fit | Can the brand credibly serve this situation? | Product truth, availability in that place or time |
| Competitive gap | Is a rival already strongly linked to it? | Brand linkage by CEP in the survey |
| Reach feasibility | Can media and distribution reach buyers in that moment? | Radio dayparts, outlet coverage, platform reach |

A CEP with high prevalence and a weak rival link is the first target. A CEP owned by a strong rival is contested only with a real product or availability advantage.

## 4. Measuring mental availability

Use a pick-any brand-association survey: show each priority CEP and ask which brands (from a list, including "none") the respondent links to it. From the responses, the usual Ehrenberg-Bass-derived metrics are (definitions paraphrased from a vendor summary, register `ZAPPI-MENTAL-AVAILABILITY-METRICS-2026`, Vendor):

| Metric | Meaning | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Mental penetration | Share of category buyers who link the brand to at least one CEP | Breadth: how many buyers have the brand in memory at all |
| Network size | Average number of CEPs linked to the brand among those who link it to any | Depth: how many situations trigger the brand |
| Mental market share | The brand's share of all brand–CEP links given in the category | Compare with market share: above it suggests headroom to grow; below it suggests the brand is under-remembered |
| CEP-level linkage | Share of buyers linking the brand to each CEP | Which situations to reinforce or claim |

Rules: interview category buyers, not only customers; include light and non-buyers; keep the brand list and CEP wording fixed across waves; report brands' figures relative to their size, because bigger brands get more links from more buyers (double jeopardy, Sharp 2010).

## 5. East Africa realism

- Survey routes that work in Uganda and Kenya: CATI phone interviews from a purchased or client-owned sample (with a lawful basis and consent script); WhatsApp or SMS panels (quick, cheap, skewed to urban, younger, smartphone users); face-to-face intercepts at markets, taxi stages, malls and outside dukas (reach non-internet buyers, but not a random sample). State the route and its bias in every report.
- Language: translate CEP wording into Luganda, Swahili, Runyankole or other local languages and back-translate; test with five people before fielding.
- Budget-limited SMEs: run a CEP list from shopkeeper and customer interviews plus a small intercept survey; report directions, not precise shares (see sample sizes in [brand tracking](brand-tracking.md)).
- Personal data: phone and WhatsApp surveys process personal data. Uganda DPPA and Kenya DPA rules apply (registers `UG-DPPA-2019`, `KE-DP-GENERAL-2021`); collect only what the survey needs and get consent.

## 6. Physical availability checklist

| Dimension | Questions | East African examples |
|---|---|---|
| Presence | Is the brand sold where category buyers already shop? | Dukas, kiosks, supermarkets, pharmacies, agro-dealers, market stalls, Jumia/Jiji listings, WhatsApp catalogue, delivery apps |
| Prominence | Can buyers see it at the point of choice? | Shelf position and facings, fridge branding, shop-front painting, top of marketplace search, pinned WhatsApp catalogue item |
| Portfolio | Do pack sizes and price points match how people buy? | Sachets and single sticks for daily cash budgets; family packs for monthly shopping |
| Payment | Can buyers pay the way they prefer? | Cash, MTN MoMo and Airtel Money in Uganda, M-Pesa in Kenya, card online |
| Delivery and hours | Is it there when the CEP happens? | Boda delivery, evening hours, stock near schools or stages |

Score each dimension red/amber/green by district or channel. Mental availability without physical availability wastes media: buyers think of the brand and then cannot find it.
