# Brand tracking and the effectiveness ladder

Read when setting brand objectives, designing a brand tracker, choosing sample sizes, or judging whether brand investment is paying back. Parent: [brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Objectives on the effectiveness ladder

Hurman and Field's Creative Effectiveness Ladder, from *The Effectiveness Code* (Cannes Lions and WARC, 2020; register `WARC-EFFECTIVENESS-CODE-2020`), ranks six kinds of effect from least to most commercially valuable. The study behind it compared 4,863 effectiveness-award cases from 2011–2019. Paraphrased:

| Rung | Effect | Typical evidence | Measurement window |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Influential idea | Engagement, sharing, media efficiency | Views, shares, earned media, cost per view | Campaign |
| 2 Behaviour breakthrough | A change in buyer (or other relevant) behaviour | Trial, sign-ups, store visits, WhatsApp enquiries | Campaign |
| 3 Sales spike | Short-term, temporary rise in sales or share | Sales against baseline or holdout | About three months |
| 4 Brand builder | Better brand health: awareness, consideration, preference, intent, attribute ownership | Tracker: mental penetration, CEP linkage, asset fame | Waves over a year or more |
| 5 Commercial triumph | Profitable growth in sales and share beyond the campaign | Sales, share, margin, price elasticity | About twelve months |
| 6 Enduring icon | Brand and sales growth sustained over three years or more with the same strategy | Multi-year sales, share, tracker trend | Three years or more |

Rules for planning:

- Write the objective at the highest rung the plan can credibly reach, and name the window. A three-month campaign is judged as a sales spike, not a commercial triumph.
- Every plan names at least one commercial measure (rungs 3, 5 or 6), even when the near-term objective is brand health. Lower rungs are lead indicators, never the result.
- Use the lower rungs to diagnose, not to declare success: engagement without behaviour or sales change is a warning, not a win.

## 2. What to track

| Layer | Metric | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mental availability | Mental penetration, network size, mental market share, linkage on priority CEPs | Pick-any CEP survey ([CEPs](cep-and-mental-availability.md) §4) |
| Distinctive assets | Fame and uniqueness of core assets | Masked-stimulus survey ([asset audit](distinctive-asset-audit.md)) |
| Physical availability | Outlet presence, prominence, pack and price coverage, payment options | Distributor data, retail audits, marketplace checks |
| Behaviour | Penetration (share of category buyers who bought in the period), purchase frequency, new versus repeat buyers | Sales and CRM data, household or retail panels where they exist, survey claimed purchase |
| Commercial | Sales, share, margin, price realised | Client finance, distributor sell-out |

Growth usually shows first as more buyers (penetration), with loyalty moving in line with size (Sharp 2010). Track penetration, not only loyalty metrics.

## 3. Sample sizes stated honestly

For a percentage near 50% from a simple random sample, the 95% margin of error is about 1.96 × √(0.25 ÷ n):

| Sample (n) | Margin of error (± percentage points) | Smallest wave-to-wave change that is clearly real (two independent waves of this size) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | about 9.8 | about 13.9 |
| 200 | about 6.9 | about 9.8 |
| 400 | about 4.9 | about 6.9 |
| 1,000 | about 3.1 | about 4.4 |

Rules:

- These figures assume random sampling. Intercepts and WhatsApp panels are not random; treat their margins as lower bounds and report "direction only" when the method is non-probability.
- Sub-groups (a district, an age band, non-buyers) have their own, smaller n; do not report sub-groups under about 100 as figures.
- Keep method, route, language, question wording, brand list and season constant across waves. A change of method breaks the trend line.
- An SME with a small budget may run one wave of 150–200 intercepts a year: enough to rank CEPs and assets, not to prove small changes. Say so in the report.

## 4. Tracking cadence

- Continuous or quarterly waves for brands with enough budget and seasonal campaigns; annual or before-and-after waves for SMEs.
- Always take a baseline before a new campaign or asset change.
- Read the tracker with the sales data: brand-health gains that never reach penetration or sales within the agreed window trigger a review of reach, branding and physical availability before more spend.

## 5. Emotional and rational evidence

Binet and Field's IPA analysis (2013; register `IPA-LONG-SHORT-2013`) reported that emotional, brand-building campaigns tend to build larger effects over several years, while rational messages tend to give bigger immediate responses that fade faster. Use this to set expectations: emotional brand work is judged on rungs 4–6 over a year or more; rational activation is judged on rungs 2–3 in weeks. Creative testing and pre-testing belong to `creative-brief-and-big-idea` and the testing skills.

## 6. Reporting

- Show observed survey figures with n, route, dates and margin of error; show modelled figures separately.
- Connect each brand metric to a commercial measure and the agreed window.
- Report results on the ladder: which rung the evidence supports, and which rungs are `not assessed`.
- Dashboards and monthly reporting layouts belong to `meta-reporting`.
