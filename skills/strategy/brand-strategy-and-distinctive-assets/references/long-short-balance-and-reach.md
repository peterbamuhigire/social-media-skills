# Long/short balance, penetration and reach

Read when splitting effort between brand building and sales activation, deciding who to reach, or planning reach to buyers who are not in the market today (including B2B). Parent: [brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets](../SKILL.md). Budget totals stay with [`advertising-strategy-and-budget`](../../../advertising/advertising-strategy-and-budget/SKILL.md).

## 1. Brand building versus activation

| Evidence | What it says (paraphrased) | Register |
|---|---|---|
| Binet and Field (2013) *The Long and the Short of It*, IPA | Meta-analysis of 996 IPA Databank campaigns (700 brands, 83 categories): the number of very large business effects peaked when activation took about 40% of the budget, so about 60% went to brand building; short-term effects are needed for efficiency, long-term effects drive growth and profit | `IPA-LONG-SHORT-2013` |
| IPA blog, *The next chapter for The Long and the Short of It* (2023) | Summarises the later IPA work: *Effectiveness in Context* (2018) put the balance at about 62:38 and found it broadly stable over a decade; warns that short-termism has grown while effectiveness declined | `IPA-LONG-SHORT-NEXT-CHAPTER` |
| Binet and Field for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, *The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing* | For B2B, recommends about an even split between brand building and activation | `LINKEDIN-B2B-5-PRINCIPLES` |

How to apply:

- Start the conversation near 60:40 brand:activation for an established consumer brand, and near 50:50 for B2B. These are starting points drawn from award-entry databanks (mainly UK and international), never rules.
- Move towards more activation when: the brand is new and needs trial and distribution first; the business is cash-constrained and needs sales this quarter to survive; the purchase cycle is short and most buyers are in-market often; the product is sold through a narrow online channel where sales response is the main lever.
- Move towards more brand building when: the category has long purchase cycles; the brand is established with good distribution; price pressure is rising (brand strength supports price); competitors outspend on activation and the category is crowded with offers.
- Say what counts as "brand" and "activation" in the plan (for example, radio sponsorship and broad-reach video as brand; retargeting, search, promo codes and click-to-WhatsApp as activation). Some work does both (brand response); classify it by its main objective.
- Record the chosen split, the reasons and the review date. Hand the total to `advertising-strategy-and-budget` and the schedule to `media-planning`.
- For East African SMEs spending small sums, the split may be expressed over a year rather than a month: a sales-driving core every month plus a brand-building burst around the season or CEP that matters most.

## 2. Growth by penetration

The Ehrenberg-Bass laws (Sharp 2010; Romaniuk and Sharp 2022) inform three planning defaults:

- Brands grow mainly by gaining more buyers; loyalty rises modestly with size. Plans that target only current loyal buyers limit growth.
- Most buyers of a brand are light buyers who buy it occasionally; they are easy to forget and easy to lose. Reach them, because they are where growth comes from.
- Non-buyers of the brand who buy the category are the main pool of future buyers.

Planning consequence: aim for broad reach of all category buyers, with continuous presence where the budget allows, rather than intensive frequency against a narrow, heavy-buyer audience. Targeting remains useful to exclude non-category buyers and to fit media to CEPs.

## 3. Out-of-market buyers: the 95:5 idea

LinkedIn's B2B Institute, drawing on its joint research with the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, states that at any time about 95% of potential B2B buyers are out of market and only about 5% are ready to buy, because business purchases (IT, banking, vehicles) are replaced every few years (register `LINKEDIN-B2B-95-5`).

How to apply:

- Estimate the client's own in-market share from its purchase cycle: if buyers switch supplier on average every five years, roughly one in five switches in a year and about one in sixty in a month.
- Lead generation and retargeting reach the in-market few; brand reach builds memory among the out-of-market majority so the brand is on the shortlist when they re-enter.
- Judge out-of-market work on mental availability (CEP linkage among target accounts) and later pipeline, not on this month's leads.
- On LinkedIn and other B2B channels, pair a broad-reach brand layer (video and thought leader ads to the full buying-committee audience; register `LINKEDIN-ADS-OBJECTIVES-TLA-2026`) with a smaller activation layer (lead forms, retargeting). Platform mechanics stay in `platform-linkedin` and `playbook-paid-social-advertising`.
- The logic also holds for consumer durables with long cycles (motorcycles, solar systems, school fees, insurance).

## 4. East Africa realism

- Broad reach on a small budget: radio (national and local-language FM), outdoor at taxi parks and junctions, shop-front branding and WhatsApp Status reach category buyers who never see targeted digital ads.
- Physical availability often limits growth before mental availability does: fix outlet presence and pack/price fit before scaling reach ([CEPs](cep-and-mental-availability.md) §6).
- Internet or platform disruption can remove digital activation overnight; a brand already remembered through radio and outlets keeps selling. Contingency planning belongs to `media-planning`.
- Most local brands have no econometrics; use before-and-after tracking waves, matched districts or holdouts as the proof route (see `advertising-attribution-and-measurement`).
