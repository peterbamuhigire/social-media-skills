# Distinctive asset audit

Read when deciding which of a brand's non-name elements to keep, build, restrict or drop, or when a redesign or new campaign threatens existing assets. Parent: [brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets](../SKILL.md).

## 1. What counts as a distinctive asset

A distinctive asset is a non-name element (colour, logo, shape, pack, character, sound, jingle, tagline, font, spokesperson, pattern) that makes buyers think of the brand. It has two properties that must be measured, not assumed (register `EBI-DISTINCTIVE-ASSETS`; Romaniuk 2018):

- **Fame:** the share of category buyers who link the element to the brand.
- **Uniqueness:** the share of those links that go to this brand rather than to rivals. Uniqueness is the harder and more important property.

An element the team likes, or one that won an award, is not an asset until buyers link it to the brand.

## 2. Audit procedure

1. **Inventory.** List every candidate element across packs, shop fronts, vans, radio idents, social templates, WhatsApp profile, uniforms and receipts. Include elements used by rivals in the category.
2. **Prepare stimuli.** Remove the brand name from each element (crop logos, mute the brand name in the jingle, show the colour alone). Use the same format for every brand's elements.
3. **Survey category buyers.** For each element: "Which brand, if any, does this make you think of?" (open or from a list with "don't know"). Include light and non-buyers.
4. **Score.** Fame = % of respondents naming the brand. Uniqueness = % naming the brand ÷ % naming any brand.
5. **Plot on the grid** (below) and assign an action.
6. **Set usage rules** for the portfolio and hand visual execution to the design engine.
7. **Re-audit** at a fixed cadence (often every 1–2 years, or before any redesign) with the same stimuli and method.

## 3. The asset grid

The Ehrenberg-Bass grid (register `EBI-DISTINCTIVE-ASSETS`) crosses fame and uniqueness. Thresholds are a client decision set before fielding (for example, 50% for each); state them in the report.

| Fame | Uniqueness | Label | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | Use or lose | Protect; use everywhere the brand appears; never change without a re-test |
| Low | High | Investment potential | Invest: use consistently and prominently, always with the brand name until fame builds |
| High | Low | Avoid solo use | Use only alongside the brand name or a stronger asset; it also prompts rivals |
| Low | Low | Ignore or test | Drop, or test a new candidate; do not rely on it to brand anything |

## 4. Portfolio management and consistency

- Choose a small core set (typically two to four assets) to build; spreading effort across many elements slows fame for all of them.
- Write usage rules: where each asset appears, minimum size or duration, first seconds of video or radio, placement on packs and shop fronts. Visual specifications are written by the design engine (https://github.com/peterbamuhigire/chwezi-design-engine), not here.
- Consistency across campaigns and years is the mechanism. Each campaign idea should carry the core assets; a new big idea changes the story, not the assets.
- Brief creative to show the brand and its assets early and clearly (for example in the first seconds of a video or the opening of a radio spot); the creative brief itself belongs to `creative-brief-and-big-idea`.
- Guard against copying: if a rival starts using a similar colour or shape, re-test uniqueness; escalate trademark or passing-off concerns to the client's lawyer.
- Before any refresh or rebrand, audit first. Evolve low-risk details; keep use-or-lose assets unless evidence shows they no longer work.

## 5. East Africa realism

- Many categories share the same colours through telecom and FMCG heritage (for example yellow, red and green are all strongly held by large brands). Expect low uniqueness for colour alone; test combinations and shapes.
- Radio is a strong route for sound assets (jingle, sonic logo, a recurring voice); test them as audio stimuli over the phone.
- Shop-front paintings, branded umbrellas, fridges and boda jackets are physical-availability and asset-building media at once: include them in the inventory.
- For a low-budget SME, a 100–150-person intercept at markets or outside dukas can rank candidate assets but cannot give precise fame figures; report direction and the margin of error ([brand tracking](brand-tracking.md) §3).
