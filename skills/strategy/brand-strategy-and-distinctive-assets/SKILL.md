# Brand Strategy and Distinctive Assets

Decides how a brand becomes easier to think of and easier to buy: which buying situations to own, which brand elements to protect or drop, how far to reach beyond today's buyers, how to balance brand building with sales activation, and how to track it. It serves owners, marketing leads and agency planners, from Kampala SMEs and online sellers to regional and B2B brands.

<!-- dual-compat-start -->
## Use When

- Buyers forget the brand in the moments they buy the category; a rival gets picked first although the product is as good.
- Which colours, logo, jingle, mascot or pack shape do buyers recognise as ours, which get confused with competitors, and which should be kept, built or dropped before a redesign?
- The owner wants a split between building the brand for next year and pushing sales this month.
- The team only chases people ready to buy now, while buyers who will need the product next year, including company buyers, forget it.
- The client wants proof memory and sales are growing over time, not only clicks, from a survey affordable in Uganda or Kenya.
- An online shop sells what every other Jumia, Kilimall or Instagram seller sells and only wins on price.

## Do Not Use When

- `marketing-foundations-stp-positioning` for choosing segments and targets, the positioning statement, value proposition and 4Ps.
- `04-brand-voice-intake` for tone of voice, vocabulary and the social media style guide.
- `creative-brief-and-big-idea` for the campaign brief, insight and big idea that will carry the assets.
- `advertising-strategy-and-budget` for the total advertising budget and its triangulation.
- Stop when there is no decision owner or no way to reach category buyers for evidence; return the research plan only.

## Required Inputs

| Artefact | Source/provider | Required? | If absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category definition, buyers, purchase cycle and main rivals | Client owner, sales team | Yes | State a working definition, label it an assumption and confirm before fielding any survey |
| Inventory of brand elements (logo, colours, pack, sounds, characters, taglines, spokespeople) with examples | Client, packs, past campaigns | Yes for the asset audit | Build the inventory from observed packs, shop fronts and posts; mark audit results `not assessed` |
| Buyer evidence: CEP links and asset recognition | Survey (phone, WhatsApp panel, market intercepts), interviews | Conditional | Deliver the survey design and a CEP long-list from interviews; metrics `not assessed` |
| Distribution and buying routes (outlets, dukas, marketplaces, WhatsApp, payment, delivery) | Sales team, distributors, marketplace listings | Yes for physical availability | Audit a sample of outlets and listings; label coverage an estimate |
| Sales, penetration and budget history | Client finance, CRM | Conditional | Plan on mental and physical availability; commercial rung `not assessed` |
| Decision owner and approval limits | Client | Yes | Draft only |

## Workflow

1. Frame the growth problem: category, buyers, purchase cycle, current penetration and the decision owner. Stop if there is no owner or no route to buyer evidence; return the research plan. If the real question is who to serve or what to stand for, route to `marketing-foundations-stp-positioning`.
2. Map category entry points: elicit buying situations, prioritise by prevalence, fit, rival strength and reach, and measure mental penetration, network size and mental market share ([CEPs and mental availability](references/cep-and-mental-availability.md)).
3. Audit distinctive assets on fame × uniqueness, place each on the grid, choose the core set and write usage rules; send visual execution to the design engine ([asset audit](references/distinctive-asset-audit.md)).
4. Check physical availability: presence, prominence, pack and price fit, payment (cash, MoMo, Airtel Money, M-Pesa) and delivery, by district or channel.
5. Set the reach and balance plan: all category buyers including light and non-buyers, out-of-market buyers (95:5 in B2B), and a brand:activation split starting near 60:40 and adjusted to the context ([long/short balance and reach](references/long-short-balance-and-reach.md)); hand totals to `advertising-strategy-and-budget`.
6. Set objectives on the effectiveness ladder with at least one commercial measure and a window, and design the tracker with honest sample sizes ([brand tracking](references/brand-tracking.md)).
7. Online-shop branch: when a seller competes on price with look-alike products, run [e-commerce differentiation](references/ecommerce-differentiation.md) (quadrant, intangible type, Soleness statement, names, packaging, community) alongside steps 2–3.
8. Run the quality checks and the anti-slop gate; fix failures and rerun. After each tracking wave, rerun steps 2–5 where priority CEPs, asset scores or availability have moved.

## Outputs

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Brand-building plan | Client owner, planner, media and creative teams | Priority CEPs, core assets, availability fixes, reach plan, brand:activation split with reasons and review date |
| CEP map and priority list | Planner, creative team | Each CEP has prevalence evidence, brand and rival linkage or `not assessed`, and a role (lead, support, watch) |
| Distinctive asset audit and grid | Client, design engine, creative team | Every element has fame, uniqueness, n, route, grid cell and an action; thresholds stated |
| Brand tracking plan | Client, analyst | Metrics by ladder rung, commercial measure, window, n and margin of error, fixed method across waves |
| E-commerce differentiation brief (online shops) | Shop owner | Meets the checks in [e-commerce differentiation](references/ecommerce-differentiation.md) |

## Evidence Produced

| Evidence | Format | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Survey instrument and fieldwork log | Questionnaire, route, language, dates, n | Wording and brand list frozen for later waves; consent script included |
| Asset and CEP score tables | Tables with n and margin of error | Non-probability samples labelled "direction only" |
| Assumption and source register | Table with register IDs | Every split, benchmark and claim has a register ID, a client source or an assumption label |

## Capability and Permission Boundaries

Read and search only; analysis is read-only. Publishing, spend, live account changes, outreach and personal-data processing need explicit, action-specific client authority. Fielding a phone or WhatsApp survey, buying sample, changing packs or registering names and trademarks is the client's decision; visual execution routes to the design engine and legal questions to counsel.

## Degraded Mode

Without buyer survey data, return the narrowest qualified result and mark the affected checks `not assessed`. A CEP long-list from interviews, an asset inventory with hypotheses, an availability audit and a costed survey design can still be delivered, with no fame, uniqueness or mental-share figures.

## Decision Rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| The plan targets only loyal or heavy buyers | Widen to all category buyers, including light and non-buyers; growth comes mainly from more buyers (Sharp 2010) | Capped growth from a shrinking pool |
| The owner asks for the brand versus sales split | Start near 60:40 (`IPA-LONG-SHORT-2013`, `IPA-LONG-SHORT-NEXT-CHAPTER`), near 50:50 in B2B (`LINKEDIN-B2B-5-PRINCIPLES`); adjust for launch, cash needs, cycle length and channel; record reasons | A databank average applied as a rule |
| B2B or long-cycle durable category | Plan broad reach for the out-of-market majority (95:5, `LINKEDIN-B2B-95-5`) beside lead generation | Fighting over the in-market few while memory fades |
| An element is famous but not unique | Use it only with the brand name or a stronger asset | Advertising that also prompts rivals |
| A redesign would change a use-or-lose asset | Block the change until the asset is re-tested; evolve, do not replace | Losing years of built recognition |
| Sample is small or non-random | Report direction and margin of error; no decimals or sub-groups under about 100 | False precision in client reports |
| An online seller competes on price with identical products | Run the e-commerce differentiation branch before any visual or promotion work | Discount war with no reason to choose |
| Brand metrics rise but penetration and sales do not within the window | Review reach, branding and physical availability before adding spend | Paying for awareness that never converts |

## Quality Standards

- Priority CEPs come from buyer situations with evidence, not from product features.
- Every asset verdict shows fame, uniqueness, n, route and thresholds; none rests on team opinion.
- Physical availability is scored by channel or district, including payment and pack/price fit.
- The brand:activation split is stated as a starting point with written reasons and a review date.
- Objectives name a ladder rung, a commercial measure and a window (`WARC-EFFECTIVENESS-CODE-2020`).
- Every survey figure carries n, route, dates and margin of error; non-random samples say "direction only".
- No positive typeface or visual-design claim; visual execution is briefed to the design engine.
- British English, UGX, and Uganda-first routes (radio, dukas, markets, WhatsApp, mobile money) are explicit.

## Anti-Patterns

- Declaring "our colour is our asset" from a team workshop. Fix: measure fame and uniqueness with masked stimuli among category buyers.
- Refreshing the logo or pack every campaign. Fix: keep core assets constant and let the campaign idea change.
- Quoting 60:40 as the law. Fix: present it as a starting point, adjust to the category and cash position, and record why.
- Spending only on retargeting and lead forms in B2B. Fix: add a broad-reach brand layer for out-of-market buyers.
- Building mental availability for a product buyers cannot find. Fix: close outlet, pack, price and payment gaps first.
- Reporting a two-point tracker change from 150 intercepts as growth. Fix: show the margin of error and call it direction only.
- Judging a brand campaign on clicks after three weeks. Fix: judge it on its ladder rung over the agreed window.
