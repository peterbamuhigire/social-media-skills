## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the Owned pillar of the PESO strategy needs its own plan: an audit of the website, email list, WhatsApp opt-in list, SMS list, blog and other assets the client controls; list-building tactics; email and WhatsApp consent and hygiene rules; a blog-to-social pipeline; and an owned-media publishing rhythm. It expands [peso-method.md § Section 3](peso-method.md) principle 1 ("Owned first") and the [peso-method.md § Section 5](peso-method.md) EA guidance on email and WhatsApp into an executable asset plan. For email programme design in depth (segmentation, welcome and reactivation flows) hand over to [07-email-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md); for WhatsApp and SMS execution, to [playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing](../../../playbooks/playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing/SKILL.md).

## Why owned media comes first

Apply the POEM model (Paid / Owned / Earned; Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022) from the start. Owned media — channels the business controls entirely — is the base that paid and earned activity should drive audiences back to. Without it, every shilling spent on paid social builds equity in someone else's platform.

- **Platform dependency risk.** Facebook, TikTok and Instagram can change algorithms, suspend accounts, restrict organic reach or close without notice. A business with 50,000 Facebook followers and no email or WhatsApp opt-in list can lose its whole audience overnight through no fault of its own. The source states that algorithm changes in 2023–2024 cut organic reach on Facebook pages to under 2% for many business accounts (verify before stating; no register record). An email list, WhatsApp opt-in list and blog archive belong to the business and cannot be taken away by a platform update.
- **The two lists are the most valuable long-term digital assets.** They give direct, permission-based access to people who asked to hear from the business. Unlike a following, the contacts can be exported, moved between tools and reached whatever a third-party platform decides. Where data costs and connectivity vary, a well-run WhatsApp broadcast list or email newsletter reaches people more reliably than a boosted Facebook post that may never appear in the feed.
- **EA context.** Platform concentration in Uganda is very high: most SMEs use Facebook as their only digital presence, often with tens of thousands of followers and not one email subscriber or WhatsApp opt-in. Owned media is therefore the most important defensive investment a consultant can recommend. Make the case early and plainly: the aim is not to abandon social media but to give the business a direct line to its customers that no platform can cut.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer than 500 email subscribers and no WhatsApp opt-in list | State in the strategy that the client is dangerously dependent on platforms it does not control; make starting at least one owned list the first priority whatever the stated objective | Paid and shared spend with nowhere to capture the audience |
| Platform dependence is the primary risk | Prioritise consented audience capture and a maintainable publishing asset | Swapping one rented channel for another |
| Budget free and under 500 subscribers | Mailchimp free tier or Brevo | Paying for tools before there is a list |
| Client cannot run Mailchimp alone | Recommend Brevo for its simpler interface, or an interim Google Form → Google Sheets → manual send workflow while capacity is built | A tool the team abandons |
| WhatsApp list above the app's broadcast limit | Move to the WhatsApp Business API (for example via Africa's Talking) and route to playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing | Manual broadcasting that breaks at scale |
| Any contact would be added to a marketing list | Require explicit, informed opt-in with a documented record and an opt-out in every message | Breach of the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 |
| A purchased, rented or imported third-party list is offered | Refuse it | No valid consent and sender-reputation damage |
| Contact inactive 90+ days | Run a re-engagement campaign first; then suppress, do not delete | Losing a contact reachable by another channel |

## Procedure

### 1. Run the owned asset audit (every client, before any recommendation)

| Website | Y / N | | |
| Blog / content hub | Y / N | | |
| Email list | Y / N | Size: | |
| Email platform (Mailchimp, Brevo, etc.) | Y / N | | |
| WhatsApp Business profile | Y / N | | |
| WhatsApp opt-in list | Y / N | Size: | |
| SMS list | Y / N | Size: | |
| Podcast | Y / N | | |
| YouTube channel | Y / N | Subscribers: | |
| PDF lead magnets / downloads | Y / N | | |

Apply the red-flag threshold (first decision rule) and state it explicitly in the document.

### 2. Choose the email platform by budget

| Budget | Recommended tool | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free (under 500 subscribers) | Mailchimp free tier or Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) | Both have drag-and-drop editors; the source states Brevo includes SMS in the free tier |
| Paid, 500–5,000 subscribers | Mailchimp Essentials (about $13/month in the source) or Brevo Starter | Automation, A/B testing, removal of platform branding |
| Agency-managed | Include the platform cost in the monthly retainer | Consultant runs sends, list hygiene and reporting for the client |

Tool tiers, prices and features are time-sensitive: verify before stating (no register record).

### 3. Build the email list (in order of ease)

1. **Lead magnet.** A free, useful PDF in exchange for an email address. Formats that work in Ugandan markets: a price list or quotation guide, a "how to choose the right [product]" checklist, a local market report, a step-by-step how-to guide for the client's industry. Host it on the website; promote it on social media and WhatsApp.
2. **WhatsApp-to-email bridge.** Put an email sign-up link in the WhatsApp Business greeting message and in monthly broadcasts. Most clients have WhatsApp contacts who are not on the email list; this turns warm contacts into owned subscribers.
3. **In-store or in-office sign-up.** A physical sign-up sheet at the point of sale or reception, or a QR code linking to a simple landing page with one email field. Highly effective in Ugandan retail and service settings where face-to-face contact dominates.
4. **Social media call to action.** A monthly Facebook and Instagram post pointing to the sign-up page, framed around the lead magnet's value, not the list: "Download our free guide to [topic] — link in bio."
5. **Purchase or booking confirmation.** Collect email in the order or booking confirmation flow; where ordering is by WhatsApp or phone, train staff to ask for an email address at confirmation.

Webinar gating and other Western-default tactics are not the primary options for EA clients.

### 4. Set email cadence and format

- **B2C:** 1–2 emails a month. More than two a month in East Africa raises unsubscribes unless every email gives consistent, tangible value. Start at one a month; increase only once open rates hold above 25%.
- **B2B:** two a month at most — one educational or thought-leadership piece, one commercial offer or announcement.
- **Format:** plain text or a minimal single-column design beats graphic-heavy email on slow mobile connections, still common across Uganda. Avoid large images, embedded video and multi-column layouts; test rendering on Android before sending.

### 5. Build the WhatsApp opt-in list

WhatsApp is the dominant messaging channel across East Africa, but no source measures its share of smartphone users in Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania or Rwanda: state no percentage unless it is a named, dated figure with its base, and check the client's own audience data (see register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026, Kaizen WA-01). For B2C clients a permission-based WhatsApp list is often worth more than an email list; the source claims open rates approaching 95% and near-immediate response (verify before stating; no register record).

Explain broadcast versus groups to every client:

| | WhatsApp Broadcast | WhatsApp Groups |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | One message sent to many contacts individually; recipients cannot see each other; it lands in each individual chat | All members see each other's messages and can reply to the group |
| Condition | Recipients must have saved the business number (source claim; verify at use, no register record) | — |
| Best for | One-way announcements, offers, updates | Community conversation, peer support, loyalty communities |
| Risk | — | Needs more moderation; reputational risk if members post inappropriate content |

Consent: the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 requires explicit, informed consent before any contact is added to a marketing list (verify at use; see register UG-DPPA-2019 and UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026). Never add customers to a broadcast list without their expressed opt-in, keep consent records, and include an opt-out in every broadcast: "Reply STOP to be removed." Messaging must also follow the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY).

Opt-in collection methods:

- In-store card or flyer with the opt-in instruction
- Website pop-up or footer link using a wa.me click-to-chat link
- Social media call-to-action post (Facebook and Instagram)
- QR code in store or on packaging
- Checkout or booking form with a WhatsApp opt-in tick box

Sample opt-in CTA: "Join our WhatsApp updates — be first to hear about new products, offers, and restocks. Send YES to [number] to opt in. You can opt out at any time by replying STOP."

List hygiene: remove contacts who have not opened or replied to any message in 90 days. In Uganda, numbers are often reassigned when SIM cards lapse or are deactivated; messaging recycled numbers wastes money and can harm the brand.

Scale:

- **WhatsApp Business App** — free, managed from one device, suits broadcasts of up to about 256 contacts each (limit as stated in the source; verify at use — no register record).
- **Africa's Talking API or the WhatsApp Business API** — needed above 256 contacts; adds automation, scheduling and CRM integration. Full implementation: [playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing](../../../playbooks/playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing/SKILL.md).

### 6. Plan the blog / content hub

A blog is the core owned asset for clients who want organic search traffic independent of social platforms. Use [blog-writer](../../../content-writing/blog-writer/SKILL.md) for posts and [content-ideas blog and article topic briefs](../../../content-writing/content-ideas/references/blog-and-article-topic-briefs.md) for topics; for a 90-day plan with article briefs, use [12-website-content-plan](../../../pipeline/12-website-content-plan/SKILL.md).

- **Minimum viable blog:** one post a month, at least 800 words, targeting a specific search query tied to the client's industry and location. Low-capacity teams can sustain this; stronger teams should aim for 2–4 posts a month.
- **Content priorities:** find the top 10 questions customers ask before buying and answer each in full in its own post. These posts draw search traffic indefinitely and cut the time sales staff spend on repeated enquiries. Priority title patterns:
  - "How to choose the right [product/service] in [city/country]"
  - "[Product category] prices in Uganda 2026"
  - "Best [product/service] for [specific use case]"
  - "[Comparison]: [Option A] vs [Option B] in Uganda"
- **EA SEO reality (source claim; verify before stating — no register record):** competition for English-language search terms in Uganda is much lower than in Western markets; a well-researched 1,000-word post on a specific Uganda-context keyword can reach page 1 of Google within 3–6 months without paid promotion or backlink campaigns — an advantage most Ugandan businesses have not used.

Blog-to-social pipeline — each published post yields, at no extra research cost:

| Derivative | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 LinkedIn article excerpt | Opening two paragraphs + link |
| 2 Facebook posts | A teaser on publication day; a "did you know?" post two weeks later using a key statistic or tip |
| 1 Instagram caption | Key takeaway + link-in-bio reference |
| 1 email newsletter section | Summary paragraph + read-more link |

### 7. Set the owned-media publishing rhythm

Owned media needs its own rhythm, coordinated with but separate from social activity. Under the RACE framework (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022), social media reaches new audiences; owned media acts, converts and engages them over time.

| Channel | Frequency | Content type | Relationship to social media |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email newsletter | 2× a month | Curated value content + one commercial CTA per send | Drive subscribers from social; share excerpts on social as teasers |
| Blog post | 1× a month minimum | SEO-optimised long form (800–1,500 words) | Promote on all social channels on publication day; link in email |
| WhatsApp broadcast | 2–4× a month | Product updates, offers, reminders, event notices | Post the opt-in CTA on social monthly; drive social traffic to opt-in |
| SMS | Event-triggered | Transactional confirmations + occasional promotion | Covers contacts without smartphones; avoid sending SMS and WhatsApp on the same day |
| YouTube | 1× a month if capacity allows | Tutorial, behind-the-scenes, testimonial, product demo | Embed in the blog post; share on Facebook and Instagram as a short clip with a full-video link |

Fold this rhythm into the master calendar with [11-content-calendar](../../../pipeline/11-content-calendar/SKILL.md); the PESO channel map and integration principles in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) govern how it fits the other three pillars.

### 8. Build the list from social traffic (Bly, 2018)

Owning the audience only happens through working list-building mechanics. Define this sequence for each client:

1. **Social media post** — creates awareness of the lead magnet and drives traffic to the landing page with a clear, benefit-led CTA: "Download our free [lead magnet title] — link in bio / in comments."
2. **Lead magnet** — a free, high-value offer exchanged for a contact detail (price guide, "how to choose" checklist, local market insight report, practical how-to template for Ugandan and EA markets). It must solve a real, specific problem, not be a disguised catalogue.
3. **Landing page** — one purpose, one CTA (the form); no navigation links or competing offers; a headline stating the specific benefit; fields for first name + email address (and/or WhatsApp number where relevant); a trust signal: "No spam. Unsubscribe any time."
4. **Double opt-in confirmation** — straight after submission, an automated email asks the subscriber to confirm by clicking a link.
5. **Welcome sequence** — once confirmed, the subscriber enters the automated five-email onboarding flow in [07-email-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md) ([strategy-document-sections.md § 3. Welcome Sequence](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/references/strategy-document-sections.md)); the full lead-magnet implementation guide (landing-page copy templates, lead-magnet format selection by industry) also routes there.

Double opt-in applies to every list, email or WhatsApp. It:

- protects list quality by excluding mistyped addresses and false sign-ups;
- demonstrates informed consent under Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 and Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 (verify at use; see register UG-DPPA-2019, KE-ODPC-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026);
- reduces hard bounces and spam complaints, protecting sender reputation.

Monthly list hygiene:

- Remove all hard bounces within 24 hours of each send.
- Run a re-engagement campaign ([07-email-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md) ([strategy-document-sections.md § 6. Reactivation Sequence](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/references/strategy-document-sections.md))) for subscribers inactive 90+ days before removing them from the active list.
- Suppress — do not delete — non-responders after reactivation, in case another channel can reach them.
- Never buy, rent or import a third-party list: it lacks the consent the Uganda Act requires, and its very low engagement damages sender reputation for every later send.

## Checklist

- [ ] Audit table complete: every row has the client's current state and a priority action.
- [ ] Red-flag threshold explicit: a client below 500 email subscribers with no WhatsApp opt-in list is named high-risk and list-building is the first priority.
- [ ] Email tool matched to subscriber count, technical capacity and monthly budget; no generic recommendation.
- [ ] WhatsApp and email opt-in language cites the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 consent requirement and includes an opt-out.
- [ ] Blog-to-social pipeline shows one post a month producing content across five or more channels.
- [ ] List-building tactics are EA-relevant (in-store sign-up, WhatsApp-to-email bridge, local lead magnets); webinar gating and similar Western defaults are not primary.
- [ ] Owned-first principle stated: owned channels are the destination all other activity drives towards, consistent with POEM (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022).
- [ ] No WhatsApp percentage, platform limit, price or reach figure is stated without verification.
- [ ] Nothing is sent, published or collected without explicit authority.
