# Customer insight and value-theme method

Read this when the client has no clear picture of why customers buy, when choosing the two or three message themes, or when re-checking positioning after a market shock. Parent skill: [marketing-foundations-stp-positioning](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Customer Insights Report (CIR)

Build from three evidence families (Stutts, 2021):

1. **Consumer research:** why people buy, delay or abandon (interviews, short surveys, reviews, complaint logs).
2. **Behaviour and context:** devices, search terms, platform attention, where purchases happen (analytics, CRM, WhatsApp enquiry themes, platform audience insights).
3. **Life stage and demographics:** household, income band, children, location — used to interpret motives, never as the target definition on its own.

**Contents:** segment map; top three motivators and top repellents per segment; attention map; purchase triggers and objections; what the top three competitors all say (the sameness to avoid); "the difference that makes the difference"; what changed since the last report; sample, method, dates and confidence.

**East Africa substitutes for data brokers:** CRM and mobile-money transaction exports (lawful basis first), WhatsApp enquiry themes (not personal data), a 150–400-response survey shared by WhatsApp/SMS link with an airtime thank-you, 8–12 depth interviews, association or SACCO focus groups, UBOS/KNBS secondary data. Register with the data-protection authority and confirm lawful basis before any list matching (see the register entries used by `biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach`).

**Repellents matter.** One scenario: affluent retirees read heavy discounts as "cheap and untrustworthy"; a bundle made them feel smart. Treat "discount in downturns, bundle in good times" as a hypothesis to test, not a rule.

**Re-survey after a shock.** When prices, regulation, elections or a public-health event change behaviour, re-ask the same questions of the same panel and re-rank motives before changing creative.

## 2. Insight statement

An insight is a truth about the audience paired with a truth about the brand that changes how you see the behaviour (Landa, 2022).

> [Audience] believe [belief], yet [observed behaviour]. [Brand] can resolve that tension because [brand truth].

Separate fixed insights (anchor a brand for years) from dynamic insights (respond to a changed situation).

## 3. Choosing two or three value themes

List every benefit the client wants to claim. Score each 1–5 on:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Customer importance | Does evidence show this drives the target's choice? |
| Proof available | Can we show it with a checkable fact today? |
| Distinctiveness | Do the top three competitors fail to claim or prove it? |

Keep the two or three themes that score high on all three. Everything else becomes supporting copy, FAQ or sales-conversation material (Stutts, 2021: talk about two or three issues out of ten).

**Frame by segment.** The same fact can need two frames: a policy benefit for one group, the effect on the family for another. Test both.

## 4. Benefit tagging for content and advertising

Tag every pillar and asset with the benefit the audience receives; reject any asset whose only benefit is "the brand talks about itself". Internal checklist (Landa's S.U.I.T.E.S., used internally, not presented to clients as an industry standard): social good, utility, information, temptation, entertainment, shareworthy. Utility content (a mobile-money fee calculator, a school-fees planner, a crop-price broadcast) is strong where data is costly and trust is low.

## 5. Brand-slice audit

The brand is every touchpoint: advertisement, website, photos, uniforms, phone and WhatsApp scripts, packaging, reviews (Stutts, 2021). For each slice, check that the value themes are visible and that proof sits where the buyer first looks. Fix critical slices before paid media starts; route the website slice through the journey handoff in `advertising/ad-to-site-journey-handoff`.

## 6. Customer-side sales and script alignment

After a positioning change, rewrite counter, phone and WhatsApp scripts so every human touchpoint repeats the same themes. A campaign that says "pet-safe and local" while the receptionist says "we are the cheapest" breaks the position.
