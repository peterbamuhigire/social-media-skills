## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client needs to collect, verify, govern and place testimonials, reviews, endorsements, certifications and other proof, not only generate new reviews. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns review generation, the GST framework, the objectivity principle and eWOM measurement; this reference adds the six-source proof taxonomy, the multiplicity rule, placement by touchpoint, the testimonial collection protocol and a living proof asset register.

If the request is really about brand voice, tone or messaging foundations rather than proof, route to [04-brand-voice-intake](../../../pipeline/04-brand-voice-intake/SKILL.md) and hand over the verified inputs already collected.

Evidence status. Fake reviews and bought indicators of influence are banned in the United States (register FTC-ENDORSEMENTS-REVIEWS-2026); for Uganda and Kenya no influencer-specific disclosure law was found, so apply FTC/ASA-standard disclosure as best practice (register UG-KE-INFLUENCER-DISCLOSURE-2026). Response-rate and review-count figures below are legacy planning figures with no register record: verify before stating.

## Inputs

Ask for these before producing the register or protocol. Items 1–4 overlap the parent skill's Required Inputs; items 5–7 are specific to proof work.

| # | Input | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client business name | Trading name |
| 2 | Industry | Sector and niche |
| 3 | Country / city | Default Uganda / East Africa |
| 4 | Primary goal | For example: raise website conversion rate, reduce sales objections, build credibility for a new product |
| 5 | Existing proof available | Testimonials, certifications, awards, media mentions, client logos, review-platform ratings |
| 6 | Target audience | B2C or B2B; generational mix if known (see the generational segment lens in [03-audience-personas](../../../pipeline/03-audience-personas/SKILL.md)) |
| 7 | Key conversion points | Where prospects drop off now: pricing page, proposal stage, checkout, first WhatsApp message |

Every proof source must be consent-backed and attributable. Without that, stop the affected decision or issue a clearly bounded partial register.

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Proof sources, claims, audience objections and placement points are current and attributable | Produce the full asset register and collection protocol and cite the evidence used | Decisions based on stale or unrelated evidence |
| A material input is missing or contradictory | Stop that decision, request clarification, or issue a labelled partial result | Fabricated precision and false confidence |
| Consent for name, organisation, photo or platform use is not documented | Do not publish the item; mark it `Restricted` or `No` in the register | Publishing proof without permission |
| Crowd-proof numbers are small or unverified | Do not use crowd proof yet; use another proof type | Inflated or fabricated volume claims |
| A celebrity or influencer endorsement is proposed | Vet audience alignment and engagement authenticity first through [08-influencer-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/08-influencer-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md) | Paying for a mismatched or fake audience |
| A certification or award has expired | Remove it from all placements | Expired proof undermining trust |
| The outcome belongs to brand-voice intake | Route to `04-brand-voice-intake` | Neighbour collision and duplicated work |

## Why social proof works

Buyers are uncertain. Social proof reduces uncertainty by showing that others, people like the prospect, have made the same decision and benefited. In East Africa, where digital commerce is newer and first-time buyers are more cautious, social proof matters even more: it bridges the trust gap before the first transaction and lowers the risk the prospect perceives in buying from a business found online.

## The six sources of social proof

Apply all six sources where available. Breadth matters: several proof types from several categories outperform any single strong testimonial (Bly, 2018). For each source the output states whether it is available now, should be actively collected, or is not yet applicable.

| # | Source | What it is | Most valuable for | How to collect or use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer testimonials | Written or video reviews from real clients with full name, organisation and photo; the most common and most trusted source for service businesses | Service businesses | Collect after every successfully completed project or positive service interaction. Prefer video: a self-recorded smartphone clip is acceptable and often more authentic than polished production; written is the fallback. Prompt: "In 2–3 sentences, describe: (1) the problem you had before working with us, (2) what we did together, and (3) the specific result you achieved." |
| 2 | Expert endorsements | Recommendations from recognised authorities: an industry association, a professor, a respected publication, a well-known consultant or a regulatory body | B2B, professional services, healthcare, education | After a notable piece of work, request a quote from an industry association president, a university department head or a sector publication |
| 3 | Celebrity and influencer endorsements | Recommendations from public figures with large, relevant audiences | Consumer brands with a locally relevant purchase | For East African clients, local musicians, athletes, media personalities and respected business leaders often outperform international names because their audiences trust their judgement on local purchases. Never deploy without vetting audience alignment and engagement authenticity (decision rules above) and disclosing any material connection |
| 4 | Crowd proof (large numbers) | Volume statements that signal popularity and safety: "Over 500 businesses served", "Trusted by 12,000 subscribers", "4.8 stars from 300 reviews" | Any client with genuinely significant numbers | Numbers must be genuine and significant; never fabricate or inflate. If the numbers are not yet impressive, wait or use another type. Where digital reviews are less common, even 50 genuine reviews is significant; state the number with context, for example "50 verified Google reviews — more than any other [industry] provider in [city]" (only if the comparison is checked) |
| 5 | Peer recommendations | Referrals from friends, colleagues or professional networks; the highest-trust source and the hardest to manufacture | All clients | Activate at scale with referral codes or introduction incentives; client advocacy programmes (recognition, exclusive access, early offers); and a WhatsApp prompt after a positive interaction: "If you know anyone who could benefit from [service], we'd love an introduction." Full referral-loop design: [word-of-mouth-and-referral-design.md](word-of-mouth-and-referral-design.md) |
| 6 | Third-party certifications and awards | ISO certifications, industry association memberships, award badges, media features ("As featured in [publication]", "Winner of [award] [year]") | Generation X and Baby Boomer audiences, who place high weight on institutional credibility signals (Rageh, 2026) | Display logos in the website header and footer and in proposals. Refresh annually; expired certifications undermine rather than build trust |

## The multiplicity principle

Several sources of proof from several categories outperform a single strong testimonial (Bly, 2018). A prospect who sees a customer testimonial, a crowd-proof number and an expert endorsement on the same page is more confident than one who sees only one excellent testimonial.

Target: deploy at least three proof types on every primary conversion page.

## Procedure

### 1. Place proof by touchpoint

| Touchpoint | Recommended proof types | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Crowd proof (total clients, star rating) plus 2–3 short testimonials | Above the fold; visible without scrolling on mobile |
| Service pages | Expert endorsements plus testimonials specific to that service | Next to the service description and CTA |
| Proposals and credentials | Case studies (full story with context and result) plus certifications | After the proposed solution; before pricing |
| Email campaigns | One testimonial per email | After the main offer; before the CTA |
| WhatsApp sales conversations | Screenshot of a relevant testimonial or review | When handling a price or quality objection |
| Checkout / payment page | Star rating plus "X clients served" plus a short reassurance quote | Alongside the payment form |

### 2. Collect testimonials

This protocol complements the parent skill's review-request timing at peak satisfaction moments.

1. **Timing.** Ask within 48 hours of a positive client interaction, before the emotion fades. The source states that response rates drop by about 80% when the request comes later (legacy figure, no register record; verify before stating to a client).
2. **Channel.** Send the request by WhatsApp with a direct Google review link or a short video prompt. WhatsApp is the main business channel in East Africa (register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026; state no usage percentage). Message only contacts with opt-in provenance (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY).
3. **Template.** House template (not a quotation): "Hi [Name], I'm glad the [project/service] went well. Could I ask a small favour? In 2–3 sentences, could you describe: (1) the challenge you had before we worked together, (2) what we did, and (3) the result you got? I'd like to feature your words on our website."
4. **Video option.** Offer a guided prompt: "Just record a 30-second voice note or video on your phone answering those three questions — no need to be formal."
5. **Consent.** Confirm consent before publishing, covering name, organisation, photo and platform use. Document consent in writing; a WhatsApp message confirmation is sufficient.

Never offer an incentive in exchange for a Google review (see the parent skill's Incentivised Advocacy rule).

### 3. Maintain the proof asset register

Keep a living register of every proof asset. Minimum fields:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Proof type | Source category: testimonial, expert, crowd, peer, certification, celebrity |
| Source name | Full name and organisation |
| Date collected | DD/MM/YYYY |
| Format | Written / video / screenshot |
| Topics covered | The service or product the proof relates to |
| Approved for use | Yes / No / Restricted |
| Placement | Where it is currently deployed |

Review and refresh the register quarterly. Archive proof more than two years old unless it refers to a long-standing track record.

## Output

A social-proof asset register and collection protocol that the client lead and the next workflow owner can act on. Every recommendation traces to an input, names an owner or next action, and marks assumptions and unassessed checks. Missing access or data is recorded as `not assessed` with its risk and recovery input, never as a pass. Planning does not authorise publishing, spending or editing a live account.
