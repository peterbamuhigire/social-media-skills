# Word-of-Mouth and Referral Programme Design

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-word-of-mouth-strategy on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [playbook-word-of-mouth-strategy.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-word-of-mouth-strategy.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client needs a complete organic word-of-mouth (WOM) programme: who will talk, what they will talk about, the tools that make sharing easy, how the brand joins the conversation, a referral loop with incentives, superfan cultivation, cause-related campaigns and WOM tracking, including WhatsApp dark social. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns review generation, the GST framework and eWOM metrics; this reference turns advocacy into a designed, repeatable programme with owners, controls and measures.

Scope boundaries:

- Organic advocacy only. Paid creator partnerships and UGC collection systems belong to [08-influencer-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/08-influencer-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md).
- Community trust sequencing (the Like-Know-Trust arc) belongs to [playbook-community-management](../../../playbooks/playbook-community-management/SKILL.md).
- Mass-reach viral content belongs to [playbook-viral-content-design](../../../playbooks/playbook-viral-content-design/SKILL.md) (see "WOM seeding versus viral content" below).

Evidence status. Where advocacy involves a reward or material relationship, disclose it clearly and track consent: FTC rules ban fake reviews and require unavoidable disclosure of material connections in the US (register FTC-ENDORSEMENTS-REVIEWS-2026); no influencer-specific disclosure law was found in Uganda or Kenya, so apply FTC/ASA-standard disclosure as best practice (register UG-KE-INFLUENCER-DISCLOSURE-2026). WhatsApp prompts and forwards to customers need opt-in provenance (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY). Facebook access in Uganda has been unstable since 13 June 2026: verify at the campaign date before relying on Facebook for monitoring or sharing (register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026). UGX incentive ranges are legacy planning figures with no register record: verify before stating.

| 6 | Primary WOM channels | Where customers talk about the category: WhatsApp groups, Facebook, in person, church, market, workplace |
| 7 | Referral incentive budget | Approximate monthly budget in UGX, or confirm a zero-budget, recognition-only approach |

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Advocacy requires a reward or material relationship | Disclose it clearly and track consent | Undisclosed incentivised endorsement |
| Net Promoter Score is negative | Fix the customer experience before scaling WOM seeding | Seeding amplifies negative sentiment |
| Trust and conversion are the constraints, or the community is defined and reachable through existing networks | Use WOM seeding | Wasting budget on unpredictable mass reach |
| Reach is the constraint and budget exists for content production | Use viral content through `playbook-viral-content-design` | Expecting a small seeded network to deliver mass awareness |
| A negative mention appears | Escalate through [playbook-crisis-communications](../../../playbooks/playbook-crisis-communications/SKILL.md) and [playbook-reputation-management](../../../playbooks/playbook-reputation-management/SKILL.md) | Treating an incident as an advocacy task |
| A cause campaign is proposed | Choose a cause with genuine brand–community alignment and a concrete, visible commitment; never pay customers to share it | Inauthentic, credibility-destroying campaigns |
| Incentive size is being set | Calibrate to customer lifetime value | Over-rewarding low-value referrals or under-rewarding the most productive advocates |

## Why WOM is the primary growth channel in East Africa

In Uganda and across East Africa, community trust precedes purchase. Advertising meets scepticism; recommendation by a known and respected person is the dominant decision signal. The source attributes to Chaffey (2024) the point that household spending decisions are routinely deferred until a trusted contact has validated the choice (verify before stating as research evidence to a client). The Like-Know-Trust arc every customer travels is owned by `playbook-community-management`; WOM is the mechanism that moves people through that arc at scale without proportional cost.

Under the POEM model (Paid / Owned / Earned; Chaffey, 2024), WOM sits in Earned media: the hardest to manufacture and the highest in credibility. A single recommendation from a trusted community figure can outperform weeks of paid advertising in the same market.

WOM marketing is not passive. It is a designed system: identify the right talkers, give them a compelling topic, equip them with tools, show up in the conversations, and measure the result. This reference applies the five-pillar WOM framework (Word of Mouth Marketing Association, as synthesised by Funk, 2011) throughout.

## Procedure

### 1. Apply the five pillars

Apply all five pillars. Each produces a specific output for the execution plan.

**Pillar 1 — Talkers: who will talk about this brand?** Talkers are predisposed to recommend; they already believe in the product or category. Identify three to five talker segments:

- existing loyal customers (repeat purchasers, high lifetime value);
- community connectors (church leaders, SACCO chairpersons, market association heads, boda-boda stage coordinators);
- professional endorsers (teachers, healthcare workers, respected tradespeople in relevant categories);
- online community members (active commenters, sharers and responders on the brand's social pages);
- local business partners and referral allies (complementary businesses whose customers overlap).

For each segment record: estimated size, primary communication channel (WhatsApp, Facebook, in person) and current relationship with the brand.

**Pillar 2 — Topics: what gives people a reason to talk?** Generic products do not get talked about. Identify what makes the brand remarkable, memorable or emotionally shareable:

- remarkable product or service quality that surprises or exceeds expectation;
- exclusive or insider access, so customers feel they have something others do not;
- humour or personality: a brand voice or moment people want to share;
- origin and values story: locally made, community-owned, ethical sourcing, founder narrative;
- controversy or a bold position on a category issue that earns attention (use with care);
- customer transformation story: a tangible before/after that makes sharing feel like helping others.

Shortlist three to five core talking points. These feed every advocate briefing.

**Pillar 3 — Tools: what enables and amplifies the conversation?** Friction kills WOM. Build the sharing toolkit:

- shareable social content: images, short videos and captions that are easy to screenshot and forward;
- WhatsApp-ready forward packages: one message with brand name, key claim, contact and CTA, formatted for forwarding, not broadcasting;
- referral links or codes: trackable URLs or promo codes for online and hybrid-channel clients;
- ready-made testimonial templates: a prompted format that helps customers write a strong review without effort (see [social-proof-asset-register.md](social-proof-asset-register.md));
- event or community moment assets: a branded photo spot at physical events; shareable recognition posts tagging advocates.

**Pillar 4 — Taking part: how does the brand join its own conversations?** A brand that ignores conversations about itself loses credibility and advocacy momentum. Engagement protocol:

- monitor brand mentions on Facebook, Instagram, X/Twitter and TikTok daily (set up through [meta-social-listening](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-social-listening/SKILL.md));
- respond to every positive mention within 24 hours with a warm, personalised reply, not a template;
- amplify advocate content: reshare, repost or publicly thank customers who post about the brand;
- join relevant community conversations in Facebook Groups, and in WhatsApp where appropriate and welcomed; contribute before promoting;
- escalate negative mentions (decision rules above).

**Pillar 5 — Tracking: how does the brand know WOM is working?** Define tracking before launch (step 7).

### 2. Match advocates to the Gladwell taxonomy

Gladwell (2000) identifies three types of people who spread ideas: Connectors, Mavens and Salespeople. The taxonomy maps directly onto East African community structures. For each candidate advocate ask: do they have broad reach across many groups (Connector)? Do people seek their advice before deciding (Maven)? Do they spontaneously enthuse and recruit others (Salesperson)? Most people lean towards one type; engage accordingly.

| Type | Power | Uganda examples | How to engage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connectors | Breadth: they know an unusually large number of people across diverse groups | WhatsApp group admins running several active groups (church, family, school, business); community and local government leaders (LC1/LC2 chairs, parish councils); church leaders and prominent congregation members; SACCO and cooperative chairpersons; boda-boda stage coordinators and taxi park officials; market association leaders | Equip, do not convince: pre-written WhatsApp forwards, shareable content and referral links they can pass on without effort. Recognise them publicly. Give them first access to new products or announcements so they become the messenger. Keep the message short and easy to pass on; do not burden them with detailed brand briefings |
| Mavens | Depth of credibility: people seek their opinion before deciding | Healthcare workers (doctors, nurses, pharmacists, community health workers); teachers and school administrators; experienced mothers and elder women in family networks; religious leaders with specialist knowledge; professional body leaders (lawyers, accountants, engineers); experienced market traders known for honest product advice | Educate before asking for advocacy: product knowledge, samples or trial access. Mavens will not advocate for what they have not verified. Never script them; an authentic Maven endorsement is worth far more than a recited message. Ask for honest feedback as well as advocacy; they respond to being treated as experts |
| Salespeople | Persuasion: enthusiastic, energetic, they actively convince others | Market traders who champion products they believe in; naturally ebullient, persuasive people in every community; informal existing brand ambassadors; enthusiastic customers who spontaneously recruit friends and family | Give them a stage and a story they can perform, not a fact sheet. A referral programme with visible rewards suits them. Public recognition (naming them in posts, celebrating their results) sustains advocacy. Create a formal or semi-formal ambassador role so they have an identity to embody |

### 3. Identify and cultivate superfans

Superfans are existing customers with a disproportionate emotional investment in the brand; their advocacy is intrinsic and authentic, which makes them the highest-yield WOM asset.

Find them by:

- reviewing Facebook and Instagram comment history for users who comment regularly, share posts and defend the brand unprompted;
- checking DM history for customers who start conversations to express satisfaction (not complaints);
- reviewing saved WhatsApp messages and screenshots of customer feedback;
- identifying repeat purchasers with above-average frequency or basket size in CRM or sales records;
- looking for customers who tag friends in brand posts or create their own unprompted content.

**Surprise and delight.** Identify five to ten top fans and deliver an unexpected reward: a product sample or gift sent without request; a public shoutout tagging the customer by name or handle (with permission); exclusive early access to a new product or menu item before public launch; Mobile Money credit (MTN or Airtel) as a spontaneous thank-you (source range UGX 5,000–50,000 depending on customer value; verify before stating); or a handwritten note from the owner or manager. The unexpectedness is the mechanism: surprise creates an emotional response the customer shares. Do not announce the programme in advance; that turns genuine delight into a reward-seeking queue.

**Build an inner circle** once five to fifteen superfans are identified and activated:

- create a private WhatsApp group with a name that confers identity and belonging (for example "Nile Fresh VIP Community", labelled scenario);
- give members exclusive first access: new flavours, seasonal offers, behind-the-scenes updates and product development input;
- ask for honest feedback on new ideas; superfans who shape a product advocate for it more intensely;
- keep minimum monthly contact: one substantive update, one exclusive offer and one personal acknowledgement per month;
- feature superfans publicly on the brand's platforms from time to time, with permission.

**Maintain the relationship over time:** record birthdays and send a personal message or small gift each year; acknowledge anniversaries ("You've been a customer for two years — thank you"); offer preferential treatment at physical touchpoints (priority service, reserved seating, advance notice of events); if the inner circle goes quiet, re-engage with personal outreach, not a broadcast.

### 4. Design the referral loop

A referral programme needs three things, or referrals stay accidental:

- **Incentive.** What does the referrer receive, and what does the new customer receive? Both sides must benefit for the loop to last.
- **Low friction.** Sharing must take no more than two steps: forward a WhatsApp message, screenshot a code or tap a link. More friction and uptake collapses.
- **Trigger moment.** Ask at peak satisfaction, immediately after a positive experience, successful purchase or compliment; not at checkout.

East African referral mechanics:

- **WhatsApp forward referral.** One pre-written message the customer forwards to contacts: brand name, a one-line claim and a contact number or link, plus a personal element ("My friend [name] told me to share this with you"). Track with a unique code per advocate.
- **Screenshot proof referral.** For offline or informal-channel businesses, ask the referred customer to show the WhatsApp message or screenshot that brought them in. No technical infrastructure needed.
- **Referral code system.** Give each active advocate a unique code (first name plus a number is enough). New customers quote it on purchase; log redemptions in a spreadsheet or simple form.
- **Mobile Money reward delivery.** Paying rewards through MTN Mobile Money or Airtel Money is instant, frictionless and personally meaningful, and the source rates it the most effective fulfilment method in Uganda. Send rewards within 24 hours of a confirmed referral.

Source incentive ranges by customer tier (planning figures only; verify before stating):

| Customer tier | Referrer reward | New-customer offer |
|---|---|---|
| Low-value purchase (under UGX 20,000) | UGX 2,000–5,000 Mobile Money credit or equivalent discount | 10% off first purchase |
| Mid-value purchase (UGX 20,000–100,000) | UGX 5,000–15,000 Mobile Money credit or equivalent product | 10–15% off first purchase |
| High-value purchase (over UGX 100,000) | UGX 15,000–50,000 Mobile Money credit or service upgrade | Free consultation, trial or delivery |

Calibrate to customer lifetime value. Do not tie any reward to a Google review (parent skill, Incentivised Advocacy).

### 5. Run cause-related WOM campaigns

Customers share campaigns they believe in. A cause that genuinely resonates with the brand's community earns advocacy motivated by values rather than incentives.

Cause categories that suit East African audiences:

- education: school fee support, scholastic materials, bursaries (resonates across all demographics);
- community uplift: infrastructure contributions, clean water, local facility improvements;
- healthcare: medical camps, health information campaigns, mother-and-child health;
- environment: tree planting, waste management, clean-environment initiatives (growing relevance in urban East Africa);
- youth empowerment: skills training, mentorship, enterprise support.

Design sequence:

1. Choose a cause genuinely aligned with the brand's values and customer community, not one chosen for PR value alone. Customers detect inauthenticity and it destroys the campaign.
2. Define a concrete, visible commitment: not "we support education" but "for every product sold in March, we contribute UGX 500 to school fees at [named local school]".
3. Show impact in real time with updates, photos and numbers. Transparency turns the campaign into ongoing storytelling people want to follow and share.
4. Invite participation beyond purchase: volunteering, in-kind donation collections, or sharing a campaign hashtag that triggers an additional brand contribution.
5. Close the loop: publish what was achieved and thank the community by name.

The sharing trigger: people share cause campaigns because doing so publicly signals their own values; the share is self-expressive, not commercial. Design the visual and caption so that sharing makes the customer look good to their network. Never pay customers to share cause content; paid sharing destroys credibility for customer and brand alike. For purpose-led positioning beyond a single campaign, see [strategy-csr-purpose-communications](../../strategy-csr-purpose-communications/SKILL.md).

### 6. Choose WOM seeding or viral content

These are different strategies; confusing them misallocates effort.

| | Viral content | WOM seeding |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Broadcast to a mass audience; relies on algorithmic amplification | Targeted activation of trusted nodes (Connectors, Mavens, Salespeople) in defined communities |
| Predictability | No formula reliably produces viral outcomes | The right talker with the right topic and tools produces measurable referrals |
| Durability | Spikes and fades; rarely builds lasting affinity | Advocacy relationships last months and years |
| Engineering | Attempts to "make something go viral" are usually detectable and counterproductive | A designed system with clear inputs produces reliable outputs |
| Use when | Launching a new brand to cold audiences, entering a new market, or creating cultural relevance quickly at scale | Building trust in a defined community, converting awareness to purchase, sustaining a brand with a limited paid-media budget |

Most Uganda and East Africa clients gain more from WOM seeding, because their category decisions are community-driven rather than mass-media-driven. The two can coexist: viral content lasts longer when it lands in communities already seeded with advocates who can anchor the conversation.

### 7. Track WOM

WOM without tracking is guesswork. Build measurement in from day one.

**Online referral tracking.**

- Tag every referral link with UTM parameters (see [measurement-tracking-plan](../../../meta-analytics-ops/measurement-tracking-plan/SKILL.md)): `utm_source=wom&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=[advocate-name]`.
- Track clicks, landing-page visits and conversions per referral source in Google Analytics or equivalent.
- Give each advocate a unique code or link so individual performance is visible.

**Offline and WhatsApp referral tracking.**

- Ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" at enquiry or purchase and record the source in a simple spreadsheet.
- Screenshot proof: ask referred customers to show the WhatsApp message that prompted them; log the referring advocate's name.
- Log each advocate's code redemptions in a master referral register (a Google Sheet is enough for most SMEs).

**WhatsApp dark social.** Forwards pass through private channels invisible to standard analytics. Estimate reach by:

- asking advocates how many active groups they forwarded to and the approximate group size;
- watching for spikes in direct traffic, branded search and inbound WhatsApp enquiries after a seeding push;
- logging the date and source of each new enquiry and noting clusters that coincide with a seeding event.

**Net Promoter Score.** Ask: "On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend [brand] to a friend or colleague?" NPS = % promoters (9–10) minus % detractors (0–6). A positive score indicates a WOM-ready customer base; a negative score means seeding will surface negative sentiment, so resolve experience problems first. Survey quarterly. (The parent skill's measurement framework gives the NPS bands and monthly eWOM metrics.)

**Brand mention monitoring** (full set-up in `meta-social-listening`). For WOM track: direct brand-name mentions on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and X/Twitter; unsolicited testimonial posts and comments, logged as earned WOM events; and sentiment trend over time, where improving sentiment correlates with WOM momentum.

**Reporting cadence.**

| Cadence | Measures |
|---|---|
| Weekly | Referral code redemptions, new-customer source log, advocate activity notes |
| Monthly | NPS, total attributed referrals, referral conversion rate, Mobile Money rewards paid |
| Quarterly | Advocate roster review: promote active advocates, re-engage dormant ones, retire inactive ones |

## Output

A WOM programme for the client owner and delivery team that uses named inputs, assigns every action an owner, timing and acceptance condition, states decisions and contains no unverified specifics; plus an assumption and gap register in which every missing source, unassessed check and required approval has an owner or next action. A worked example uses a labelled scenario, never fabricated client evidence.

## Checklist

- [ ] Advocate specificity: named advocate types (Connector, Maven, Salesperson) with realistic Uganda / East Africa community examples for the client's industry, not generic descriptions.
- [ ] Operational precision: referral mechanics, incentives and WhatsApp tools are described at execution level, so a client could act without further clarification.
- [ ] Cause authenticity: any cause campaign rests on genuine brand–community alignment, and the emotional sharing trigger is explained.
- [ ] SMART objectives: at least three WOM goals with metrics, timeframes and baselines (for example "achieve NPS of +30 within six months, up from a current baseline of +12").
- [ ] WOM versus viral: the plan applies seeding logic (targeted, relationship-based, durable) and does not conflate it with viral content or paid influencer strategy.
- [ ] Dark social: the tracking plan addresses WhatsApp dark social with specific proxy measures rather than dismissing it.
- [ ] Community network specificity: talker and connector analysis names real Uganda / East Africa network types (church, SACCO, boda-boda, market associations) where relevant.
- [ ] Disclosure: every reward or material relationship is disclosed and consent is tracked.
- [ ] Cross-skill coherence: links to `playbook-community-management`, `meta-social-listening`, `measurement-tracking-plan` and `08-influencer-marketing-strategy` without duplicating their content.
