# Webinars, Live Streams and Virtual Events

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-webinars-live-events on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [playbook-webinars-live-events.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-webinars-live-events.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the experience is a webinar, a live stream (Facebook Live, Instagram Live, YouTube Live), a Zoom webinar, a Google Meet session or a hybrid in-person event broadcast online, and the client needs the operating playbook: platform choice, a four-week plan, a day-by-day promotion calendar, a timed run sheet, an East African technical checklist and post-event actions. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns the experience concept, Schmitt's four features, experience design principles and the ExM measurement framework; this reference turns a live or virtual session into a repeatable run with owners, controls and measures.

Evidence status. Facebook access in Uganda has been unstable since 13 June 2026 (Facebook was blocked from January 2021): verify at the event date before making Facebook Live the default for Ugandan audiences, and keep a YouTube Live or WhatsApp fallback (register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026). Ugandan mobile download speeds vary widely by operator and town (UCC means 5.3–16.3 Mbps in 2024; register UG-MOBILE-SPEED-2026), so test the actual location. WhatsApp broadcasts need opt-in provenance and approved templates outside the 24-hour window (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY). Platform prices, free-tier limits, upload-speed minimums, image sizes and boost amounts below are legacy figures with no register record: verify before stating.

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| The live session lacks moderation, recording consent or a failure backup | Do not go live until each control has a named owner | Public harm or unusable event evidence |
| Broad consumer audience on mobile data | Default to Facebook Live if access is confirmed at the event date; otherwise YouTube Live | Viewers unable to join or buffering |
| Professional or B2B audience needing structured Q&A | Run a Zoom Webinar and stream it simultaneously to Facebook Live using Zoom's stream-key feature (verify current feature and plan) | Choosing between interactivity and reach |
| Small internal training group (up to about 100) | Google Meet | Paying for a webinar platform unnecessarily |
| Physical venue with remote audience | Hybrid: in person plus Facebook Live (if access is confirmed at the event date) or YouTube Live, with a dedicated digital host | One MC failing both audiences |
| Only one speaker | Replace the panel block with extended Q&A or a live demonstration | Dead time in the run sheet |
| Duration would exceed 60 minutes | Cut to 60 minutes or less | Losing viewers who pay for mobile data |
| Event longer than 30 minutes | Put a UPS or generator on standby | Stream loss during load-shedding |
| Publishing, spend, messaging or production changes are proposed | Require explicit approval before action | Unauthorised external impact |

## Procedure

### 1. Select the event type

| Format | Platform | Best for | Bandwidth need (viewer) | Cost (verify) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Live | Facebook | Broad consumer audience; existing Page followers | Low: viewable on 2G/3G | Free |
| Instagram Live | Instagram | Urban, younger audience (18–35) | Medium | Free |
| YouTube Live | YouTube | Structured, longer content; searchable after broadcast | Medium–high | Free |
| Zoom Webinar | Zoom | Professional B2B, interactive, structured Q&A | High: poor on mobile data | Free (40 min) / about UGX 56,000 per month |
| Google Meet | Google | Internal training, small groups up to 100 | Medium | Free |
| Hybrid (in person plus Facebook Live if access is confirmed at the event date, or YouTube Live) | Facebook or YouTube | Maximising reach from a physical venue | Low for remote viewers | Venue or studio cost only |

The source rates Facebook Live the most accessible format for Uganda because viewers can watch on slow 3G/4G without buffering; apply that only once Facebook access is confirmed (evidence status above).

Position the event with the RACE framework (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022):

- **Reach:** promotional posts and paid boosting to attract new audiences;
- **Act:** registration or RSVP as the first conversion step;
- **Convert:** the event itself drives the primary CTA (purchase, enquiry, sign-up);
- **Engage:** replay, FAQ content and follow-up nurture after the event.

### 2. Plan over four weeks

**Week 4 (28 days out)**

- Lock topic, date and time. Default windows: 12:00–13:00 EAT (lunchtime) or 18:00–19:00 EAT (after work). Avoid before 10:00 EAT and after 20:00 EAT.
- Confirm all speakers; send a calendar invitation with a one-page briefing covering topic scope, session length and technical requirements.
- Choose the platform and create the event page: a Facebook Event, Eventbrite (free tier available; verify) or a simple landing page.
- Write the event description, 150 words maximum: who it is for, what attendees will learn, and exactly how to join (link, app or dial-in).
- Set a SMART objective (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022), for example "Register 200 attendees by D-1 and retain 60% for at least 30 minutes of the live session."

**Week 3 (21 days out)**

- Brief the designer on promotional graphics: landscape (1200×628 px) for Facebook/YouTube, square (1080×1080 px) for the Instagram feed, portrait (1080×1920 px) for Stories and Reels. For paid placements, current Meta specifications differ (feed 4:5 at 1440×1800; Stories and Reels 9:16 at 1440×2560, register META-CREATIVE-SPECS-2026); check before briefing. This playbook does not produce graphic files.
- Launch promotion: three social posts, one email to the mailing list, one WhatsApp broadcast.
- Open registrations and start tracking RSVPs.
- Publish the first speaker spotlight on Facebook and Instagram.

**Week 2 (14 days out)**

- Second promotional push: countdown Stories and "what you will learn" posts listing three key takeaways.
- Confirm panellist talking points; share a slide template if speakers are presenting.
- Run a full technical rehearsal: internet upload speed (source minimum 5 Mbps for live streaming; verify against the platform), audio quality, camera framing and screen share.
- Send speakers a technical guide covering camera, audio, lighting and backup connection.

**Week 1 (7 days out)**

- Final reminders: post three days before, one day before and on the morning of the event.
- Prepare the run sheet (step 4) and circulate it to all speakers and co-hosts.
- Run a live-stream test from the actual broadcast location, not only from a home office.
- Confirm the WhatsApp broadcast list is populated and the day-of message is drafted and ready.

Assign a named owner to every task: host, co-host, designer or speaker.

### 3. Build the promotion calendar

Fill in client-specific copy for each entry.

| Day | Channel | Content type | Message focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-28 | All social + email | Announcement post | Event announced; registration/RSVP link prominent |
| D-21 | Facebook + Instagram | Speaker spotlight | Speaker 1 with photo, title and a one-sentence expertise statement |
| D-21 | WhatsApp broadcast | Text message | Topic, date, time EAT, join link |
| D-14 | All social + email | "What you will learn" | Three takeaways; registration link |
| D-14 | Facebook + Instagram | Speaker spotlight | Speaker 2 (if applicable) |
| D-7 | Email + social | Urgency post | "One week away — have you registered?" plus registration link |
| D-3 | Facebook + Instagram | Countdown post | Behind-the-scenes preparation; build anticipation |
| D-1 | All channels | Final reminder | "Tomorrow at [time] EAT — join us here: [link]" |
| D-0 (08:00 EAT) | WhatsApp + email | Day-of reminder | Join link plus instructions for joining on mobile |
| D-0 (at go-live) | Facebook | Go-live post | "We are LIVE now — join here: [link]" |
| D+1 | All channels | Recording share | Replay link, three highlights and the next CTA |

Classify each channel with the POEM model (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022): Facebook Page posts and WhatsApp broadcasts are Owned; organic shares and word of mouth are Earned; boosted posts are Paid. If the client has budget, recommend a small paid boost on the D-7 and D-1 posts (source range UGX 30,000–50,000 per post; verify before stating; spend needs explicit authority).

### 4. Prepare the run sheet

Populate with the client's speaker names and topic titles. The host manages time; a co-host monitors comments and questions throughout.

| Time (EAT) | Segment | Owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| −15 min | Speakers join; tech check | Host | Verify audio, camera, slides and internet for all participants |
| 00:00 | Welcome and introduction | Host | Three minutes maximum; topic, format and speakers |
| 00:03 | Speaker 1 presentation | [Name, Title] | 12 minutes; Key Topic 1 |
| 00:15 | Q&A — Speaker 1 | Host + co-host | 5 minutes; co-host reads questions from comments |
| 00:20 | Speaker 2 presentation | [Name, Title] | 12 minutes; Key Topic 2 |
| 00:32 | Q&A — Speaker 2 | Host + co-host | 5 minutes |
| 00:37 | Panel discussion | All speakers | 10 minutes; two pre-agreed questions plus live audience questions |
| 00:47 | Wrap-up and call to action | Host | What to do next; where to find the recording; next event teaser |
| 00:50 | End broadcast | Host | Thank attendees; close the stream |

Keep total duration at or under 60 minutes: East African audiences on mobile data are sensitive to session length.

### 5. Run the East African technical checklist

Complete at the broadcast location at least 30 minutes before going live.

**Internet and connectivity**

- [ ] Use a wired ethernet connection where possible; do not rely on Wi-Fi alone.
- [ ] On mobile data, confirm full 4G signal at the exact broadcast spot; signal varies by room and floor.
- [ ] Have a second mobile data hotspot on a different network (for example MTN primary, Airtel backup) ready to switch within 60 seconds.
- [ ] Test upload speed: source minimums are 5 Mbps for Facebook Live or YouTube Live and 10 Mbps for a Zoom Webinar with video (verify against each platform's current guidance).

**Power**

- [ ] Charge all devices (laptop, phone, ring light) to 100% before the event.
- [ ] Plug into mains power during the broadcast; do not run on battery.
- [ ] For events longer than 30 minutes, have a UPS or generator on standby; load-shedding in Uganda is unpredictable.
- [ ] Confirm the venue's power supply is reliable; for hybrid events, brief the venue on UPS requirements.

**Audio**

- [ ] Use a wired lapel microphone or USB headset; built-in laptop microphones pick up background noise and echo.
- [ ] Test audio with a colleague on the same platform before going live.
- [ ] Reduce background noise: close windows, silence phones, post a "Recording in Progress" notice outside the room.

**Video and lighting**

- [ ] Face a window or use a ring light; never sit with a window or bright light behind you.
- [ ] Frame the camera at eye level; avoid looking down into the lens.
- [ ] Use a clean, professional background; a branded backdrop is recommended for client events.
- [ ] Set camera resolution to at least 720p; 1080p where bandwidth allows.

**Pre-broadcast test**

- [ ] Go live in a private or unlisted test stream 30 minutes before the real broadcast.
- [ ] Assign a co-host: one person presents, one monitors comments, questions and technical issues; never combine both roles.
- [ ] Prepare a contingency post in case the stream drops: "We are experiencing a technical issue — we will be back in 5 minutes. Watch here: [backup link]".
- [ ] Confirm recording consent from speakers and, where attendees appear, a notice to attendees.

### 6. Complete post-event actions within 24 hours

**Recording and distribution**

- Download the recording from Facebook video manager, YouTube Studio or the Zoom recording dashboard.
- If the event was not on YouTube, upload the recording there immediately. Optimise the title (include the topic keyword), the description (250 words minimum with timestamps) and tags.
- Trim dead air at the start or end: brief a video editor (this playbook does not produce edited video files).

**Follow-up communications**

- Within 6 hours, email all registrants: thank-you, replay link, three key takeaways and one clear next CTA (book a call, download a resource, register for the next event). Sequence design: [07-email-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md).
- Send a WhatsApp broadcast with the replay link and a one-line summary (broadcast rules: [playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing](../../../playbooks/playbook-sms-whatsapp-marketing/SKILL.md)).
- Post the replay on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn (if B2B) and YouTube with a 100–150-word highlight caption.

**Content repurposing**

- Turn the top five questions from the event into standalone social posts, a FAQ blog post or short video clips; full workflow in [meta-content-repurposing](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-content-repurposing/SKILL.md).
- Extract two or three strong quotes or statistics for pull-quote graphics on Instagram and LinkedIn (brief a designer with the text).
- Add the recording to the client's YouTube playlist and embed it in a blog post; structure in [blog-writer](../../../content-writing/blog-writer/SKILL.md).
- Aim for at least five derivative assets from each recording.

**Reporting (within 48 hours)**

- Record peak concurrent viewers, total replay views (at 7 days), registrations versus attendees (show-up rate), top questions and CTA click-through rate; add them to the monthly report using [meta-reporting](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-reporting/SKILL.md). These sit alongside the parent skill's ExM metrics (attendance rate, post-event NPS, 30-day conversion).
- Update the media contacts log if journalists, influencers or public figures attended or shared the event.

Fold the promotion calendar into the client's monthly schedule with [11-content-calendar](../../../pipeline/11-content-calendar/SKILL.md).

## Output

A webinar or live-event playbook for the client owner and delivery team that uses named inputs, assigns every action an owner, timing and acceptance condition, and contains no unverified specifics; plus an assumption and gap register in which every missing source, unassessed check and required approval has an owner or next action. A worked example uses a labelled scenario, never fabricated client evidence.

## Checklist

- [ ] Platform selection is justified with explicit reference to East African bandwidth conditions, Facebook access at the event date and the target audience, not a generic recommendation.
- [ ] The pre-event timeline covers all four weeks with specific, dated tasks assigned to named owners (host, co-host, designer, speakers).
- [ ] The promotion calendar is day by day, with channel, content type and message focus for every entry from D-28 to D+1.
- [ ] The run sheet gives exact timings in EAT with each segment, speaker name, duration and host/co-host responsibilities.
- [ ] The technical checklist is location-specific and includes power backup (UPS/generator), dual-network internet redundancy and audio hardware.
- [ ] Moderation, recording consent and a failure backup each have an owner before going live.
- [ ] Post-event actions are time-bound (within 6, 24 and 48 hours) and include recording upload, follow-up email, WhatsApp broadcast and replay posts.
- [ ] The repurposing plan turns the recording into at least five derivative assets (social posts, FAQ blog, quote graphics).
- [ ] Prices, limits, speeds and image sizes are verified or marked "verify before stating".
