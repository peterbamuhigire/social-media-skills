# Humanising rewrite passes

Merged from skills/ai-marketing/ai-content-humaniser on 2026-09-29 at 7c60138; preservation map: [ai-content-humaniser.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/ai-content-humaniser.md)

## When to use this reference

Use it when an AI-assisted draft already exists and must be reviewed and rewritten before client delivery: captions, blog posts, emails, strategy documents and proposals. The `anti-ai-slop` guardrails prevent slop while writing; these passes are the quality-control process that closes the gap between an AI draft and content that reads as written by a knowledgeable expert who knows Uganda and East Africa. The banned vocabulary for the sweep is the single list in the parent skill's "Banned / high-risk vocabulary" section; it already contains every word and phrase from the former humaniser list.

## Principles

- **The Golden Rule.** The agency is AI-powered. Every output (text, captions, proposals, blog posts, emails, strategy documents) must look, feel and sound as if skilled human creatives made it with close attention to detail and deep knowledge of the target audience. The test is not "does this pass a grammar check?" but "would an expert in this market, reading this cold, believe a skilled human wrote it?" Apply these passes to every piece of AI-assisted content before client delivery.
- **Earn the place, do not conceal.** AI tools produce fast drafts that are also generic, culturally misaligned and over-smooth, which reveals their origin to any experienced reader. The goal is not to hide AI involvement; it is to make the output accurate, specific, culturally grounded and written in the client's voice.
- **AI as creative partner** (Johnsen, 2024). AI drafts are raw material, never finished work. Editors have full authority, and full responsibility, to rewrite rather than polish. A light polish that leaves AI fingerprints is a failure of this process.
- **Human-English overlay.** Load the [human English craft standard](../../../language/language-standards/references/human-english-craft-standard.md). Humanisation means repairing audience fit, specificity, evidence, structure, grammar, collocation, rhythm and truthful claims. It does not mean disguising AI with deliberate errors or slang. Require a read-aloud/proof pass and record the human reviewer.
- **Improve, do not disguise.** Humanise by improving truth, specificity, audience fit and useful structure. For AI-assisted workflows, require specific disclosure and a human correction path.

## Inputs

Confirm before any humanisation review:

1. **Client business name**: whose brand voice does this content represent?
2. **Industry**: what sector is the client in?
3. **Country / city**: where is the audience? (Default: Uganda / East Africa.)
4. **Primary goal**: what is the content meant to achieve?
5. **Content type**: caption, blog post, email, strategy document, proposal or other.
6. **Draft to review**: the full AI-generated draft.
7. **Brand voice notes**: existing tone-of-voice guidelines, sample copy, or adjectives the client uses for its brand (for example "professional but warm", "bold and direct").

Also confirm the audience situation, the narrative job of the piece, first-screen information hierarchy, readability, accessibility, disclosure, permission, and correction/escalation needs before sign-off.

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Three or more uncanny-valley signs present (Pass 2) | Full rewrite, not a polish. | Almost-human text that readers reject without knowing why. |
| Draft fails more than two Human Voice questions (Pass 4) | Rewrite, do not patch. | Patched AI text that keeps its fingerprints. |
| One section is strong, others weak | Review and approve every section independently. | Approving the whole because one part is good. |
| Any statistic, citation, named entity, date or product/service claim | Run the Hallucination Gate (Pass 5). | Fabricated data reaching a client. |
| Content translated or adapted into Luganda, Swahili, Amharic or another EA language | Commission a native-speaker review before delivery. | Linguistically correct but culturally foreign idioms, proverbs or examples. |
| AI-generated content depicts people, cultures, identities or communities | Run the cultural bias check with a reviewer who has direct cultural knowledge; mandatory for EA clients. | Western-centric, gender-stereotyped or racially inaccurate depictions. |
| Substantially AI-generated piece the client intends to register or licence | Flag for legal review by a qualified IP solicitor. | Assuming copyright in work that may not qualify. |
| AI disclosure is provided | Make it specific: "AI-generated [element], art-directed and revised by [human team]". | Vague "Made with AI" labels that mislead audiences. |
| Review load exceeds the volume limits (Sign-off) | Schedule more sessions; never compress review time. | Degraded attention and missed issues. |
| Draft polished but audience problem or next action still unfixed | Return to the brief, narrative job, proof and CTA. | Polishing a generic draft. |
| Disclosure offered in place of review | Verify facts, permissions, accessibility, cultural fit and correction routes anyway. | Treating disclosure as a substitute for review. |

## Pass 1: the five AI quality risks

Every AI-generated draft carries these five risks (Ltifi and Johnsen, forthcoming). Check all five before approving any piece.

1. **Originality.** AI is trained on existing content and blurs into plagiarism territory; output may reproduce phrases, structures or ideas without attribution. Run any unusually polished passage through a plagiarism check before delivery.
2. **Quality inconsistency.** AI does not consistently meet professional standards; one draft may hold one strong paragraph and three weak ones. Review every section independently.
3. **Bias and stereotyping.** AI tools are trained mainly on Western data and carry Western assumptions about audiences, markets, behaviours and values. Content for a Ugandan audience without local review often reflects these biases, from irrelevant to actively alienating.
4. **Misinformation.** AI produces convincing but false claims, invented statistics and hallucinated citations. Treat every statistic, figure and citation as unverified until confirmed against a primary source. Never pass fabricated data to a client.
5. **Flatness.** AI lacks the texture, finesse, inventiveness and specificity that come from lived expertise. Content can be technically correct yet hollow. Flatness is the hardest risk to quantify and the most damaging to brand credibility over time.

## Pass 2: uncanny valley calibration

Almost-but-not-quite-human text triggers a stronger rejection than obvious AI text: readers cannot name what is wrong, but they feel something is off and disengage (Ltifi, 2024). Run this pass before Pass 4.

Structural signs:

- [ ] Every paragraph is roughly the same length.
- [ ] Transitions are consistently smooth, with no abrupt shifts in register.
- [ ] No natural hesitation, qualification or colloquialism.
- [ ] Phrasing is suspiciously balanced, with no strongly held positions.
- [ ] Vocabulary comes from the same formal register throughout.

Three or more signs: full rewrite. Otherwise apply the four corrective moves:

1. **Break the symmetry.** Vary paragraph length deliberately. Follow a long paragraph with one short sentence. Use a sentence fragment for emphasis.
2. **Insert a real detail.** Add one specific, locally grounded detail only a person familiar with the market would include: a Kampala neighbourhood, a named local brand, a Uganda-specific price point, a cultural reference that lands for this audience.
3. **Take a position.** Replace "one approach is…" with "I recommend…" or "the evidence suggests…". AI defaults to false balance; human experts have opinions.
4. **Introduce a controlled imperfection.** A conversational aside, a rhetorical question, a sentence that starts with "And", a regional idiom used correctly. These are authenticity signals, not mistakes.

## Pass 3: vocabulary sweep

Word-search the draft against the parent skill's banned list (words, phrases, over-smooth connectors, weak hedges). Presence of any item signals an unreviewed draft: remove or rewrite every instance, and do not swap in a synonym from the same register; rewrite to be direct.

## Pass 4: Human Voice Checklist

Apply all eleven questions. Failing more than two means rewrite, not patch.

1. Does the opening hook grab attention without being generic or clichéd? If not, replace the first sentence entirely; do not smooth it.
2. Is there at least one concrete detail: a number, a named place, a named person, a real event? Generality is the primary signal of AI authorship.
3. Does the content take a clear position ("I recommend…", "the evidence points to…") rather than false balance?
4. Is any sentence over 35 words? Split it. Long, subordinate-clause-heavy sentences are an AI signature; short sentences carry authority.
5. Does the vocabulary feel natural to the audience, or like a marketing textbook? Read aloud; if you stumble, the audience will.
6. Are any facts, statistics or citations unverified against a primary source? Mark every unverified claim before proceeding.
7. Does it read as written by someone who knows Uganda and East Africa as the normal context, not a foreign market? If not, run Pass 6.
8. Is the CTA clear and direct? "Learn more" is not a CTA; "Send us a WhatsApp message on 0700 000 000 before Friday" is.
9. Is all banned vocabulary absent? Word-search; do not rely on reading alone.
10. Would you be comfortable if the client knew exactly how this was produced (AI draft, reviewed and revised by this process)? If not, it is not ready.
11. Does it have the natural micro-variation in sentence length, register and vocabulary of human writing, or AI's mechanical uniformity? If every sentence feels structurally similar, add deliberate variation before it can pass.

**"Ineffable something" test** (Ching and Mothi, 2025, p. 64, citing Tyler Cowen's March 2024 review of Suno AI). Does the content move, surprise or connect in a way that is unmistakably human? If a skilled reader would feel nothing in particular, it fails the Golden Rule however correct its grammar. The test is subjective by design. A technically correct piece that leaves the reviewer with no reaction needs a rewrite at the level of voice and meaning, not vocabulary. Apply it as the final quality gate after every checklist item is satisfied.

**Micro-moment register matching** (Ltifi, 2024). At high-stakes customer touchpoints (a cart-abandonment message, a post-browse retargeting caption, a comparison-shopping landing page) the copy must match the emotional register of that exact moment. Generic AI output cannot detect emotional context; editors must impose it.

- **Urgency without pressure:** time-sensitive offers need energy, not aggression.
- **Reassurance without sycophancy:** trust-building moments need warmth, not flattery.
- **Confidence without arrogance:** authority content conveys expertise, not dismissiveness.

Before approving content for a known customer-journey touchpoint, ask: does the tone match what the audience feels at this moment?

## Pass 5: editing by content type

### Captions

1. Check the first line (the hook). Rewrite if it opens with a question, a generic statement or any banned phrase; it must earn the tap to expand.
2. Remove every banned word and phrase; rewrite to be direct rather than substituting a same-register synonym.
3. Add one specific local detail: a place name, local reference, Ugandan price point or relevant local event. This alone separates local from generic content.
4. Make the CTA direct and platform-appropriate; on WhatsApp-first platforms, reference WhatsApp where relevant.
5. Read aloud. If it sounds like a press release, simplify. If it sounds like a list, restructure around a single idea.

### Blog posts

1. Rewrite the opening if it starts with a definition ("According to the Oxford Dictionary…"), a decontextualised statistic or any "In today's…" variant. Open with the central argument or the reader's problem, immediately.
2. Include at least two clear opinions or recommendations per article.
3. Verify every statistic and citation against its primary source; replace any unverifiable one with a verified alternative or a first-hand observation.
4. Add at least one specific, accurate East African example, case study or reference. Generic "African" examples are not sufficient.
5. Check each section heading: does the section answer a genuine question, or restate the heading as a paragraph? If the latter, restructure.

### Email copy

1. **Subject line:** genuine curiosity without clickbait. Would you open it? Would this client's audience?
2. **Preview text:** adds new information rather than repeating the subject line; extend the subject line's idea.
3. **First paragraph:** addressed to the reader as an individual; replace "our valued customers" and "businesses like yours" with direct, specific address.
4. **CTA:** exactly one, specific about what to do, where and when. Multiple CTAs split attention and reduce conversion.
5. **Tone:** matches how the client actually talks to its customers; cross-check against brand voice notes.

### Strategy documents and proposals

1. Every recommendation is specific and actionable. "Leverage synergies across digital touchpoints" is not a recommendation; "Post three times per week on Facebook, focusing on video content between 18:00 and 20:00 EAT" is.
2. The document reflects the client's situation, not boilerplate with the name inserted; replace every generic section with a client-specific observation.
3. Verify all AI-generated market data (penetration figures, demographics, platform usage) against a named, dated primary source, and footnote it.
4. Show knowledge of the Uganda and East Africa market specifically; check for Western assumptions (credit-card penetration, broadband access, regulatory environment, platform algorithm behaviour in the region).

### Hallucination Gate (all content types)

Sources: Evelyn (2025); Mizrahi (2024). Check statistics, citations, named entities, dates and product/service claims. For any output making factual claims, run: "Use web search to find the latest news and resources, and cite your sources", then verify each cited source directly before delivery. Never assume web-search output is correct. The gate is a production standard for any content that makes claims of fact, not an option.

## Pass 6: cultural localisation checklist for East Africa

Run on every piece before client delivery.

- [ ] No assumption of Western payment methods (credit cards, PayPal, Stripe) where Mobile Money (MTN, Airtel) dominates for the audience.
- [ ] Examples, case studies and statistics are not drawn from the US, UK or Europe when East African equivalents exist or should be used.
- [ ] Tone matches Ugandan professional norms (warm, respectful, relationship-first, community-oriented) rather than the transactional directness common in Western copy.
- [ ] Prices are in UGX for local audiences, or the relevant local currency for the market.
- [ ] The WhatsApp-first mobile environment is acknowledged where relevant. In Uganda WhatsApp is the primary customer channel; directing audiences to email-first or website-first contact points underperforms.
- [ ] Unfamiliar or meaningless cultural references, idioms or examples are replaced with locally resonant equivalents.
- [ ] Mentions of internet access, streaming or data use account for mobile data costs and connectivity patterns in the region.
- [ ] Content translated or adapted into Luganda, Swahili, Amharic or another EA language is checked for cultural idiom accuracy, not only linguistic accuracy, by a native speaker before delivery.
- [ ] **Cultural bias audit** (Ching and Mothi, 2025): AI-generated content depicting people, cultures, identities or communities reflects the actual demographic and cultural reality of the audience, and has been reviewed by someone with direct cultural knowledge of the community. AI tools trained mainly on Western datasets systematically produce Western-centric, gender-stereotyped and racially inaccurate depictions. For East African clients this review is mandatory for all AI-generated imagery descriptions, character references and community representations.

## Pass 7: the "Proof of Human" standard

The final test before sign-off (after Schaefer, 2023). Passing content reads as written by a human with real expertise in the subject and the market. It need not be perfect; it needs to be real.

Signs of human authorship:

- Specific local details only someone familiar with the market would include.
- Clear opinions and recommendations, not false balance.
- Natural imperfections: a conversational aside, a sentence that bends a grammar rule for emphasis, direct address to the reader.
- Cultural specificity: references that land for this audience, not a generic global one.
- Voice consistency with the client's existing content history.

When in doubt: add a real detail, take a stronger position, cut a smooth transition. These three moves resolve most flatness problems.

## Sign-off protocol

**Junior content (captions, short-form copy, email)**

- Reviewed by a senior consultant before delivery, against the Human Voice Checklist and the cultural localisation checklist.
- Approval is per piece, not per batch.

**Strategy documents and proposals**

- Reviewed line by line by the lead consultant against the client brief.
- Every recommendation cross-referenced to the client's stated objectives.
- All market data footnoted with a verified source before the document leaves the agency.

**Production record**

- Note in the project file where AI tools were used in producing any deliverable.
- Record the AI tool, the prompt type (first draft, structural outline, research summary) and the extent of human revision.
- The record is for internal quality tracking, not client disclosure, unless the client asks.

**IP and copyright note** (Ching and Mothi, 2025, p. 82). Before delivering any substantially AI-generated piece, note in the production record that AI-generated content may not qualify for copyright protection without substantial human creative contribution. If the client intends to register or licence the work, flag it for legal review by a qualified IP solicitor.

**Transparency spectrum** (Ching and Mothi, 2025, p. 19). Where AI disclosure is given it must be specific; "Made with AI" is insufficient. Standard: "AI-generated [specific element], art-directed and revised by [human team]", or equivalent specificity, distinguishing the AI's contribution from the human's. Vague disclosure misleads audiences and undermines trust.

**Volume quality gate.** At scale (many captions, an email series, a batch of blog posts) review becomes the bottleneck, and an editor reviewing too many pieces per session misses issues. Limits per editing session:

| Content | Maximum per session |
|---|---|
| Captions and short-form copy | 15 pieces, then a break |
| Blog posts (800–1,500 words) | 3 |
| Strategy documents | 1 |

If the pipeline exceeds these, schedule more sessions rather than compressing review time. The Golden Rule applies to every piece, not a batch average.

## Content recycling pipeline (Roth and neuroflash, 2024)

One well-researched piece can become 10 platform-ready assets in under 60 minutes.

| Step | Output | AI tool |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Master content (blog/article) | 800–1,200-word source piece | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 2. Extract 5 key points | Bullet summary | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 3. Facebook caption | 100–150 words, conversational | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 4. Instagram caption | 50–80 words + hashtags | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 5. LinkedIn post | 150–200 words, professional | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 6. TikTok/Reels script | 30-second spoken script | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 7. X/Twitter post | Under 280 characters | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 8. Carousel outline | 5-slide structure with headlines | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 9. Email snippet | 80-word newsletter paragraph | Claude/ChatGPT |
| 10. Quote card text | Single compelling sentence | Claude/ChatGPT |

Platform adaptation checklist:

- [ ] Facebook: conversational, question or call to action, link preview works.
- [ ] Instagram: emotional hook first, hashtags at the end or in a comment, no clickable links in the caption.
- [ ] LinkedIn: insight or lesson first, professional register, invites discussion.
- [ ] TikTok/Reels: hook in the first 3 seconds, spoken register, suggest trending audio.
- [ ] WhatsApp Status: under 700 characters, image-first, direct call to action.

Quality gate: every variant passes these humanising passes before publishing. Volume without quality is worse than no content at all.

## The rising quality bar

Platform algorithms increasingly detect and suppress low-quality AI-generated content (Roth and neuroflash, 2024). The human layer (strategic direction, local cultural context, brand voice) becomes more valuable, not less, as AI content volume rises. Signs that AI content will be flagged or underperform:

- Generic phrasing with no specific local context.
- No real point of view or opinion.
- Repetitive sentence structures.
- Missing cultural references or local events.
- Tone inconsistency within a single post.

These passes exist to prevent those failure modes, making each AI output more engaging and more trustworthy.

## Acceptance checklist

A humanised piece is ready when:

1. **No banned vocabulary remains**; every item is replaced with direct, plain language that serves the reader.
2. **Every claim is verified**; no statistic, citation or factual assertion lacks a confirmed primary source, and invented data is removed or replaced.
3. **Cultural localisation is complete** for Uganda/East Africa: payment references, local examples, correct currency, and a tone matching Ugandan professional norms.
4. **A clear human voice is present**: at least one specific local detail, at least one clear opinion or recommendation, and natural sentence variation.
5. **The CTA is direct and actionable**: one specific action on the most relevant channel (WhatsApp, email, in-person visit or other).
6. **The opening hook is earned**, without a question opener, a cliché or a banned phrase.
7. **The sign-off protocol has been followed**, with the correct review level (junior or senior) recorded in the production record before delivery.
