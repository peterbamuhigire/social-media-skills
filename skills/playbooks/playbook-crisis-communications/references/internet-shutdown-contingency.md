# Internet shutdown and platform-block contingency (East Africa)

Added 29 Sep 2026 (Social Kaizen S10-T01, gap G05). This is operational planning guidance, not legal advice. It covers two events a client cannot control: a government-ordered suspension of mobile internet or social media, and a longer block on one platform.

## 1. What the register supports

| Event | What is known | Register ID and status |
|---|---|---|
| Uganda 2026 general election | The Uganda Communications Commission (UCC) ordered a nationwide suspension of mobile internet and data services on 13 Jan 2026, ahead of the 15 Jan vote. UCC directed restoration of public internet access on 18 Jan 2026, describing the suspension as a proportionate measure against misinformation and incitement. Social media stayed restricted until about 26 Jan 2026, when the Chief of Defence Forces announced full restoration. | `UG-INTERNET-SHUTDOWN-2026` (UCC, verified for the 18 Jan restoration); `UG-SOCIAL-RESTORATION-2026` (trade press, partial) |

| Other EAC markets | No shutdown record admitted for Kenya, Tanzania or Rwanda. | `NOT_ASSESSED`; check at the campaign date |

Do not predict whether or when a shutdown will happen. Plan for it wherever the calendar holds a national election, a referendum, national examinations or major unrest, and state the plan as a contingency.

## 2. Pre-flight checks (campaign and crisis plans)

1. Mark high-risk windows on the calendar: by default, seven days before election day to fourteen days after the results are declared, plus any announced national event. This window is a planning default, not evidence; in 2026 the suspension began two days before the vote and social media stayed restricted for about eleven days after it.
2. Keep one owned channel that does not depend on mobile data or social media: SMS to an opted-in list, radio, email for office-based audiences, and printed or outdoor notices for field operations.
3. Store holding statements, contact lists and approvals offline and with a named person outside the affected country.
4. For paid media, set a pause rule: if delivery or landing-page traffic falls sharply in one market, pause that market's spend and log the time, rather than letting budgets re-optimise into the wrong audience.
5. Record which platforms the plan relies on and the fallback for each (Facebook → WhatsApp, Instagram or SMS; WhatsApp → SMS or voice).

## 3. During a shutdown or block

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile internet is suspended | Switch customer and staff updates to SMS and radio; post a dated notice on channels that still work for diaspora and international audiences | Silence read as indifference; missed service messages |
| Only social media is restricted | Use the website, email and SMS; do not tell audiences to use a VPN, and follow the client's counsel on any advice about circumvention | Exposing the client or its audience to regulatory risk |
| Paid campaigns are live | Pause affected markets, keep a log with timestamps and ask platforms for make-goods where the terms allow | Paying for undelivered or distorted delivery |
| A crisis breaks during the outage | Issue the holding statement through the fallback channel and promise an update time; repeat it online when access returns | A backlog of unanswered complaints on restoration |
| Access returns | Publish one clear update; answer the queued messages oldest first; review metrics with the outage window excluded or flagged | Reports that treat the outage as a performance drop |

## 4. Reporting

Mark outage days in every performance report and exclude them from test comparisons and benchmarks. Record the source (UCC statement, platform status page or client observation), the start and end times and the affected regions. A figure from an outage window is `not assessed`, not a result.
