# Networking and Referral Playbook

A recommendation from someone the buyer already trusts is usually the cheapest and most persuasive route to new work in East African markets; this playbook manages the relationships that produce those recommendations, on top of reliable delivery.

<!-- dual-compat-start -->
## Use When
- Most clients arrive by word of mouth: should events and introductions lead the growth plan?
- Plan which business events, chamber meetings or conferences to attend and what to do before, during and after each one.
- Past clients and partners should refer more often, with a simple ask and a thank-you routine.
- Start or join a referral group and agree how members pass leads to each other.
- Build a follow-up system, a map of connectors and a personal board of advisers.

## Do Not Use When
- `biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach` for cold outreach sequences, contact-list sourcing and consent handling.
- `playbook-social-selling` for turning an online audience into a sales pipeline.
- `strategy-personal-brand` for an individual's positioning and authority content.
- Stop before adding event contacts to any mailing or WhatsApp list without their consent; keep the follow-up personal.

## Required Inputs

| Artefact | Source/provider | Required? | If absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client business name, industry/sector and country or city | Client brief | Yes | Default the market to Uganda / East Africa; ask for name and sector. |
| Primary goal: new client acquisition, referral activation, community presence, or all three | Client owner | Yes | Plan for all three and ask the client to rank them. |
| Current networking activity | Client | Yes | Treat as none and start from the event routine and referral ask. |
| Budget and time available per week for networking | Client owner | Yes | Stop the channel-mix choice; do not guess time or money. |
| Existing contacts, past clients and referral sources, with a lawful basis for follow-up | Client CRM or spreadsheet | For follow-up and gatekeeper lists | Build the list template only; mark named contacts `not assessed`. |

## Workflow

1. Ask the intake questions in [networking and referral method](references/networking-and-referral-method.md) and confirm the owner and consent boundary; stop if the goal or weekly capacity is missing.
2. Choose the channel mix from what the client has more of: time and relationships, budget, or both.
3. Name the specific organisations and events for the next 90 days and set the before, during, straight-after and 48-hour event routine.
4. Write the referral activation script, the gatekeeper target list (five professions, two or three people each) and the written-reference template.
5. Design the referral group and its tracking log where the client will run or join one.
6. Set the dated follow-up schedule, the network map with gap audit and A/B/C/D cadence, and the weekly connector habits.
7. Run the quality checks and the anti-slop gate; correct any failed item and rerun before handover.

## Channel mix by client resources

| Client has | Lead with | Add later |
|---|---|---|
| Time and relationships, little budget (new firms, independents) | Networking, speaking, referral cultivation, useful content | Paid channels once revenue allows |
| Budget, little senior time | Paid advertising, sponsored placements, direct mail | A light referral routine run by the account lead |
| Both | Referral partners and events as the base, paid media to scale | n/a |

## Outputs

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| 90-day networking action plan and short spoken pitch | Client owner | Names specific organisations and events; the pitch answers "What do you do?" in 15–30 seconds, benefit-led. |
| Referral activation script and gatekeeper target list | Client owner | Script describes the ideal client; list has 10–15 named referral sources with a cultivation plan. |
| Referral group design document | Group facilitator | Member profile, meeting structure and referral tracking log are included. |
| Follow-up schedule with message templates | Client owner | Every contact type has an action and a specific timing. |
| Network map and cadence | Client owner | Groups, gap audit, A/B/C/D plan and a quarterly gathering plan are set. |

## Evidence Produced

| Evidence | Format | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Referral tracking log | Table: source, recipient, outcome, value | Reviewed quarterly; a referral without an outcome is marked open. |
| Follow-up register | Spreadsheet or CRM with next action and due date | Each contact has a lawful basis for follow-up recorded or is removed. |

## Capability and Permission Boundaries

Read and search only; analysis is read-only. Publishing, spend, live account changes, outreach and personal-data processing need explicit, action-specific client authority. Scripts and lists are drafts; the client makes every personal approach.

## Degraded Mode

Without the client's goal and weekly time or budget, return the narrowest qualified result and mark the affected checks `not assessed`. The event routine, referral script and follow-up timings can still be delivered, with the channel mix and target list left open.

## Decision Rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| A contact has no mutual relevance or permission for follow-up | Do not add them to a sequence. | Transactional spam disguised as networking. |
| A client has just had a successful delivery | Ask for a referral now, describing the ideal client; not at renewal. | Vague asks that produce nobody. |
| A contact was met at an event | Send a personal message within 24 hours; a referral gets thanks and an introduction within 48 hours; a proposal gets one call 5–7 working days later. | Contacts lost for want of a second message. |
| The contact base passes 100 contacts | Move from a spreadsheet to a CRM or database with next action and due date. | Missed follow-ups. |
| Networking touches public procurement | Guard against conflict of interest; never approach evaluators during a live tender. | Procurement breach and reputational damage. |
| The person knows the client socially but not professionally | Acknowledge the relationship, say whom you help in one line, and leave the choice with them. | Seeming to have joined the group to sell. |

## Quality Standards

- The action plan names specific organisations and events, not generic "networking events".
- The spoken pitch is specific, benefit-led and memorable, not a job title.
- Referral scripts are direct and comfortable to say aloud, never pressuring.
- The gatekeeper list names professions that genuinely meet the client's buyer first.
- The referral group design includes a tracking mechanism, not just a list of attendees.
- All follow-up timings are specific (24 hours, 48 hours), not "soon".
- Content reflects the relationship-first business culture of Uganda and East Africa.
- The network map names gaps by role and sets a cadence; introductions give value before any ask.

## Anti-Patterns

- Selling at the event. Fix: ask more than you tell and keep the sale for the follow-up.
- Asking for referrals from "anyone". Fix: describe the ideal client in one sentence.
- Following up with "let's have coffee sometime". Fix: set a specific next step and date.
- Cold-pitching gatekeepers. Fix: meet them through an introduction or shared event and give value first.
- Attending passively and irregularly. Fix: join a few organisations, take a committee or volunteer role and turn up consistently.
- Calling A-list contacts only when you need something. Fix: call when you have an opportunity for them and make one introduction a week.
- Adding event contacts to a mailing or WhatsApp list. Fix: keep follow-up personal and check the lawful basis first.
