# Social Selling Playbook

Most East African service businesses either post only promotional content (skipping trust-building) or only awareness content (no conversion mechanism); this playbook bridges that gap, turning an engaged audience into a client pipeline without burning goodwill.

<!-- dual-compat-start -->
## Use When
- Regular posting brings few enquiries; build an educate, proof, soft ask, offer and hard ask sequence.
- Scripts for answering enquiries and DMs and moving buyers to WhatsApp to close.
- Write a LinkedIn connection and message sequence and a daily routine for the founder.
- Get staff sharing or reposting company updates on their own LinkedIn, Facebook or WhatsApp through an employee advocacy programme with monthly content packs, incentives and posting guidelines.
- Sell a premium or high-ticket offer to senior, affluent or enterprise buyers through LinkedIn authority, lead magnets, nurture and executive outreach.

## Do Not Use When
- `biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach` for sourcing contact lists, consent and cold outreach sequences.
- `platform-linkedin` for the LinkedIn channel plan.
- `social-commerce-strategy` for catalogue and Mobile Money selling of products in chat.
- Stop before sending messages from a client's or employee's personal account without their written consent; deliver the scripts for them to send.

## Required Inputs

| Artefact | Source/provider | Required? | If absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business and trading name, industry, primary service, country/city and average service fee (UGX or USD) | Client | Yes | Default the market to Uganda/East Africa; stop if the service or fee is unknown, because offer and price presentation depend on them. |
| Primary goal (for example 5 new client enquiries per month) | Client lead | Yes | Stop and request it; do not set a target on the client's behalf. |
| Platforms in use, current follower counts and posting frequency | Client; platform insights | Yes | Use approximate counts labelled as such and pick the benchmark row conservatively. |
| WhatsApp Business number for client communication | Client | Yes | Leave wa.me links as marked gaps; do not invent a number. |
| Existing testimonials or case studies, with permission to use them | Client | Yes | Plan the testimonial request first; hold proof posts until permission is recorded. |
| Staff willing to share content, or a premium buyer segment | Client leadership | Conditional | Omit the advocacy or high-value layers and say so. |

## Workflow

1. Collect the intake details and define the right-fit buyer; stop if the goal, service or fee is missing.
2. Pick at most two selling platforms from the platform-selection logic; WhatsApp is always the closing channel.
3. Draft the 5-step sequence (Educate, Social Proof, Soft CTA, Offer Reveal, Hard CTA) as a cycle every 2–4 weeks, keeping about 80 % of posts value-led and 20 % inviting action. See [the sequence and scripts](references/social-selling-sequence-and-scripts.md).
4. Write the DM first-response script, qualifying questions, objection responses and the 3-message WhatsApp conversion path with the client's service name and a draft price sentence.
5. Write the testimonial request and set honest benchmarks from the client's follower count.
6. For B2B or professional services, add the four-message LinkedIn sequence, trigger events and the daily FEED routine from [LinkedIn outreach and daily routine](references/linkedin-outreach-and-daily-routine.md).
7. Add the employee advocacy programme or the high-value selling layer when the conditions below apply; run the premium gate before publishing a premium strategy.
8. Check against the quality standards, the "What NOT to Do" checklist and the anti-slop gate; correct failures and rerun the check before hand-off.

## Market, message and offer discipline

Use Kennedy's market-message-offer discipline and Wiebe's voice-of-customer discipline throughout:

- define the right-fit buyer before scripting content
- state the problem in the buyer's own language
- choose the offer type before choosing the CTA
- treat DMs and WhatsApp replies as objection-rich research, not just sales admin

## Outputs

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Customised 5-step sequence with draft post angles per step | Client content owner | Matched to the client's service, audience and chosen platforms; no offer or CTA in Steps 1–2. |
| DM, objection and 3-message WhatsApp conversion scripts | Person who answers enquiries | Uses the client's service name and a draft price sentence; price appears only after qualifying. |
| LinkedIn outreach sequence and daily FEED routine | Founder or sales lead | Four messages with timings; connection request within 300 characters and without a pitch. |
| Benchmarks and "What NOT to Do" checklist | Client lead and team | Benchmarks tied to the current follower count; checklist printable for weekly review in the first month. |
| Employee advocacy or high-value selling plan (when triggered) | Client leadership | Follows the linked reference and passes its gate. |

## Evidence Produced

| Evidence | Format | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonial permission record | Log of client, date and permitted use (photo, name, screenshot) | Every proof post traces to a recorded permission. |
| Voice-of-customer banks | Three lists: problem, objection and desire phrases from DMs | Phrases are quoted from real conversations and fed back into posts and scripts. |
| Benchmark basis | Note of follower count, cadence and response time assumed | Expectations state the conditions under which the benchmark applies. |

## Capability and Permission Boundaries

Read and search only; analysis is read-only. Publishing, spend, live account changes, outreach and personal-data processing need explicit, action-specific client authority. Messages from a client's or employee's personal account are delivered as scripts for them to send; staff participation in advocacy is voluntary.

## Degraded Mode

Without the client's service, fee and follower counts, return the narrowest qualified result and mark the affected checks `not assessed`. A generic 5-step sequence skeleton with placeholder angles and the "What NOT to Do" checklist can still be delivered.

## Decision Rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| A prospect shows no relevant need or engagement | Stop the sequence and return to value-led public interaction | Persistent unsolicited selling |
| Staff, not only the brand or a sales lead, will share content | Run a voluntary advocacy programme per [employee advocacy](references/employee-advocacy-programme.md) | Coerced or undisclosed staff posting |
| Buyer is senior, affluent, enterprise or high-ticket | Switch to proof-led, selective outreach per [high-value selling](references/high-value-social-selling.md) and its gate | Volume tactics damaging premium positioning |
| The audience has not yet seen authority content | Run Steps 1–4 before any Step 5 hard CTA | An offer ignored by a cold audience |
| Scarcity, wait-list or "fully booked" framing is proposed | Use it only when genuinely true and explain why slots are limited | Artificial urgency that EA audiences distrust |
| A price is requested before qualifying | Send the price after the first qualifying WhatsApp conversation | Price comparison instead of value evaluation |
| Enquiry-to-client conversion is below 15 % or above 35 % | Review pricing and qualifying (below) or check for under-pricing (above) | Misreading a pricing problem as a content problem |
| No reply after two follow-ups | Stop following up | Disrespecting boundaries and losing goodwill |

## Quality Standards

- Addresses the specific business type (coaching, consulting, professional services or agency) with tailored examples, not generic social media advice.
- Includes a fully customised 5-step sequence with draft post angles for each step, matched to the client's service and target audience.
- Provides a ready-to-use 3-message WhatsApp conversion script with the client's service name and a draft price presentation sentence.
- Supplies at least three platform-specific soft CTA options for the platforms the client actually uses.
- Includes a social proof request template personalised to how the client delivers the service (in-person, online, hybrid).
- Sets honest benchmarks from the client's current follower count before the playbook is deployed.
- Produces the "What NOT to Do" checklist as a printable or shareable reference for weekly review during the first month.
- British English throughout, with no generic motivational language and no unsubstantiated superlatives.

## Anti-Patterns

- Posting prices before trust is established. Fix: reveal the offer without price in Step 4 and give the price in WhatsApp after qualifying.
- Answering every comment with a pitch. Fix: answer the comment genuinely first; interested people will ask.
- Using social media like a product catalogue. Fix: keep catalogues in WhatsApp Business or on a website and use social media for relationships.
- Ignoring comments and DMs. Fix: respond within 4 hours in business hours; if that is too much, cut posting frequency and reinvest the time in follow-up.
- Buying followers. Fix: build a real engaged audience; 800 real followers outperform 10,000 bought ones.
- Giving up after one post. Fix: commit to 4–8 weeks of consistent execution before judging results.
- Copying competitors' content. Fix: use original voice, real experience and genuine opinions; the full checklist is in [the sequence and scripts](references/social-selling-sequence-and-scripts.md).
