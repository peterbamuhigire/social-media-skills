# Employee Advocacy Programme

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-employee-advocacy on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [playbook-employee-advocacy.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-employee-advocacy.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client wants staff, not only the brand page or a sales lead, to share company content through their own networks: an employee advocacy programme with voluntary participation, monthly content packs, LinkedIn profile preparation, incentives, a pilot launch and one-page posting guidelines. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns the selling sequence, DM handling and the daily routine; this reference owns staff-led distribution that feeds that sequence. Drafting packs and guidelines does not authorise posting for anyone or messaging staff on the client's behalf.

Evidence status. Reach multipliers, organic-reach factors, algorithm timing and incentive amounts below are legacy figures with no register record: verify before stating, or present them as planning assumptions. LinkedIn conduct follows the Professional Community Policies ("Be trustworthy; Be professional", register PREMIUM-LI-POLICY-2026). Facebook access in Uganda is unstable (register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026): verify at the campaign date before planning Facebook personal-profile advocacy for Ugandan staff; do not assert it as blocked or open.

## Why employee advocacy works

- **Reach.** Employees' combined networks typically reach about 10 times more people than the brand's owned channels (legacy claim; verify before stating). A shared post reaches people who would never see the official content, and it arrives with the trust of a personal connection rather than the commercial intent of a brand advertisement. Worked planning example: 20 employees with 200–500 Facebook connections each give a potential reach above 10,000 people from one coordinated post, at no added ad cost.
- **Media category.** Advocacy sits in Earned Media in the POEM model (Paid/Owned/Earned), which makes it one of the highest-return activities in a social programme.
- **Credibility transfer in East Africa.** Professional reputation in Uganda and East Africa is deeply personal. A professional who is well regarded on LinkedIn or active in WhatsApp professional networks carries institutional credibility a brand page cannot replicate. When a senior accountant shares the firm's thought-leadership content, fellow professionals take it more seriously than any brand post. For B2B and professional-services clients this supports the Reach and Act stages of RACE (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022).

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing voluntary or required participation | Recommend voluntary participation with clear incentives; justify it by content quality and employee motivation | Compelled, unenthusiastic posts that audiences recognise and that damage the brand |
| Client insists on a requirement | Frame it as a professional-development expectation, not a compliance rule | Advocacy read as coercion |
| Participation is pressured or disclosure is unclear | Make participation voluntary and supply disclosure wording | Coerced or deceptive advocacy |
| Choosing the platform | Start where employees already have an audience; never ask them to join a platform they do not use | Empty profiles and zero reach |
| Planning content supply | Provide ready-to-share content; never ask employees to create from scratch | The most common reason advocacy programmes fail |
| LinkedIn is the main advocacy platform | Complete staff profiles before asking anyone to share | A blank profile undermining the credibility the programme is meant to build |
| Leadership is not visibly behind it | Brief leadership first and have the managing or country director front the launch | Low participation; programme seen as an HR or marketing task |
| Rolling out to all staff | Pilot with 3–5 enthusiastic, social-media-active employees for one month first | Full launch without proof of concept |

### Platform fit

| Platform | Best fit |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | B2B, professional services, NGOs, formal-sector clients |
| Facebook personal profiles | Consumer brands, SMEs, retail, community-facing businesses (Uganda: see the evidence status above) |
| WhatsApp | Relationship-led sales, professional referral networks, warm outreach |
| X/Twitter | Employees who are opinion leaders, public-sector professionals, journalists |

## Procedure

### 1. Make the five programme decisions

1. **Voluntary vs required** — per the decision rules above.
2. **Platform focus** — per the platform-fit table.
3. **Content provision** — ready-to-share content only:
   - pre-written LinkedIn/Facebook post drafts in 2–3 versions per piece, so employees choose the tone that fits their voice;
   - pre-approved images or graphics sized for each platform (image production is out of scope: brief the design team separately);
   - a simple sharing instruction with each post: "Copy this, personalise the opening line if you wish, then post on LinkedIn";
   - for WhatsApp, a short forwarding message for professional groups, 2–3 sentences at most.
4. **Incentive structure** — include at least one non-monetary and one tangible option:
   - **Recognition:** "Employee Advocate of the Month", a public shout-out on the brand's page and at the monthly team meeting.
   - **Priority access:** advocates get early access to company news, launches or internal training before the general announcement.
   - **Tangible reward:** UGX 20,000–50,000 airtime or data top-up for the top advocate each month, a meaningful low-cost incentive in Uganda (legacy amount; confirm with the client's budget).
   - **Career benefit:** frame advocacy as professional development; LinkedIn reach builds the employee's personal brand, not only the company's. Make this argument explicitly at launch.
5. **Measurement** — track monthly:
   - number of employees who shared at least one piece of content;
   - estimated reach of shares: sum of participating employees' connection/follower counts × a 10% average organic-reach factor (legacy planning factor; verify before stating);
   - engagement on shared posts (likes, comments, shares/reposts);
   - referral enquiries or leads attributed to employee shares, where trackable.

   Set SMART objectives at launch (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012), for example: "By Month 3, at least 10 of 30 employees share one piece of content per month, generating an estimated combined reach of 15,000 per post."

### 2. Build the monthly Advocacy Content Pack

Prepare one pack a month for all participants. Deliver it through the programme's shared WhatsApp group, a monthly email or a shared Google Drive folder, whichever the team will actually use. Align themes with the content calendar from [11-content-calendar](../../../pipeline/11-content-calendar/SKILL.md) and write drafts in the approved voice from [04-brand-voice-intake](../../../pipeline/04-brand-voice-intake/SKILL.md).

```
[COMPANY NAME] Employee Advocacy Pack — [Month YYYY]

This month's themes: [Theme 1], [Theme 2]

─────────────────────────────────────────
POST 1 — LinkedIn / Facebook

Option A (formal tone):
[Full post text, ready to copy. 100–150 words. Include a hook, body, and call to action.]

Option B (casual tone):
[Same message, warmer register. 80–100 words. More personal, conversational opener.]

Image: [Attach image file or share Canva link — brief design team separately]
Hashtags: #[BrandHashtag] #[Industry] #Uganda
Note: Add your personal perspective at the start if you wish — posts with a personal
opener consistently outperform straight brand shares.

─────────────────────────────────────────
POST 2 — LinkedIn / Facebook

Option A (formal tone):
[Full post text — 100–150 words]

Option B (casual tone):
[80–100 words]

Image: [Attach or link]
Hashtags: #[BrandHashtag] #[Industry] #Uganda

─────────────────────────────────────────
QUICK SHARE — WhatsApp (for professional networks and groups)

[Short forwarding message, 2–3 sentences. Suitable for dropping into a professional
WhatsApp group. No hashtags. Conversational. Ends with the company's contact or link.]

─────────────────────────────────────────
This month's advocate leaderboard will be shared on [Date].
Questions? Message [Advocacy Coordinator Name] on WhatsApp.
```

Generate a new pack each month.

### 3. Prepare LinkedIn profiles (B2B clients)

Encourage every participant to complete their profile before advocating. An employee sharing thought leadership from a profile with no photo and an empty summary does more harm than good. Profile essentials for East African professionals:

- **Photo:** a clear professional headshot, not a WhatsApp group photo cropped from a party.
- **Headline:** a value statement, not only the job title, for example "Helping SMEs grow through clean energy | Engineer, [Company Name]".
- **Summary:** 3–5 sentences on expertise, professional passion and what the employee brings to the sector.
- **Current role:** with the company listed and the Company Page linked, so the logo shows and the connection to the brand is visible.
- **Skills:** at least three endorsed and, where possible, one recommendation.

Explain two platform points to the client and participants, labelled as legacy claims to verify before stating:

- **Personal vs page reach:** personal posts receive 3–5 times more organic reach than equivalent Company Page posts. This is the structural reason advocacy outperforms boosted brand content on LinkedIn; it is how the platform is designed, not a workaround.
- **Engagement timing:** the author (or a colleague) comments on the shared post within the first 60 minutes, because early engagement increases distribution to the employee's connections.

For a full LinkedIn strategy use [platform-linkedin](../../../platforms/platform-linkedin/SKILL.md); to develop individual employees as thought leaders rather than sharers, use [strategy-personal-brand](../../../strategy/strategy-personal-brand/SKILL.md).

### 4. Launch the programme

1. **Brief the leadership team first.** Advocacy must be seen as a leadership initiative, not an HR or marketing task; if the managing or country director is not visibly behind it, participation will be low.
2. **Run a 30-minute internal session.** Cover what advocacy is, why it benefits employees personally (their professional profile, not just the company's), what participation involves, how content will be supplied and how advocates will be recognised. Keep it practical and low-pressure.
3. **Start with a pilot group** of 3–5 enthusiastic, social-media-active employees for the first month; use their results as proof of concept for the wider team.
4. **Celebrate early wins publicly.** Share screenshots of high-engagement employee posts in the internal WhatsApp group or at team meetings, naming the employee and acknowledging the reach. This creates social proof inside the organisation.
5. **Review quarterly.** Is participation growing month on month? Which content types generate the most sharing and engagement? Are any departments or roles under-represented? Adjust the pack format, incentives or delivery method accordingly.

### 5. Issue one-page posting guidelines

Advocacy without clear guidelines creates reputational and legal risk. Give every participant a one-page guideline at launch covering:

- Always associate yourself clearly with [Company Name] when posting about the company; never share company content anonymously or without attribution.
- Never share confidential client information, financial data, personnel matters, or unreleased product or service details.
- Do not comment on legal disputes, regulatory investigations or allegations involving competitors; refer any such enquiry immediately.
- If a journalist, media outlet or public figure contacts you on social media about the company, refer them to [name and contact details]; do not respond on the company's behalf.
- Personal opinions are your own; make this clear when posting personal views alongside brand content, using phrases such as "My own view:" or "Speaking personally:".
- If in doubt whether something is appropriate, ask [Advocacy Coordinator] before posting.

For a full employee social media policy, use [playbook-social-media-policy](../../playbook-social-media-policy/SKILL.md).

## Release checklist

The advocacy deliverable is ready when:

1. The voluntary vs required decision is explicit and justified by content quality and employee motivation.
2. Platform selection matches where the client's employees are demonstrably active, not consultant preference.
3. The Content Pack template is complete, correctly formatted and ready to hand to the client.
4. LinkedIn profile guidance includes the East African professional context and the personal-vs-page reach explanation, labelled for verification.
5. The incentive structure includes at least one non-monetary option (recognition, early access, career framing) alongside any tangible reward.
6. The launch plan specifies a pilot group before full rollout, with a clear sequence of steps.
7. Posting guidelines are included, cross-refer to `playbook-social-media-policy`, and cover confidentiality, media enquiries and personal-opinion disclosure.
8. Volatile figures (reach multipliers, reach factor, algorithm timing, Facebook access in Uganda) are verified or marked "verify before stating".
