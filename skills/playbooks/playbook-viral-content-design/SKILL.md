# Playbook: Viral Content Design

Content that spreads is structured, not accidental: this playbook applies social science and platform mechanics so consultants can design content that travels organically, ethically and in the Uganda/East Africa context. It covers earned reach, not manufactured virality or paid amplification; paid distribution sits outside it (see the POEM model, Paid/Owned/Earned, for how organic content fits a broader channel mix; Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022).

<!-- dual-compat-start -->
## Use When
- Posts get views but no shares; explain what makes content spread and redesign around it.
- Build posts on proven shareable structures: the unexpected reversal, number list, shared struggle, behind-the-curtain, hot take and practical toolkit.
- Adapt for TikTok, Facebook, Instagram Reels and WhatsApp forwarding, with East African seasonal and cultural triggers.
- Make safe, forgettable content bolder: score it on an audacity index, design contrarian takes and a talk trigger, and screen the risk.
- Check a bold idea against ethical limits before it goes out.

## Do Not Use When
- `content-ideas` for a prioritised list of post and article topics.
- `11-content-calendar` for scheduling the posts.
- `ad-copy-and-hook-lab` for paid ad hooks and headlines.
- Stop before publishing content that mocks people, spreads unverified claims or stages fake controversy; return it with the risk-screen notes.

## Required Inputs

| Artefact | Source/provider | Required? | If absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client business name, industry and country/city | Client | Yes | Default the market to Uganda/East Africa; stop if the business is unknown. |
| One primary platform (TikTok, Facebook, Instagram Reels or WhatsApp) and content type | Client or channel plan | Yes | Ask the client to select one; do not design for all four at once. |
| Campaign objective (awareness, engagement, shares, leads or community growth) | Client lead | Yes | Stop and request it; the share trigger depends on it. |
| Target audience: age, location, aspiration, daily frustration | Client; audience research | Yes | Return discovery questions; do not write to a generic audience. |
| Previous high-performing content and the last 5 posts | Client; platform insights | Conditional | Mark the baseline `not assessed` and skip the Audacity Index score. |
| Real figures and client approval for price reveals or behind-the-curtain content | Client | For transparency content | Choose a different structure; never invent figures. |

## Workflow

1. Collect the intake details and fix one primary platform; stop if the objective or audience is missing.
2. Name the sharer's benefit and the specific emotion (awe, amusement, inspiration, righteous anger or anxiety) or the practical utility before writing anything.
3. Choose exactly one of the six structures (unexpected reversal, specific number list, shared struggle, behind-the-curtain, hot take, practical toolkit). See [the method](references/viral-structures-and-platform-method.md).
4. Complete the content brief in full, with the hook written out and the share-trigger sentence answered, before producing content.
5. Apply the platform tactics for the chosen platform and an East African trigger where it fits.
6. When the brief asks for bold or contrarian content, score the last 5 posts and screen the idea with the [bold idea risk screen](references/bold-idea-risk-screen.md).
7. Apply the ethical boundaries; stop and return any idea that needs invented figures, engineered outrage or disproportionate fear.
8. Run the quality standards and the anti-slop gate; correct any failed item and rerun the check before hand-off.

## Outputs

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Shareable-content brief | Content writer (`caption-writer`) | Every brief field completed, none "TBD"; one structure named; hook written in full. |
| Structure drafts with formula, template and EA example | Client content lead | Uganda/EA examples, not generic global ones; share trigger answerable for each. |
| Platform virality guidance | Client content lead | At least three actionable tactics per platform in scope. |
| Bold-idea risk screen (when requested) | Client approver | Audacity score, STEPPS and Talk Trigger recorded with the risk decision. |

## Evidence Produced

| Evidence | Format | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Claim and figure verification | Table of each number or exposé claim with its source | Every figure is real and every exposé claim verified before publishing. |
| Transparency approval | Client sign-off note | Behind-the-curtain and price-reveal content carries informed client consent. |
| Performance check plan | Metrics: reach, share rate, save rate, comment velocity | Set with `meta-testing-framework` before the post goes out. |

## Capability and Permission Boundaries

Read and search only; analysis is read-only. Publishing, spend, live account changes, outreach and personal-data processing need explicit, action-specific client authority. Posting into Facebook Groups or WhatsApp groups as a member is also publishing and needs that authority.

## Degraded Mode

Without a named audience and a primary platform, return the narrowest qualified result and mark the affected checks `not assessed`. The six structures with templates and a blank content brief can still be delivered.

## Decision Rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Shareability depends on deception, outrage or unverified novelty | Reject the mechanism | Reach bought with trust loss |
| Brief asks for bold, audacious or contrarian content, a Talk Trigger or a Proof of Human signal | Score the last 5 posts and screen the idea with [bold-idea-risk-screen](references/bold-idea-risk-screen.md) before choosing a structure | Manufactured controversy or forgettable "pandemic of dull" content |
| A post seems to need two structures | Keep one structure per piece and split the rest into another post | Diluted tension and no clear share trigger |
| Hot take proposed | Use it only if it reflects a genuine, defensible position the client holds | Controversy without substance eroding trust |
| Exposé of unfair practices or a price reveal | Verify all claims and use real figures, with client approval for internal data | Brand damage when inaccuracy is discovered |
| Content intended for WhatsApp forwarding | Use clean, unbranded, screenshotable images and no tracking links | Privacy-conscious groups refusing to forward |
| TikTok video | Put the most surprising or valuable element in the first 1.5 seconds, not a logo | The video scrolled past |
| Fear-based angle | Keep anxiety proportionate to a real risk or drop it | Manipulative content harming long-term credibility |

## Quality Standards

- All six viral structures are named, each with a reusable formula, a fill-in template and a concrete Uganda/EA example, not a generic global one.
- Platform guidance covers all four primary EA platforms (TikTok, Facebook, Instagram Reels, WhatsApp), each with at least three actionable tactics.
- The content brief is completed in full before any content is produced; no field blank or marked "TBD".
- The sentence "Someone shares this because it makes them look/feel ___" is answerable for every viral structure.
- EA triggers are applied to the client's specific industry and audience, not listed generically.
- The hook rule is met: 1.5 seconds for TikTok video; the first line carries the arresting idea in text content.
- Ethical boundaries are applied: all claims verifiable, transparency content approved, no content exploiting disproportionate anxiety.

## Anti-Patterns

- Fabricating numbers, testimonials or outcomes for a price reveal or behind-the-curtain post. Fix: use real figures; invented specifics do more damage than vague claims when discovered.
- Engineering outrage for its own sake. Fix: base the hot take on a position the client holds and can defend.
- Forcing trending audio onto content where it jars. Fix: use trending audio only where the tone fits.
- Voice-only Reels. Fix: subtitle all spoken content, because many EA viewers watch without sound in public spaces or shared households.
- Posting high-utility content only from the brand Page. Fix: have a real person post it in relevant Facebook Groups as a member.
- Using Luganda words, Kampala landmarks or local humour as decoration. Fix: use them deliberately, tied to the message.
- Branding WhatsApp content like an advertisement. Fix: design it to look like useful information that forwards readily.
