# White-Label and Partner Delivery

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-white-label-partnerships on 2026-09-29 at 69cca10 (S06 tree); preservation map: [playbook-white-label-partnerships.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-white-label-partnerships.md)

## When to use this reference

The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) runs the agency's own operation: onboarding, retainers, project management, invoicing, quality control, reporting and team. Use this reference when delivery runs through another firm:

- A consultant or small agency is approached by an agency to supply work under that agency's brand (white-label).
- Work that cannot be delivered in-house is sub-contracted, or a recurring arrangement with another firm needs formalising.
- An existing partner arrangement has payment, brief-quality, confidentiality or exit problems.

The procedure is written from the supplier's side (the consultant delivering for the agency). When the agency is the one sub-contracting out, apply the same checks in mirror: the agency sets the brief standard, the payment terms and the confidentiality rules that it would insist on as a supplier.

Evidence status. The rates, payment-term limits, penalty and dispute threshold below are planning figures carried from the retired playbook. None has a source-register record: verify before stating (no register record) and route contract wording to legal counsel. End-client files that hold personal data fall under Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 (register UG-DPPA-2019). This is operational guidance, not legal advice.

## Definitions

- **White-label:** the agency sells the consultant's work under the agency's own brand. The end client never knows the consultant exists.
- **Sub-contracting:** the agency or client engages the consultant directly, with the agency acting as account manager. The consultant may or may not be introduced to the end client.
- **Referral partnership:** a looser arrangement where both parties refer clients to each other. Out of scope here; use [`biz-dev-positioning`](../../../business-development/biz-dev-positioning/SKILL.md) for referral partnerships and personal positioning.

This reference covers white-label and sub-contracting only.

## Inputs

Ask for these before producing any deliverable:

1. Consultant name and primary service offering: which services are being white-labelled?
2. Agency name and type: digital agency, PR agency, creative agency or marketing consultancy.
3. Services to be provided white-label: every deliverable in scope.
4. Proposed monthly volume: estimated number of deliverables or clients per month.
5. Existing agreement or starting from scratch: does an NDA or contract already exist?

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery ownership, attribution or client access is ambiguous | Resolve it in the partner agreement before work starts | Hidden accountability and client conflict |
| Any of the six evaluation questions (step 1) gets a weak answer | Negotiate harder or walk away | An unprofitable or risky partnership |
| A red flag in step 1 is present | Do not proceed | Unpaid work, disputes, reputational damage |
| Agency offers payment terms beyond Net 45 | Reject; Net 60 is a cash-flow risk for a solo consultant | Cash-flow failure |
| Agency cannot supply a brief to the eight-field standard (step 4) | Charge a briefing fee for the discovery work before production | Poor work and disputes from lost information |
| A deadline is at risk | Notify the agency at least 48 hours in advance, not on the day | Ending the partnership through one unannounced miss |
| Any step-6 exit condition applies | Run the exit process | Sustaining a loss-making or conflicting relationship |

## Procedure

### 1. Evaluate the partnership

Answer all six questions before agreeing to work with any agency. A weak answer to any one is a reason to negotiate harder or walk away.

1. **Reputation:** is the agency well regarded in the East African market? Ask peers, check its LinkedIn profile and search for online reviews or testimonials.
2. **Client quality:** what type of clients does it serve? Are they aligned with your niche and capabilities?
3. **Payment terms:** when does it pay? Net 30 is acceptable; Net 60 is a cash-flow risk for a solo consultant and should be rejected.
4. **Volume commitment:** how much work will it send each month? A vague "as needed" arrangement is usually unprofitable; insist on a minimum monthly commitment.
5. **Brief quality:** does it provide detailed client briefs, or will you work from nothing? Poor briefs produce poor work and fuel disputes.
6. **IP and confidentiality:** does it have a standard NDA? Is there a clear policy on who owns the content after payment?

Red flags (do not proceed):

- Requests for free sample work with no commitment to pay.
- Payment terms beyond Net 45.
- No written agreement ("let's just start and formalise later").
- Agencies that criticise their current suppliers (they will criticise you too).
- Agencies whose own social media is poor quality: they cannot write good briefs if they do not understand the craft.

### 2. Price white-label services

White-label pricing must cover the agency's margin and the administrative overhead of a layered relationship.

- **Pricing principle:** set the white-label rate 30–50% below your direct client rate. The agency typically marks up 50–100% when billing the end client.
- **Volume discount:** offer 10% for commitments of three or more clients a month, or a signed 12-month minimum term.

East African white-label rate reference (planning figures from the retired playbook; verify before stating, no register record; adjust to the client's industry and scope; UGX unless the agency invoices in USD):

| Service | Direct client rate | White-label rate (agency) |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Instagram posts/month | UGX 1,500,000 | UGX 900,000–1,050,000 |
| Monthly social media strategy | UGX 800,000 | UGX 500,000–560,000 |
| Content calendar (monthly) | UGX 400,000 | UGX 240,000–280,000 |
| Community management | UGX 600,000/month | UGX 360,000–420,000/month |
| Training session (half-day) | UGX 1,200,000 | UGX 720,000–840,000 |

Payment-terms progression:

- New agency relationship: 50% upfront before work begins.
- After three months of on-time payment: move to Net 15 or Net 30.
- Hard rule: never extend Net 45 or longer to any agency partner.
- Accepted methods: MTN Mobile Money (MoMo), Airtel Money or direct bank transfer. Confirm the method in the written agreement.

### 3. Structure the agreement

Always have a written agreement: a WhatsApp conversation is not a contract. Include all nine elements in every white-label or sub-contracting agreement:

1. **Parties:** full legal names and/or registered business names of both parties.
2. **Services:** exact deliverables, quantities, formats and turnaround times.
3. **Pricing:** fee per deliverable or monthly retainer amount, with the currency (UGX).
4. **Payment terms:** due date, accepted payment method and a late-payment penalty (the retired playbook's example was 1.5% per week on overdue amounts; the parent's own client contracts use 1.5% per month. Agree the rate with counsel; verify before stating, no register record).
5. **Confidentiality (NDA):** the consultant will not contact the end client directly or disclose that the work is outsourced (see the NDA clause under Templates).
6. **Intellectual property:** all content produced becomes the agency's property on receipt of full payment; the consultant keeps no right to publish or reuse it.
7. **Revisions:** rounds included in the fee and the rate for extra rounds.
8. **Termination:** 30 days' written notice by either party, and the procedure for in-progress work on termination.
9. **Dispute resolution:** first, direct discussion (WhatsApp or in person); second, mediation; no litigation for amounts under UGX 5,000,000 (a commercial choice for the parties, not a legal threshold; confirm with counsel).

If the agency does not supply an NDA, propose your own and keep it simple (clause under Templates). Both parties sign and date. A WhatsApp message confirming agreement to the text is the acceptable minimum; a signed PDF is preferable.

### 4. Manage briefs, quality and deadlines

White-label work carries an extra quality risk: you are briefed by the agency, not the end client, so information is lost in translation. Compensate with a strict briefing standard. Refuse to start without all eight fields:

1. Client business name and industry.
2. Brand voice description: at least three tone-of-voice words.
3. Vocabulary avoid list: words, phrases or topics that are off-brand or legally sensitive.
4. Content pillars: the approved themes the content must address.
5. Reference examples of previously approved content.
6. Platforms in scope and any platform-specific requirements.
7. Target audience description: demographics, psychographics, pain points.
8. Campaign goals and any current promotional offers or key dates.

If the agency cannot brief to this standard, charge a briefing fee for the discovery work before content production begins.

Quality control:

- Apply the same process as for direct clients (parent § Section 5): run the [`anti-ai-slop`](../../../ai-marketing/anti-ai-slop/SKILL.md) humanising rewrite passes on any AI-assisted output, check brand voice and verify every factual claim.
- Deliver 48 hours before the agency's deadline with the end client, leaving time for the agency's review round.
- Keep a version log of all deliverables. Never overwrite a previous version; save each revision with a date stamp.
- Confirm every delivery on WhatsApp with a short delivery note (template below).

Deadline discipline:

- Quote realistic turnaround times: under-promise and over-deliver.
- If a deadline is at risk, tell the agency at least 48 hours in advance, not on the day.
- Never miss a deadline without advance notice. White-label relationships rest on reliability; one unannounced miss can end the partnership.

### 5. Protect end-client confidentiality

In white-label work the end client belongs to the agency. Breaching that boundary is a serious professional breach that can destroy the partnership and your reputation.

Do not:

- Contact the end client directly without the agency's explicit written permission.
- Reveal to the end client, or anyone else, that the work is outsourced.
- Use the end client's brand name, results or content in your own portfolio without the agency's written consent.
- Keep end-client assets after the engagement: delete briefs, files, login credentials and brand materials when it ends (where files hold personal data, handle deletion under UG-DPPA-2019).
- Discuss end-client details with peers, colleagues or on social media.

You may:

- Use anonymous, category-level descriptions in your portfolio (template below).
- With the agency's written consent, name the agency as a client (template below).
- Count white-label work towards your experience when discussing credentials in general terms, without naming the end client.

### 6. Exit a partnership

Exit when any of these apply:

- Payment is consistently more than 14 days late after the due date.
- Brief quality has not improved after two formal written requests.
- The agency changes your work without telling you and the end client complains about quality.
- The volume commitment is not met and the relationship is making a net loss after time spent.
- The agency uses your work in a way that conflicts with your professional values or ethical standards.

Exit process:

1. Give 30 days' written notice: a WhatsApp message kept as a screenshot, or email.
2. Complete all in-progress work to the agreed standard during the notice period.
3. Hand over all files, assets and access credentials within 5 working days of the final delivery.
4. Do not publicly criticise the agency: your reputation in the East African market depends on being known as a professional even when partnerships end.

## Templates

**NDA clause (minimum):**

> "Neither party will disclose to any third party the existence or terms of this arrangement without the prior written consent of the other party."

**Delivery note (WhatsApp):**

> "Delivered: [X] posts for [client category] — [date]. Please confirm receipt."

**Anonymous portfolio line (illustrative wording; use only a result you can evidence):**

> "A financial services brand in Kampala — grew Instagram engagement by 45% in 90 days."

**Agency credit (with written consent):**

> "I have delivered content services for [Agency Name]."

## Release checklist

- [ ] The six-question evaluation is applied explicitly before recommending whether to proceed.
- [ ] The pricing table has at least five service types with UGX direct and white-label rates side by side, labelled as planning figures to verify.
- [ ] The agreement lists all nine named elements, with the NDA clause written out in full.
- [ ] The brief standard names all eight specific fields, not vague categories.
- [ ] Confidentiality rules cover both prohibited actions and permitted portfolio uses.
- [ ] The exit section states the 30-day notice and the 5-working-day handover explicitly.
- [ ] East African payment terms are present: MTN MoMo as a method, the Net 15/30 progression after three months and the Net 45 hard limit.
- [ ] British English with imperative framing throughout.
