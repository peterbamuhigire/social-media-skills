# Agency economics and governance

Read when setting agency margins, accepting or re-pricing clients, structuring teams, running creative reviews and meetings, or organising brand assets. Parent skill: [playbook-agency-operations](../SKILL.md). Sources: Kelley, L. D. and Sheehan, K. B. (c. 2021–22) *Advertising Management in a Digital Environment: Text and Cases*, Routledge; Marcos, J. et al. (c. 2025) *The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page (research-backed team frameworks only); Kupsh and Graves, *High-Impact Presentations* (meeting management); *Graphic Design Rules* (design-practice guardrails); Landa, R. (2022) *Strategic Creativity*, Routledge. Case figures in these books are illustrative, not benchmarks.

## 1. The four Ps of advertising management (health check)

Score the agency 1–5 on each: **People** (talent is the product; the manager coaches), **Process** (fast collaboration without killing creativity), **Planning** (a three-to-five-year view of people, media and consumer trends), **Profit** (budget control and proof of return). Act on the lowest score first.

## 2. Client profitability controls

- **Client P&L:** track hours and direct costs by client monthly. A loss-making client means inefficient staffing or inadequate fees; re-fee when scope grows.
- **Time capture:** record time in quarter-hours, submitted weekly; client-attributable travel counts. Enforce through culture first; check labour law before any pay-linked sanction.
- **Margin gate:** set a minimum contribution margin for accepting new clients. New business that raises revenue while cutting net income (lower-margin accounts, uncapped entertainment, write-offs) is a warning; root-cause every write-off.
- **Concentration limit:** agree a maximum share of revenue from any one client. A dedicated pod for one large client ("agency within an agency") needs a signed fee that covers the pod and a plan to reduce dependence.
- **Fiscal calendar:** plan campaigns and reviews against each client's financial year; budgets are often cut in its final quarter.
- **Advertising as an expense:** never describe media spend as an asset or liability category in client documents; route accounting treatment to `chwezi-accounting-doctrine`.

## 3. Managing creative people and reviewing creative work

**Support behaviours:** know each creative as an individual; be empathetic; say *what* is needed, not *how* (the brief carries the what); listen early; expect excellent work; inspire.

**Review rules:** judge against the brief and business requirement, not taste; critique specifically ("the palette drifts from the brand's greens"); look for the platform idea that extends across channels; check the insight comes through; see the work through the customer's eyes. **Block inappropriate feedback:** dictation without reason ("make the logo bigger"), feedback from people who have not read the brief, feedback that changes the brief mid-stream, and comments that turn creatives into production staff. Detailed critique protocols live in `advertising/creative-brief-and-big-idea`.

**Player → coach:** new managers move from doing the work to working alongside the team to coaching; a star who rewrites everyone's work burns out and slows delivery. Prefer cognitive and compassionate empathy over draining emotional empathy.

## 4. People practices

- Staff often rank interesting work and recognition above pay; give continuous feedback, not annual surprises, with role-specific criteria agreed in advance.
- Conflict arises from needs, perception, power, values and feelings; on campaign disputes aim for collaboration rather than compromise, which waters down ideas.
- Managing up: frame updates around the client's strategy, bring early warning of problems, never let the boss be blindsided.
- Cultural intelligence: feedback that saves face (private, specific, about the work) suits collectivist and high-context teams; build relationship time before pitches.
- Fair hiring: inclusive job descriptions, blind CVs, the same questions in the same order, scores recorded immediately, candidates compared question by question.

## 5. Account pods (for strategic clients)

- 5–9 people covering commercial, delivery, finance and digital skills.
- A RASIC per phase (responsible, accountable, supporting, informing, contributing): opportunity, qualification, solution design, proposal, negotiation, delivery, review.
- A Belbin role mix check (coordinator, implementer, specialist, resource investigator and others).
- Team-contingent bonuses: individual bonuses paid only when the pod meets its objectives.
- Quarterly five-question pulse on Lencioni's dysfunctions: do we admit mistakes, debate strategy, commit to the plan, hold each other to account, and put the client's results first?
- Scenario pod for a regional brewer: account lead, strategist, media buyer, analyst, producer, community manager, partnerships lead.

## 6. Meetings

- **Cost rule of thumb:** hourly meeting cost ≈ 2 × the sum of attendees' hourly base pay (covers overheads and preparation). Scenario: eight managers at about UGX 34,000 an hour → ≈ UGX 545,000 an hour; a weekly two-hour meeting ≈ UGX 57m a year.
- **Do not meet** when a memo or message would do, homework is incomplete, the decision is made, members are hostile, or the matter is personal (hold those one-to-one).
- Agenda sent ahead with owners and outputs; start and end on time; last minutes go to actions, owners and deadlines; minutes and action log within 48 hours by email or WhatsApp.
- Remote meetings: smaller invite list, one participation protocol, rotating facilitator, timekeeper and scribe, call quiet participants by name.
- Client meetings on bad news: never through a casual group chat; use a documented note, then a call.

## 7. Difficult clients and work mistakes

**Difficult-client protocol:** stay calm → listen without defence → respond promptly → check the record → offer a solution (admit fault fast; if the client is wrong, show the record and offer better communication routes) → absorb reasonable cost for the relationship → review root cause.

**Work-mistake protocol:** understand what went wrong → fix it (make good lost delivery; honour a wrongly published price) → tell the client proactively → agree remuneration → change the process.

## 8. Design-practice commercial guardrails

Written brief, deliverables and costs before work; challenge an unrealistic budget early; never present an option you do not want chosen; redirect a client's prescribed solution to the underlying problem; invoice per phase; no free speculative work; estimate honestly and include contingency; never criticise the predecessor agency; keep client information confidential; dress and conduct to the client's culture.

## 9. Digital asset management (DAM)

A digital asset is a file the brand has rights to, that has value and is recognisably the brand's: logos, photographs, video, jingles, decks, copy, reports.

1. Find everyone holding assets (agency and client).
2. Centralise them (a structured shared drive is a workable minimum).
3. Define metadata: year, name, description, creator, keywords (from a controlled list), rights status.
4. Keep a **rights register**: stock licences, model releases, music licences, usage periods.
5. Keep an "approved / current" folder; withdraw outdated assets so no old price or logo stays in market.
6. Limit access to current account staff; record approvals.
7. Deliver the structure at onboarding as a premium differentiator.

Visual naming and versioning standards come from design-system-skills.

## 10. Advertising ethics in agency operations

Keep competing clients apart where conflict norms require it; publish a gift cap and apply it to everyone; obtain competing bids for production; escalate unethical client requests immediately; remember staff personal accounts reflect on clients. Claims must be substantiated (see `docs/quality-gates/legal-market-release-gate.md`).
