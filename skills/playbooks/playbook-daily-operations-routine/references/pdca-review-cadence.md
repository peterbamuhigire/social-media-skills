# PDCA Review Cadence

Merged from skills/strategy/strategy-pdca-workflow-design on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [strategy-pdca-workflow-design.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/strategy-pdca-workflow-design.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client needs the improvement cycle that sits above and around the daily routine: a Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) loop run on three timeframes at once.

- **Daily**: Do plus a light Check (execute tasks, catch urgent issues).
- **Weekly**: Check plus Act (review metrics, make small adjustments).
- **Monthly**: Plan plus Act in full (reset strategy, document learnings).

The output is an operational manual that tells the client, or their team, exactly what to do, when to do it and what to do when something is not working. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns the multi-client working day (capacity, time blocks, response queue, client communication and tools); this reference owns review cadence, decision triggers and the learning log. For a single account run by the owner or one staff member, the daily routine in step 1 below replaces the parent's multi-client blocks.

## Inputs

Ask for these before generating the manual (in addition to the parent's inputs where the operator runs several clients):

1. Client business name and industry.
2. Country and city (affects timing defaults and platform mix).
3. Platforms in scope, for example Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, WhatsApp. Facebook access in Uganda has been unstable: check register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026 before building a routine around it.
4. Who manages day to day: the client, an in-house team member or a consultant.
5. Time available per day for social media management.
6. Existing analytics access: Meta Business Suite, Google Analytics 4, TikTok Analytics, LinkedIn Analytics, other.
7. Current pain points: what is disorganised or falling through the cracks?

## Decision rules

| # | Signal or condition | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The team needs decision triggers and continuous improvement, not only a task list | Define owner, cadence, threshold, action and learning record for each trigger | A routine that repeats activity without changing underperformance |
| 2 | Engagement rate below the agreed benchmark for 2 consecutive weeks | Review content quality: is it relevant, well timed and visually strong? | Slow decline treated as noise |
| 3 | Follower count declining for 3 or more consecutive weeks | Review recent content: has tone, topic or posting frequency changed? | Audience loss discovered at the monthly report |
| 4 | Complaint volume rises noticeably | Check community-management response time and the quality of replies | Service failure becoming a reputation problem |
| 5 | One post clearly outperforms all others | Analyse and replicate its format, topic and timing | Losing a proven pattern |
| 6 | One platform consistently underperforms the others | Consider reducing posting frequency or deprioritising the platform | Effort spent where it returns least |
| 7 | Primary metric (enquiries or leads) drops for 2 consecutive months | Review the whole content-to-conversion path and find where the drop-off occurs | Fixing content when the loss is after the click |
| 8 | Several triggers fire at once, or a trigger looks like a crisis | Use the [daily-routine-method.md § Section 2](daily-routine-method.md) incident check and escalate through `playbook-crisis-communications` | Treating a crisis as an optimisation task |

Rows 2–7 call for an Act decision straight away; do not wait for the monthly review. "Benchmark" means the benchmark agreed in the metrics framework for that platform and market; the source gave no figure, and none has a register record, so set it from the client's own baseline.

## Procedure

Time defaults are East Africa Time (EAT). Adjust for the client's time zone.

### 1. Daily routine (Do + light Check), single account

| Block | Time | Tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Morning, 30 minutes | 08:00–08:30 EAT | 1. Check overnight notifications (comments, DMs, mentions) and answer anything urgent. 2. Review what is scheduled to publish today; confirm it is ready and accurate. 3. Post anything not pre-scheduled (Stories, quick updates, timely content). 4. Check for negative mentions or complaints needing an immediate response. 5. Brief review of competitor activity: any significant posts or announcements in the past 24 hours? |
| Midday, 10 minutes | 12:30–13:00 EAT | 1. Respond to comments and DMs received since the morning. 2. Engage with the audience on current posts (acknowledge genuine comments, answer questions). 3. Confirm today's scheduled post has gone live. |
| End of day, 10 minutes | 17:00–17:30 EAT | 1. Final check of comments and DMs. 2. Log content ideas or audience questions from today in the content ideas file. 3. Confirm tomorrow's scheduled content is in the queue. |

Daily time investment (planning defaults for the manual):

| Scope | Total daily time |
|---|---|
| One active platform | 50 minutes |
| Two active platforms | 65 minutes |
| Three active platforms | 80 minutes |
| Each additional platform | +15 minutes |

State the client's total daily time at the top of the manual so expectations are set from day one.

### 2. Weekly routine (Check + Act): every Monday, 45 minutes

1. **Pull weekly metrics (20 minutes)** from native analytics for each active platform: reach and impressions for the week; engagement rate per post (average and per post); follower change (net gain or loss); best-performing post (screenshot it; note format, topic and time posted); worst-performing post (note what not to repeat).
2. **Compare with the previous week (10 minutes)**: is the primary metric improving, stable or declining? Is engagement rate above or below the agreed benchmark for this platform? Is follower growth on trend for the monthly target?
3. **Review and prepare the coming week (15 minutes)**: are all posts for the week written, approved and scheduled (see [11-content-calendar](../../../pipeline/11-content-calendar/SKILL.md))? Choose one optimisation action for the week, for example a different posting time, a stronger CTA on promotional posts or a new content format. Note timely opportunities (events, awareness days, news) for the queue.
4. **Write the weekly review note** (template below) and file it in the running weekly log.
5. **WhatsApp log.** WhatsApp community management is not tracked in native analytics, so add a manual weekly log: number of inbound messages; number resolved; recurring questions to answer in content; complaints or escalations.

### 3. Monthly routine (Plan + Act in full): 3 hours across the month

- **Week 1, review (60 minutes).** Pull the monthly analytics report for all platforms (see [meta-reporting](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-reporting/SKILL.md) and [meta-social-metrics-framework](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-social-metrics-framework/SKILL.md)). Calculate Net Sentiment Score (NSS) from the month's comments: (positive comments − negative comments) ÷ total comments × 100. Compare primary metrics with the SMART targets set at the start of the month. Answer: which content pillar performed best and which underperformed? Which platform drove most results against the primary metric?
- **Weeks 1–2, plan (60 minutes).** Review next month's content calendar; adjust pillar balance from last month's data; plan campaign content and seasonal moments; pick three evergreen posts to recycle; set one optimisation hypothesis: "If we [change X], we expect [result Y] to improve by [measure Z] within [timeframe]." Write the hypothesis in the PDCA log before the month begins.
- **Week 4, act (60 minutes).** Prepare the monthly client report (see meta-reporting). Record the hypothesis result in the log: did the change work, and why or why not? Document one learning: "We learned that [audience insight]. Next month we will [action]." Book the next monthly review before closing the month.

### 4. Act on triggers between reviews

Apply decision rules 2–8 as soon as a signal appears. Every Act decision goes in the PDCA log; no undocumented change to strategy is allowed.

### 5. Keep the log

Create a simple optimisation log and update it every month without exception. Store it in the same shared folder as the content calendar and monthly reports. It is the institutional memory of the client's social media performance: it stops the team repeating mistakes and builds a picture of what works for this audience, on these platforms, in this market.

### 6. Run the Kaizen learning loop at weekly and monthly reviews

Observe the audience and the process, baseline the decision, select one small improvement, test it, check guardrails, standardise verified learning, teach it into the operating template and re-measure (see [Kaizen campaign learning loop](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-testing-framework/references/kaizen-campaign-learning-loop.md)).

## Templates

**Weekly review note** (internal, not client-facing; one paragraph after every Monday review):

> "This week: [best post and why]. [Platform] engagement was [up/down X%]. Next week: try [one specific change]."

This note becomes the working memory of the programme.

**PDCA log**

| Month | Hypothesis | Change made | Result | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Month Year] | [If we do X, Y will improve] | [Exact change made] | [Measured outcome] | [Continue / reverse / iterate] |

**Worked example** (labelled illustrative scenario, not client evidence):

| Month | Hypothesis | Change made | Result | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| March 2026 | Posting at 19:30 EAT will increase ER vs 12:00 EAT | Moved 3 posts a week to 19:30 for 4 weeks | ER increased from 2.8% to 3.4% | Continue 19:30 for all evening content |

## East Africa operating notes

Apply these for clients in Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda and neighbouring markets.

1. **Peak engagement timing.** The source treats Monday morning as the highest-traffic time for professional content in East Africa and advises B2B clients to post on Sunday evening (20:00–21:00 EAT) or Monday morning (07:30–09:00 EAT) to catch the start-of-week audience. Verify before stating (no register record); confirm against the client's own analytics.
2. **Mobile-first review.** Most East African social media managers check analytics on a smartphone rather than a desktop. Confirm that every dashboard (Meta Business Suite, TikTok Analytics, LinkedIn) is accessible and readable on mobile before handing the workflow to an in-house team.
3. **Load-shedding and power disruptions.** Scheduled power cuts in parts of Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania can disrupt scheduled posts if the scheduling tool loses connectivity. Mitigate by keeping mobile data as a backup to Wi-Fi; enabling push notifications from the scheduling tool so missed posts are flagged at once; and keeping the midday confirmation step in the daily routine to catch posts that did not go live.
4. **WhatsApp management.** WhatsApp is the dominant customer channel across East Africa (see register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026 before quoting any share). It sits outside native social analytics, so include the weekly WhatsApp log from day one.
5. **Public holidays and cultural calendar.** Build the local calendar into monthly planning: Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Liberation Day (Uganda, 26 January), Independence Day (Uganda, 9 October), Christmas, Easter and major regional sporting and cultural events. Check the gazetted dates each year; Eid dates move.

## Release checklist

1. The daily routine has named tasks with a time estimate for each block (morning, midday, end of day).
2. The weekly routine produces a documented one-paragraph note, not merely a prompt to "review analytics".
3. The monthly routine follows the review–plan–act structure mapped across four weeks.
4. Optimisation triggers are specific signals with specific, actionable responses.
5. The PDCA log template is included with a fully worked (labelled) example.
6. East Africa notes cover mobile-first management, load-shedding risk and WhatsApp tracking.
7. Total daily time is stated numerically for one platform and for each additional platform.
8. Every Act decision is logged; the manual permits no undocumented change to strategy.
9. For each optimisation, the record names the audience problem, narrative job, metric denominator, accessibility or readability guardrail, owner, stop rule, rollback and next baseline.

## Anti-patterns

- Repeating a calendar because it is familiar while the audience signal is worsening. Fix: select one bounded countermeasure and record the result in the PDCA log.
- Waiting for the monthly review when a decision trigger has fired. Fix: act on the trigger and log it.
- Setting a hypothesis after the month has started. Fix: write it in the log before the month begins.
