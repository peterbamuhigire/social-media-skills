# PR evaluation: Barcelona Principles 4.0 and the AMEC Integrated Evaluation Framework

Read when setting PR objectives, building the evaluation plan before a publicity campaign, writing the monthly earned-media review, or when a client asks for "the advertising value" of coverage. This file owns PR evaluation for the engine and replaces the earlier practice of showing advertising value equivalents (AVEs) as context.

## 1. The standard

AMEC (the International Association for the Measurement and Evaluation of Communication) published the Barcelona Principles 4.0 in June 2025, the fourth version since 2010 (register `AMEC-BARCELONA-PRINCIPLES-4-2025`; AMEC's own eBook read 29 Sep 2026). The seven principles, summarised in the engine's words:

| # | Principle (paraphrased) | What it means for a Ugandan PR plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear, measurable objectives come before planning, measurement and evaluation | Write the objective as a change in a named audience, with a number and a date, before pitching. |
| 2 | Define and understand every stakeholder audience | List the audiences (customers, regulators, employees, investors, community leaders) and what each should know, feel or do. |
| 3 | Measure across all relevant channels | Newspapers, radio, TV, online news, social, podcasts, WhatsApp shares and AI answers, not only print clippings. |
| 4 | Use qualitative and quantitative analysis | Counts plus reading what was said: message pick-up, tone, spokesperson quoted, accuracy. |
| 5 | Do not use invalid measures such as AVEs; judge communication by its outcome and impact | No AVE figure in any report, including "as context"; no multipliers on AVE. |
| 6 | Report outputs, outcomes and impact for the organisation and its audiences | Do not present clip volume or tone as proof of effect on the audience. |
| 7 | Ethics, governance and transparency with data, methods and technology | State sources, sampling and any AI tools used for analysis; never overstate results. |

AMEC's wider "say no to AVEs" position treats AVEs and their derivatives as a cost measure presented as value.

## 2. The Integrated Evaluation Framework (IEF)

AMEC's free, interactive Integrated Evaluation Framework (register `AMEC-INTEGRATED-EVALUATION-FRAMEWORK`, partial: stage names read 29 Sep 2026) lays out evaluation in seven stages. Build the PR evaluation plan as one row per stage:

| Stage | Question | PR examples (Uganda) |
|---|---|---|
| Objectives | What change, in whom, by when? | "Raise awareness of the new SACCO loan among Mbarara traders from X to Y by March" |
| Inputs | What did we put in? | Budget in UGX, staff days, spokesperson time, research |
| Activities | What did we do? | Releases, pitches, media briefings, radio talk-show appearances, newsjacking comments |
| Outputs | What did we produce and place? | Stories published or broadcast, outlets, estimated audience with its source, key message included, spokesperson quoted |
| Outtakes | What did the audience take from it? | Recall of the story, message understanding, website or WhatsApp visits from coverage, comments and shares |
| Outcomes | What changed in the audience? | Awareness, attitude or intent measured by survey; enquiries, applications or sign-ups traced to coverage |
| Impact | What changed for the organisation or society? | Loan uptake, sales, policy change, reputation or trust scores over time |

Outputs are counted; outtakes, outcomes and impact are evidenced. A report that stops at outputs must say so.

## 3. Evaluation plan procedure

1. Before any pitching, set one to three objectives at outcome or impact level, each with a baseline or a plan to get one.
2. For each objective, pick the measures per stage from the table above and name the data source and owner.
3. Add qualitative coding for coverage: key message present (yes or no), spokesperson quoted, tone towards the organisation, factual accuracy, prominence (front page, lead item, headline mention).
4. Plan outcome measurement the client can afford: a short phone or WhatsApp survey of the target audience before and after; tracked links and a dedicated WhatsApp keyword or phone line in releases; "how did you hear about us?" in sales and enquiry forms.
5. Report monthly with the IEF stages as rows; mark any stage without data `not assessed` rather than filling it with outputs.
6. At campaign end, place the result on the outcome ladder in [effectiveness-ladder review](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-reporting/references/effectiveness-ladder-review.md), which owns the post-campaign review.

## 4. When the client asks for AVE

- Explain in one line: AVE measures what the space would have cost as advertising, not what the coverage achieved, and the industry standard (Barcelona Principle 5) says not to use it.
- Offer what answers the real question: outputs with audience source, message pick-up, share of voice in tracked outlets, and the outcome measures in the plan.
- If a board or funder insists on AVE, record the request and the advice given; the engine does not produce the figure.

## 5. Share of voice and reach, honestly

- Share of voice = the client's mentions ÷ all category mentions in the same tracked outlets and period; state the outlet list.
- Reach or audience figures come from the outlet, a media-measurement provider or a published survey, with the source and year; radio and TV audiences in Uganda are often estimates; label them so.
- AI-answer presence (from newsjacking work) is reported as observed prompts and dates, not as an audience size.
