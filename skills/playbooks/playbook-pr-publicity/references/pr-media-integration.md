## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client wants press coverage in Ugandan or East African outlets and wants social media to earn and extend it: choosing outlets, testing whether a story is pitchable, drafting email and WhatsApp pitches, amplifying coverage once it lands, and building lasting journalist relationships. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns the news release, publicity kit, one-page query letter, publicity calendar and earned-media tracking; use both together.

Drafting a pitch does not authorise sending it. Contacting journalists, broadcasting to WhatsApp lists and posting coverage all need the client's explicit approval.

Evidence status. Outlet contact addresses and routes below are legacy entries with no register record: verify each before use. Facebook access in Uganda is unstable (UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026): check status at the campaign date before relying on Facebook steps. WhatsApp broadcasts follow the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY): opted-in contacts only.

## How social and earned media work together

Earned media — coverage in outlets such as Daily Monitor, NBS TV or The Africa Report — is the most credible channel a brand can get. Audiences in Uganda and across East Africa trust a newspaper article or television feature far more than a sponsored Facebook post, because editorial selection is third-party validation that paid media cannot buy. But earned media is short-lived: an article runs for a day, a broadcast airs once, and both fade unless the brand amplifies them. Social media extends each piece of coverage into weeks of credible, shareable content.

The relationship runs both ways. An active, professional Facebook Page, a founder with a credible LinkedIn profile and consistent X engagement make a brand easier to feature: they show legitimacy, supply ready quotes and visuals, and cut the journalist's verification work. Build the social presence before pitching. Use Chaffey's (2024) POEM model — Paid, Owned, Earned — to map how channels support each other: owned social amplifies earned coverage; earned credibility strengthens owned content; paid promotion can extend coverage when organic reach falls short (see [peso-integrated-strategy](../../../strategy/peso-integrated-strategy/SKILL.md)).

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| A claim works socially but is not source-ready for media | Verify and document it before pitching | Reputation damage from unsupported publicity |
| The hook meets fewer than three of the six news values | Do not pitch; reframe the hook or wait for a stronger moment | Burning journalist goodwill on a weak story |
| Pitching by WhatsApp | Send 08:00–10:00 EAT; no follow-up within 24 hours; one polite follow-up after 48 hours with no reply | Being ignored or blocked |
| Coverage has landed | Start amplification within 24 hours | Wasting the credibility spike of fresh coverage |
| Several outlets fit the story | Offer one journalist a 48-hour exclusive before widening; never blast one pitch to all outlets at once | Losing the most prominent placement |
| Partisan outlet (NRM or opposition media) is proposed | Political clients only; consult the client on risk first | Unintended political alignment |
| Journalist asks to see copy, or client asks to approve copy | Do not seek copy approval; offer factual corrections only; do not negotiate tone or framing | Damaging editorial trust |

## Procedure

### 1. Build the media list

Pick 5–8 target outlets per campaign and match the story angle to each outlet's editorial focus: a fintech launch suits Business Focus and Nile Post; a community-impact initiative suits Daily Monitor and NTV Uganda; a pan-African expansion suits The Africa Report. Offer exclusives first (see decision rules).

| Outlet | Type | Audience | Best for | Contact route (legacy; verify before use) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily Monitor | National newspaper + online | General, educated, urban | Major business news, policy, social impact | editorial@monitor.co.ug or direct to section editors |
| New Vision | National newspaper + online | General, government, rural | Government relations, public sector, wider national reach | Via newsdesk or reporters on X |
| NBS TV | Television | Urban, national | Business features, CEO interviews, sector analysis | Via presenters on social media |
| NTV Uganda | Television | Urban, national | Business, entertainment, lifestyle features | Via TV producers; WhatsApp pitches acceptable |
| The Observer | Online + print | Political, business, professional | Opinion, investigative angles, policy analysis | observer@observer.ug |
| Nile Post | Online | Urban, youth | Breaking business news, tech, start-ups | Via editor on X |
| Chimp Reports | Online | Broad consumer | Consumer brand news, entertainment, PR announcements | Via WhatsApp to editors |
| Business Focus | Online business news | Business professionals, investors | Growth stories, financial performance, sector profiles | editor@businessfocus.co.ug |
| The Africa Report | Pan-African online / print | African investors, diaspora, policy makers | Pan-African business narrative, regional expansion | Website submission form |
| NRM / opposition media | Partisan outlets | Political audiences | Political clients only; handle with care | Consult the client on risk before any contact |

### 2. Test the story against six news values

Rate the client's hook strong / weak / absent on each value and recommend proceed, reframe or wait. Pitch only if you can tick at least three (the source's threshold). (The parent skill's Part 1 lists story types worth and not worth pitching; this is the scoring step.)

1. **Timeliness** — is it happening now? Tie it to a current trend, regulation, national event or season (Budget Day, school term, harvest, a new regulatory development).
2. **Impact** — how many people does it affect? Concrete numbers (jobs created, loans disbursed, patients treated) strengthen it.
3. **Proximity** — is it in Uganda, Kampala, a named region or a named community? Local specificity raises relevance for Ugandan editors.
4. **Prominence** — is the spokesperson a recognised name in the sector: first mover, record-setter, award winner, pioneer?
5. **Human interest** — a founder from humble origins, a business that survived COVID-19, a woman-led enterprise breaking barriers, a young innovator solving a local problem.
6. **Conflict or contrast** — a problem solved, an industry norm challenged, an unexpected data finding, or a surprising contrast such as global technology in a Ugandan village.

### 3. Pitch formats

**Email pitch (under 200 words).** For editors and section journalists; this is a short news pitch, distinct from the one-page feature query letter in the parent skill. Journalists receive dozens of pitches a day; brevity signals professionalism.

```
Subject line: [One-line story summary — what happened and why it matters]

Dear [Journalist's first name],

[Opening: the story in 2 sentences. What happened? Who does it affect?]

[The hook: why this matters now — link to a current trend, regulation, or local event.]

[Key facts:
- Fact or data point one
- Fact or data point two
- Fact or data point three]

[Spokesperson: [Name], [Title], is available for interview.
Contact: [phone number] / [email]]

[Offer: Are you offering an exclusive? Is there an embargo date? Is a photo
opportunity or background briefing available?]

[Your name and contact details]
```

**WhatsApp pitch.** Many Ugandan journalists and editors respond faster on WhatsApp than by email. Use the email structure, adapted:

- 3–4 short paragraphs; no walls of text.
- Send 08:00–10:00 EAT, before editorial meetings, when journalists plan coverage.
- Open with the story in one sentence, then add context.
- Close with the spokesperson's direct number and your contact.
- No follow-up within 24 hours; one polite follow-up after 48 hours with no response.

**Generating the drafts.** From the confirmed inputs, draft both formats; build the opening sentences on the strongest news values; use the spokesperson's name and title; confirm embargo dates or exclusivity terms before anything is sent.

### 4. Amplify earned coverage (start within 24 hours)

The source headed this a seven-step checklist but lists eight steps; all eight are kept.

1. **Screenshot and share** — headline and lead paragraph on all active channels, with a caption that explains what was said and why it matters to the audience, not just "We were featured!".
2. **LinkedIn** — quote the strongest line verbatim; tag the publication's page where available; add 2–3 sentences on what the milestone means for the business and its clients.
3. **Facebook** — warmer, conversational tone ("We made it into the Daily Monitor. Here is what they said about us…") with a direct link (check Facebook status in Uganda first).
4. **WhatsApp broadcast** — the link to the opted-in list with a short first-person message from the founder or spokesperson; first-person tone lifts click-through (source claim).
5. **Email newsletter** — in the next send, under a heading such as "As featured in Daily Monitor"; one sentence and a link is enough.
6. **Pin the post** — pin the coverage post to the top of the Facebook Page for 7 days.
7. **Google Business Profile** — add a post linking to the article; supports local search credibility and shows active brand management.
8. **Credentials update** — add the outlet, date and headline to the client's credentials document ([biz-dev-credentials](../../../business-development/biz-dev-credentials/SKILL.md)) for future proposals and pitches.

Draft platform-specific captions for steps 1–4 as part of the deliverable.

### 5. Build ongoing media relationships

One-off pitches give one-off results; sustained relationships bring unprompted coverage.

- **Follow and engage first** — follow 10–15 relevant journalists on X; comment with insight and share with added context for 4–6 weeks before a first pitch. Cold pitches from unfamiliar contacts are routinely ignored.
- **Offer value without pitching** — a data point for a story they are covering, a source contact in the industry, a comment on a developing story. Position the spokesperson as a reliable expert source.
- **Acknowledge published coverage** — a one- or two-sentence thank-you WhatsApp with no ask attached; record it in the contacts log.
- **Offer exclusives** — one journalist, 48 hours, before widening (the parent skill allows 48–72 hours for a major story).
- **Respect editorial independence** — never seek to review or approve copy; offer factual corrections if contacted; do not negotiate tone or framing.

**Media contacts log** — keep it per client and update it after every interaction; pre-fill it from any contacts given at intake. (The parent skill's Part 6 tracks coverage; this log tracks people.)

## Release checklist

1. The media list uses the outlet table (type, audience, best-fit angle, contact route), with every contact route verified.
2. All six news values are applied to the client's hook with a clear pitch / reframe / wait recommendation.
3. A draft email pitch under 200 words in the template format, plus a WhatsApp version.
4. The amplification checklist with platform-specific draft captions.
5. The media contacts log, pre-filled where contacts were supplied.
6. Relationship guidance with concrete timelines (4–6 weeks of engagement, 48-hour exclusivity).
7. The spokesperson's name and title appear in every pitch draft and amplification caption.
8. Every claim in a pitch is verified and documented; nothing is sent without client approval.
