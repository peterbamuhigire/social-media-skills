# Value-first renewal and relationship health

Read before a retainer renewal, a relationship reset, after the agency misses a commitment, or when designing how clients want to hear from the agency. Parent skill: [playbook-client-retainer-management](../SKILL.md). Sources: Marcos, J. et al. (c. 2025) *The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page (research-backed frameworks only); Hunter, V. L. with Tietyen, D. (1997) *Business to Business Marketing*, NTC Business Books; Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press; Nelson, J. (2019) *The Seven Figure Agency Roadmap*.

## 1. Client council (value-based contact design)

Once a year invite six to eight retainer clients to a 90-minute session (lunch works). Ask each to list and rank the reports, insight notes, reviews and alerts they value, the format they prefer (WhatsApp summary, PDF, dashboard, in-person review) and how often. Reconcile into a contact model per client grade: content × frequency × channel, coded preferred / acceptable / support. If a council is impractical, gather the same answers two questions at a time in monthly check-ins. Every contact must leave the client better informed or better served; "just checking in" is not a contact.

## 2. Relationship health review (quarterly)

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Loyalty segment | Secure (share of their marketing spend with us rising), complacent (flat), at risk (neutral, flat at best), defector (falling)? |
| Behaviour | Approvals on time? Briefs requested? Invoices paid on time? Replies timely? |
| Coverage | How many of their people do we know, at what levels? Single-threaded? |
| Trust drivers | Shared goals and KPIs; information sharing; reciprocity; personal ties |
| Share of wallet | What do they spend with other agencies or in-house, and why? |
| Repurchase intent | "Based on this quarter, how likely are you to renew?" and "Would you recommend us to a colleague?" (named, not anonymous) |

**Underselling check** (Maltz et al.): compare what the client spends across the category with what they spend with the agency; ask why the agency does not have the rest; prescribe the full solution with options rather than the smallest safe scope.

## 3. Trust-recovery protocol

1. Acknowledge within 24 hours and take ownership.
2. State the impact on the client plainly.
3. Issue a resolution plan with steps, owners and dates (make good at the agency's cost where fair).
4. Send progress updates until closed.
5. Share a short root-cause note and the process change.
6. Rebuild through consistent follow-through.

Script: "We missed [commitment]. The impact on you was [impact]. Here's what we're doing, who owns it, and when you'll hear from us next."

## 4. Value-first renewal

**Six weeks before the end date:**
1. Recap results against the baseline agreed at the start (enquiries, bookings, cost per lead, share of voice), with sources.
2. Show value realised and documented; flag what did not work and what will change.
3. Prepare next year's plan tied to the client's stated priorities for the year.
4. Build three **MESO** packages of equal value to the agency that differ in scope, support level, reporting and risk-sharing (for example a strategic package with quarterly workshops, a growth package, and an essentials package).
5. Prepare a concession worksheet.

**Concession worksheet:** list every tradeable variable (term length, payment terms, reporting frequency, response time, exclusivity, case-study rights, training hours, price). Score each for cost to the agency and value to the client. Pair cheap-for-us, valuable-to-them gives with the reverse asks. Order from minor to major. Pre-write the conditional wording.

**In the meeting:**
- Start with shared interests ("It's encouraging that we both want [outcome]").
- Value first, price second. Never apologise for price.
- Present the three packages together: "We've prepared three options of equal value to us. They differ in how much support and risk-sharing you want. Which comes closest to how you'd like to work?"
- Trade only conditionally: "If you commit to 12 months, then we can hold current rates" / "If you can give us quarterly case-study rights, then we'll add a strategy workshop each quarter."
- Label each concession and make each successive one smaller.
- Disagree with reasons and questions, not labels: "Here's what our delivery data shows… given that, how would a six-week timeline protect your launch?"

**After signing, a post-settlement session:** "Our agreement stands whatever happens in the next half hour. Can we look for anything that would make it better for both of us: timing, payment, extra services, introductions?"

**Ethics:** no exaggerated complexity, no hidden terms, no false deadlines.

## 5. Closing language

- Replace "So, what do you think?" with a choice: "There are two sensible ways to continue: [A] or [B]. Which fits your budget cycle better?"
- Next-step close: "Here's how we'll continue: we sign the renewal this week, review priorities on [date], and you'll see the first plan by [date]."
- Reassurance for hesitation: "It's normal to pause before a decision like this. Here's what protects you: [stage gate, exit point]."

## 6. If the client leaves

Separate research from win-back. Research: ask for 15 minutes of honest feedback ("I'm not calling to reopen the decision; I'm trying to get better. Where did we fall short?"). Classify the reason (price, service, results, internal change, budget). Win-back later addresses the specific unmet need ("You needed weekly reporting; we now provide it").
