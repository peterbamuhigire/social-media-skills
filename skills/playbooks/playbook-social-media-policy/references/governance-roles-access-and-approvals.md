# Governance: Roles, Access and Approvals

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-social-media-governance on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [playbook-social-media-governance.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-social-media-governance.md)

## When to use this reference

The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) writes the staff-facing social media policy (what employees may and may not post). This reference builds the operating governance behind it: who owns the function, who approves what, who holds account access, how incidents escalate, how agencies are controlled and how the organisation learns. Use it when one or more of these apply:

- The client is a medium-to-large organisation with several people posting to social channels.
- Nobody clearly owns social media internally: anyone can post and no one is accountable.
- A past crisis was made worse by unclear decision authority or slow escalation.
- The client is bringing social media in-house after outsourcing it to an agency.
- The social media function is growing and needs a formal operating structure.
- Leadership wants documented accountability before approving a larger social media budget.

Evidence status. The governance structure and Social Media Command Centre follow Funk (2013); the operational and measurement layers follow Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2022). Advise the client to have the framework reviewed by legal counsel and aligned with HR policies before rollout. Legal points in the certification module follow registers UG-CMA-2022-VOID-2026 and UG-CMA-SECTIONS-STRUCK-2026; they are not legal advice. Tool tiers and features have no register record: verify before stating.

## Inputs

Collect these before drafting (in addition to the parent's required input):

- Client organisation name and sector (bank, NGO, government body, large SME and so on).
- Country and city; default Uganda/East Africa.
- Primary goal for formal governance, for example fewer response failures, clear ownership or preparation for an in-house transition.
- Current team size: how many people post or manage social media.
- Platforms in active use (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp Business, LinkedIn, X/Twitter, TikTok, YouTube).
- Current agency or vendor relationships: which social functions are outsourced, and to whom.
- Key internal stakeholders: names and titles of the Social Media Manager (or equivalent), Communications Director, Legal/Compliance contact and HR contact.
- Reporting line: who the social media function reports to (Marketing Director, CEO, Communications and so on).

## Decision rules

| # | Condition | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No one owns a high-risk account or approval step | Stop rollout and assign an accountable role | Orphaned access and decisions |
| 2 | Two people are Accountable for the same function, or a function has no Responsible party | Resolve the RACI before finalising the document | A broken governance framework |
| 3 | A person has account access but no current certificate | Withdraw posting rights until certified: no certificate, no account access | Untrained staff publishing for the organisation |
| 4 | An agency asks for primary admin or master credentials | Refuse; the client holds master credentials, the agency secondary access only | Losing control of the organisation's accounts |
| 5 | An out-of-hours incident reaches Level 2 or above | Escalate immediately, whatever the time | Incidents growing overnight |

## Procedure

### 1. Set the organisational structure

Define the function's place in the organisation before assigning roles. In Uganda and East Africa, governance must respect existing hierarchies: make accountability explicit without creating a parallel power structure that bypasses established reporting lines.

Recommended reporting line for most East African organisations: Social Media Manager → Head of Communications (or Marketing Director) → CEO/Executive Director. Where no Communications Director exists, the Social Media Manager reports to the most senior marketing or operations lead. Document the line in writing and put it in job descriptions.

Specify which functions sit inside the social media function and which are shared:

| Function | Recommended owner |
|---|---|
| Content strategy | Social Media Manager |
| Content creation | Social Media Manager / content team |
| Community management | Social Media Manager / Community Manager |
| Analytics and reporting | Social Media Manager |
| Crisis response | Communications Director (leads); Social Media Manager (executes) |
| Paid campaigns | Marketing Director (approves); Social Media Manager (executes) |
| Platform administration | IT and Social Media Manager (joint) |
| Policy and governance | HR and Communications Director |

### 2. Build the RACI

R = Responsible (does the work); A = Accountable (owns the outcome); C = Consulted (input needed before action); I = Informed (told after action). Fill the table with the client's real names and titles; where a role does not exist yet, record it as a gap in the staffing plan.

| Function | Social Media Manager | Comms Director | Legal/Compliance | HR | IT | CEO/ED |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Content creation | R | A | — | — | — | I |
| Content approval | C | A/R | C (sensitive topics) | — | — | I |
| Community management (daily responses) | R | A | — | — | — | I |
| Crisis escalation | R | A | C | C | — | I |
| Paid campaign management | R | A | — | — | — | I |
| Analytics and reporting | R | A | — | — | — | I |
| Platform administration (passwords, settings) | R | A | — | — | C | I |
| Policy updates | C | A | C | R | — | I |

Adapt the column headings to the client's job titles. Every function has exactly one A (decision rule 2).

### 3. Run a certification programme

Funk (2013) treats staff certification as a foundational control: anyone posting for the organisation shows competence before receiving posting rights.

Who must be certified:

- Anyone with admin access to an official account.
- Anyone who publishes for the organisation, including guest authors and subject-matter experts who post from time to time.
- Agency and vendor staff with direct account access (not only content delivery).
- IT staff who hold platform credentials.

| Module | Content | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Brand voice and tone | Tone of voice, messaging hierarchy, what to say and what to avoid | Self-study and written test |
| 2. Crisis protocol | Escalation path, response times, holding-statement templates, what not to do | Workshop and scenario exercise |
| 3. Legal and compliance | Uganda Computer Misuse Act 2011 as it stands after the 17 March 2026 ruling (the 2022 Amendment Act is void; several provisions and criminal libel were struck; verify the section list on ULII, registers UG-CMA-2022-VOID-2026 and UG-CMA-SECTIONS-STRUCK-2026); defamation risk (criminal libel struck in Uganda; civil liability and other speech offences remain); copyright and image rights; disclosure rules (see [staff-policy-template.md § 5](staff-policy-template.md)) | Self-study and written test |
| 4. Platform mechanics | How each active platform works: scheduling, admin settings, Stories versus feed, DM management | Practical exercise |
| 5. Approval workflow | End to end: draft → review → approval → publishing → reporting | Practical exercise |

Assessment:

- Written test of 20 questions covering modules 1, 3 and 5; pass mark 80%.
- Practical: the candidate drafts three posts (one standard post, one complaint response, one crisis holding statement) for assessor review.
- Both parts must be passed. A failed practical may be retaken once after a two-week remediation period.

Recertify annually at the start of each financial year; immediately after a major policy change or significant platform update; and immediately after a staff member is involved in a crisis incident. Issue the certificate described under Templates and attach posting rights to it (decision rule 3).

### 4. Design the escalation path

Informal norms (relying on someone to "just know" to call the boss) fail during a fast-moving incident, so set explicit triggers and response times.

| Level | Trigger | Response time | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Community Manager handles | Routine complaint, question or comment needing a response | Within 2 hours in operating hours | Respond with approved templates; log the interaction; no further escalation |
| 2 — Senior Manager notified | Complaint gaining traction (50+ interactions); a sensitive topic (religion, politics, ethnic identity); a post that could be read as an official position | Community Manager alerts Senior Manager within 30 minutes of the trigger; Senior Manager responds within 1 hour | Senior Manager approves the response before publication; the Community Manager does not act alone |
| 3 — Communications Director leads | 200+ interactions on a negative post; any media involvement; any post touching legal risk, safety or a fraud allegation | Senior Manager alerts the Communications Director within 15 minutes; the Director takes ownership within 30 minutes | All responses paused until the Director approves; holding statement issued; content calendar paused |
| 4 — Legal and CEO involved | National media; regulatory or government involvement; criminal allegation; reputational threat at executive level | Communications Director alerts Legal and the CEO together; response within 60 minutes | No public statement without CEO and Legal sign-off; the Director coordinates; the Social Media Manager executes approved content only |

Interface matrix: how the social team connects with adjacent functions during an escalation.

| Situation | Social team contacts | Social team provides |
|---|---|---|
| Customer complaint needing a refund or resolution | Customer Service | Screenshot of the complaint; customer contact details (share only what the handoff needs: register UG-DPPA-2019) |
| Legal risk found in a post or comment | Legal/Compliance | Screenshot; platform link; timeline of events |
| Media enquiry received by DM | Communications Director | Full DM thread; sender's follower count; any earlier interactions |
| PR campaign support needed | PR team | Content calendar; approved messaging; platform reach data |

For the crisis response itself, hand over to `playbook-crisis-communications`.

### 5. Set up the Social Media Command Centre (SMCC)

Funk (2013) defines the SMCC as the dedicated monitoring and response function through which all social activity flows. It need not be a physical room; it is an operating structure with defined tools, staffing and schedules. It:

- monitors all active platforms and brand mentions in real time during operating hours;
- routes incoming messages and comments to the right responder;
- tracks response times and flags overdue items;
- alerts the escalation chain when trigger conditions are met;
- archives all interactions for reporting and audit.

| Organisation size | SMCC staffing model |
|---|---|
| Micro (under 10 staff; 1–2 platforms) | 1 person part-time (20% of the role); self-monitoring; no formal rota |
| Small (10–50 staff; 2–4 platforms) | 1 FTE Social Media Manager covering operating hours; out-of-hours alerts through notifications |
| Medium (50–200 staff; 4–6 platforms) | 2–3 FTE on a defined rota; at least one person monitoring at all times in operating hours |
| Large (200+ staff; 6+ platforms; high-volume channels) | Dedicated SMCC team with tiered roles (Community Manager, Senior Manager, Analyst); possibly a 24/7 rota |

Technology stack (East Africa options; verify current tiers and availability before recommending):

| Function | Paid option | Free or low-cost option |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and listening | Brandwatch, Sprout Social | Google Alerts; native platform notifications; TweetDeck for X (verify its current name, price and access terms) |
| Scheduling | Hootsuite, Buffer Pro | Buffer free tier; Meta Business Suite (Facebook/Instagram) |
| Inbox management | Sprout Social, Zoho Social | Meta Business Suite; individual platform apps |
| Analytics and reporting | Sprout Social, Hootsuite Analytics | Meta Insights; native analytics; Google Looker Studio |
| Internal communication | Slack, Microsoft Teams | WhatsApp group, clearly labelled "Social Media SMCC" |

Operating hours: define them in the governance document. For most East African organisations, 08:00–18:00 EAT, Monday to Friday, with weekend cover during active campaigns. Out-of-hours plan:

- Push notifications from all platforms to the on-call person's phone.
- One on-call contact per week, rotated weekly.
- Platform auto-responses for DMs outside operating hours.
- Every out-of-hours Level 2+ incident escalated immediately, whatever the time; no waiting until morning.

### 6. Control agencies and vendors

When an agency or freelancer is engaged, the client keeps governance ownership. The agency executes inside the client's framework; it does not set the rules.

| Responsibility | Client retains | Agency manages |
|---|---|---|
| Brand and messaging approval | Yes | No |
| Platform passwords and admin access | Yes (primary admin) | Secondary access only |
| Content strategy and pillars | Yes (approves) | Develops options for approval |
| Daily content production | No (delegates) | Yes |
| Community management responses | Approves templates | Executes within templates |
| Analytics and reporting | Reviews and approves | Produces |
| Crisis response | Leads and decides | Executes approved responses only |

Review the agency quarterly on:

- **Delivery rate**: percentage of agreed deliverables delivered on time.
- **Quality score**: client rating (1–5) of content quality across the quarter.
- **Response compliance**: percentage of community responses within the SLA time.
- **Reporting accuracy**: are reports accurate, complete and on time?
- **Strategic value**: has the agency found opportunities or flagged risks beyond the basic brief?

Use the SLA and monthly-meeting templates below.

### 7. Learn through after-action reviews (AAR)

The AAR is the organisation's main mechanism for institutional learning; without it the same governance failures repeat. Hold one:

- within 5 working days of any Level 2 or Level 3 crisis being resolved;
- within 10 working days of the end of any major campaign;
- when a significant change in platform performance cannot be explained by known factors.

Attendees: Social Media Manager (always); Communications Director (always); content creators involved; agency lead (if the agency was involved); Legal/Compliance (crisis AARs only).

Document each AAR on the one-page template and file it in the governance folder. Track AAR actions in the monthly governance meeting until closed. If an AAR exposes a gap in the governance framework, the policy or the certification programme, update those documents within 30 days and log the version change (this also triggers the [staff-policy-template.md § 8](staff-policy-template.md) policy review).

## Templates

**Certificate (one page, internal).** Staff member name; role; date of certification; modules completed; certifying manager's name; signature; expiry date. Store in the HR file.

**Agency SLA structure** (include in any contract or scope of work):

- Deliverables: every output by name, for example 12 feed posts a month, 4 Stories, 1 monthly report.
- Turnaround: content submitted for approval X working days before scheduled publication (recommend 3–5 days).
- Approval rounds: at most 2 rounds of revisions per piece before extra fees apply.
- Response time to client queries: within 24 hours on weekdays.
- Performance review: monthly scorecard against agreed KPIs; quarterly strategic review.
- Access and credentials: agency holds secondary admin access only; client holds master credentials at all times.
- Termination: 30 days' written notice from either party.

**Monthly governance meeting (45–60 minutes)**

1. Performance against last month's KPIs (10 minutes).
2. Content review: what performed well and what did not (10 minutes).
3. Upcoming campaigns and content-plan approval (15 minutes).
4. Issues, escalations or policy clarifications (10 minutes).
5. Actions and owners for the coming month (5 minutes).

Circulate a written summary within 48 hours; the agency drafts it and the client approves it.

**AAR (one page).** Answer four questions in writing:

1. What was planned? The campaign goal, content plan or crisis protocol as it stood before the event.
2. What happened? Performance data, timeline of events, audience response.
3. Why was there a gap? The specific root cause: resourcing gap, process failure, approval bottleneck or external factor.
4. What do we do differently? At least three concrete actions with named owners and deadlines.

**Social media business plan** (internal document for leadership buy-in and budget; not public; new version each year; adapted from Funk, 2013):

1. **Executive summary**: one page; purpose of the function, primary platforms, team structure and headline KPIs for the year. Written last, placed first.
2. **Positioning statement**: one paragraph on how the organisation's social presence should be seen relative to peers and competitors: what audiences should think of us online, and how that differs from what competitors project.
3. **Mission**: one sentence on the function's purpose in terms of organisational value, not platform activity. Example: "To build public trust in [Organisation] through consistent, accurate, and responsive digital communication across all active channels."
4. **Market analysis**: the social media picture in the sector and geography: platform reach for Uganda/East Africa (Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn; cite registers DATAREPORTAL-UG-KE-2026 and WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026, and check Facebook access in Uganda against UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026), target-audience digital behaviour and key industry trends.
5. **Competitive analysis**: how 3–5 peers or direct competitors use social media: posting frequency, content types, engagement rates where visible, observable strengths and weaknesses.
6. **SMART goals**: at least four annual goals, each Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012), each with a baseline metric, a target and a measurement method.
7. **Platform roles**: a table giving each platform's role in the RACE framework (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022): Reach, Act, Convert or Engage. This prevents duplication and clarifies investment priorities.
8. **Staffing plan**: current headcount, proposed roles, role definitions, reporting structure, each member's certification status and the training investment needed.
9. **Operations plan**: content-calendar cadence, approval workflow, community-management rota, SMCC coverage, agency coordination and reporting schedule.
10. **Budget**: itemised annual budget for staffing, tools and technology, content production (design, photography, video), paid amplification, training and certification, and agency or vendor fees, with a narrative justifying each line in terms of expected return.

## Release checklist

1. The RACI uses the client's real job titles; every function has exactly one Accountable owner and a Responsible party.
2. Each escalation level has specific, quantified triggers, not vague words such as "significant" or "serious" without thresholds.
3. The certification programme names the roles to be certified, the pass mark and the recertification schedule, not just a list of topics.
4. The SMCC section recommends a technology stack suited to the client's size and budget, with at least one free or low-cost option per function.
5. The agency section states without ambiguity what the client retains and what the agency manages, especially credentials and approval authority.
6. The AAR produces a one-page document with named owners and deadlines, not a reflective discussion.
7. The business plan template includes all ten sections with enough explanation to draft from without further instruction.
8. British English throughout; no American spellings in the deliverable.
