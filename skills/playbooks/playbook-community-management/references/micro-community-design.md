# Micro-Community Design

Merged from skills/strategy/strategy-micro-communities on 2026-09-29 at ce3299a; preservation map: [strategy-micro-communities.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/strategy-micro-communities.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client wants to build, relaunch or monetise a niche community (a WhatsApp group or Community, a Facebook Group, a LinkedIn Group or a private forum) around member value, rather than manage comments and messages on a brand page. It covers the community-versus-audience test, platform selection, five design elements (purpose, member profile, value proposition, house rules, content rhythm), a week-by-week launch, monetisation and business integration, and East African operating defaults. The parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns day-to-day moderation, escalation and community health metrics once the community is running.

Only design a community when a specific member need and operating capacity exist; then define the membership promise, moderation and cadence before recruiting. An unmanaged broadcast group creates spam and trust loss.

Evidence status. Platform features, group size limits and monetisation tools change often and have no register record: verify before stating. Facebook in Uganda: follow register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026 (consumer access reported restored from 13 June 2026 without official confirmation; official status not assessed), so treat the Facebook-first defaults below as legacy engine calibration to check against the client's own audience data. WhatsApp group messaging follows register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY where business tools are used. Price points are test ranges, not benchmarks.

## Inputs

Collect all seven before producing a deliverable:

| # | Input | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client business name and industry | For example "Kampala Bakers Ltd — food and beverage" |
| 2 | Country / city | Default Uganda/East Africa |
| 3 | Primary audience | Who would join and why: demographics, shared interests and motivations, stated specifically |
| 4 | Business objective | Customer retention, lead generation, product feedback, brand advocacy, or a combination |
| 5 | Moderation capacity | Hours per week the client or team can give to moderating and posting |
| 6 | Existing community | Any Facebook Group, WhatsApp group, forum or informal community; if yes, its current state (member count, activity, problems) |
| 7 | Platform preference | Facebook Group, WhatsApp Community, LinkedIn Group or private forum; if unsure, apply the platform selection rules |

## Community versus audience

An **audience** receives content passively: Page followers, newsletter subscribers, channel viewers. The brand broadcasts and the audience consumes; the brand controls what is posted, when and who sees it. Growth is measured in reach and impressions, and the relationship runs one way.

A **community** is a group of people with shared interests who interact with each other, not only with the brand. The brand facilitates and members create value for one another: a question from one member is answered by five others before the brand responds; a success story inspires three more members to try something new. The brand does not fully control the conversation, which is what makes the community credible. Growth is measured in engagement depth, member retention and peer-to-peer interaction.

Why it matters in East Africa: in Uganda and across the region community is a cultural norm, not a marketing tactic. People trust networks, referrals and shared group membership far more than brand advertising; a Ugandan consumer is more likely to act on a recommendation from someone in their WhatsApp group than on a sponsored post. A genuine community earns trust no advertising budget can replicate. The POEM model (Chaffey, 2024) classifies a community as owned media, but a thriving community generates earned media (peer-to-peer advocacy) at scale.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Specific member need and operating capacity exist | Define membership promise, moderation and cadence before recruitment | Unmanaged broadcast group; spam and trust loss |
| Client names a platform | Validate it against the selection table before proceeding | Choosing a platform the audience or team cannot sustain |
| Broad, consumer-facing audience in Uganda | Default to a private Facebook Group for trust or a public one for discovery (check current Facebook access and the client's audience data first) | Building where the audience is not |
| High-frequency, mobile-first communication with a small trusted group | WhatsApp Community or a single WhatsApp group | Low engagement on a slower platform |
| B2B lead generation or professional credibility | LinkedIn Group | Consumer platform for a professional audience |
| Existing loyal audience willing to pay for exclusive access | Evaluate Circle or Mighty Networks | Paid tier with no equity to support it |
| Client cannot moderate at the platform's load | Do not recommend that platform; a dead community is worse than none | Abandoned community damaging the brand |
| Purpose statement could be replaced by "follow our page" | Reject and rewrite it | An audience mislabelled as a community |
| No conversion pathway demonstrated within six months | Trigger a strategy review | Cost with no business value |
| Evidence is contradictory or materially incomplete | Pause the affected recommendation and request the accountable source | Advice built on an unresolved premise |

## Procedure

### 1. Select the platform

| Platform | Best for | East Africa suitability | Moderation load | Monetisation options |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Group (public) | Discovery-led communities, broad topics | High in the source's calibration (widest reach across demographics); check current Uganda access (register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026) | Medium | Facebook Stars, paid subgroups (verify availability) |
| Facebook Group (private) | High-trust communities, exclusive access | High (same caveat) | High | Paid membership (verify availability) |
| WhatsApp Community | Operational, high-engagement, mobile-first | Very high — WhatsApp is ubiquitous in Uganda and East Africa (register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026: state no penetration percentage) | Very high | Not native — link to an external offer |
| WhatsApp group (single) | Small, high-trust groups (source gives a ceiling of 1,024 members; verify the current limit) | Very high | High | Not native |
| LinkedIn Group | Professional / B2B communities | Medium — lower penetration outside the formal sector | Low–medium | Lead generation |
| Telegram | High-privacy, tech-savvy audiences | Low–medium outside the tech sector | Low | Paid channels |
| Private forum (Circle, Mighty Networks) | Premium, paid communities | Low — needs strong existing brand equity to justify sign-up | Low | Subscription revenue |

Justify the recommendation against this table, with moderation load weighed against the client's stated capacity.

### 2. Design the five community elements

Produce all five, specific to the client's business, audience and objective.

**2.1 Purpose statement.** One sentence: "This community exists so that [audience] can [do/achieve/experience something specific]." Member-centric, not brand-centric: "This community exists to promote [Brand]" describes an audience, not a community. Example: "This community exists so that Ugandan women entrepreneurs can share practical advice on running a business while raising a family." Test: read it aloud; if it could be replaced with "follow our page", reject and rewrite.

**2.2 Member profile.** Vague profiles produce communities useful to no one. Include demographics (age range, location, gender if relevant); professional or life-stage context; the shared problem, interest or aspiration; and what would motivate joining and staying active. Example: Example (house wording): "Ugandan women aged 25–45 who own an SME with 1–5 employees, have been operating for at least one year, and are looking for peer support and practical business advice — not inspirational content."

**2.3 Member value proposition.** Three specific things a member gains that they cannot get elsewhere; generic answers ("connection", "support") are not acceptable. Example:
1. Access to other vetted business owners in the same sector who share real numbers and real challenges, not curated highlight reels.
2. First access to the client's product launches, promotions and free resources before the public.
3. Monthly Q&A sessions with the founder or an invited expert, exclusive to members.

**2.4 House rules.** Publish in the group description and pin as the first post. Adapt this template:
1. Introduce yourself when you join — tell us who you are and what brought you here.
2. Share your knowledge and experience freely — this is a giving community, not a selling community.
3. No spam, unsolicited promotion or advertising without prior admin approval.
4. Treat every member with respect — no harassment, personal attacks, or discrimination based on gender, religion, ethnicity or any other characteristic.
5. Do not share false or unverified claims, particularly on health, finance or legal matters.
6. Tag the admin if you see a post that breaks these rules.
7. Consequences: first offence → admin warning by private message; second → post removed; third → permanent removal from the group.

**2.5 Content rhythm.** Communities need consistent admin content, especially in the first three months.

| Day | Post type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Opening question or weekly prompt | "What is your biggest challenge this week? Drop it below — let's help each other." |
| Wednesday | Educational post, how-to or resource | A short tip, checklist or article relevant to the community purpose |
| Friday | Celebration post | Member spotlight, wins-of-the-week thread, or a member-submitted success story |
| Daily | Active moderation | Respond to all member posts and comments within 24 hours; welcome every new member personally |

From Month 2, reduce admin posts to a minimum of three per week once member content sustains the feed. Never drop below three admin posts per week in the first six months.

### 3. Run the launch sequence

Adapt timings only for a compelling reason.

- **Week 0 — pre-launch set-up.** Complete the description, cover-image direction (a brief for the designer) and house rules. Write the welcome post before anyone joins. Select 10–20 founding members the client knows personally, respects and trusts to set the culture; they are chosen, not random followers.
- **Week 1 — soft launch.** Invite founding members personally by direct message, not a mass broadcast. Post the welcome message. Publish five posts this week so the feed is full before others arrive. Ask founding members to introduce themselves. A community that launches to an empty feed dies immediately.
- **Week 2 — activation.** Post one question designed to generate replies, not likes. Respond to every comment within 24 hours. Message any member who joins but does not post: "Welcome! Tell us a little about yourself and what brought you here." This single action markedly increases early engagement.
- **Weeks 3–4 — growth push.** Promote externally: a post on the brand's Page, a WhatsApp broadcast to existing opted-in contacts (register WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY), a link in the email newsletter. Continue daily moderation without exception. Highlight founding-member contributions publicly to set behavioural norms.
- **Month 2 onwards.** Post at least three times per week. Spotlight one member per week. Run one Q&A or live discussion per month. Track member growth, post engagement rate and the ratio of admin posts to member posts; the member share should rise steadily, aiming for 60% member-generated content by Month 4.

### 4. Integrate with the business and monetise

Report community returns with the ROI formula (TLV − COCA) ÷ COCA (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012), where TLV is total lifetime value and COCA the cost of customer acquisition.

- **Lead generation.** Pin a post in the group header linking to a lead magnet, free consultation or exclusive offer. Community enquiries are warm leads who have already shown trust; track them separately from cold inbound leads.
- **Product feedback.** Use the community as a free research panel: polls or open questions when testing a product idea, pricing model or service change. Contributors feel invested in the outcome and are more likely to buy.
- **Brand advocacy.** Active members recommend the brand in other WhatsApp groups and Facebook Groups, the highest-value earned media in East African markets. Capture referral source on new enquiries and attribute community-sourced referrals explicitly.
- **Paid community tier.** For clients with strong brand equity, add a paid tier with exclusive benefits: early product access, private Q&A sessions, premium downloadable resources, on Circle.so or Mighty Networks. Price in UGX at a level the target member can afford; the source suggests testing at UGX 20,000–50,000 per month before scaling (a test range, not a benchmark).
- **Community-to-sales funnel.** Map the journey: New member → Engaged member (posts or comments within 30 days) → Warm enquiry → Customer → Advocate. Track and report monthly how many sales originate from community membership. No demonstrable conversion pathway within six months triggers a strategy review.

### 5. Apply East African operating defaults

- **Platform priority.** The source calibration treats Facebook Groups as the dominant format for scale in Uganda (check against register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026 and the client's data) and WhatsApp groups and Communities as the preferred format for high-trust, high-frequency communication with smaller audiences. Do not default to LinkedIn unless the audience is explicitly professional and formally employed.
- **Language.** Large East African communities naturally mix English and Luganda, or English and Swahili. Do not suppress it: mixed-language posts signal authenticity and belonging, and correcting members' register damages trust. Brand posts keep the tone set by the purpose statement; member language is member-led.
- **Mobile-first design.** Most members in Uganda use Facebook and WhatsApp only on mobile: no PDF attachments (use typed posts or images with embedded text); short paragraphs with line breaks between them; videos under 60 seconds and subtitled; voice notes used sparingly in WhatsApp communities, since some members cannot play audio in public.
- **Religious and cultural sensitivity.** Uganda is religiously diverse. House rules must explicitly prohibit discrimination based on religion, ethnicity and gender. Avoid scheduling major events or promotions on Sundays (Christian observance) or Friday afternoons (Muslim Jumu'ah prayer). Acknowledge national holidays and mourning periods; promotional content during a national tragedy is a reputational risk.
- **WhatsApp administration.** Appoint at least two admins for any WhatsApp group or Community; never rely on one. Put the admin response commitment in writing before launch (for example "Admins respond within 4 hours between 7am and 9pm"). Admin burnout is, in the source's experience, the main reason WhatsApp communities fail in East African markets.

## Release checklist

1. The purpose statement is member-centric, specific and clearly distinct from a brand audience or broadcast channel.
2. The platform recommendation is justified against the selection table, with moderation load weighed against stated capacity.
3. All five design elements (purpose, member profile, value proposition, house rules, content rhythm) are produced and tailored to the client.
4. The launch sequence is week by week, actionable and includes the founding-member strategy.
5. Business value is mapped with at least three monetisation or integration pathways and a defined conversion funnel.
6. A complete house-rules template is included, adapted to the client's community.
7. East African platform defaults, language mixing, mobile-first design and cultural sensitivity are applied throughout.
8. Platform limits, monetisation features and Facebook availability are verified or marked "verify before stating".
