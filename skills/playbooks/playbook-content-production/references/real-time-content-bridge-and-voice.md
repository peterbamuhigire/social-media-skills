# Real-Time Content Bridge and Human Voice Contract

Read this reference when producing timely content (news, trend, customer question, event) or when a team of contributors must sound like one recognisable, human brand. It is concept guidance; current platform rules and metrics come from the source register or a current primary source.

## 1. The real-time bridge

A real-time social operation is a human handoff system. People discover, question, discuss, compare, click, ask for help and report back across several surfaces. Search, social, content, community and customer response share one purpose, evidence boundary and handoff model.

**Sequence for a timely opportunity:**

`listen → frame the human job → verify → draft the native asset → approve → publish/respond → route to the canonical destination → moderate → measure → learn`

**Practices to keep:**

- Define audience, purpose, voice, destination, approval boundary and business outcome before choosing a post or platform.
- Keep an evergreen/timely portfolio; each item carries source, owner, expiry or review date, rights status, moderation route and canonical destination.
- Adapt one verified idea natively to each channel. Do not cross-post mechanically or turn communities into link-distribution lists.
- Treat comments, questions and community norms as research and service inputs, not only engagement counts.
- Route social, SEO, content, paid, PR, sales, support, legal/compliance and analytics through explicit handoffs.

**Stop and roll back** when evidence is contradictory, the audience job is unclear, or a material claim is unverified. Guardrails: no fabricated proof, no privacy or rights breach, no seeded or manufactured mentions, no spam or link-dropping, no accessibility or cultural regression.

## 2. Human voice contract

Accessible language should still have a point of view. Voice shows in diction, syntax, rhythm, mood, tone and the values made visible by what is said and left out.

1. Write a short voice contract with examples of warmth, directness, humour, local specificity and prohibited claims.
2. Preserve each contributor's distinct perspective while editing for clarity, factuality, inclusion, accessibility and channel fit.
3. Use workshops and peer review to expose blind spots, not to flatten every post into a template or an AI-generated average.
4. Leave room for honest uncertainty, correction, disagreement and escalation.
