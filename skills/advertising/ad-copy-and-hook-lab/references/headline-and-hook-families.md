# Headline and Hook Mechanisms

When to read: every time a headline bank, video hook set or primary-text opening is needed. The lines are organised by the job the opening does for the buyer, not by any book's list. Templates and examples are original and localised; where a technique is widely associated with a named practitioner, a short credit is given.

## 1. The 60-minute headline sprint

1. (10 min) Gather the raw material: the buyer's main outcome, the non-obvious outcome that matters most to them (for a school-fees product it may be "no more borrowing in January"), their main worry, anything genuinely new, the strongest verified number, and three phrases customers actually use.
2. (30 min) Write at least five lines for each mechanism in section 2.
3. (10 min) Screen every line with section 3.
4. (10 min) Shortlist five and send the top three per test cell to `ad-testing-and-scaling`. A pun or clever line never ships untested against a plain benefit version.

## 2. Mechanisms, with original slot templates and examples

| Mechanism (what the opening does) | Slot template | Original example (Uganda/Kenya) |
|---|---|---|
| Delivers real news | "From [date], [audience] in [place] can [new capability]." | "From March, Mbarara clinics can take bookings on WhatsApp after hours." |
| Promises a method | "How [audience] [reach outcome] without [cost or pain]." | "How Kikuubo wholesalers restock without a trip to Nairobi." |
| Asks the reader's own question | "Is your [process] costing you [loss] every [period]?" | "Is your salon losing Saturday bookings to no-shows?" |
| Calls out a specific group | "[Trade] in [town]: [outcome] before [season]." | "Coffee exporters in Bugisu: certify before the October harvest." |
| Flags a risk before a decision | "Before you [sign/spend on] [category], check these [n] things." | "Before you sign for a boda loan, check these three clauses." |
| Gives reasons | "[n] reasons [audience] now choose [approach]." | "Four reasons Entebbe hotels now take mobile-money deposits." |
| Lets a customer speak | "'[Specific result in their words],' — [name], [role], [town]." (consented, real) | "'We filled Term 1 by December,' — head teacher, Mukono." |
| Tells a first-person story | "I spent [time] fixing [problem] the slow way. Here is the fast way." | "I spent two years chasing unpaid invoices. Here is what finally worked." |
| States the offer and its real limit | "[Offer] for the first [n] [audience] who [act] by [date], because [true reason]." | "Free installation for the first 20 Kira homes this month — our team has 20 slots." |
| Concedes a weakness | "We are not the cheapest [category] in [city]. Here is what the price covers." | "We are not the cheapest caterer in Kampala. Here is what the price covers." |
| Reveals the hidden outcome | "[Product] that also [valued side effect]." | "The solar kit that also ends the Friday generator run." |
| Contrasts wrong and right | "The costly way and the sensible way to [task] in [country]." | "The costly way and the sensible way to import a used car into Kenya." |
| Instructs | "Stop [habit]. Start [better habit] this week." | "Stop printing price lists. Send one WhatsApp catalogue instead." |
| Opens a loop (test against a specific version) | "Which of these [n] [habits] is quietly [cost]?" | "Which of these five stock habits is quietly eating your margin?" |

Formulas prompt thinking; they do not replace an original idea (a caution often credited to John Caples).

## 3. Screening questions

- What is the strongest outcome and the strongest offer here?
- Is it about the buyer, or about us?
- Could a competitor paste their name on it unchanged? If yes, add something only this client can say.
- Would it still pull if it ran alone as a one-line classified with only a way to respond? (a test associated with Dan Kennedy)
- Is it plain enough for the niche? Language that looks dull to a generalist can be exactly what a specialist buyer searches for — for example "Cold-room repairs within 24 hours, Nakawa" will stop a dairy manager scrolling. Judge dullness from the buyer's chair.
- Does the visual complete the line rather than repeat it? Put the product inside a compelling line, not as the whole line.

## 4. Short-video and feed hooks

Carry the mechanism into the first line of primary text, the first seconds of video and the cover text. Platform behaviour (length limits, safe zones, text treatment) is a check against current specifications (register AD-05, AD-08, checked 2026-09-23).

| Principle | Hook practice |
|---|---|
| The opening decides whether the rest is seen | Lead with the mechanism; no brand intro before it |
| One offer per ad set | Do not mix offers in one creative |
| Proof early | Consented testimonial clip, genuine review rating or verified result in the first frame |
| Specific beats general | Numbers only from verified data |
| Length follows temperature | Longer text or video for warm audiences; short for cold — test it |
| Customers selling to customers | Consented client or creator testimonials; disclose paid relationships (AD-09, checked 2026-09-23) |

Hook openers to test: "[Audience] in [place]: [verified surprising fact]." · "The [n]-second fix for [pain]." · "If you [situation], watch this before [decision]." · "[Customer] was [before]. Then [mechanism]." · "Three signs your [asset] is [problem]."

## 5. Borrowing structure honestly

Learn from strong ads by studying their structure, never copying their words: keep the underlying move (for example a warning with a reason), write your own benefit, name the villain your own buyer actually faces, and make the promise one you can prove. Keep a headline bank organised by mechanism with the test result of every line.

## 6. Comparative lines

Compare upward, never downward; prefer a category rival ("the long queue at the bank hall", "another sugary fizzy drink") over a named competitor; let the reader draw the conclusion; any named comparison needs evidence and legal review because advertising codes differ by country (Stutts 2021).
