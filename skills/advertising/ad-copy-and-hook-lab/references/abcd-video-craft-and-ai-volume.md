# ABCD video craft check and AI-assisted creative volume with labelling

Read when writing or reviewing hooks, scripts and end cards for video ads (YouTube, Reels, TikTok, Facebook in-stream, WhatsApp Status), or when AI tools are used to produce many copy or creative variants. This file owns the ABCD check for the engine; `strategy-video-content` points here for organic video.

## 1. YouTube and Google ABCD (platform creative framework)

Google publishes ABCD as creative guidance for effective video ads (register `YOUTUBE-ABCD`, read live 29 Sep 2026). The current page names the four principles Attention, Branding, Connection and Direction; the older 2019 reference guide used Attract, Brand, Connect and Direct. Google states the guidance rests on correlational data, does not guarantee performance and varies by brand, category and region.

| Principle | Engine check on the script or edit | East Africa note |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Does the first second show something unexpected, a person or a tight product shot, and does it hold with sound off? | Most viewing is mobile and muted; open with a face or the product, with burned-in captions. |
| Branding | Is the brand or product clearly shown or said within the first five seconds, then again, using more than one brand cue (pack, logo, colour, sound, character)? | Use the brand's own distinctive assets (see the distinctive-asset audit below), not a generic logo flash. |
| Connection | Does the viewer think or feel something tied to the product: a human story, humour, a simple tangible benefit? | Local language, humour and settings the audience recognises; check the cultural-bias protocol. |
| Direction | Is there one specific, visible and audible call to action that reinforces the main message? | "Tap to chat on WhatsApp", "Dial *xxx#", a mobile-money pay code; urgency only if real. |

Evidence status. Google cites a Google/Kantar study (April 2021, about 11,000 ads) reporting average lifts in short-term sales likelihood and long-term brand contribution for ads that follow ABCD (Vendor). Treat those figures as illustrative platform research, never as a target or a promise to a client.

Use ABCD as a checklist in step 5 of the parent workflow (screening), not as a replacement for the brief. Where ABCD and the brief conflict (for example, a teaser that withholds the brand by design), the brief wins and the choice is recorded.

Branding early depends on assets people already link to the brand. Before relying on a colour or character as the "brand cue", check it in the distinctive-asset audit of [`brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets`](../../../strategy/brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets/SKILL.md) ([distinctive asset audit](../../../strategy/brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets/references/distinctive-asset-audit.md)).

## 2. High creative volume with AI tools, labelled

AI tools make it cheap to produce many headlines, hooks, scripts, voice-overs and image variants. Volume helps only when each variant still carries a testable hypothesis and passes the same screens as human work.

1. Generate from the approved brief and offer block, never from a blank prompt; keep the brand-voice material and claim register in the prompt context (see `brand-voice-ai-training` and `prompt-engineering-library` where used).
2. Screen every AI variant exactly as human copy: pre-flight questions, ethics filter, claim register, `anti-ai-slop`. Delete, do not "fix later", any variant with an invented number, quote, testimonial or deadline.
3. Group variants into test cells by hypothesis (mechanism or angle), not by tool output order; three to five cells beat fifty unstructured ads.
4. Labelling rule (engine rule). Every asset that is AI-generated or materially AI-altered carries an internal provenance label in the copy deck: tool, what was generated or altered, human reviewer and date. Public disclosure follows the platform's current rules and the risk test: realistic synthetic people, voices, scenes or events, and anything in a social-issue, political, health or financial context, are disclosed. Meta's AI labelling rules are in register `META-AI-INFO-LABELS` (owned and verified by its register owner; check the live page before stating a rule to the client). The risk-based disclosure decision itself belongs to [`policy-ai-content-ethics`](../../../policies/policy-ai-content-ethics/SKILL.md).
5. No cloned voice or likeness of a real person without written consent (route to `strategy-video-content` and its AI-avatar reference).
6. Log which variants were AI-assisted in the test register, so later learning can compare AI-assisted and human-originated winners.
