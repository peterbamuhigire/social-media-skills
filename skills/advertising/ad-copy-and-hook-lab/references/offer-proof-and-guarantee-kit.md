# Offer, proof and guarantee kit

When to read: before any copy when the offer is weak or unclear, when proof is missing or placed late, and when designing a guarantee or deadline.

## 1. Offer workshop (run before copy)

1. Immense promise: the single outcome the buyer covets most.
2. Believability ceiling: go right up to it with specifics, then add the proof that makes it credible; never past it.
3. Sweetener: a bonus that is genuinely useful and, ideally, helps recover the price (e.g. a setup session, a template pack, extended support).
4. Risk reversal: choose type, length and wording (section 3).
5. Limited offer or scarcity — only if real, with the reason: a limited offer is a special term for a defined group (first 20 clinics); scarcity is a real capacity or stock cap (25 seats because the venue holds 25).
6. Write the offer as a block of four to six short paragraphs that could stand alone; rest it overnight; peer review.
7. Price-test plan: two price points or two bonus stacks, not both at once.

Ideal-customer triad (Serling): they want it, can afford it and have authority to buy. In family businesses and SMEs the owner often holds the purse; write for the payer, not the curious employee.

## 2. Proof

Placement: put proof as close to the start as possible ("preemptive credibility") so it answers the doubt the promise creates.

Strength order (use several, not one): consented customer testimonials with specific results → independent reviews or ratings → survey or study figures with source → long guarantees → client logos and association (with permission) → demonstrations.

Testimonial specification:
- specific result, timeframe and context;
- from someone like the prospect;
- name, role and town where lawful and consented;
- tagged by the objection it answers (price, trust, "will it work for me?", delivery time) and placed next to that claim;
- written consent covering name, photo and use; the customer reviews the edited version.

Collection routine: at the "thank you" moment, ask for a short quote or review; record consent in the claim register; refresh proof so claims are dated within 12 months.

Customer-sells-customer ads (client voice, with permission and review rights): subdued letter-like tone with a strong result; qualify the prospect; admit a real limitation near the end. Use for B2B credentials and LinkedIn case posts.

## 3. Guarantees and risk reversal

| Type | Use | Wording slot |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional satisfaction | Products and low-risk services | "Use it for [period]. If you're not satisfied, tell us and we refund in full." |
| Process guarantee (preferred for services) | Agency and professional services | "If we miss [milestone] by [date], [consequence, e.g. the month's fee is waived]." |
| Better-than-risk-free | Info products, training | "Keep the [bonus] even if you ask for a refund." |
| Conditional result | Only where results are controllable and lawful | Avoid income or health result promises; check consumer and financial-promotion rules |

Rules: honour every guarantee; state it near the price and near the CTA; name it in plain words; results guarantees on income, health or investment returns are blocked until legal review.

## 4. Price presentation

- State the price wherever the buyer must decide; price timing depends on position — premium offers build value before price, commodity offers lead with price (Wiebe 2011).
- Discounts: show old price, new price and saving together, with the end date and its reason.
- Instalments: mobile-money or bank instalment options are shown with the total cost; check the payment provider's terms.
- Reframe carefully: cost per day or per use only when accurate.

## 5. Reason-why and honesty devices

- Explain any too-good offer and admit that you profit ("we'd rather fill the Tuesday slots at a lower price than leave them empty").
- Damaging admission: concede an obvious limitation before the reader raises it; it raises belief in the rest.
- "Back to earth": after building desire, add a realistic expectation right before the price.

## 6. Two-step lead offers

The ad sells the request (guide, diagnostic, sample, price list), not the product; one benefit; a non-threatening first step (click-to-WhatsApp message, short form); one qualifying question where sales capacity is the bottleneck; follow-up pre-written and sent within minutes of a response; a consent-based three-touch value cadence that stops on request (see the ethics filter and PL-01/PL-02 in the register).
