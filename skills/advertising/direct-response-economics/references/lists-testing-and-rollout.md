# Audiences, Test Cells and Scaling Up

Read when choosing lists or audiences, designing test cells, reading results or deciding how fast to scale. Parent: [direct-response-economics](../SKILL.md). Consent overlay per the currentness register (checked 2026-09-23). Example figures are scenarios.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Break-even orders per thousand | [campaign-pnl-and-break-even.md](campaign-pnl-and-break-even.md) | Build it first; no test can be read without it |
| Where each list or audience came from and its consent record | Data owner | Treat the list as unusable |
| Past response by list and medium | List history (section 6) | Assume break-even response for forecasting |

## 2. Rate the list or audience

| Score | Source | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Own recent buyers, clean and segmented by recency, frequency, value and payment record | Consent or lawful basis recorded; opt-outs honoured |
| 8–9 | Own enquirers from other media | Same |
| 6–7 | Buyers of closely related products, reached through a partner mailing its own opted-in list | The partner sends; no data transfer without a lawful basis |
| 3–5 | Business directory or association lists, addressed by job function | Lawful basis assessed; business contacts only; opt-out in every message |
| 1–2 | Unknown, stale, scraped or "bargain" lists | Reject |

**Data-protection overlay**
- **Uganda:** annual registration with the PDPO; consent to collect personal data (s.7); a data subject may object to direct marketing in writing and the controller must respond within 14 days (s.26). The Act has no ePrivacy-style opt-in rule, but s.7 consent still applies to collection. Do not claim the Act "requires opt-in for marketing" (register PL-01).
- **Kenya:** direct marketing needs consent and a free, simple opt-out; objections are absolute; businesses wholly or mainly in direct marketing must register with the ODPC regardless of size (register PL-02).
- Rwanda and Tanzania direct-marketing provisions are `NOT_ASSESSED` (PL-03, PL-04); check before use.
- WhatsApp business-initiated messages need opt-in and approved templates under platform policy; check the current policy.

Renting or selling the client's list for extra income is not an option without specific, lawful consent.

## 3. Decision rules for testing

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Choosing where to test first | Use the best-matching list; if the offer fails there, it will fail everywhere |
| Sizing a cell | Use the smallest cell whose result can be read with confidence |
| Deciding what to vary | One variable per cell: list, offer, price, creative or format |
| Forecasting a list with no history | Assume break-even response |
| Many new lists are available | Test a few at a time so failures do not sink the project |
| Reading results | Wait for the planned horizon — most replies arrive within weeks, but inserts and catalogues trickle in for months; set the horizon per medium from the client's own history |

Code every cell: WhatsApp keyword, short code, UTM, QR or promo code.

## 4. Scaling up in steps

Move up only when the previous step holds; stop at any step where cost per order or selling cost as a share of sales breaks the P&L.

| Step | Size | Move up when |
|---|---|---|
| First test | Smallest readable cell | Cost per order is below break-even |
| Confirmation | The same size again | The result repeats |
| Wider test | A larger slice | Cost per order holds |
| Large test | A much larger slice | Selling cost share holds |
| Full use | The remaining names or whole audience | Keep monitoring |

## 5. Keep or merge a segment

| Segment | Extra cost to tailor | Best-case extra sales | Keep? |
|---|---|---|---|

Keep a segment only when extra sales clearly exceed the tailoring cost, the segment is reachable and large enough, and it will pay the price.

## 6. List history record

| Date | List/audience | Quantity sent | Undelivered | Orders | Returns % | Refusals / non-payment % | Opt-outs | Use again? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Digital equivalents: undelivered = failed SMS or WhatsApp delivery; non-payment = refused cash on delivery or failed mobile-money collection. Drop sources with unusual refusal or opt-out rates. Fixed groups (a professional body) tire of frequent contact; groups that renew themselves (new graduates, new parents) can be contacted more often.

## 7. How many leads the sales team needs

- Qualified leads per month = salespeople × calls or visits a day × working days × share of calls that need a fresh lead.
- Raw leads = qualified leads ÷ qualification rate.
- Media budget = raw leads × cost per raw lead.

Scenario: 6 salespeople × 3 visits × 22 days = 396 visits; half need a fresh lead → about 200 qualified leads; with 25% of lead-ad enquiries qualifying → about 800 raw leads; at an assumed UGX 8,000 each → UGX 6.4m a month. Compare with radio call-ins, which give fewer but better-qualified leads.
