# Response Campaign Profit Check and Break-Even Line

Read before approving any campaign that asks people to order, book or enquire directly. Parent: [direct-response-economics](../SKILL.md). All figures are scenarios; margins, costs and tax treatment come from the client's accounts with `chwezi-accounting-doctrine`.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Price, unit cost, delivery and handling cost | Client accounts and supplier quotes | Stop; the break-even line cannot be drawn |
| Returns and non-payment history (refused cash on delivery, failed mobile-money collections) | Order records | Use a labelled assumption and test it |
| Media or contact cost per thousand people reached | Rate card or platform estimate | Get a quote before approval |
| Size of the reachable market | Research or client estimate | Mark the realism check `not assessed` |

## 2. Is the offer suited to direct response?

Check before spending:
- **Channel conflict:** is the same item sold cheaper elsewhere?
- **Fulfilment:** is supply dependable; does it need special packing (fragile, perishable); can it reach upcountry buyers reliably (courier, boda, bus parcel)?
- **Payment risk:** cash-on-delivery refusals, mobile-money availability.
- **Growth:** is there something else to sell the buyer next; is it a short-lived fad?
- **Reach:** do lists, audiences or media exist that reach this buyer lawfully?
- **After-sale:** can returned goods be resold; which guarantees must be honoured?
- **Rules:** standards marks, health, financial-promotion or sector rules.

Turn every feature into the benefit the buyer gets; never leave the reader to work it out.

## 3. Decision rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Break-even needs an unrealistic share of the reachable market in year one | Do not launch; change price, cost, offer or market first |
| Selling costs, returns or non-payment are high, or you are renting others' audiences, or the product is new, or the market is small | Require a higher gross margin before approving |
| Selling only to your own customers, costs are low, or you are deliberately building a customer base | A lower margin can be accepted, with the reason recorded |
| Cheap media lowers the break-even line | Check list quality and consent — a cheap line is worthless if the audience will not respond or may not be contacted |
| Tailoring for a segment costs more than it can add | Merge the segment |

## 4. Profit per order and the break-even line

Build the calculation in four blocks.

**Revenue per order:** selling price.

**Direct costs per order:** goods, any royalty or licence, handling, delivery, any free gift (with its own delivery), plus overhead charged as a share of price.

**Risk adjustments:**
- Returns cost per order = return rate × (handling and delivery both ways + refurbishing).
- Non-payment cost per order = non-payment rate × price.
- Profit before returns = price − direct costs − overhead − returns cost − non-payment cost.
- Profit on kept orders = profit before returns × (1 − return rate).
- Resale credit = return rate × goods cost (goods that come back resaleable).

**Result:**
- Net profit per order = profit on kept orders + resale credit.
- Orders per thousand needed to break even = cost per thousand people reached ÷ net profit per order.
- Break-even response rate = orders per thousand ÷ 1,000.

**Scenario — Kampala water-filter kit, price UGX 150,000:** direct costs UGX 60,000 (goods 40,000; handling 5,000; delivery 10,000; spare-cartridge gift 5,000); overhead at 10% UGX 15,000; returns at 5% cost about UGX 1,000 per order; non-payment at 3% about UGX 4,500 → profit before returns UGX 69,500 × 0.95 ≈ UGX 66,000, plus a resale credit of about UGX 2,000 → net profit per order ≈ UGX 68,000. A flyer door-drop at about UGX 1.2m per thousand homes breaks even at ≈ 17.6 orders per thousand (≈ 1.8%). An opted-in SMS at an assumed UGX 35 per message breaks even at about 0.5 orders per thousand — but only an opted-in list can be used.

## 5. Break-even units and minimum price

- Contribution per unit = price − average variable cost.
- Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit.
- Minimum price = average fixed cost + average variable cost + target profit per unit, then adjusted for competition and perceived value.

Scenario: a short course with UGX 21m fixed costs, UGX 200,000 variable cost per learner and a UGX 1,000,000 fee → UGX 800,000 contribution → 27 learners to break even. If about 600 HR officers form the reachable market, that is roughly 4.5% penetration — test whether that is realistic before launch.

## 6. Profit-planning questions for the plan

- Can the target market be found and counted?
- How many units break even, and what share of the market is that?
- What is the profit goal, how many units does it need, and what share of the market?
- Are those shares realistic for year one?

## 8. Sentence patterns

- "At UGX [x] per thousand people reached and UGX [y] net profit per order, the campaign breaks even at [z] orders per thousand ([z/10]%). The test will be judged against this line."
- "Tailoring the offer for [segment] adds about UGX [x]; the best-case extra sales are UGX [y]. Because [y] does not clearly exceed [x], we will merge it with [segment]."
