# Handling Enquiries, Selling After the First Order and Choosing a Response Format

Read when planning how enquiries will be answered, what to offer after a purchase, or which format a response campaign should use. Parent: [direct-response-economics](../SKILL.md). Example figures are scenarios.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Expected enquiry volume and type | Past campaigns or a small test | Assume enquiries will arrive even for an order-only campaign |
| Who answers enquiries and what their time is worth | Sales lead | Cost it before launch (section 3) |
| Price level, list or audience quality, house-list size, budget | Campaign plan and P&L | Default to the low-cost format column (section 5) |

## 2. Prepare for enquiries before launch

Every campaign produces questions, so match the reply to the effort the person made:

| How the person asked | Effort shown | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| One tap, a form or an "info" keyword | Low | A short WhatsApp reply with the price list and one qualifying question |
| A specific written question | Medium | A tailored answer and an offer of a call |
| A phone call or detailed request | High | A senior salesperson replies the same day |

Have quick replies, PDF price lists and catalogue links ready for each tier, and route high-intent chats to senior sales.

## 3. Should salespeople answer enquiries?

Compare what they lose in selling time with what enquiries earn:
- Lost sales value = sales calls given up per day ÷ calls per sale × value per sale.
- Gained sales value = enquiries handled per day ÷ enquiries per sale × value per sale.
- Net = gained − lost − materials cost.

Scenario (UGX): an agent gives up 6 calls a day; at 12 calls per sale and UGX 100,000 a sale that loses 50,000. The agent answers 15 enquiries a day; at 20 enquiries per sale that gains 75,000. Materials at 1,000 each cost 15,000. Net +10,000 per agent per day, so agents handle enquiries. When the numbers are close, test both arrangements.

## 4. Selling after the first order

- **In the parcel:** a leaflet, coupon, price list, small catalogue, sample or short questionnaire. Test by alternating two inserts in orders of the same product.
- **Digital equivalents:** an offer on the order-confirmation page, a post-delivery WhatsApp message to opted-in customers, a receipt message offer where permitted.
- **Reassurance:** a service number, easy returns, a request for suggestions.
- **Keep the house list clean:** add paying customers; flag people who return repeatedly; remove repeat non-payers; record opt-outs at once.
- **Track monthly:** follow-on sales arrive late, so judge them over months, not days.

## 5. Choosing the format

From cheapest to most expensive: small classified or text ads → larger display ads → parcel inserts → a single simple piece (flyer, postcard, one SMS) → a standard pack or multi-message sequence (letter, brochure, order form) → a premium personalised pack with involvement devices → a special dimensional mailing to a few hundred named prospects.

| Choose cheaper formats when | Choose richer formats when |
|---|---|
| The price is low | The price is high |
| Lists are small or untested | Lists are large and proven |
| The product or idea is risky | The product is proven |
| There is no house list | The house list is strong |
| The budget is tight | The budget is adequate |

Agree the main benefit and the budget first; the creative team then picks the format inside those limits.

## 6. Writing the response piece

- Agree the main benefit, supporting selling points and secondary benefits before the brief goes out.
- Keep a control and test each new version against it.
- Put the most important point first and work downwards, so the same copy can be cut to any space.
- Follow a four-move flow: win attention with the main benefit → make it about the reader → show the proof (selling points, evidence, guarantee, value) → ask for the action, make it easy and restate the guarantee.
- Spend nothing just to look good; every shilling must bring more back.
- Apply the [direct-marketing ethics filter](../../../content-writing/references/direct-marketing-ethics-filter.md): real deadlines, real testimonials, lawful lists.
