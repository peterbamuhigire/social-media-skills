# Decision memo and agency governance

Read when a client decision is needed quickly, or when defining how the agency and client work together and how the agency is paid. Parent skill: [advertising-strategy-and-budget](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Six-part decision memo

Method adapted from Kelley and Sheehan (c. 2021–22) *Advertising Management in a Digital Environment*, Routledge. Business decisions run at about 60–80% confidence; "more research" is not a recommendation.

| Part | Content | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Purpose | One sentence: the problem and why the reader must act now | Names the urgency |
| 2. Situation | Three to five facts that matter; research goes in an appendix | No term paper |
| 3. Alternatives | Three markedly different options, one of them hold/monitor | Not variations of one idea |
| 4. Recommendation | Commit, with about three reasons | No "we could possibly consider" |
| 5. Action plan | Money (UGX), people, dates, approvals, the measurement read | Approvable in five minutes |
| 6. Contingency | "If X happens, we do Y within Z days" | Trigger is measurable |

One-page variant: drop parts 3 and 6.

Slot templates:
- Purpose: "This memo recommends how [brand] should respond to [event] in [market] before [date]."
- Situation: "[Competitor] has raised [channel] activity in [region] since [date]; our unaided awareness moved from [a]% to [b]% ([source, date])."
- Options: "A: hold and monitor [metric] for [n] weeks. B: match weight at UGX [x] from [date]. C: test two spend levels in [town 1] and [town 2] and read [metric] by [date]."
- Recommendation: "We recommend option [X] because (1) [risk avoided], (2) [timing], (3) [capacity or financial reason]."
- Action: "Approve UGX [x] by [date]; plan to you by [date]; first flight [date]; tracking read [date]."
- Contingency: "If [trigger], we will [switch/scale/stop] within [days]."

Check any competitor claim that the client wants to answer for substantiation before replying with a comparative claim.

## 2. Account planning

The account planner is the consumer's voice in the agency: insight, brief, pre-test and post-campaign learning. Where the agency has no planner, assign the role explicitly for each account; do not let it fall to whoever writes the brief.

## 3. Agency–client operating rules

- One accountable decision-maker attends the briefing and each key review; work is never "taken upstairs" by a messenger.
- Budget disclosed at the start. If withheld, float an honest range; re-scope or decline.
- The brief is the agreement; changes are written change orders.
- Scope creep is named early by whichever side sees it first.
- Feedback comes from people who have read the brief, with a reason tied to the brief or business.
- Critical news is never delivered by casual group chat; use a documented note, then a call.
- Keep single-client concentration below an agreed share of agency revenue; re-fee when scope grows.

## 4. Compensation models (established practice; no rates stated)

| Model | Basis | Fits | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Media commission | % of media spend | Media-heavy accounts | Incentive to over-spend; disclose the basis |
| Fixed fee / retainer | Agreed monthly fee for defined scope | Ongoing programmes | Scope drift; review quarterly |
| Labour-based | Hours × rate (staff cost + overhead + margin) | Variable workloads | Needs honest time capture |
| Project / output | Price per deliverable or phase | Defined projects | Change orders must be priced |
| Performance / value | Base fee plus bonus against agreed KPIs | Measurable outcomes both sides control | Attribution disputes; the agency cannot control product, price or stock |
| Hybrid | Base retainer + commission or bonus | Mature relationships | Complexity; write the formula down |

Rules:
- Media spend is client money passed through, not agency revenue; say so in the contract.
- Any performance component names the metric, data source, baseline, measurement window and exclusions (operational failures).
- Route tax and invoicing questions (withholding, VAT) to chwezi-accounting-doctrine.

## 5. Agency evaluation (both directions, annually)

| Dimension | Client rates agency on | Agency rates client on |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Insight quality, brief quality | Clarity of objectives, access to data |
| Creative | Effectiveness-scale results | Timely, brief-anchored feedback |
| Media | Delivery vs plan, cost efficiency | Budget stability |
| Service | Responsiveness, proactivity | Decision speed, payment on time |
| Results | Four-level measurement | Operations readiness |

## 6. Agency profitability guard-rails

- Monthly profit and loss per client; a loss-making account means inefficient staffing or inadequate fees.
- Weekly time capture; client-attributable travel is billable where the contract says so.
- Gate new business on margin; cap entertainment; root-cause every write-off.
- An "agency within an agency" pod for one large client needs a signed fee that covers the pod.
