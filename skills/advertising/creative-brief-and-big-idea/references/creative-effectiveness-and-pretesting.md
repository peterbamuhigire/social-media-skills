# Creative effectiveness: emotional or rational, distinctive assets early, and pre-testing

Read when deciding the creative's emotional or rational weight, when checking that the brand shows up early through its own assets, or when a client asks whether to pay for a pre-test. It extends step 8 (test pack) of the parent workflow; the internal concept screen stays in [concept screen and critique](concept-screen-and-critique.md).

## 1. Choosing emotional or rational creative from evidence

Concept input: Binet and Field's analysis of the IPA Effectiveness Databank, *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013; register `IPA-LONG-SHORT-2013`). In the engine's words:

- Rational product and price messages tend to produce a large, fast response that fades quickly; they suit activation aimed at people ready to buy.
- Emotional priming works more slowly and builds over repeated exposure; it is associated with long-term brand effects, lower price sensitivity and profit.
- Most brands need both, integrated; the question is the balance for this objective and time horizon.

Decision table for the brief:

| Evidence in the brief | Lean | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Objective is growth, penetration or pricing power over a year or more; broad audience of future buyers | Emotional, fame-seeking platform idea, with activation variants built from it | The time horizon and how brand effects will be tracked (brand-tracking pointer in `meta-reporting`) |
| Objective is a sale, lead or sign-up this month from people already in the market | Rational: offer, proof, price, how to buy | Why the short horizon is the right one, and what keeps the brand visible meanwhile |
| Both, with one budget | Emotional platform idea that carries rational offer executions ("brand response") | The planned split of effort and why |
| No evidence of horizon or audience | Do not choose; ask the client | Mark the choice `not assessed` |

Do not quote Binet and Field ratios as targets for East African SMEs; the databank is mostly large UK campaigns. Use the direction of the finding, not its numbers, and state that limit to the client.

## 2. Distinctive assets early in the creative

The brand must be recognisable early and without the logo doing all the work: a colour, character, sound, pack, tagline or shape that people already link to the brand.

- Before the concept screen, pull the brand's asset list and its strength rating from the distinctive-asset audit in [`brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets`](../../../strategy/brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets/SKILL.md) ([distinctive asset audit](../../../strategy/brand-strategy-and-distinctive-assets/references/distinctive-asset-audit.md)).
- Idea card addition: name which strong asset appears in the first seconds or first glance of each channel sketch.
- Screen rule: an idea that can only work by dropping or disguising the brand's strongest assets needs a stated reason (a deliberate teaser) or goes back for rework.
- Video executions then pass the ABCD branding check in [ABCD video craft](../../ad-copy-and-hook-lab/references/abcd-video-craft-and-ai-volume.md).

## 3. Pre-testing: when to pay for validated predictive metrics

Pre-testing asks a sample of the audience to react to a finished or near-finished ad and predicts in-market effect from normed metrics.

Example method (Vendor, illustrative only). System1 describes three ratings (register `SYSTEM1-METHODOLOGY`, read live 29 Sep 2026): a Star rating for long-term potential, built from the emotional response viewers choose from a pictorial scale of basic emotions; a Spike rating for short-term impact, combining emotional intensity with branding; and a Fluency rating, the share of viewers who can name the brand by the end. System1 states its ratings are validated against effectiveness data, including IPA Databank cases; those validation claims are the vendor's own and are not verified by this engine.

Other research agencies also sell creative pre-testing with their own normed databases; compare them neutrally on method, norms for the country and category, sample source (online panels are thin in much of Uganda outside Kampala), turnaround and price. No vendor figures are given here; ask each vendor for its validation evidence in writing.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Large TV, radio or YouTube budget; finished film; vendor norms exist for the country and category | A paid pre-test is proportionate; set the action thresholds before results arrive |
| SME budget, social-first, no local norms | Use the SME-feasible test pack in the concept screen (message test, execution test, brand-linkage diagnostic) and an in-market split via `ad-testing-and-scaling` |
| Pre-test and in-market split disagree | Trust the in-market result for this audience; log the disagreement to judge the pre-test's value |
| Client wants the pre-test to pick the most novel idea | Explain that tests favour the familiar; use the pre-test to diagnose and fix, not to choose |

A pre-test score is a prediction, not an outcome. Carry the prediction into the paid test card (see [test rigour](../../ad-testing-and-scaling/references/test-rigour-srm-and-preregistration.md)) and into the post-campaign review.
