# Brief and insight method

When to read: before writing a strategic creative brief, when repairing a brief that produced weak work, or when the team needs an insight rather than a description of the audience.

## 1. Strategic brief template (one page)

| Field | What to write | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Project and problem | "The challenge is not making an ad; it is persuading [audience] who currently believe [perception] to [action]." | A stranger can state the problem in one sentence |
| Business goal | Commercial result with baseline, target and date | SMART: number, place, date |
| Communication goal | What must change in the mind: awareness, belief, consideration | Measurable at the communication level of the measurement architecture |
| Audience | Demographic + mindset archetype: "[Name the mindset]: [who], values [value], currently [behaviour], because [barrier]" | Not "everyone"; one primary segment |
| Current perception | What they think and feel now, in their words | Sourced (interview, review, listening) |
| Misconceptions | False beliefs the work must correct | Each linked to evidence |
| Positioning / value proposition | What differentiates, with reason to believe | Links to the positioning statement |
| Core message | One sentence in the audience's voice: "[Brand truth], so it's right for me." | One idea only |
| Permitted deviation | How far the work may move from current positioning and assets | Agreed with the client |
| Pitfalls to avoid | Mandatory don'ts, regulated-category rules, cultural sensitivities | Checked against the legal/market gate |
| Evaluation criteria and weights | e.g. on-brand 30%, distinctive 25%, practical across touchpoints 20%, timeless 15%, excites the organisation 10% | Weights sum to 100 and are agreed before work starts |
| Mandatories | Logo rules, disclosures, legal lines | Complete list |
| Timeline, reviews, decision-maker | Dates; one named accountable decision-maker present at key reviews | Named person, not a committee |
| Budget | Media, production, and a separate durable-asset line (bespoke type, illustration style, photography library, sound) | Disclosed; if withheld, float an honest range and re-scope |
| KPIs and past learnings | What worked and failed before | Optional but recommended |

Writing rules: assume zero prior knowledge; plain language; no visual solutions in the brief; treat it as a living document and an agency–client agreement, so major changes are renegotiated in writing, never improvised.

### Five-question brief test

1. Single-minded: one message, one audience, one action?
2. Logical: does the story flow from problem to message?
3. Insightful: is there a compelling human or brand truth?
4. Specific: could a competitor sign it? If yes, it fails.
5. Committed: does it take a point of view?

### Brief diagnostic when work misses

Return to the brief and check: was the target clear; the purpose stated; the permitted deviation defined; the evaluation criteria and weights agreed; the decision-maker present? Most "bad creative" traces to one of these gaps.

## 2. Insight discovery method

Definition used in this engine: a revelation about how the audience thinks, feels or behaves in relation to the category that has not yet been articulated and that changes how you see the behaviour. Formula: a truth about people + a truth about the brand.

Procedure:

1. Read the brief twice; extract key phrases; write the one-sentence directive.
2. Answer: who are we talking to; what must happen; why are we doing this; why should the audience act; what is the brand's "why"?
3. Listen: brand, competitor and category mentions; ask why people say what they say, why they prefer competitors, what they think is wrong or right, and what they think of *themselves* when using the category.
4. Field observation: watch the category in use; read reviews; build or refresh personas ([persona discipline](../../../pipeline/03-audience-personas/SKILL.md)).
5. Gap questions: which needs do competitors leave unmet; what are people doing now and next; pain points, barriers, motivations; which problem could the brand solve that it has not yet?
6. Audience research prompts: what do they do; what shapes their experience; what context do they have for the brand; what would an effective and an outstanding solution look like; how do they feel about the brand now and how do we want them to feel?
7. Write the insight as tension: "[Audience] believe [belief], yet [observed behaviour]." Record source, date and sample.

Fixed vs dynamic insight: fixed insights can anchor a brand for years (keep them); dynamic insights respond to a shift in the audience's situation (review each campaign).

### Evidence standard

| Evidence type | Acceptable as | Not acceptable as |
|---|---|---|
| 8–15 depth interviews with target people | Insight hypothesis with quotes | Market-wide statistic |
| Structured survey with stated sample, method and date | Quantified support | Proof of causation |
| Social listening sample | Language bank and tension signal | Consent to reuse named individuals' words |
| Team intuition | Starting hypothesis | Insight |

## 3. Five-stage ideation process

Adapted by Landa from Wallas (1926) and Young (1940):

1. Preparation: define the problem, gather information, hunt for the insight.
2. Initial ideation: analyse and synthesise research; first round of ideas.
3. Incubation: step away; schedule it in the plan (practitioners ask for more than 48 hours).
4. Illumination: a second, fresher round; aim for substantially different ideas.
5. Verification: does each idea carry a benefit, answer an aspiration, feel fresh, fit the brand's larger narrative, and invite sharing or engagement?

Idea starters: observe everyday behaviour; overstate a grain of truth to an absurd extreme; bring data to life physically; combine unrelated things; turn a weakness into the selling point; multisensory thinking; "what else can I do with this?"; turn a convention on its head; ambient use of the built environment; spot "that's a thing" trends; aspiration lists.

## 4. Brand story spine

| Element | Options | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Human driver | survival, pleasure, safety, community, love, self-esteem, status, rivalry, influence, convenience, development, exhilaration | Which driver does the product genuinely serve? |
| Brand role | alchemist, catalyst, coach, entertainer, guardian, guide, helper, reliever | Does the role match how the audience sees itself? (A supermarket cast as "caregiver" offended mothers who saw themselves as the caregivers.) |
| Plot | overcoming the monster, rags to riches, quest, voyage and return, rebirth | Does the plot fit real brand actions? |

East Africa: guardian and guide roles suit fintech, insurance and SACCOs where trust deficits are high; rags-to-riches and quest plots resonate (enterprise, diaspora return); never cast the brand as the saviour of a community.
