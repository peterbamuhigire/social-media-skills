# Economics and incrementality

Read when setting what the business can afford per result or when proving that advertising caused a change. Parent: [advertising-attribution-and-measurement](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Acquisition budget waterfall (allowable cost per lead)

Method adapted from Hunter and Tietyen (1997) *Business-to-Business Marketing: Creating a Community of Customers*, NTC Business Books.

1. Estimate lifetime profit per new customer from look-alike current customers.
2. Decide the share to invest in acquisition (agree with the owner and finance).
3. Subtract the cost of closing (sales cost per meeting × meetings per close).
4. Multiply the remainder by the qualified-lead-to-customer rate → maximum cost per qualified lead.
5. Multiply by the raw-to-qualified rate → maximum cost per raw lead.
6. Subtract qualification cost → maximum media cost per enquiry.

Illustrative (agency new-client acquisition, UGX): lifetime contribution 20m × 40% = 8m; three pitch meetings × 0.5m = 1.5m → 6.5m; × 33% → 2.15m per qualified lead; × 25% → 536k per enquiry; − 50k qualification call → about 486k maximum media cost per raw enquiry.

## 2. Break-even and allowable figures

| Figure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Break-even ROAS | 1 ÷ gross margin |
| Allowable first-order CPA | First-order gross margin (break-even) or a share of LTV (approved) |
| Time to customer break-even | CAC ÷ monthly contribution per customer |
| Viral assist | Effective CAC ≈ spend ÷ (paid customers ÷ (1 − K)), K below 1 |

Lifetime value logic (Stutts (2021) *The Undefeated Marketing System*, Lioncrest): judge acquisition on lifetime value and repeat rate, not only first-sale ROAS; staged goals with enabling milestones replace "made-up" multiples.

## 3. Incrementality methods

| Method | Design | Use when | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holdout (audience) | Randomly withhold ads from a share of the target | Platform supports it or you control the list | Contamination; enough volume |
| Geo test | Matched areas: ads on vs off (or high vs low weight) | Offline, WhatsApp or radio sales | Match areas on history; seasonality |
| Platform lift study | Platform randomises exposure and measures lift | Available and eligible for the account | Check current availability and thresholds |
| Time-series read | Compare against a baseline period | No other option | Weakest evidence; label it |
| Marketing-mix model (MMM-lite) | Regression of weekly sales on spend by channel, price, season | Two or more years of weekly data | Needs clean paid, owned and earned series; open-source tools exist — check current versions |

Pre-register: hypothesis, test and control, duration, primary metric, guard-rail metric, line in the sand and action if hit or missed (Croll and Yoskovitz (2013) *Lean Analytics*, O'Reilly).

## 4. Echo (halo) effect

Method from Stockwell and Shaw (1994) *Direct Marketing Checklists*, NTC Business Books.

1. Measure a baseline of untraced sales (walk-in, phone, uncoded orders) over a comparable earlier period.
2. During the campaign, measure traced campaign sales and untraced sales.
3. Echo % = (untraced during campaign − baseline) ÷ traced campaign sales.

Illustrative: traced WhatsApp orders 150 a week; untraced baseline 120; untraced during campaign 155 → 35 ÷ 150 ≈ 23%. Report total effect with the seasonality caveat.

## 5. Isolating advertising from operations

Record site uptime, stock-outs, response time and staffing during the flight. An unanswered WhatsApp inbox or a broken checkout invalidates a read of creative or media performance.
