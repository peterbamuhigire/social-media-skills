# Test design and reading

When to read: when writing test cards, sizing a test budget or reading results.

## 1. Test hierarchy (working hypothesis)

Test in this order unless the client's data says otherwise: offer → angle/hook → format and visual → copy length → minor elements. Big swings first: changes that could plausibly move results several-fold (a new offer, a new landing page, a new first frame) before micro-tweaks (Weinberg & Mares 2014). Record the hierarchy as a hypothesis the account's own history confirms or overturns.

## 2. Lines in the sand

Every test card carries three columns before launch:

| Metric | Line | Source of the line | Action if hit | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per qualified lead | ≤ UGX [x] | Business-model maths (allowable CPA) / client's own baseline / self-derived trend | Extend | Stop and revise offer |

Sources of a line (Croll & Yoskovitz 2013): business-model maths (what the model needs), an external baseline with source and year, or a self-derived asymptote (plot daily results of an optimisation effort and read where the trend flattens). Move a line only with segment-level evidence; never lower it to pass.

## 3. Test budget sizing

- Use a capped share of the campaign's media for testing; the rule-of-thumb band (e.g. 5–15%) is a heuristic to agree with the finance owner, not a benchmark.
- Size by the number of results needed to decide, not by a calendar. If the budget cannot buy enough results to read a difference, test bigger differences or fewer cells.
- Duration covers at least one full weekly cycle where behaviour varies by day.

## 4. Reading results

1. Confirm tracking integrity (no double-counting, no gaps).
2. Compare each cell with its line and the control.
3. Look at segments (age, gender, district, placement, device) for strong pockets and costly slices.
4. Look downstream: lead quality, sales conversion, refund or no-show rate.
5. Check brand linkage for awareness tests (unprompted recall of who the ad was from).
6. Check whether differences are larger than normal day-to-day variation; route formal significance questions to `meta-testing-framework`.
7. Decide: focus, retest, extend, iterate or drop; record the reason and what would reverse it.

## 5. Old-versus-new package split

When a client has a legacy approach (a thick brochure, a long catalogue, a boosted post habit), run it as the control against the new approach on matched audiences, coded separately (Stutts 2021 describes a brochure-versus-postcard-plus-matched-digital split). Code every arm with unique links, phone numbers or WhatsApp keywords.

## 6. Test register fields

Date, owner, channel, audience, hypothesis, variable, cells, spend, results by cell and segment, downstream quality, decision, learning, next test.
