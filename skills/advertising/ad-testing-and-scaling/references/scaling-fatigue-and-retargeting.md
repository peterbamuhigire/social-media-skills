# Scaling, fatigue and retargeting

When to read: when a test has a winner, when performance is decaying, or when designing retargeting and re-engagement pools.

## 1. Scaling steps

1. Confirm the winner holds for the agreed window and downstream quality is acceptable.
2. Retest at the same size if the evidence is thin.
3. Extend: raise budget or audience in steps; re-read cost per result and frequency after each step. Platform behaviour when budgets change (re-entering a learning period, delivery instability) is checked on the live help centre with the date; do not rely on remembered percentages.
4. Balance: much larger budget or additional markets; add segment-specific creative where segments differ.
5. Roll out: full budget or market; keep a small holdout where measurement allows (see `advertising-attribution-and-measurement`).
6. If cost per result breaks the line after a step, return to the last stable level and diagnose before trying again.

Horizontal scaling (new audiences, placements, markets, formats) often holds cost better than vertical scaling (more money into the same ad set); test both.

## 2. Fatigue and refresh

Signals: frequency rising, click-through or hook-rate falling, cost per result rising, comments repeating ("seen this"). Actions in order: rotate a prepared variant from the same angle; introduce a new angle from the matrix; widen or rotate audience; rest the ad and return later. Keep a refresh calendar set by the client's own decay pattern, faster in small markets.

## 3. Retargeting-pool funnel (Stutts 2021, adapted)

| Stage | Who | Creative job |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Whole target market | Emotional, data-backed creative until the brand is recognised |
| Discovery | Anyone who clicks, watches deeply or messages | Enter the pool; different ads, more depth |
| Nurture | Pool members | Proof, demonstrations, answers to objections; drop people who don't re-engage within the pool window you set and test |
| Engagement | Visitors to the destination | Landing page must match every value in the ads |
| Conversion rescue | Abandoners (cart, form, chat) | Real testimonials, answer the likely objection, a genuine small incentive only if real |
| Retention | Customers | Thank-you, onboarding, values content; exclude from acquisition ads |
| Advocacy | Happy customers | Referral and review asks with consent |

The first "conversion" is a click or deep view into the pool, not a sale. Stop spending on people who have seen the ad many times and never engaged.

## 4. Pool types and consent

| Pool | Source | Consent / notice |
|---|---|---|
| Website visitors | Pixel or conversions API | Privacy notice disclosing tracking; EEA/UK/CH consent via CMP and Consent Mode v2 where Google tags are used (CW-10) |
| Video viewers, engagers | Platform-native | Platform terms |
| Lead-form openers | Platform-native | Form privacy notice |
| Customer lists (email, phone) | CRM | Lawful basis; Uganda s.7 consent for collection and s.26 objection right (PL-01); Kenya consent plus free simple opt-out, objections absolute (PL-02) |
| WhatsApp / SMS re-engagement | Opted-in contacts | Business-initiated messaging templates and opt-in per WhatsApp Business policy (check current policy); opt-out honoured |

## 5. Comparative ("comparatising") ads — rules

1. Comparison, not annihilation; no disparaging factual claim you cannot prove.
2. Punch up, never down.
3. Pick the "rival" from research — often a category or behaviour rather than a named brand.
4. Let the reader reach the conclusion ("that's right").
5. Test comparative against positive ads; do not assume either wins.
6. Named competitors: legal review and evidence file; advertising codes and competition law vary by country and sector.

## 6. Roll-out across markets

When moving a winner from Kampala to Nairobi or from urban to upcountry: re-check language and transcreation, payment and delivery promises, platform reach (DataReportal ad-reach figures are not user counts; Uganda Meta reach was measured while Facebook was blocked — use as a floor, MK-02), and local legal gates. Run a small confirmatory test in the new market before full roll-out.
