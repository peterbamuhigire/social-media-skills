# Organic-to-Paid Amplification

Merged from skills/strategy/strategy-organic-paid-hybrid on 2026-09-29 at 8eacccb; preservation map: [strategy-organic-paid-hybrid.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/strategy-organic-paid-hybrid.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when a client's Facebook or Instagram Page "feels quieter", when the team boosts posts without a rule, or when a small monthly boost budget needs a disciplined way to decide which organic posts earn paid support. It treats organic publishing as the test cell and a boost as the scale step: the same logic as the SKILL.md test cards and roll-out ladder, applied to Page posts rather than built ads.

The deliverable is a strategic framework document: the organic reach explanation, the organic-then-amplify workflow with thresholds, the WhatsApp owned-audience plan, the post decision tree, the boost budget band and the client expectation scripts. It stays out of paid campaign structure (owned by [09-campaign-strategy](../../../pipeline/09-campaign-strategy/SKILL.md)), graphic design direction and video production.

## Inputs

Ask for the following before producing the document:

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name and industry | For example "Kampala Coffee Roasters — retail food and beverage" |
| Country / city | Default Uganda / Kampala |
| Primary business goal | What the client is ultimately after: sales, leads, awareness or community growth |
| Current Facebook Page stats | Follower count, average organic post reach, average engagement rate, and how long the Page has been active |
| Current paid spend | Monthly UGX spent on Facebook or Instagram boosts or ads, or zero |
| Available boost budget | What the client is willing or able to spend per month on paid amplification, in UGX |
| Content production capacity | Posts per week and who creates content (in-house, agency, freelancer) |
| WhatsApp Business number | To assess the owned-channel opportunity |
| Top 3 performing posts of all time | Screenshots or links of the highest-reach or highest-engagement posts from Page Insights |

If Page stats or the top posts are missing, deliver the framework with thresholds marked provisional and the boost decisions `not assessed`.

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| A new post has just gone live | Do not boost at publishing; let it gather organic data first | Paying to test content the audience has not yet judged |
| Engagement rate ≥ 3 % within 4 hours, ≥ 5 % within 6 hours, 3+ unprompted shares within 4 hours, or buying-signal comments | Treat as a boost candidate (any one primary criterion is enough) | Boosting on instinct or seniority |
| Candidate has a commercial CTA (product, offer, lead capture) | Boost at UGX 50,000–200,000 depending on priority | Paid reach that produces vanity metrics |
| Strong engagement but no commercial CTA | Leave it organic; note the format for future CTA use | Spending on posts that cannot convert |
| Post is under 2 hours old and below 3 % | Wait until the 4-hour mark before deciding | Retiring a post that had not yet had its window |
| Engagement 1–3 % after the window | Leave organic, do not boost, log the format result | Boosting weak content into relevance |
| Engagement below 1 % after 6 hours | Retire the format; do not repeat it | Repeating formats the audience has rejected |
| Comments are mostly negative | Do not boost; route to community or crisis handling | Amplifying a reputational problem |
| Monthly boost budget below UGX 200,000 | Advise that paid amplification results will be unreliable; build the WhatsApp audience and organic base first | Drawing conclusions from 1–2 boosts |
| Follower base above 50,000 | Graduate from boosting to a formal Meta Ads campaign strategy via [09-campaign-strategy](../../../pipeline/09-campaign-strategy/SKILL.md) | Running a large account on ad-hoc boosts |

## Procedure

### 1. Explain the organic reach decline (context, not blame)

Present this as a market condition, never as a client failure. Facebook organic reach for business Pages has fallen sharply over roughly a decade. The trajectory the source carried:

| Year | Approximate organic reach of a Page's follower base |
|---|---|
| 2012 | About 16 % (Edgerank Checker, cited in Chaffey, 2024) |
| 2014 | About 6 %, after the first major update favouring friends-and-family content |
| 2018 | About 2 % for large Pages; small and mid-size Pages 5–8 % depending on engagement history |
| 2024–2026 | Stabilised at 1–4 % for most business Pages in sub-Saharan Africa; higher variance for Pages investing in video and Stories |

Instagram moved from a chronological feed to an engagement-ranked feed in 2016; organic reach for business accounts now ranges from 3–8 % depending on account size and content type. These percentages are time-sensitive platform benchmarks: verify before stating (no register record), and replace them with the client's own Page Insights reach wherever that exists.

State plainly that this is a permanent structural shift, not a temporary quirk. Meta (owner of Facebook and Instagram) sells reach to businesses, and the shift served two ends at once:

1. **User experience:** a feed of friends, family and groups raises daily active use and time on platform, which justify Meta's advertising rates.
2. **Revenue:** when organic reach falls, businesses pay to stay visible, turning free distribution into a paid product.

Because the incentive is permanent, no update will restore 2012-level free reach; a strategy that relies on organic reach alone is built on a declining asset.

Uganda note: Facebook was blocked in Uganda from January 2021 and access was reported restored on 13 Jun 2026 without an official UCC statement. Treat Facebook's status in Uganda as unstable and verify access on the campaign date (register UG-FACEBOOK-ACCESS-2026).

### 2. Set the strategic response: organic-then-amplify

Organic publishing is the testing mechanism; paid spend is a precision amplifier. Paid does not substitute for good content, and organic is not the primary distribution channel. The one-line principle for every post: **prove it organically; amplify what wins.**

- **POEM (Paid / Owned / Earned):** owned content quality is validated before paid spend is committed.
- **10-4-1 rule (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012):** of every 15 posts in a cycle, 10 share third-party value content, 4 share original owned content and 1 is promotional. Only the promotional post and the highest-performing owned posts are amplification candidates.

Why it fits the East African market:

- **Budget efficiency:** Ugandan SME marketing budgets typically run UGX 500,000–3,000,000 a month. Boosting every post is wasteful; boosting proven content keeps budget for posts with shown commercial potential.
- **Algorithm reinforcement:** posts with strong early organic engagement already receive preferential distribution before paid support starts, so boosting a post with momentum compounds.
- **Client confidence:** when the client's own engagement data drives the boost decision, the client trusts the spend and loses the "we are just guessing" view of paid advertising.

### 3. Run the workflow each content cycle (weekly or fortnightly)

**Step 1 — Publish organically.** Post to the Facebook Page or Instagram account with no paid spend attached, at peak hours for the target audience. The source's Uganda windows (EAT): Facebook Tuesday–Thursday 12:00–14:00 and 19:00–21:00; Instagram Wednesday and Friday 11:00–13:00 and 20:00–22:00. Replace these with the client's own activity data using [posting-time and frequency tests](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-algorithm-guide/references/posting-time-and-frequency-tests.md). Do not boost at publishing.

**Step 2 — Monitor the early engagement window.** Check at 2, 4 and 6 hours after publishing in Page Insights or Meta Business Suite. Record:

| Metric | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Organic reach | Page Insights → Posts → Reach |
| Engagement rate | (Reactions + Comments + Shares) ÷ Organic reach × 100 |
| Saves (Instagram) | Post Insights → Saves |
| Link clicks (if applicable) | Page Insights → Posts → Link Clicks |
| Comment quality | Manual review: substantive, or emoji-only? |

Menu paths are platform interface claims: verify at use (no register record).

**Step 3 — Apply the amplification threshold.** A post must meet at least one primary criterion.

| Criterion type | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Primary (any one triggers a boost decision) | Engagement rate ≥ 3 % within 4 hours of posting |
| Primary | Engagement rate ≥ 5 % within 6 hours of posting |
| Primary | 3 or more unprompted shares within the first 4 hours |
| Primary | Comments include buying signals: enquiries, pricing requests, "how do I order?" |
| Supporting (strengthens, never triggers alone) | Video format with watch-through rate above 30 % |
| Supporting | Post directly features a product, service or limited-time offer |
| Supporting | Post is tied to a campaign objective with a defined conversion deadline |
| Do not boost | Engagement rate below 1 % after 6 hours: the content has not resonated |
| Do not boost | Comments predominantly negative: amplifying crisis content speeds reputational damage |
| Do not boost | No clear call to action: paid reach without a CTA produces vanity metrics |

**Step 4 — Set up the boost** (the authorised operator does this after written budget approval).

| Setting | Rule |
|---|---|
| Audience | Start from a saved audience built from Page Insights (demographics of engaged followers); layer interests relevant to the product or service |
| Location | Uganda → Kampala by default; add Entebbe, Jinja, Gulu and Mbarara for national campaigns |
| Budget, standard post | UGX 50,000–100,000 over 3–5 days |
| Budget, high-priority campaign post | UGX 150,000–200,000 over 5–7 days |
| Lifetime budget | Campaign posts with a fixed end date (promotions, events, launches): set a fixed total and end date; Meta paces spend to finish with the campaign |
| Daily budget | Evergreen posts with no fixed end (general awareness, brand content): UGX 15,000–30,000 a day, letting Meta optimise delivery over time |
| Placement, default | Facebook Feed + Instagram Feed for awareness and engagement posts |
| Placement, Facebook Feed only | Posts with heavy text or complex infographics (the source holds that Instagram suppresses text-heavy images; verify before stating, no register record) |
| Placement, Stories | Add only when the creative is vertical (9:16) |

**Step 5 — Review and record.** At boost completion, add to the client's content tracker: total reach (organic + paid), total engagement and final engagement rate, link clicks or WhatsApp messages generated, cost per engagement (total spend ÷ total engagements), and the decision (repeat the format, retire it, or test a variation). Feed the decision into the test register described in [test design and reading](test-design-and-reading.md).

### 4. Build the WhatsApp owned audience in parallel

WhatsApp sits outside the Meta feed algorithm: a Broadcast List or Channel message reaches all opted-in subscribers at near-zero cost, which makes it the most cost-efficient distribution channel in the region. The source stated that WhatsApp penetration exceeds 90 % of smartphone users; the register marks that wording unsupported (Pew 2023 median 73 % of adults across eight middle-income countries; Yazi estimates about 95 % of internet users in Kenya and Uganda, about 80 % in Tanzania and Rwanda). Say "WhatsApp is the dominant messaging channel" and use the client's own audience data (register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026).

- Every Facebook and Instagram post that features a product, offer or announcement carries a prompt to join the WhatsApp Broadcast List or Channel, with a direct link (wa.me/256XXXXXXXXX).
- Transition line for posts: "Want to be the first to hear about new arrivals and exclusive offers? Join our WhatsApp list — link in the first comment."
- Broadcast list membership requires opt-in and honoured opt-outs (see the SKILL.md East Africa notes, PL-01/PL-02).

| WhatsApp surface | Use for | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Broadcasts | Time-sensitive offers, product launches, event announcements | Under 200 words; one clear CTA with a link or phone number |
| Channels | Content-led value: tips, how-to guides, behind the scenes | Builds affinity without the transactional feel of broadcasts |
| Status | Short updates, product photos, behind the scenes, daily | Views come from existing contacts: retention, not acquisition |

The goal is to convert social followers into an owned WhatsApp audience the algorithm cannot restrict; once a contact opts in, the client controls that channel.

### 5. Prioritise formats that hold organic reach

| Format | Why it outperforms | East Africa notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short video (Reels / Facebook video) | Meta's ranking actively favours video to compete with TikTok and YouTube | Under 60 seconds; subtitles, because most EA users watch without sound on mobile data |
| Stories | Outside the main feed ranking; shown in a dedicated strip with near-complete delivery to active followers | 3–5 Stories a day; use polls and questions |
| Polls and question posts | Votes and answers count as high-value interactions | Ask about audience opinions, not purchase intent: "Which do you prefer?", not "Would you buy this?" |
| Carousels (Instagram) | Each swipe counts as an engagement signal; more time on post | How-to, before/after, multi-product showcases |
| Live video | Facebook Live is prioritised in feed during broadcast; followers are notified | Q&A sessions, launches, tutorials; announce 24 hours ahead via WhatsApp |
| User-generated content | Genuine customer posts shared with permission carry social proof | Tag the original poster, add a brief caption, do not edit the UGC |

Ranking behaviours in this table are platform claims: verify at use (no register record); [meta-algorithm-guide](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-algorithm-guide/SKILL.md) holds the current ranking-signal reference.

### 6. Classify every post with the decision tree

```
Is the post driving strong early engagement (≥3% within 4 hours)?
├── YES → Does it have a commercial CTA (product, offer, lead capture)?
│         ├── YES → BOOST IT (UGX 50,000–200,000 depending on priority)
│         └── NO  → Let it run organically; note the format for future CTA integration
└── NO  → Is it under 2 hours old?
          ├── YES → Wait until 4-hour mark before deciding
          └── NO  → Is the engagement rate 1–3%?
                    ├── YES → Leave organic; do not boost; log format performance
                    └── NO  → Under 1% engagement after 6 hours → RETIRE the format; do not repeat
```

Review retired formats quarterly. A format that consistently underperforms leaves the content mix; it is not boosted into relevance.

### 7. Propose the monthly boost budget

Cross-check against [meta-budget-planner](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-budget-planner/SKILL.md) for the full channel allocation.

- **Minimum viable boost budget:** UGX 200,000 a month (about USD 54 at the source's 2026 rate; recompute at the day's rate before quoting). It supports 2–4 boosts a month at UGX 50,000–100,000 each; below it, paid amplification results are statistically unreliable.

| Follower base | Recommended monthly boost budget | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Under 2,000 | UGX 200,000–400,000 | Focus on audience building; boost 2–3 high-engagement posts |
| 2,000–10,000 | UGX 400,000–800,000 | Enough to amplify 4–6 posts a month; adds lead-generation campaigns |
| 10,000–50,000 | UGX 800,000–2,000,000 | Sustains reach for a larger base; test multiple audiences per boost |
| Over 50,000 | UGX 2,000,000+ | Graduate to a formal Meta Ads campaign strategy via 09-campaign-strategy |

### 8. Set client expectations

Aim for clarity without blame and confidence without over-promising. Use or adapt:

> **Opening framing:** "The reason your Facebook page feels quieter than it used to is not anything your team has done wrong. Facebook changed how it distributes content from business Pages about ten years ago, and has continued moving in the same direction every year since. Today, even a well-run Page with 10,000 followers might only reach 200–400 people organically from a single post — that is 2–4%. This is the reality for every business on the platform globally, including multinational brands with full-time social media teams."

> **Introducing paid spend:** "The good news is that Facebook's advertising product is extremely precise and relatively affordable compared to radio, television, or print. Rather than spending your budget broadly, we use the data from your organic posts to identify which content your audience genuinely responds to — and we only put paid support behind those posts. This means your budget works harder because we are amplifying what has already proven itself."

> **Managing ROI expectations:** "On a boost budget of UGX 100,000, you should expect to reach an additional 3,000–8,000 targeted people in Kampala, depending on the audience and creative. Whether that reach converts to enquiries and sales depends on the quality of the content and the clarity of your call to action. We track cost per engagement and cost per lead so you always know what each shilling is producing."

These scripts are the engine's own client wording. The reach figure (3,000–8,000 per UGX 100,000) is an unregistered estimate: present it as a range to be replaced by the client's first boost results.

What not to say:

- Do not promise specific reach or engagement numbers before a boost runs; Meta delivery varies with audience saturation, creative quality and competition.
- Do not call the decline "Facebook being broken"; that frames the platform as unreliable rather than changed.
- Do not suggest that posting more often will recover organic reach; frequency without engagement quality speeds Page suppression.

## Release checklist

- [ ] The organic decline is explained factually and diplomatically, as a market condition, so the client understands why it happened, not only that it did.
- [ ] The workflow is numbered and sequenced with concrete thresholds (engagement rates, time windows, UGX amounts) a consultant or client can apply without ambiguity.
- [ ] Budget figures are in UGX; platform data reflects the East African market; WhatsApp is treated as a first-class channel alongside Facebook and Instagram.
- [ ] The WhatsApp section is substantive and presents owned-audience building as a structural alternative to algorithm-dependent reach.
- [ ] The boost / no-boost / retire tree can be applied by a junior team member without further guidance.
- [ ] The expectation scripts are professional and non-technical, avoiding both alarm and false reassurance.
- [ ] The document points to meta-budget-planner, 09-campaign-strategy and the POEM model where relevant, without duplicating them.
- [ ] Scope holds: no paid campaign structure, design direction or video production.
- [ ] Time-sensitive figures (reach percentages, USD rate, reach per UGX, Facebook status in Uganda) carry their verify note.
