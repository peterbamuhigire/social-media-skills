# Media maths and medium selection

Read when computing delivery or comparing media. Parent: [media-planning](../SKILL.md). Concepts are standard media-planning practice; the medium-classification thresholds and CPIM method follow Stockwell and Shaw (1994) *Direct Marketing Checklists*, NTC Business Books.

## 1. Four-point medium test (every medium must pass)

1. **Reaches the target** — check the audience breakdown, not the title or station name.
2. **Generates the response needed** — responsive, engaged audiences beat passive or controlled distribution for response goals.
3. **Competitors use it persistently** — a competitor staying in a medium is free research; a medium nobody uses may not pay (test before assuming opportunity).
4. **Data is current and accurate** — audited circulation, dated listenership survey, current platform estimate.

## 2. Audience-share classification

| Class | In-target share of the medium's audience | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Above about 50% | Carries most weight |
| Secondary | About 30% or more | Adds reach or a second job |
| Tertiary | Below about 20% | Only when it is the sole route to a segment |

Adjust thresholds when no medium qualifies, and state the adjustment.

## 3. Formulas

| Measure | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | Cost ÷ impressions (or circulation) × 1,000 | Headline efficiency only |
| Target CPM / CPIM | Cost ÷ in-target audience × 1,000 | Use for all comparisons |
| Incremental CPIM | Added cost ÷ added in-target people × 1,000 | When adding a second medium that overlaps the first |
| GRPs | Reach % × average frequency | Sum of ratings across insertions |
| TRPs | Target reach % × target average frequency | Plan in TRPs when the target is narrower than the medium's audience |
| CPP | Cost ÷ rating points | Broadcast buying |
| Reach from GRPs | Not linear: reach rises with diminishing returns as GRPs grow | Use the seller's or platform's reach curve; label estimates |
| CPR / CPO | Cost ÷ (enquiries + orders) / Cost ÷ orders | Known only after running |

Worked comparison (illustrative figures): daily paper half page UGX 4.5m, 60,000 copies, about 20% SME owners → CPM 75,000, CPIM 375,000. Niche SME magazine UGX 1.2m, 5,000 copies, about 80% SME owners → CPM 240,000, CPIM 300,000. The niche title is cheaper per interested reader despite the higher CPM.

## 4. Effective frequency (set per plan)

Established practice debates this: the three-exposure view and later "effective frequency 3+" planning conventions versus recency planning, which values reaching people close to purchase at least once. Decide per plan using:

| Factor | Pushes frequency up | Pushes frequency down |
|---|---|---|
| Message | New, complex, needs demonstration | Simple reminder of a known brand |
| Brand | New or low awareness | Established leader |
| Competition | Heavy clutter from rivals | Quiet category |
| Purchase cycle | Infrequent, considered | Frequent, habitual (reach near purchase matters more) |
| Creative | Low distinctiveness | Highly distinctive, wears in fast |

Write it as: "We assume [n] exposures within [period] because [reasons]; we will check brand linkage at [date] and adjust."

Digital frequency caps and platform frequency behaviour change; check current platform guidance before stating a cap.

## 5. Media objectives per insertion

Give each insertion one primary objective (sell, gather names, test a market, support retail, remind, research) and a response device matched to it. Easier response (WhatsApp keyword, short code, QR) raises response and cost per response.

## 6. Magazines, newspapers, radio, outdoor, digital — planning traits

| Medium | Strength | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Radio (FM, vernacular) | Local reach, frequency, language fit, presenter credibility | Proof of airing; spot logs |
| TV | Demonstration, emotion, broad reach | Production cost; wear-out |
| Newspapers | Time-sensitive offers, older and business readers | Short life |
| Magazines / trade titles | Niche precision, longer life, pass-along | Long lead times |
| Outdoor and transit | Repetition, local presence | Permits; site photographs for proof |
| Social and video platforms | Targeting, response, creative testing | Ad reach ≠ users; consent and privacy rules |
| Search | Harvests existing intent | Only works where demand exists |
| Programmatic display/video | Scale and audience buying across sites via DSPs, SSPs, private marketplaces or open exchange | Brand safety, viewability, fraud; availability and inventory quality in EA must be checked |

## 7. Share of voice

SOV = brand weight (spend or GRPs) ÷ category weight. Build local SOV estimates from media monitoring, the Meta Ad Library (active ads, and for political or social-issue ads spend data; register AD-04, checked 2026-09-23) and observed outdoor presence. Record method and date.
