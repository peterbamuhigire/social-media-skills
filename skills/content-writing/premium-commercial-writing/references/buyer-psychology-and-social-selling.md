# Buyer Psychology And Social Selling

Parent skill: [`premium-commercial-writing`](../SKILL.md).

This original synthesis uses the supplied *The Bezos Letters*, *Yes!*, *Buyology*, and *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* as durable concept inputs. It does not reproduce their text, examples, or statistics. Social content must persuade through usefulness, relevance, proof, and voluntary action; publication, targeting, current platform behaviour, and performance claims require separate verification and authority.

## Social belief sequence

`stop -> recognise -> learn -> trust -> self-qualify -> reply/click/book/buy -> receive promised value`

### Hook and message

- Start with a specific audience moment, tension, result, contrast, or useful question.
- Make the first line earn the next line; do not spend the opening on brand announcements.
- Carry one message and one CTA per post.
- Use a point of view or observation that could not be pasted onto a competitor unchanged.

### Proof and social context

- Use real customer outcomes, demonstrations, process evidence, or named expertise with permission.
- State whether a result is illustrative, historical, expected, or verified.
- Use comments, counts, testimonials, or creator authority only when authentic and relevant; do not imply consensus from a small or unrepresentative sample.
- Build owned relationships through useful replies, DMs, email, or WhatsApp follow-up; do not bait engagement without delivering value.

### Choice and commitment

- Match the CTA to readiness: save/reply, DM/download, book, or buy.
- Ask for a small action that helps the reader evaluate fit, not one that hides a later sales step.
- When presenting packages, show the recommended fit, criteria, exclusions, and price/terms where applicable.
- Use scarcity or deadlines only when capacity or timing is real, visible, and honoured.

### Sensory and memory cues

Repeat a recognisable editorial or visual cue—voice, recurring series, demonstration format, local context, sound, or ritualised handover—so the audience can identify the brand. Test its observable contribution to recall, qualified response, or repeat engagement. Avoid unsupported “brain hack”, subliminal, or guaranteed subconscious claims.

## Channel adaptations

| Channel | Best persuasion job | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram/TikTok | Recognition, demonstration, aspiration, and low-friction response | Visual text remains legible; no deceptive before/after or hidden sponsorship |
| Facebook | Education, community proof, offer explanation, and response | Keep claims, comments, and moderation context visible |
| LinkedIn | Authority, diagnosis, case evidence, and qualified conversation | Avoid borrowed status, inflated credentials, and generic leadership language |
| WhatsApp | Personal follow-up, clarification, order, or booking | Consent, frequency, privacy, and one clear next action |
| X or threads | Point of view, useful contrast, and conversation | Cite evidence for current claims; do not use outrage as a substitute for substance |

## Test and correction loop

Log `changed belief -> audience slice -> format -> CTA -> primary measure -> guardrail -> result -> decision`. Measure qualified actions, not only reach. Guardrails include negative feedback, hides/unfollows, complaints, misleading replies, accessibility, lead quality, and fulfilment capacity. If persuasion improves clicks but harms trust or downstream quality, reject the local win and recover the transparent baseline.

## Release checklist

- [ ] Audience, channel job, message, proof, and CTA are explicit.
- [ ] Claims and examples are sourced, permissioned, or visibly labelled.
- [ ] The post gives value before asking for action.
- [ ] The CTA and destination make the same promise.
- [ ] Persuasion is transparent and culturally respectful.
- [ ] AI output has human review, rights/provenance, and platform-policy checks.
