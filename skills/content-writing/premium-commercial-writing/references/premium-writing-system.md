# Premium Writing System

Use this reference when a draft is accurate but ordinary, vague, too cheap-sounding, too corporate, or not commercially strong enough.

## Core Principle

Premium writing is not fancy writing. It is specific, controlled, useful, and confident.

The reader should feel:

- this was written for someone in my situation
- the author understands the real problem
- the offer is clearly different from cheaper alternatives
- the next step is worth my time

## Message Spine

Build every premium asset around these nine elements:

1. **Reader:** the precise buyer, decision-maker, influencer, or user.
2. **Moment:** what is happening now that makes the message relevant.
3. **Pain or risk:** the cost of staying where they are.
4. **Desired outcome:** what they want to achieve, protect, or be seen as.
5. **Point of view:** the belief, diagnosis, or recommendation the brand stands behind.
6. **Mechanism:** how the offer, method, process, or product creates the outcome.
7. **Proof:** evidence that the mechanism works.
8. **Objection:** the reason they may hesitate.
9. **Next step:** the single action that fits their readiness.

If any of these are missing, the draft will usually sound generic.

## Premium Voice Rules

- Prefer plain authority over grand language.
- Write with a point of view. Avoid neutral summaries when the client is expected to lead.
- Use concrete nouns, strong verbs, named places, real constraints, and visible proof.
- Replace claims about excellence with evidence of standards, process, results, or judgement.
- Use status cues carefully: quality, discretion, access, expertise, risk reduction, time saved, and association.
- Make complexity feel handled, not hidden.

## The Five Editing Passes

### 1. Commercial Clarity

Check:

- What is this piece trying to make the reader think, feel, know, or do?
- Is the audience clear enough to make the writing sharper?
- Does the CTA match buyer readiness?

### 2. Specificity

Replace:

- "high-quality service" with the actual quality controls
- "expert team" with credentials, experience, or process evidence
- "affordable solutions" with value, payment structure, or ROI context
- "tailored approach" with what changes based on the client's situation

### 3. Proof Density

Add at least one form of proof per major claim:

- number or benchmark
- named case result
- testimonial theme
- process evidence
- credential
- third-party source
- before/after comparison
- founder or practitioner experience

### 4. Rhythm And Restraint

Premium writing should feel controlled:

- vary sentence length
- keep paragraphs short
- avoid overexplaining
- cut hype
- use one strong claim instead of three weak claims
- avoid excessive exclamation marks, emojis, and urgency words

### 5. Search And Reuse

Ask:

- Can this idea become a caption, blog section, email, FAQ, or proposal paragraph?
- Is the main answer or argument easy to extract?
- Are key terms present naturally?
- Would an AI search engine understand who the brand serves and why it is credible?

## Red Flags

- The headline could belong to any business in the category.
- The copy relies on "premium", "quality", or "professional" without proof.
- The reader is asked to buy before the writing has created trust.
- The CTA is generic: learn more, get in touch, contact us today.
- The copy uses discounts to create demand for a premium offer.
- The brand talks more about itself than the reader's desired outcome.
- The writing has no named mechanism, no opinion, and no objection handling.

## Premium Rewrite Pattern

Use this pattern when rewriting weak copy:

1. State the reader's recognisable situation.
2. Name the cost of the problem.
3. Give a clear point of view.
4. Show the mechanism or process.
5. Add proof.
6. Reduce the main objection.
7. Ask for one next step.
