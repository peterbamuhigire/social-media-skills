## When to use this reference

Use it when the requested idea set is for a **blog or long-form article programme** rather than a social post batch: 15–25 targeted blog post ideas, each written as a 200-word hybrid summary (narrative brief plus structured specs), saved as a topic list the `blog-writer` skill can draft from. The method adapts to the client and to how much information is available.

For social-platform idea batches (30 ideas plus 5 evergreen series), stay with the main `content-ideas` workflow. For the drafting itself, hand the approved brief to [blog-writer](../../blog-writer/SKILL.md).

## Inputs

### Client documents (mandatory read)

Read every available file to build a complete picture:

1. `docs/en/company-profile.md` (and all enabled language versions)
2. `docs/en/services.md` — service offerings, target customers
3. `docs/en/pages.md` — existing website pages and content
4. `docs/sector-brief.md` — industry context (if present)
5. `docs/style-brief.md` — brand voice and tone
6. `docs/blogs/topics.md` — existing topics (avoid duplicates)
7. `src/pages/en/blog/` — existing articles (avoid overlap)
8. All other `docs/en/` files — testimonials, FAQ, portfolio, about-story

Extract and note:

- what the business does (core services, products);
- who it serves (audience segments, industries, company sizes);
- where it operates (geographic focus, markets);
- what makes it different (competitive advantage, methodology);
- what expertise the author has (experience, credentials, stories);
- what problems customers face (pain points, challenges);
- what content already exists (published articles, covered topics).

### Guided interview (3–5 questions)

After reading the documents, ask targeted questions to fill the gaps. Ask one at a time and skip any question the documents already answer. Core questions (ask what is missing):

1. **Audience specifics** — "Who is your ideal reader? (Job title, company size, industry, location)"
2. **Top pain points** — "What are the top 3 problems your customers face that your business solves?"
3. **Content goals** — "What should readers DO after reading? (Contact you, book a demo, understand a concept?)"
4. **Competitors** — "Name 2–3 competitors. What topics do they cover?"
5. **Unique knowledge** — "What do you know that competitors don't? What's your unfair advantage?"
6. **Customer questions** — "What questions do customers ask most before buying?"
7. **Content gaps** — "Topics you've wanted to write about but haven't?"
8. **Context / audience** — any additional context the user provides (specific themes, campaigns, seasonal needs).

Fold any extra context the user gives (audience details, campaign goals, seasonal focus) into the assessment. When almost no context exists, use the 12-question client questionnaire in [ideation-frameworks.md](ideation-frameworks.md#client-questionnaire-graves).

## Decision rules

### Rule 1 — Score the available information

Score each dimension; the scores decide which ideation methods will work.

| Dimension | Rich (3) | Moderate (2) | Sparse (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Client docs** | Detailed company profile, services, testimonials, stories | Basic company profile and services | Minimal — just a business name and description |
| **Competitor visibility** | Named competitors with active blogs | Competitors named but blogs unknown | No competitor information |
| **Audience specificity** | Named segments with pain points | General audience description | Vague ("businesses") |
| **Industry dynamism** | Active news cycle, regulations, trends | Moderate change rate | Stable / static industry |
| **Existing content** | 5+ published articles to spin off | 1–4 articles | No existing content |
| **Customer interaction** | Direct customer questions available | Some FAQ data | No customer feedback |

### Rule 2 — Select 5–7 methods; Methods 1 and 2 always run

| Method | Best when | Minimum score |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Category Drilldown | Always | — (always include) |
| 2. Buyer Awareness Stages | Always | — (always include) |
| 3. Pain Point Mining | Client docs ≥ 2 or customer interaction ≥ 2 | — |
| 4. Competitor Gap Analysis | Competitor visibility ≥ 2 | Competitor 2+ |
| 5. Customer Question Mapping | Customer interaction ≥ 2 | Customer 2+ |
| 6. They Ask, You Answer | Customer interaction = 3 | Customer 3 |
| 7. Amazon / Review Mining | Product-based business | Client docs 2+ |
| 8. Spin-Off Posts | Existing content ≥ 2 | Content 2+ |
| 9. Media Mashup | Brand voice is informal / creative | Client docs 2+ |
| 10. Highlight Good / Bad | Industry has notable examples | Industry 2+ |
| 11. How-To / Tutorial Mining | Product or service has teachable processes | Client docs 2+ |
| 12. Success / Failure Stories | Client has real project stories | Client docs 3 |
| 13. Holiday / Event Mapping | Content calendar needs seasonal hooks | Any |
| 14. Newsjacking / Trends | Industry dynamism = 3 | Industry 3 |
| 15. Use Any Object | Need creative / lateral ideas | Any (creative fallback) |
| 16. Curated Roundups | Industry has notable resources | Industry 2+ |
| 17. Prediction Posts | Industry dynamism ≥ 2 | Industry 2+ |
| 18. Jargon / Glossary | Technical niche with newcomer audience | Audience 2+ |
| 19. Contrarian / Negative | Audience is sophisticated | Audience 3 |
| 20. Topic-Category Matrix | Need high volume quickly | Any (volume fallback) |

Announce the choice before generating: "Based on available information, I'm using methods: [list]. Here's why: [brief rationale]." Method instructions and examples are in [ideation-frameworks.md](ideation-frameworks.md).

For seasonal hooks (Method 13) in Uganda and East Africa, use the calendar in the main `content-ideas` SKILL.md (Framework 6), not US/UK defaults.

### Rule 3 — Filter every raw idea

Remove any idea that fails one of these tests:

| Filter | Test |
|---|---|
| **High-value goal** | Does it help the reader make or save money, reduce risk, save time, or gain advantage? |
| **Unique angle** | Does it require knowledge that isn't commonly available? |
| **So-what test** | Would the target reader care enough to click? |
| **Longevity** | Will it still be relevant in 12 months? |
| **No overlap** | Not already published or listed in `docs/blogs/topics.md`? |
| **Searchable** | Would someone type it into a search engine? |

### Rule 4 — Balance the three tiers

| Tier | Purpose | Target count |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: SEO drivers** | Attract organic traffic through long-tail keywords | 6–8 ideas |
| **Tier 2: Authority builders** | Establish expertise with deep guides and analysis | 5–7 ideas |
| **Tier 3: Thought leadership** | Build the brand with opinions, predictions, stories | 4–5 ideas |

### Rule 5 — Titles pass the 4 U's test

Score every title on Useful, Unique, Urgent and Ultra-specific (1–4 each). Keep it only if at least 3 dimensions score 3 or more. Generate three headline options per article, each carrying the topic, the unique angle and the audience appeal (see [ideation-frameworks.md](ideation-frameworks.md#three-headline-method-graves)).

### Rule 6 — The user's feedback overrides the assessment

When the user's refinement conflicts with the scoring, follow the user.

## Procedure

1. **Gather context.** Read the client documents and run the guided interview (Inputs above).
2. **Assess available information.** Score the six dimensions (Rule 1).
3. **Select ideation methods.** Pick 5–7 methods from the matrix, always including Methods 1 and 2 (Rule 2), and announce the choice with a rationale.
4. **Generate ideas.** Run the selected methods in sequence. Aim for 25–35 raw ideas, then filter to the best 15–25 (Rule 3) and classify them into tiers (Rule 4). Consult [ideation-frameworks.md](ideation-frameworks.md) for each method.
5. **Write 200-word hybrid summaries.** Use the template below for every approved idea; assign a format from [content-formats.md](content-formats.md).
6. **Present and refine.** Show the ideas grouped by tier with full summaries, then ask:
   - Which ideas excite you most?
   - Any ideas to remove or modify?
   - Any topics you expected but don't see?
   - Any specific campaigns or seasonal needs to address?

   Refine on the feedback (Rule 6).
7. **Save the output.** Save the approved list to `docs/blogs/topics.md` using the file template below. If the file already exists, merge the new ideas — do not overwrite existing topics — and mark previously written topics `[PUBLISHED]`.

## Templates

### 200-word hybrid summary (use this exact format)

```markdown
### [Number]. [Working Title]
[3-4 sentence narrative brief: What this article is about, who it serves,
why it matters now, and the unique angle that makes it worth reading. This
paragraph should make someone want to write — and read — this article. It
captures the creative direction and emotional tone.]

- **Audience:** [specific reader segment — job title, industry, company size]
- **Buyer Stage:** [Awareness / Consideration / Decision]
- **Format:** [How-to / Case study / List / Opinion / Guide / Story / Comparison / Interview / Roundup / FAQ]
- **Angle:** [the specific twist that differentiates from competitors — 1 sentence]
- **Key Points:**
  1. [what the article must cover — specific enough to outline from]
  2. [second key point]
  3. [third key point]
  4. [fourth key point — optional]
  5. [fifth key point — optional]
- **CTA Goal:** [what action the reader should take after reading]
- **SEO Keywords:** [primary keyword], [secondary keyword]
- **Tier:** [1: SEO driver / 2: Authority builder / 3: Thought leadership]
- **Est. Words:** [1,500-2,500]
```

Summary quality rules:

- The narrative reads like a creative brief, not a dry description.
- Key points are specific enough to outline section headings from.
- Keywords are realistic long-tail phrases someone would search.
- The angle is genuinely different from what a Google search would surface.
- Every title passes the 4 U's test (Rule 5).

# Blog Topic Ideas — [Client Name]

Generated: YYYY-MM-DD
Methods used: [list of methods applied]
Target audience: [summary]
Content categories: [list]

## Tier 1: SEO Drivers
### 1. [Title]
[Full 200-word hybrid summary as above]

## Tier 2: Authority Builders

## Tier 3: Thought Leadership
...

## Content Calendar Suggestion
| Month | Article 1 (Tier) | Article 2 (Tier) |

Tier sections: **Tier 1: SEO Drivers**, **Tier 2: Authority Builders**, **Tier 3: Thought Leadership**, followed by the **Content Calendar Suggestion** (two articles a month, labelled by tier). For the monthly / quarterly / annual format rotation, see [content-formats.md](content-formats.md#content-calendar-mix).

## Quality checklist

Before finalising:

- [ ] At least 15 ideas across all 3 tiers
- [ ] Each idea has a complete 200-word hybrid summary
- [ ] No duplicate angles (each idea is distinct)
- [ ] At least 2 ideas per buyer awareness stage
- [ ] Ideas span at least 3 content categories
- [ ] Every title passes the 4 U's test (3+ dimensions at 3+)
- [ ] No overlap with existing published articles
- [ ] Mix of content formats (not all lists, not all how-tos)
- [ ] At least 3 ideas that showcase the author's unique expertise
- [ ] At least 2 ideas based on real customer questions (if data available)
- [ ] Content calendar covers at least 6 months at 2 articles a month
- [ ] All SEO keywords are realistic long-tail phrases
- [ ] Narrative briefs are compelling — they make you want to write the article
- [ ] British English throughout; the `anti-ai-slop` gate has been run on the briefs
