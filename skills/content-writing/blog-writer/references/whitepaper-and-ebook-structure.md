# Whitepaper and eBook Structure

Merged from skills/content-writing/content-whitepaper-ebook on 2026-09-29 at 7c60138; preservation map: [content-whitepaper-ebook.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/content-whitepaper-ebook.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this when the brief asks for a long-form owned document rather than a single article: a whitepaper, an eBook, a gated lead magnet, a donor report, an investor document, a conference handout or a document that supports a media pitch. It sets the whitepaper/eBook decision, the section templates, the long-form writing standards, the lead-generation pack and the donor/investor variant.

The output is paste-ready body copy organised by section and written to PDF-ready standard. This work does not produce `.pdf` or `.docx` files: it produces the text that a designer or the client pastes into their chosen layout tool. Apply [east-african-english](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md) for every tone and register decision; read it before writing any section.

## Inputs

Ask for all ten before generating any content (these replace the blog-length inputs in [article-build-method.md § Required Input](article-build-method.md) for this job):

1. **Client business name** — the trading name as it should appear on the document.
2. **Industry** — the sector (for example agri-finance, health NGO, professional services, FMCG).
3. **Country/city** — default Uganda/East Africa if not specified.
4. **Document type** — whitepaper or eBook (apply the decision rule below).
5. **Topic or working title** — a draft title is enough; refine it during generation.
6. **Target reader** — job role, organisation type and the specific problem they are trying to solve.
7. **Key argument / central thesis** — the single most important thing the document must convince the reader of.
8. **Data or research the client can provide** — internal data, market surveys, programme results, third-party reports.
9. **Intended use** — lead magnet, donor report, investor document, conference handout, media pitch support.
10. **Distribution channel** — email gate, LinkedIn post, website download, conference handout, WhatsApp broadcast.

If the client cannot answer item 7 or item 8, pause and prompt them before proceeding. A whitepaper without a thesis is a brochure. An eBook without a reader problem is a catalogue.

## Decision rules

### Whitepaper or eBook

Apply this distinction before choosing a structure template.

| Attribute | Whitepaper | eBook |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Research-led, evidence-based, formal register | Practical guide, how-to or curated advice; reader-centric, actionable, accessible register |
| How it works | Makes an argument using data, case studies and analysis | Gives tools and steps |
| Common contexts | B2B, NGO/donor, public sector, professional services, policy advocacy | B2C thought leadership, service marketing, training, SME audiences |
| Length | 3,000–6,000 words | 2,000–4,000 words |
| Format | Sections with headers, executive summary, numbered references | Conversational, bulleted, visual-friendly, chapter-based |
| Reader motivation | "I need to understand this sector/problem at a deep level" | "I need tools and steps I can use immediately" |
| Client signal | The client wants to be seen as an expert who has done the research | The client wants to give the reader practical value |

**Rule:** if the primary output is an argument supported by evidence, write a whitepaper. If the primary output is a set of actions the reader can take, write an eBook.

When the client is unsure, ask: "After reading this document, do you want the reader to think differently about a problem, or to do something differently?" Thinking → whitepaper. Doing → eBook.

### Variant by intended use

| Intended use | Add to the deliverable |
|---|---|
| Lead magnet or gated download | The lead-generation pack: email-gate recommendation, landing page copy block and four-channel launch brief |
| Donor report or investor document | The donor/investor variant in full: formal register, impact metrics table, framework citations, Theory of Change, participant language, citation proofread, financial accountability (donor) |
| Conference handout or media pitch support | The base template only, with the thesis stated in the Introduction and Conclusion |
| Blog repurposing of the finished document | Return to [article-build-method.md § Article Structure](article-build-method.md) and cut the document into articles |

## Procedure

1. Collect the ten inputs; stop on a missing thesis or missing data (items 7 and 8).
2. Apply the whitepaper/eBook rule and record the choice.
3. Generate the sections of the matching template below, in order.
4. For a whitepaper, write the Executive Summary last, then position it first.
5. Apply the long-form writing standards on top of the `east-african-english` standards.
6. Add the variant blocks the intended use requires.
7. Run the Human Authenticity Gate and the quality checklist before delivery.

## Templates

### Whitepaper structure

Generate each section in order. Write the Executive Summary last, then position it first.

1. **Cover page** — title, subtitle, client name, date, classification label ("Public" or "Confidential").
2. **Executive Summary** (300–400 words) — problem statement + 3 key findings + 3 recommendations. Write this last.
3. **Introduction** — context, why this topic matters now in Uganda/East Africa, and the scope and limitations of the document.
4. **Background / Market Context** — data on the sector, platform or problem; cite every statistic.
5. **Key Findings / Analysis** (2–4 sections) — each section presents one finding with supporting data and interpretation.
6. **Case Study or Example** — one real-world example from the East African market; anonymise it if the client requires.
7. **Recommendations** — 3–5 specific, actionable recommendations for the target reader; one recommendation per bullet.
8. **Conclusion** — restate the central thesis; close with a clear call to action.
9. **References / Sources** — full citations in Harvard style.

### eBook structure

Generate each chapter in order. The introduction sets the reader's expectations; every chapter delivers on them.

1. **Cover page** — title, subtitle and a clear "what you will learn" statement (3 bullets maximum).
2. **Introduction** — who this is for, what problem it solves, and what the reader will be able to do after reading.
3. **Chapter 1** — the problem or the first step (establish the context before offering the solution).
4. **Chapter 2** — the solution or second step (the core practical guidance).
5. **Chapter 3** — deepening the solution (nuance, common variations, sector-specific application).
6. **Chapter 4** — advanced application or common mistakes to avoid (raise the reader's confidence).
7. **Conclusion** — recap the key points; tell the reader exactly what to do next.
8. **About [Business Name]** — one short paragraph on who the client is and what they offer, with contact details.
9. **Call to Action page** — one clear action the reader should take now (book a consultation, join the newsletter, download a template).

## Long-form writing standards

Apply these on top of the `east-african-english` standards and the article standards in [article-build-method.md § Writing Standards](article-build-method.md).

- **Executive Summary is written last, positioned first.** Complete every other section, then write the Executive Summary as a standalone 300–400 word synthesis. Never draft it first.
- **Cite every statistic.** If the client cannot provide data, draw from publicly available East African sources: Uganda Bureau of Statistics (UBOS), World Bank Uganda open data, GSMA *Mobile Economy* Africa reports, Statista (note that a subscription is required), Meta Audience Insights, TikTok for Business Africa data. Record each in the source/assumption register and check the release date before use.
- **State the central thesis in both the Introduction and the Conclusion.** The reader must never finish the document wondering what it was trying to prove or recommend.
- **One recommendation per bullet.** Never bundle two recommendations into one point.
- **Match language to the target reader.** A whitepaper for Ugandan SME owners uses plain English and local market references. A whitepaper for FCDO programme officers (formerly DFID) uses development-sector terminology and international benchmarks. Ask about the reader before writing.
- **Sub-headings every 400 words at most.** East African professional readers frequently read on mobile, and long unbroken blocks lose them. Use descriptive sub-headings, not decorative ones.
- **Avoid vague conclusions.** Every recommendation must answer: who should do this, what exactly, and by when or with what resources?

For thesis strength, proof density, authority signals, value framing and premium commercial polish, also apply [premium-commercial-writing](../../premium-commercial-writing/SKILL.md).

## Lead-generation pack (lead magnet or gated download)

**Email gate.** Instruct the client to gate the document behind a sign-up form using one of the following. Plan limits change; confirm the current free-tier limit on the provider's pricing page before quoting it to a client.

| Tool | Free-tier note carried from the source (verify before quoting) | Recommend for |
|---|---|---|
| Mailchimp | Free up to 500 contacts | Clients starting from zero |
| Brevo | Free up to 300 emails/day | Clients who need automation |
| ConvertKit (now trading as Kit) | Not stated | Clients building a creator or coaching business |

**Landing page copy template.** Generate this copy block as part of the deliverable:

```
[Headline: the specific benefit the reader gets from downloading]
[Sub-headline: who this is for — job role or situation in one sentence]

What you will learn:
- [Benefit 1 — concrete and specific]
- [Benefit 2 — concrete and specific]
- [Benefit 3 — concrete and specific]

[Form: First name + Email address only — no additional fields]
[Button label: "Download the free guide" or "Get the whitepaper" — match document type]
[Trust signal: "No spam. Unsubscribe any time."]
```

**Four-channel launch brief.** Generate a brief for each channel at launch:

1. LinkedIn post — professional context, why this topic matters, link to the landing page.
2. Facebook post — broader audience hook, visual description, link or DM instruction.
3. Email to the existing list — subject line + 3-sentence body + download link.
4. WhatsApp broadcast — one-sentence hook + shortened link (Bitly or similar).

For the nurture sequence that follows a gated download, hand off to [07-email-marketing-strategy](../../../pipeline/07-email-marketing-strategy/SKILL.md). For how the document fits the client's wider owned-media ecosystem, use [peso-integrated-strategy](../../../strategy/peso-integrated-strategy/references/owned-media-assets.md) (owned-media assets). To place the document in a campaign sequence of articles, see [series-and-launch-engine.md](series-and-launch-engine.md).

## Donor / investor variant

Apply this section in full when the intended use is a donor report or an investor document.

- Use a formal register throughout: no contractions, no colloquialisms, no first-person informality.
- Include an **impact metrics table** with the columns: Indicator | Target | Achieved | Variance | Notes. Populate it with the client's programme data and flag any gaps for the client to fill.
- Cite international frameworks where relevant: UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), UNDP Human Development Index, World Bank poverty and inclusion data, IFC investment principles.
- Include a **Theory of Change description**: inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact. Write it as a structured paragraph the designer can render as a diagram, and label each stage.
- Replace "beneficiaries" with "participants", "community members" or a specific descriptor agreed with the client (for example "smallholder farmers", "adolescent girls in Karamoja").
- Proofread every citation against the original source before delivery. Donor and investor audiences verify citations, and errors undermine credibility immediately.
- For a donor document, add a **financial accountability section**: budget allocated, budget spent, variance explanation, and auditor or board sign-off status.

Impact metrics template:

| Indicator | Target | Achieved | Variance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Client indicator] | [Target value] | [Achieved value or GAP — client to supply] | [Achieved − Target] | [Source, period, explanation] |

## Human Authenticity Gate (long-form)

Every whitepaper or eBook passes through the [anti-ai-slop](../../../ai-marketing/anti-ai-slop/SKILL.md) humanising rewrite passes before client delivery. AI-generated or AI-assisted long-form documents must meet the Golden Rule: every whitepaper or eBook must look, feel and sound as if it was researched and written by the most skilled human expert with genuine authority in the subject and deep knowledge of the East African context. Generic, flat or culturally misaligned output is not acceptable, however efficiently it was produced.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] The whitepaper/eBook distinction has been applied and the matching structure template used.
- [ ] The Executive Summary was written last, is positioned first and sits within 300–400 words (whitepaper).
- [ ] Every statistic carries a named source; no unsourced claims appear.
- [ ] The central thesis is stated explicitly in both the Introduction and the Conclusion.
- [ ] Every recommendation is specific and actionable, one per bullet with no bundling, and names who, what and by when or with what resources.
- [ ] Sub-headings appear at least every 400 words and are descriptive.
- [ ] Lead magnet: a landing page copy block and a four-channel launch brief are included.
- [ ] Donor or investor: the impact metrics table, Theory of Change description and formal register are applied in full; donor documents include the financial accountability section.
- [ ] The document passed the long-form Human Authenticity Gate.
