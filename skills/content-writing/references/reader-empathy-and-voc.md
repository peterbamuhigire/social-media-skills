# Reader Empathy And Voice-Of-Customer

Use this reference when copy feels generic, self-centred, overpolished, or detached from how real customers speak.

Primary sources:

- Ann Handley, *Everybody Writes*
- Joanna Wiebe, *Where Stellar Messages Come From*

## Core rule

Do not start from brand adjectives.
Start from audience reality, problem language, desired outcome, and proof.

## Strong source material

Prefer:

- customer DMs
- comment threads
- sales call notes
- WhatsApp chats
- testimonials
- reviews
- objections heard in person
- language used by the audience in public communities

## Four message buckets

Sort findings into:

1. pain
2. outcome
3. objection
4. proof

The best copy uses all four.

## Pathological empathy questions

Before writing, answer:

1. What is the reader trying to achieve right now?
2. What are they frustrated by?
3. What would make them trust this message?
4. What would make the next step feel easy enough?

## Red flags

- the copy sounds like it could belong to any agency or creator
- the audience appears only as an afterthought
- there is no real objection or proof language
- the headline is clever but non-specific
