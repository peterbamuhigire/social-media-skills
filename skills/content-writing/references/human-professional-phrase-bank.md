# Human, Professional Phrase Bank — Posts, Ads, Emails, Blogs, Pages and Plans

Read this when a draft is correct but sounds generic, templated or machine-made, or when you need sentence patterns for a post, ad, email, blog introduction, service page, rate card, retention message, sales conversation or digital-marketing plan section. All templates are original slot patterns; fill every slot with verified, client-specific facts. Run the [direct-marketing ethics filter](direct-marketing-ethics-filter.md), the [human English craft standard](../../language/language-standards/references/human-english-craft-standard.md) and the `anti-ai-slop` gate on every result.

Slot notation: `[Client]` `[Brand]` `[n]` `[%]` `[UGX amount]` `[timeframe]` `[place]` `[named alternative]` `[metric]` `[outcome]`.

## 1. The power paragraph (the core unit)

A three- or four-sentence block in which each sentence has one job:

1. **Fact the reader cares about** — named subject, number, place, time.
2. **Why it matters** — the cost, impact or reason.
3. **Emphasis** (optional) — the angle that fits this strategy or audience.
4. **Forward consequence** — the position, opportunity or next step it makes possible.

Rules: a named party is the subject of sentence 1; at least one number appears in sentence 1 or 2; every superlative carries evidence; keep to roughly 45–110 words; the conclusion looks forward, never back ("In conclusion, as shown above…"); reuse the key phrase, not the whole paragraph, elsewhere.

**Phrase grammar that reads as expert:** named-subject statements ("[Client] is considering…"); numbers early; cause → consequence ("…which is eating into margins"); a named alternative ("instead of driving to [place]…"); forward conclusions ("…positions [Client] as…"); ownership verbs (cut, shorten, recover, free up, secure, expand, establish).

## 2. Channel adaptations of the power paragraph

| Channel | Pattern |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn / Facebook post | Line 1: a specific fact with a number. Line 2: why it matters. Line 3: the consequence or a real question. |
| Ad primary text | Reader's situation in their words → the result they want → proof → one action. |
| Service page hero | "You're [perceived need in the customer's words]. [Why it matters now]. [How we help you reach the longer-term benefit]." |
| Service page "Why us" | "[This work] requires [x]. Our team [matching proof with numbers]. We have done it for [n comparable clients]." |
| Investment page | "[Price] covers [scope]. You get [goal link]. Also included: [extra]. It's built for [who]." |
| Blog / article introduction | "[Dated driver]. It is pushing [readers] to [action]. Most are [costly workaround]. Here is a better way." |
| Thought-leadership close | "Fix [immediate issue] and you also get [second-order benefit], which opens [future opportunity]." |
| Cold or warm email | "[Observed situation at their organisation]. If [goal], [benefit]. We helped [peer type] do that in [time]. Worth a 20-minute call?" |
| Case study | Context → goal → what was delivered → verified result → what it made possible next. |

## 3. Digital-marketing plan sentence bank

Use with SMART, location-specific objectives. Each sub-section follows fact → why it matters → emphasis → consequence.

- **Situation:** "[Brand]'s task for [period] is to [win/grow/retain] [n] [customers] in [named places], who currently [behaviour or alternative]." / "The opportunity exists because [market driver], which is pushing [customers] to [action]."
- **Channels:** "[Customers] decide on [top two criteria] and hear about providers mainly through [channels]. The plan therefore concentrates [%] of the budget where they already look."
- **Segments:** "Primary: [n] [households/businesses] in [parish/suburb/district] who [defining behaviour], reachable through [channel]." / "We will not actively target [group] this period because [margin, capacity or distance]."
- **Positioning:** "[Brand] is the [category] for [customer] who want [primary benefit] without [pain]." / "Every channel repeats one promise: [single sentence]."
- **Pricing:** "[Offer] is priced at [UGX amount], [premium/parity/below] against [named alternatives], delivering a [%] gross margin (finance check pending)."
- **Low-cost tactics:** "Each tactic has one measurable job: [tactic] → [n] [enquiries] at under [UGX amount] each." / "Paid advertising starts only after [organic/referral] tactics show a conversion rate above [%]."
- **Alliances:** "[Partner] will [display/recommend/bundle] [offer] in return for [benefit]."
- **SMART objectives:** "By [date], secure [n] paying customers in [place], [%] of them from referrals." / "Raise [metric] from [baseline, source, date] to [target] in [district] within [n] months." / "Hold cost per qualified lead under [UGX amount] for [n] consecutive months before increasing spend."
- **Budget sufficiency:** "The [UGX amount] budget is sufficient because [n leads × conversion % × average sale]." / "Spend is released in stages: [%] in [quarter], more only when [threshold] is met."
- **Control:** "Any tactic that misses its cost-per-lead target for [n] months is stopped and its budget moved to [best performer]."
- **Close:** "With [focused segment], [single message], [low-cost tactics] and [named alliances], [Brand] can reach [headline target] by [date] within [budget]."

## 4. Pricing tables, rate cards and packages (choice architecture)

- Show two to four options per group; never a single option.
- Keep unrelated offers on separate tabs or pages.
- Align rows: same features, same order, same wording in every column.
- Use exactly one badge ("Most chosen", "Best value", "Recommended for [who]") and only if it is true.
- Put the option you most want to sell in the middle; on mobile, badge it and order it clearly.
- Decide the order deliberately (high-to-low for revenue per visitor, low-to-high for customer count) and log it as a test, not a rule.
- Subordinate "Free" or replace it with a trial or guarantee placed where the eye lands (below the price, above the button).
- Put a human contact route directly under the table ("Not sure which fits? Call or WhatsApp [name] on [number]; we reply within [time] during office hours.").
- Show discounts as old price, new price and saving together.
- Name tiers after the buyer or outcome ("Solo practice / Clinic / Hospital group"), not "Basic / Pro".

**Price timing:** premium offers build value before price (the home page sells outcomes and proof; an Investment page gives ranges); commodity and offer-led campaigns show the price up front; never hesitate where price is expected (pricing page, product page, checkout). Returning visitors can see the price sooner.

## 5. Retention, exit and loyalty microcopy (honest versions)

- Cancel button: "Cancel my plan" plus "You'll lose [earned benefit]. Need a break instead? Pause for up to [n] months."
- Before-you-go heading: "Before you go: here's what you've built with us." followed by a factual list.
- Switching headlines (never "Quit [competitor]"): "Get back [n] hours a week by moving your [task] to [Brand]." / "Bring your [data] across — we'll move it for you."
- Loyalty with real head-start: "Your first stamp is on us. [n] more and your [reward] is free." / "You're 2 visits from a free [service]."
- Referral forms: "As a thank-you, we only need 4 names today, not 5."
- Early-stage proof (only if true): "Join the first [cohort] of [who]. Founding members get [benefit]."

Never: confirm-shaming ("No thanks, I don't care about my business"), hidden cancel flows, fake counters or invented "most popular" labels.

## 6. Sales conversation and proposal language

- **Diagnosis openers:** "Before I suggest anything, how does [process] work for you today, and what is it costing you?" / "What made this a priority now rather than last year?" / "If we fixed only one thing, which would make the biggest difference?"
- **Value blocks:** logical — "This means [feature] → [measurable result] → [what it saves you]"; emotional — "and it means you stop being the person who has to [unpleasant task] every Friday." Check acceptance: "Is that important to you, or a nice-to-have?"
- **Honest-flaw formula:** "In fairness, [limitation]. Here's why we chose that, and how we cover it: [mitigation]."
- **Price difference:** "There are cheaper options. The question is what the cheaper option costs you when [failure scenario]."
- **Closes:** "There are two sensible ways to start: [A] or [B]. Which suits you better?" / "Here's how we'll get started: [dated steps]." Never "So, what do you think?"
- **Reassurance:** "It's normal to hesitate before a decision like this. Here's what protects you: [guarantee, stage gate, exit point]."
- **Gatekeepers and introductions:** "I wonder if you could help me with something — I'm trying to find the right person to speak to about [topic]."
- **Presentations:** open with a question, a sourced local fact or a short true story; never an apology; end with "Here's exactly what to do next: [1], [2], [3]."

## 7. Personal and professional voice

- Cut empty fillers: literally, basically, obviously, I mean, kind of, sort of, I think (when not needed), you know.
- Lock in value instead of dismissing effort: "I appreciate you saying that — I was glad to make it happen" rather than "No problem".
- Elevator pitch (30 seconds): "I help [specific audience] [achieve outcome] without [common pain]. Last quarter, for example, [short proof]. Right now I'm working on [current project]."
- Point of difference: "Yes, we [parity item] like everyone else. The difference is that we also [point of difference], so you [benefit]."

## 8. Interview and spoken-content starters (for repurposable answers)

Questions: "What's the biggest mistake [audience] makes with [topic], and what should they do instead?" / "Walk us through how you'd fix [problem] in the first 30 days." / "What's one number every [role] should watch, and why?"
Answer starters that restate the question: "The biggest mistake [audience] makes with [topic] is…" / "The way to fix [problem] in the first 30 days is…" / "Most people think [myth]. In fact…"

## 9. Words and habits to avoid

- Writer-centred needs ("You need a new website") → client-centred ("[Client] wants to stop losing weekend bookings").
- Burying or apologising for price.
- Five differentiators → one key advantage with proof.
- Unbounded markets ("all SMEs in Africa") and market sizes without source, year and scope.
- Superlatives without proof: perfect, ideal, best, unmatched, world-class, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, can't-miss.
- Benefit words with no object ("efficiency", "growth", "value") → efficiency of what, by how much, by when.
- Machine-made tells: "in today's fast-paced world", "leverage synergies", "unlock the full potential", "seamless", "robust solution", "a testament to", "delve", "navigate the landscape", "holistic", "empower" as filler.
- US hype idioms in East African professional copy ("slam dunk", "game-changer").

## 10. Before → after calibration set

1. "We offer high-quality services at affordable prices." → "Our monthly bookkeeping for Kampala SMEs costs UGX [amount] and includes EFRIS reconciliation, which most firms charge extra for."
2. "We have many years of experience." → "Since [year] we have delivered [n] websites for professional-services firms in Uganda and Kenya, [n] of them for law firms."
3. "We will increase your sales." → "The target is [n] additional qualified enquiries a month by [quarter], measured through tagged WhatsApp links."
4. "The client needs better marketing." → "In our meeting, your sales manager said, 'We don't know which adverts bring customers in.' This proposal answers that first."
5. "We are passionate about customer satisfaction." → "Every complaint is answered within four working hours, and the reply time is published on our dashboard."
6. "We look forward to a mutually beneficial relationship." → "If the pilot meets the [n]-enquiry target by [month], the same system can roll out to your [town] and [town] branches."
7. "Our Plans & Prices" → "Pick the plan that matches how many patients you see each month."
8. "20% off everything! Click here." → "Website care plan: UGX 500,000 → UGX 400,000 a month until 30 Nov."
9. "So that's our proposal. What do you think?" → "There are two sensible ways to start: the full rebuild in ten weeks, or a four-week launch of the three pages that bring most enquiries. Which fits your budget cycle better?"
10. "Sarah is a passionate digital marketing expert with years of experience." → "Sarah has run paid-search programmes for [n] private hospitals in Uganda and Kenya. On this project she owns the booking-conversion targets."
