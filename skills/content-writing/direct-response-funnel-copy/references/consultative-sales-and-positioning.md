# Consultative Sales and Positioning — Working Reference

Read this when the funnel ends in a conversation (call, meeting, WhatsApp chat, pitch) rather than a checkout, when writing sales scripts, or when building the authority that makes selling easier. Apply the [direct-marketing ethics filter](../../references/direct-marketing-ethics-filter.md). For agency new business also see `biz-dev-proposal` and `biz-dev-lawful-prospecting-outreach`.

## Stage 1 — Before the conversation

**Be sought out rather than chasing.** Build authority through writing (articles, reports, a short guide), speaking (associations, chambers, webinars, podcasts), earned media and a narrow specialism ("the firm that wrote the guide on clinic bookings"). Authority comes from describing the buyer's problem better than anyone else, not from declaring expertise; any stature claimed must be true.

**Let buyers raise their hand.** Run a message that offers something useful (guide, checklist, recording) rather than advertising the product; deliver it with consent to follow up; use a short sequence that educates and qualifies; let the buyer ask for the conversation. Ask only for the qualifying details you will use (role, organisation) and say why.

**Rehearse what could go wrong.** In writing, before any important meeting:
- every objection the buyer could raise, including the uncomfortable ones;
- technical failures (projector, connection, power, a missing document) and the fallback for each;
- the competitor's likely counter-claims and your evidence-based reply;
- the people in the room — the sceptic, the cost-controller, the quiet decider — and how you will involve each;
- the price challenge and the value restatement you will use instead of a discount.
Rehearse the answers aloud until calm; confidence comes from having already met the hard cases.

**Name and package the method.** Give your method a name, a clear number of stages and a true origin story, and prepare one clear demonstration; you can reveal the method and still keep the value in the execution. Every touchpoint — profile photo, email signature, office, proposal — is copy about you; present as the trustworthy version of your role is expected to look.

**Check your own footing.** Sell only what you genuinely believe works; know the offer and the buyer's world well enough to answer without notes; bring real, not performed, enthusiasm.

## Stage 2 — During the conversation

1. **Ask permission.** Begin the substance only once the buyer agrees to talk ("I have fifteen minutes").
2. **Question one level deeper than expected.** Useful prompts: "What would have to be true for this to work for you?", "Tell me more about that.", "What did you try before, and what disappointed you?", "Who else will weigh in, and what will they care about?" Listen far more than you speak, pause after important answers, take notes and mirror their words back. Watch word choice and hesitation for the real concern.
3. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Restate their situation in their words and confirm it.
4. **Present to what you heard.** A rehearsed core adapted with their data, language and local context; one honest limitation with its mitigation; the offer stated specifically (price, terms, timeline, deliverables).
5. **Weigh value visibly.** Each benefit they accept adds weight; each doubtful point subtracts — so drop anything they do not value. Give both feeling (relief, standing, security) and logic (return, guarantee, comparison), and help them picture the result ("Imagine the Monday queue gone because patients booked on WhatsApp").
6. **Prove it.** Claim nothing you cannot prove; prove everything you can; keep evidence current (review at least yearly); lean towards more proof; use only proof you trust. Strongest to weakest: specific consented testimonials and before/after evidence, then figures, third-party validation, demonstrations, guarantees and client logos.
7. **Treat objections as directions.** Answer with a question where you can ("Compared with what?"). On price, show the cost of the cheaper option (rework, failure, lost revenue); never apologise for the price.
8. **Close plainly.** Offer two or three clear options and ask which suits them, or say "Here is how we would start…" with dated steps. Avoid weak closes such as "So, what do you think?". Buyers often fear deciding; calm, firm reassurance helps them act. Accept a "no" without taking it personally.
9. **Be selective honestly.** State real qualification criteria, a real capacity limit, or that you would rather not proceed if fit is poor. Never invent scarcity or exclusivity.

## Stage 3 — After the conversation

**Next-day confirmation.** Within 24 hours of a yes: a welcome, reassurance about the decision, the benefits restated, next steps explained, and — when appropriate — a request for a referral or testimonial.

**Follow-up as a system.**
- Log every prospect, conversation, promised next step and date in a CRM (a spreadsheet or WhatsApp Business labels are enough for a solo practice).
- Build the automated follow-up (sequence, replay link, reminders) before the campaign starts; treat an email subject line as the envelope and its first two lines as the opening promise.
- Keep a demonstration ready on phone or laptop.
- Early "no"s often mean not yet, not in this form, wrong person or not enough information: revisit on a set cadence (for example quarterly) with something useful, and stop on request.
- If the same approach keeps failing, change it rather than repeating it; move closer as the deal matures (message → call → video → meeting).

**Learn from objections and losses.**
- Keep an objection register: wording, stage, root cause, current response, win rate, next response to test.
- Trace each recurring objection to its root: the wrong prospect (fix targeting), not seen as the expert (fix positioning), or value not shown (fix the presentation).
- For each persistent objection, brainstorm around twenty possible answers and ten to twenty questions, then test the best.
- Debrief lost deals: "I am not reopening the decision; I want to improve. Where did our proposal fall short for you?"

**Habits to remove:** feature lists before discovery; apologising for price; talking more than listening; chasing poor-fit prospects; trading margin for volume without the arithmetic; stopping follow-up too early; discounting to close; rigid scripts that stop adaptation; jargon the buyer does not use; touring your process instead of their outcome.
