# Direct-Mail Letters and Packs

Merged from skills/content-writing/direct-mail-writer on 2026-09-29 at 7c60138; preservation map: [direct-mail-writer.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/direct-mail-writer.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this when the deliverable is a direct-mail letter, a letter with inserts, a postcard, a self-mailer, or the same letter-style piece sent by email or WhatsApp broadcast to a known list. It covers the checks before writing, the letter skeleton, list selection, the cost-per-piece viability check, format choice and test design. For longer sales letters and sequences use the [long-copy sales letter system](long-copy-sales-letter-system.md); for offer design use [offer, proposition and price integrity](offer-proposition-and-price-integrity.md). Every piece passes the [direct-marketing ethics filter](../../references/direct-marketing-ethics-filter.md) and the [direct-mail pre-release copy checklist](galletti-27-points.md) before release.

## Inputs

Ask for these before producing any deliverable:

| Input | Notes |
|---|---|
| Client business name | As it should appear on the signature and letterhead |
| Industry / sector | Shapes proof points and format |
| Country or city | Default: Uganda / East Africa |
| The specific offer | Product, service, free consultation, etc. |
| The target audience | Existing customers, prospects, or a specific segment |
| The desired response action | Phone call, website visit, WhatsApp message or in-store visit |
| Budget context | Affects the format recommendation (see the cost-per-piece check) |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Any of the four prerequisites (list, offer, copy, timing) is absent | Resolve it before writing copy | A strong letter wasted on a poor list or a weak offer |
| Drafting has started from the feature list | Rewrite from the benefits list; keep features as supporting evidence only | A letter that instructs but does not sell |
| One of the Three Tells is missing from the opening or the P.S. | Treat the letter as incomplete and add it | A reader who cannot tell what is offered, why, or how to respond |
| Choosing who to mail from a customer list | Score each contact on FRAT; mail the highest scores first, most often and in the most premium formats | Spreading the budget evenly across low-value contacts |
| Using a rented or purchased list | Require contact recency within 90 days, match list demographics to the offer's buyer, and test a small cell (minimum 500 pieces) before rolling out | Rolling out to a stale or mismatched list |
| Recommending a format | Run the $20 Rule on projected revenue per transaction first | A format the transaction value cannot pay for |
| About to roll out | Run a test first — even a 50-piece email test beats no data | Scaling an untested piece |

## Procedure

### 1. Confirm the four prerequisites

Before writing a word of copy, confirm all four conditions are met (adapted from Edwards, Edwards and Douglas, 1991):

| # | Prerequisite | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The right list** | Is the audience targeted, current and relevant? A mediocre piece to a great list outperforms a great piece to a poor list. |
| 2 | **The right offer** | Is the offer clear, compelling and risk-reducing? A weak offer cannot be rescued by strong copy. |
| 3 | **The right copy** | Is the message benefit-led, action-oriented and reader-focused? |
| 4 | **The right timing** | Does the mailing align with seasonal need, budget cycles or purchasing windows? |

If any prerequisite is absent, resolve it before proceeding to copy.

### 2. Build two lists before writing

- **List 1 — Features:** every factual attribute of the product or service.
- **List 2 — Benefits:** for each feature, ask "What does this mean to the specific reader receiving this letter?"

Write the letter from List 2 only. Use List 1 as supporting evidence, never as the lead. "Features instruct. Benefits sell." — Hahn (2003).

### 3. Answer the Three Tells

Every direct-mail letter must answer three questions, in the first paragraph AND again in the P.S.:

1. **What** are you offering?
2. **Why** should they want it?
3. **How** do they get it? (the response mechanism)

If any of the three is absent, the letter is incomplete.

### 4. Draft to the letter structure

```
[HEADLINE or opening hook — strongest benefit or most important fact]

[Opening paragraph: State the problem the reader has. Paint it vividly.
Then pivot: "There is a solution."]

[Second paragraph: Introduce the offer. State it clearly. Lead with the outcome,
not the features. Use specifics — not "great results" but "34% more enquiries
in the first month."]

[Third paragraph: Build credibility. Testimonial, case study, statistic,
or credential. One proof point is enough here; more can follow.]

[Fourth paragraph: Address the most likely objection. Do not wait for the reader
to think it — raise it and answer it.]

[Fifth paragraph: The offer in full. State exactly what they receive, what they
pay (or don't pay), and any time limitation or bonus.]

[Closing paragraph: The call to action. Exact next step. Make it frictionless.
State the deadline if applicable.]

[Signature — handwritten name, full title]

[P.S. — Restate the single strongest benefit and the call to action.
The P.S. is the second-most-read element after the headline. Never waste it.]
```

The "34% more enquiries" line is an illustration of specificity; use a figure only when it is true for the client.

### 5. Run the pre-release copy checklist

Run the [direct-mail pre-release copy checklist](galletti-27-points.md) before finalising any copy; it groups the checks into focus, opening, argument and proof, offer and risk, structure, action and final tests. The checks that most often decide the outcome:

- The strongest true thing you can say comes first.
- The piece is written to one named persona, not "our valued customers".
- "You" and "your" clearly outnumber "we" and "our".
- Risk is reduced with a guarantee, trial or free first step the client will honour.
- The headline is tested against an alternative before anything else is polished.

### 6. Choose the list with the FRAT formula

When advising on mailing lists, apply the FRAT formula to decide who to contact first (adapted from Hahn, 2003):

| Letter | Factor | Question |
|---|---|---|
| F | **Frequency** | How often has this contact bought or enquired? |
| R | **Recency** | How recently did they buy or enquire? |
| A | **Amount** | What is their average transaction value? |
| T | **Type** | What type of products or services do they purchase? |

Score each contact on all four criteria. Mail the highest-FRAT contacts first, most often and with the most premium formats.

For rented or purchased lists:
- Require recency of contact within 90 days.
- Match list demographics to the offer's target buyer.
- Test with a small cell (minimum 500 pieces) before rolling out.

### 7. Check budget viability — the $20 Rule

Before committing to a mailing programme (Hahn, 2003):

> For every UGX 20,000 (or USD $20) of projected revenue per transaction, the client can reasonably invest up to UGX 1,000 (or USD $1) per piece in mailing costs.

| Projected revenue per transaction | Affordable cost per piece | Format it points to |
|---|---|---|
| UGX 100,000 | UGX 5,000 | Postcard or simple letter |
| UGX 500,000 | UGX 25,000 | Allows a folded brochure insert |

### 8. Choose the format

| Format | Best for | Cost tier |
|---|---|---|
| Single A4 letter | Professional services, B2B offers, relationship selling | Low |
| Postcard (A5) | Event invitations, short offers, reminders | Low |
| Letter + insert | Product launches, complex offers with supporting evidence | Medium |
| Self-mailer (folded brochure) | Retail promotions, multiple products | Medium |
| Email (cold or warm list) | Digital-first audiences; fastest test vehicle | Very low |
| WhatsApp broadcast | Known contacts only; the primary channel in East Africa | Very low |

### 9. Design the test

- **Test one variable at a time only** — headline vs headline, offer vs offer, list A vs list B.
- **Minimum test size:** 500 pieces per cell for digital; 2,000+ for print.
- **Code every response mechanism:** a different phone number, URL parameter, promo code or reply address per test cell.
- **Track:** response rate, cost per response, conversion rate and revenue per piece mailed.
- **Never roll out without a test** — even a 50-piece email test beats no data.

For break-even targets per cell, see [direct-response economics](../../../advertising/direct-response-economics/SKILL.md).

## Acceptance checklist

Good direct-mail output:
1. Leads with the strongest benefit — never the company name or "We are pleased to offer…".
2. Has the Three Tells in both the opening paragraph and the P.S.
3. Uses "you" and "your" more than "we", "our" and "I" throughout.
4. Makes a specific, risk-reducing offer — not a vague one.
5. Carries at least one proof point (testimonial, statistic or case reference).
6. Ends with a single, unambiguous call to action with a response mechanism.
7. Has a P.S. that restates the strongest benefit and the call to action.
8. Has had the $20 Rule checked before any format was recommended.
