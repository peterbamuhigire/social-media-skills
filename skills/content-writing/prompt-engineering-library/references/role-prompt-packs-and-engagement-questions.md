# Role Prompt Packs and Engagement Question Banks

Use this reference when a client team needs prompts organised by job role, or when a content calendar needs audience questions that invite genuine replies. Read it after the main library structure in `SKILL.md`; it does not replace the evidence-first prompt standard.

## 1. Role-coded prompt packs

A role pack is a small, versioned set of prompts that a person in one marketing role reuses every week. It shortens onboarding because a new team member starts from approved prompts for their own tasks rather than the whole library.

Build one pack per role in use at the client:

| Role | Typical prompts in the pack | Reviewer |
|---|---|---|
| Copywriter | caption variants from a brief; headline options; email subject lines; rewrite for a named register | Brand or account lead |
| Social media manager | weekly post set from content pillars; reply drafts for common comments; community-question prompts | Account lead |
| Content strategist | article outline from a research brief; repurposing map for one source asset; content-gap review | Strategist or editor |
| Brand strategist | voice consistency check against the brand context block; positioning statement drafts for review | Senior strategist |

Procedure:

1. List the role's recurring tasks from the last month of real work, not a generic job description.
2. Write one prompt per task using the library's structure (outcome, audience, context boundary, constraints, output shape, acceptance rule) with placeholder variables for anything client-specific.
3. Attach two approved example outputs and one rejected example with the reason.
4. Version the pack, name its owner and review it monthly with the rest of the library; retire prompts that keep producing heavy edits.

Keep packs small (five to ten prompts). A large, unowned pack becomes the generic output the engine is trying to avoid.

## 2. Engagement question bank (self-disclosure prompts)

People reply more readily when a post invites them to share their own experience, preference or story rather than to judge the brand. Use this as a hypothesis to test on the client's own accounts, not as a guaranteed effect.

Question families, with slot-templates:

| Family | Slot-template | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| First experience | "What was the first [product/service] you ever [bought/used] in [town]? What made you choose it?" | B2C, nostalgia, local pride |
| Small habit | "What is one thing you do every [morning/week] that makes [task] easier?" | Lifestyle, productivity, services |
| Lesson learned | "What is one lesson [your first job/your first business year] taught you about [topic]?" | B2B, personal brand, LinkedIn |
| Preference with reason | "[Option A] or [option B] for [situation], and why?" | Low-effort replies; always ask for the reason |
| Advice to a newcomer | "What would you tell someone starting [activity] in [city] this year?" | Communities, professional audiences |
| Local knowledge | "Where do you go in [area] for [need], and what makes it worth the trip?" | Hospitality, retail, place brands |

Rules:

- Ask one question per post. Stacked questions reduce replies.
- Make the question answerable in one line from memory; avoid questions that need research or expose private information.
- Never ask for sensitive personal data (health, finances, religion, political views, children's details) in public comments; move those conversations to a private, consented channel or do not ask.
- Reply to answers. A question the brand ignores teaches the audience not to answer again.
- Record which families produce replies from the target audience (not only volume) and retire families that attract off-target or low-quality responses.
