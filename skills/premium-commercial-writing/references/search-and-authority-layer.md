# Search And Authority Layer

Use this reference when writing must support traditional SEO, Generative Engine Optimisation, thought leadership, or AI-search citation.

## Core Principle

Search-friendly premium writing is answerable, attributable, and useful. It gives humans and AI systems a clear structure for understanding what the brand knows, who it serves, and why it is credible.

## GEO-Friendly Structure

For blogs, service pages, guides, whitepapers, and public reports:

1. Put the direct answer or thesis near the top.
2. Use headings that match real customer questions.
3. Define important terms in plain language.
4. Cover adjacent questions naturally in the same piece.
5. Add author, organisation, location, publication date, and last-updated date where the format allows.
6. Include visible proof: data, case examples, sources, credentials, or first-hand experience.
7. Add a short FAQ section for high-intent questions.
8. Keep paragraphs short and extractable.

## Authority Signals

Add only what is true and supportable:

- named author or expert
- relevant credentials
- client category served
- named process or method
- location-specific experience
- third-party source or citation
- case study or result
- visible update date
- clear contact path

## Semantic Depth Checklist

Before publishing, ask:

- What exact query should this content answer?
- What related questions would the reader ask next?
- What terms would an expert naturally use?
- What local details make this more useful than a generic global article?
- What facts need citation?
- What comparison or decision criteria would help the reader choose?

## Social-To-Search Adaptation

When a social post contains a strong insight, make it reusable:

- Turn the post's main claim into a blog H2 or FAQ question.
- Capture audience questions from comments and DMs.
- Convert repeated objections into FAQ entries.
- Use social proof themes as case study prompts.
- Link social campaigns to evergreen content rather than letting them disappear after the campaign ends.

## Things To Avoid

- keyword stuffing
- fake expertise
- undated claims
- statistics without sources
- vague authority language such as "leading", "world-class", or "trusted" without evidence
- hiding essential facts behind images, PDFs, or forms when the web page needs to be citable
