# Buyer-Question Content Plan

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-question-engine on 2026-09-29 at cda737c (S04 tree); preservation map: [playbook-question-engine.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-question-engine.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the website content plan must be fed by the questions customers actually ask, rather than by topics the business wants to talk about. Typical requests: "generate content ideas from customer questions", "build an FAQ library", "find blog topics that shorten sales calls", "set up a monthly question engine", or "use content to prepare prospects before sales meetings". It turns the Big 5 priority named in [content-plan-build-method.md](content-plan-build-method.md) (Priority 2 — Honest Education) into a repeatable monthly harvest, prioritisation and publishing cycle, and it adds assignment selling for the sales team.

Sources: Westergaard, N. (2016) *Get Scrappy: Smarter Digital Marketing for Businesses Big and Small*, AMACOM; Sheridan, M. (2019) *They Ask, You Answer*, revised edn, Wiley (both confirmed, Open Library catalogue, 29 Sep 2026); Marcus Sheridan's River Pools case study as reported in those works.

## Principle and rationale

The content that performs best answers questions customers are already asking. Sheridan's River Pools business moved from near-bankruptcy to the most-visited swimming pool website in the world by answering every customer question in writing, including the uncomfortable ones: what a pool costs, what goes wrong with fibreglass pools, and how the company compares with named competitors. Westergaard (2016) treats answering the questions competitors avoid as the highest-value content tactic open to a small business. (Case facts as reported in the cited books; verify before stating as current — no register record.)

East African framing: aim to be the business in the market that everyone turns to with a question. Where trust spreads through sharing information, the business that gives knowledge away freely is the one people recommend.

Why the method works:

| Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|
| Search engines reward specific answers | A post titled "How much does solar panel installation cost in Kampala?" ranks for searches a generic "Our Services" page never reaches |
| Pre-educated buyers close faster | A prospect who has read the answer before calling arrives partly sold; the source claims the sales call shortens by 30–50% (unsourced figure — verify before stating; no register record) |
| Competitors avoid price and problem questions | The gap is large and open to any business willing to answer |
| WhatsApp saved replies are evidence | Each saved reply marks a question asked repeatedly; publishing it as content reduces manual answering |

## Inputs

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name | Trading name |
| Industry | Sector |
| Country / city | Default Uganda / East Africa |
| Primary goal | For example: content ideas, FAQ library, blog topics, fewer sales-call objections |
| Existing content | Blog posts, FAQ page, WhatsApp saved replies, video library — or confirmation that none exists |
| Customer-facing team members | Receptionist, sales staff, customer service, technicians — whoever hears customer questions |
| Primary search and discovery channels | Google, YouTube, Facebook search, WhatsApp word of mouth; this sets format priority |
| Google Search Console access | Yes / No; decides whether search-query data is available |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Only one question source is available | Run the other four as a gap list and label the harvest partial | A plan built on one team's view of the customer |
| Google Search Console is not set up | Use the "People Also Ask" box for the client's main service or product instead | Losing the search-data source entirely |
| A cluster falls in the Big 5 | Schedule it before any non-Big 5 topic | Filling the calendar with low-value topics while price and comparison gaps stay open |
| A cluster scores High on both frequency and sales relevance | Put it in the monthly top 5 | Effort spent on questions nobody asks or that do not move a sale |
| The client refuses to publish price, comparison or problem answers | Record the refusal as a decision and explain the lost search and trust gap | Silent removal of the highest-value pieces |
| A question invites sensitive disclosure or has no response plan | Replace it with a safer, answerable prompt | Engagement that creates unmanaged risk |
| A consumption benchmark (for example "30 pages" or "80% close rate") is proposed for a client report | Omit it unless the original study, population, period, definitions and local evidence are available | Presenting an unverified universal rule as fact |
| Question research also informs AI-search discoverability | Apply the AI-search bridge below | Collapsing different outcomes into one "AI rank" |

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Collect (first week of each month)

Review all five sources below without filtering. Target at least 20 raw questions a month. Record each question exactly as the customer put it — in their words, not the business's.

| Source | Action | Tools / detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frontline staff | Brief every customer-facing team member to capture one question a week that a customer asked and the content does not yet answer. Many businesses never ask these people, who hear questions daily | Shared WhatsApp group or a simple Google Sheet. Sample briefing: "Every time a customer asks you something this week, note it down. We're going to turn those questions into content. There are no silly questions — the more specific, the better." |
| 2. Social media comments and DMs | Review the last 90 days of comments and DMs on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and WhatsApp; export questions that appear more than twice. A question asked by 10 people in different ways is a content gap, not a one-off query | Meta Business Suite comment history; TikTok Creator Studio comments; WhatsApp Business chat search (tool names as in the source; verify current names before stating — no register record) |
| 3. Search data | Google Search Console (free) → Performance → Search Results; filter to question queries (containing "how", "what", "why", "can I", "is it", "how much"); export the top 50 question queries by impressions; add to the list. These include partial matches and long-tail phrases from real searchers | If Search Console is not set up, search the client's main service or product and record every question in Google's "People Also Ask" box — Google's most common related queries (menu path as in the source; verify at use — no register record) |
| 4. FAQ logs and saved replies | List the existing FAQ page, every WhatsApp saved reply, email templates and printed customer information. Treat each as an answered question; if the answer exists as a saved reply but not as a blog or social post, it is a content gap | Cross-reference against the content library |
| 5. Sales call notes | Review notes or recordings of the last 20 sales enquiries: objections raised, what the prospect did not understand, which advance answer would have reduced hesitation. Ask the owner or sales lead: "In the last month, what question came up most often in sales conversations that slowed down the decision?" | That question becomes the first content piece |

### Step 2 — Cluster

Group questions that ask the same thing. "How much does X cost?", "What is the price of X?", "Is X expensive?" and "Can I afford X?" form one cluster and one content brief.

### Step 3 — Prioritise with the Big 5 (Sheridan, 2019)

The Big 5 categories carry the most search volume and the greatest sales acceleration. Address them before any other topic.

| Category | What buyers ask | Why it is urgent |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing and cost | "How much does [product/service] cost?" "What affects the price?" "Is there a cheaper option?" | Price is the most-searched topic in almost every industry; the business that answers first owns the category in search |
| Problems and limitations | "What are the problems with [product/service]?" "When does [solution] not work?" | Buyers do due diligence before purchase; honest answers build trust faster than silence |
| Comparisons | "[Brand A] vs [Brand B]" "What is the difference between [option 1] and [option 2]?" | Buyers in consideration actively compare; appear in their research or lose the sale to whoever does |
| Reviews | "Is [company/product] any good?" "What do customers say about [brand]?" "Best [product category]" | Social proof decides many purchases; review content appears in Google and YouTube search |
| Best in class | "Best [product type] in [city/country]" "Which [service] should I choose?" "Top [service providers] in Uganda" | Buyers seeking the best option are close to deciding; content ranking here converts directly |

Then score every cluster on two dimensions and rank those High on both first. Price, comparison and problem questions usually score highest on both. Non-Big 5 clusters fill the calendar once the primary gaps are covered.

| Dimension | Scoring |
|---|---|
| Search volume / enquiry frequency | How often is the question asked? High / Medium / Low |
| Sales relevance | Would answering it speed up a purchase decision? High / Medium / Low |

### Step 4 — Assign format

| Question type | Best format | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing and cost | Blog post + short video | Website, YouTube, Facebook |
| Comparison ("us vs competitor") | Long-form article | Website, LinkedIn |
| Problems / limitations | Honest FAQ (text) | Website, WhatsApp saved reply |
| How-to / process | Video + checklist | YouTube, Instagram, WhatsApp |
| Best in category / buyer's guide | Long-form article or PDF | Website, email newsletter |
| Quick facts | Instagram carousel or Facebook post | Social media only |

Carry the chosen format into the nine-element article brief in [content-plan-build-method.md § Section 1: 12 Blog Post Briefs](content-plan-build-method.md); the question cluster supplies element 5 (five key questions).

### Step 5 — Publish and index (only with publishing authority)

- Publish blog content on the website and submit it to Google Search Console for indexing.
- Publish video on YouTube with the exact question as the title and in the description.
- Share each piece across all active social platforms within 48 hours of publication.
- Add the answer (with the content link) to the sales team's WhatsApp saved replies so they can send it when the question comes up.

### Step 6 — Review quarterly (every 90 days)

- Which pieces produced the most organic traffic, enquiries or shares?
- Which question topics are still unanswered?
- Which new questions have appeared in the five sources in the last 90 days?

Put more effort into the topics and formats producing the most enquiry traffic. Retire or update content that no longer matches current pricing, processes or services.

## Answer what competitors avoid

Most businesses will not answer:

- **Price questions** — for fear of scaring prospects off or revealing rates to competitors.
- **Comparison questions** — for fear of naming a competitor or looking defensive.
- **Problem questions** — for fear that admitting a limitation damages credibility.

That avoidance is exactly why these pieces carry the most value. A prospect who searches "how much does [service] cost in [city]" and finds an honest, detailed answer on the client's site arrives pre-qualified and ready to proceed. Stating limitations also builds trust: "Here is when our service is not the right fit for you" signals confidence, honesty and selectivity, and prospects who continue after reading it are more committed buyers.

## Assignment selling (Sheridan, 2019)

Once the library exists, use it in the sales process: assign specific pieces to a prospect before key sales conversations so the conversation starts from shared understanding.

1. Identify the two or three questions a prospect must have answered before they can decide.
2. Make sure content answers each one (Big 5 content is ideal).
3. Before any sales meeting, email or WhatsApp the prospect: "Before we speak on [date], I'd like to share [article/video] — it will make our conversation much more productive."
4. Open every sales conversation by confirming: "Did you get a chance to review the content I sent?" If yes, skip the basics; if no, revisit the assignment.

Consumption is a useful signal, not a universal benchmark. Track which pieces a prospect viewed before each appointment, but do not carry a fixed "30 pages" or "80% close rate" rule into a client report without the original study, population, period, definitions and local evidence. Read consumption alongside qualification, source self-report, sales stage and qualified outcome.

Pre-qualification questions to add to every lead form:

- "Which article or video brought you to us today?" (tracks source)
- "What is the most important question you'd like answered before deciding?" (surfaces unmet content gaps)
- "How did you hear about us?" (attribution)

Insourcing content creation: the best answer to "we don't have time to create content" is to involve the people who already hold the answers — sales staff, customer service and in-house subject experts, who answer questions every day. A content manager with a journalist's instincts can turn their answers into published content from a 20-minute monthly interview per staff member. One filming session with four employees on a job site can yield five or more videos. No specialist knowledge is needed — only willingness to share what is already known.

## AI-search bridge

When question research also informs AI-search discoverability, use the Carter synthesis in the [AI search and social discovery rules](../../../ai-marketing/ai-generative-search-optimisation/references/ai-search-and-social-discovery-rules.md) (which also holds the customer-language bank and intent map) and the [real-time bridge and voice contract](../../../playbooks/playbook-content-production/references/real-time-content-bridge-and-voice.md). Keep the customer's wording, then classify each resulting content unit by job: evergreen brand/offer fact, current sourced update, or deep decision support. Record observations separately as representation, retrieval or citation, referral, qualified action, or sale; never merge them into an "AI rank". A prompt or question is a research seed, not evidence that the market uses that wording.

## Template — monthly question engine report

1. **Question harvest** — the 20+ raw questions collected this month from all five sources.
2. **Clustered question list** — questions grouped by theme, with the number of clusters.
3. **Priority content brief (top 5)** — per cluster: question cluster, recommended format, target platform, suggested headline, three-bullet outline.
4. **Content backlog** — all remaining questions ranked by priority for future months.
5. **Last month's performance** — where applicable: which published pieces produced traffic, enquiries or shares.

## Checklist

- [ ] All five question sources are covered; the output does not rely on one source.
- [ ] Collection runs monthly with a minimum target of 20 questions.
- [ ] The prioritisation scores each cluster on both search frequency and sales relevance, with Big 5 clusters first.
- [ ] Format is matched to question type, not assigned generically.
- [ ] Price, comparison and problem questions are prioritised explicitly, not avoided.
- [ ] WhatsApp saved replies appear both as a question source and as a distribution channel.
- [ ] When AI-search visibility is in scope, the question-to-content handoff records response mode, source status, destination and separate outcome measures.
- [ ] The six-step process is complete: collect, cluster, prioritise, assign, publish, review.
- [ ] British English throughout ([East African English standard](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md)); imperative mood in instructional sections.
- [ ] Nothing is published, messaged or scheduled without explicit authority.
