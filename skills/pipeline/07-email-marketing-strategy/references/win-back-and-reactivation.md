# Win-Back and Reactivation

Merged from skills/business-development/biz-dev-reactivation-campaign on 2026-09-29 at cda737c (S04 tree); preservation map: [biz-dev-reactivation-campaign.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/biz-dev-reactivation-campaign.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client holds a dormant list of past customers (email addresses, WhatsApp numbers, phone numbers or a mix) that it has stopped contacting, and wants a customer reactivation campaign pack: a four-message win-back sequence, a WhatsApp adaptation, a one-page campaign brief for sign-off and a results report.

It differs from [strategy-document-sections.md § 6. Reactivation Sequence](strategy-document-sections.md). That section is a three-email housekeeping series for subscribers who have gone quiet on an active list, ending in suppression. This reference is a commercial campaign aimed at past buyers the business itself stopped talking to, built on gratitude, value, an exclusive offer and a referral close.

The core idea to put in front of the client: most businesses chase new customers while ignoring the people who have already bought from them. Fihn (2025) calls a dormant customer list a "trash can asset": invisible, undervalued, and often worth more than the whole active marketing budget. His illustration is Carl, a lawyer who had seen no results from $15,000 of Facebook advertising and then generated $100,000 in 48 hours from one reactivation sequence sent to his 25-year customer list. Present it as the author's case, not as a forecast for the client.

## Inputs

Ask for all of the following before producing any output:

| # | Input | What to capture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client business name and industry | Legal or trading name and sector |
| 2 | Country / city | Defaults to Uganda/Kampala if not specified |
| 3 | Customer list details | Approximate size of the dormant list, how old the records are, format (email, WhatsApp numbers, phone numbers or a combination) |
| 4 | Time since last contact | When the business last communicated with this list (6 months / 1 year / 3+ years) |
| 5 | Reason for dormancy | Did the business stop sending, or did customers stop responding? |
| 6 | Best-performing product or service | The offer customers liked most; it becomes the reactivation hook |
| 7 | Current offer or incentive | Any discount, bonus or exclusive perk for returning customers; if none, suggest one from the offer options below |
| 8 | Preferred delivery channel | Email only / WhatsApp only / email + WhatsApp combined / SMS |
| 9 | Brand voice | Formal, warm or casual; phrases the client uses or avoids |
| 10 | Primary goal | Immediate sales / reactivated relationships / referral acquisition / all three |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Client is unsure whether to prioritise reactivation over new acquisition | Put the reactivation economics below in the brief or presentation that accompanies the campaign | Budget spent on cold ads while a warm list sits unused |
| Contact numbers have no recorded marketing opt-in (common for "past customer" WhatsApp lists) | Send Message 1 only, with the explicit opt-in line "Reply YES to continue hearing from us."; continue the sequence only to those who reply | Messaging people without consent under the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 |
| Channel is WhatsApp | Cut the sequence to three messages by merging Messages 2 and 3; space messages at least 48 hours apart | Being blocked or reported for a four-message WhatsApp sequence |
| Client is an in-person business | Add the VIP referral card to Message 4 | A referral ask that is forgotten once the message is read |
| Campaign brief not yet approved in writing | Do not write the full copy | Scope creep after the copy is written |
| Client has no returning-customer incentive | Propose one from the offer options for Message 3 | A desperate generic discount, or an offer message with nothing exclusive in it |

## Why reactivation outperforms cold acquisition

Include this framing in any client presentation or brief that accompanies the campaign.

The economics:

- Acquiring a new customer costs 5–9 times more than retaining or reactivating an existing one. This ratio is widely cited in CRM literature; verify before stating (no register record).
- A weekly repeat customer, even at the same average order value, is worth 100 times more over a year than a one-time visitor. Fihn (2025) gives a Groupon restaurant case: one-time customer lifetime value $12.50 against $5,200 a year for a weekly repeat customer.
- A dormant customer is not a lost customer. They stopped hearing from the business; they did not stop wanting what it sells.
- In Uganda and the wider East African market, relationship and trust are prerequisites for commercial decisions, so re-establishing personal contact carries more weight than cold advertising.

What a reactivation campaign needs:

- A list: email, WhatsApp or phone numbers from past customers.
- A reason to reach out. The gratitude message removes the awkwardness of a cold reappearance.
- A low-friction offer: an exclusive appreciation, not a desperate discount.
- A human voice. The messages must sound personal, not automated.

## Procedure

1. Collect the ten inputs and confirm the consent position of every channel.
2. Produce the one-page campaign brief (template below) and obtain the client's written approval.
3. Write all four messages for the chosen channel in the client's voice. Each message is complete and standalone, not a fragment.
4. For any Uganda/EA client, add the WhatsApp-first version.
5. Run the sequence only with separate sending authority, then measure and report within 10 days of the final message.

### The four-part reactivation sequence

**Message 1: the gratitude message (Day 1)**

Purpose: re-establish contact as a thank-you, not a sales pitch. Arriving with gratitude removes the awkwardness of the gap and does not read as a marketing email.

Structure:

- Warm opening that refers to the relationship, not the product.
- Genuine appreciation for their past business.
- One personal or specific detail that makes it feel individual: their niche, a common challenge in their industry, or a shared community reference.
- No offer. This message only re-establishes the relationship.
- Close with forward-looking warmth, not a call to action.

Email template (adapt to the client's voice):

> Subject: A quick thank-you from [First Name] at [Business Name]
>
> Hello [Customer Name],
>
> I wanted to take a moment to genuinely thank you for your support of [Business Name]. You chose us when you didn't have to, and that means more than a standard email can convey.
>
> Things have been moving fast here — [brief personal update: new location / new team member / expanded range / quiet period of reflection]. Through all of it, we keep coming back to the same thought: the customers we most want to serve are the ones who have already given us a chance.
>
> That's you.
>
> No ask today. Just a genuine thank-you for being part of our story.
>
> Warmly,
> [Sender First Name]
> [Business Name]

WhatsApp adaptation: shorten to 5–7 lines, open with their name, no subject line, end with a simple sign-off and no call-to-action button or link.

**Message 2: the value message (Day 3)**

Purpose: give something useful with no strings attached. It positions the business as an authority and continues re-engagement before any offer.

Structure:

- Callback to Message 1 ("since I was thinking about you...").
- One specific, actionable piece of advice relevant to their situation or industry, drawn from the client's expertise: a tip, a short guide or a resource they did not have to ask for.
- Frame it as a gift, not marketing content.
- Soft close: "Let me know if this was useful."

The value must be real. Never send a promotional message disguised as a tip; this message earns the right to make the offer in Message 3.

**Message 3: the exclusive offer (Day 5)**

Purpose: a time-limited, customer-specific offer not available to the general public. The exclusivity is the message.

Offer options (choose the one that fits the client):

| Offer type | Example |
|---|---|
| VIP early access | Access to a new product or service before public launch |
| Returning-customer discount | "20% off your next booking — for past clients only, this week only" |
| Bundled value package | "For this week only, [product] includes [bonus service] at no extra charge" |
| Free consultation, assessment or audit | Especially for service businesses: a low-risk first step back into the relationship |
| Mutual referral reward | "Bring a colleague and we both win" |

Structure:

- Direct, warm opening that refers to the relationship.
- The offer, stated clearly: what it is, what it includes, the specific deadline (day and time) and how to claim it.
- One sentence on why they deserve it: they are a past client, not a random recipient.
- A single call to action: reply, WhatsApp or click a link. One action only.

WhatsApp call to action: "Reply YES to this message and I'll send you the details / booking link."

**Message 4: the referral close (Day 8)**

Purpose: turn the reactivated relationship into a source of referrals. The best time to ask is straight after a positive re-engagement, when trust and goodwill are highest.

Structure:

- Thank them for their engagement or for any purchase made from Message 3.
- The referral ask, framed as an invitation, not a sales request.
- The incentive: what the referring customer receives (a reward, a credit, a thank-you gift) and what the referred contact receives.
- Simple mechanics: "Forward this message to one person", "Give them my WhatsApp number" or "Tag them in our post".
- Close with warmth; this is the last message in the sequence.

VIP card for in-person businesses: give the client a card template reading *"[Business Name] is offering a free [assessment/consultation/tasting/session] to any business owner you refer. Present this card to receive your personal thank-you gift."* Hand three cards to each happy reactivated customer. The card makes the referral ask physical and memorable.

### WhatsApp-first adaptation for Uganda/EA

In Uganda and East Africa, WhatsApp is the dominant personal and professional channel, and many customers have a mobile number on record but no email address (see register WHATSAPP-USAGE-EA-2026 for current usage evidence). Apply these rules:

- Maximum five lines per message. WhatsApp is read on mobile; dense paragraphs are not read.
- Use the customer's first name in every opening line.
- Avoid formal email sign-offs; use "Warm regards" or the sender's first name.
- Include one reply prompt in every message instead of a link. Reply-based engagement performs far better than links on WhatsApp.
- Space messages at least 48 hours apart. WhatsApp feels more intrusive than email, so do not compress the sequence.
- Reduce the sequence to three messages by merging Messages 2 and 3. A four-message WhatsApp sequence risks being blocked or reported.

Consent (GDPR and Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019): never send WhatsApp marketing to a number that has not opted in. "Past customer" does not equal consent under the Act. Advise the client to send Message 1 only, with the explicit line *"Reply YES to continue hearing from us."*, and to continue only with those who reply. Verify at use; see register UG-DPPA-2019 and UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026, and WHATSAPP-BUSINESS-POLICY for the platform's own opt-in rules.

### Measuring success

Track these for every reactivation campaign. The benchmarks are the source skill's working ranges, not verified market data; label them as provisional comparators in client reports.

| Metric | How to measure | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate (email) | Email platform analytics | 40–60% for existing customer lists (against 20–25% for cold lists) |
| Reply rate (WhatsApp) | Count replies to Message 1 | 15–30% of opted-in recipients |
| Offer redemption rate | Orders, bookings or sessions booked | 5–15% of the total list |
| Revenue generated | Client reports within 7 days | Client defines the acceptable threshold |
| Referrals generated | Referral codes and VIP cards redeemed | Track separately for 30 days after the campaign |

Report to the client within 10 days of the final message. Use the results as the case-study anchor for the next client pitch (with the client's permission).

## Release checklist

- [ ] All four messages are complete, copy-ready pieces, not outlines or templates with unfilled placeholders; each uses the client's real business name, product or service and brand voice.
- [ ] Message 1 contains no offer and reads as a personal note, not a marketing email.
- [ ] Message 3 states the specific offer, a deadline as a day and date (not "this week") and a single clear call to action.
- [ ] Message 4 includes the referral mechanics and incentive, not a general ask.
- [ ] Any Uganda/EA client has a WhatsApp version that respects the five-line maximum and the consent note.
- [ ] The campaign brief table is present and ready for client sign-off.
- [ ] The reactivation economics are present and cited, to support the conversation on why to prioritise this campaign.
- [ ] British English throughout, with no American spelling variants.
