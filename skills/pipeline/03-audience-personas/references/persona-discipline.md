# Persona Discipline — Build, Choose and Keep Personas Honest

Read this when building or reviewing personas, choosing a primary persona, defending a persona against feature or message creep, or validating a new offer's audience before strategy work. Parent skill: [`03-audience-personas`](../SKILL.md). Synthetic personas follow the extra controls in `ai-synthetic-personas`.

## 1. Stories are the deliverable

- A persona is a person in a situation, with a problem and a hoped-for outcome — not a demographic profile. Give every persona a (fictional) name, a setting and a quotable problem statement.
- Start with a frustrating situation to find the pain, then add context and setting. The opportunity lives in how the story could end better.
- **Too-perfect check:** if the story feels too easy or too flattering, stop and rewrite it from evidence. It is easy to write about the customers you wish you had.
- Do not "make it up": ground every persona in interviews, listening data, sales and support records, or a clearly labelled assumption.

## 2. Required attributes

| Attribute | Content |
|---|---|
| Name and label | Fictional first and last name; user group or segment |
| Photo reference | Consented volunteer or licensed stock; never a real customer without consent |
| Demographics | Age band, education, location, language — only what matters to the decision |
| Goals and motivations | What they are trying to achieve and why |
| Environment | Social, technical and physical context (device, data cost, connectivity, workplace) |
| Pain points and stress points | Where the current way fails them |
| Short biography | Role, main tasks, usage story, concerns, biggest obstacles |
| Evidence status | Source for each attribute or "assumption — to validate" |

## 3. Choosing the essential (primary) persona

- The design, message or offer built for the essential persona must at least work for the others; one built for another persona may not work for the essential one.
- Never average users into a composite; an averaged persona fits nobody.
- Use the persona to stop scope creep from rare cases: "Would [persona] need this? If not, it is out of scope for this campaign." Delighting a well-defined group beats half-pleasing everyone.
- Guard against self-substitution: specific, richly described personas stop the team (or an AI model) from writing for itself.

## 4. Keep personas visible

Put the persona's name, a photo placeholder and one memorable quote on every page and brief that relies on it, not only on the persona card. Physical reminders (posters, cards) are optional; the requirement is that every reviewer can recall who the work is for.

## 5. Provisional personas and problem validation (new offers)

1. Define the primary customer segment precisely (behaviour, context, place).
2. Identify the segment's single biggest problem.
3. Write a **provisional** persona, labelled as a hypothesis.
4. Validate with problem interviews before any solution pitch:
   - Screener questions confirm the person fits the persona.
   - Open behavioural questions: "How did you last handle [problem]?", "What was hardest?", "How did you get round it?", "Have you tried [closest alternative]?"
   - You are learning, not selling; do not present the solution.
5. Revise the value proposition and persona from what you learn; repeat until the evidence is stable.

Interview recruitment must be consent-based (Uganda DPPA / Kenya DPA duties apply to any personal data collected); record who, when and how.

## 6. East African adaptation

- Include device, data cost, language and payment behaviour (mobile money, cash, bank) in the environment field.
- Politeness norms can hide criticism in interviews; use indirect questions ("What do people like you say about…?") and a neutral interviewer.
- For B2B, a persona is an individual with a business application inside an account; see `strategy-b2b-customer-community` for buyer-group mapping.
