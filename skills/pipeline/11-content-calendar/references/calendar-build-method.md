# 90-day calendar build method

Moved from `SKILL.md` in Social Kaizen S09 (29 Sep 2026, start commit `0e0af8a`); wording tightened; every item, date, figure, rule and citation kept. Read when building the monthly tables, choosing observances and awareness days, or running the pre-publish sampling check.

## Intake questions

Ask for the following before generating:

- **Client name**: trading name of the business.
- **Platforms in scope**: all active platforms (default: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp).
- **Content pillars**: names and percentages from `10-content-pillars`.
- **Posting frequency per platform**: times per week per platform (for example Facebook 5×/week, Instagram 3×/week).
- **Campaign dates**: confirmed campaign windows from `09-campaign-strategy` (name and date range).
- **Three high-revenue seasons**: the three periods when the client earns most or has the highest demand.
- **Calendar start date**: the Monday the calendar begins.
- **Country/city**: defaults to Kampala, Uganda.

## Calendar table format

Use this column structure for every row. Produce one row per content piece per week (summarised by week, not by day; daily scheduling happens in the weekly workflow):

| Week | Date Range | Platform | Content Type | Pillar | Topic / Headline | 2-Sentence Content Brief | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Week**: W1, W2, W3 … W13.
- **Date Range**: for example 24–28 Mar 2026.
- **Platform**: Facebook / Instagram / WhatsApp / LinkedIn / TikTok / X (one row per platform per week per pillar).
- **Content Type**: post, carousel, reel, story, broadcast, article, video (the dominant format for that week's theme).
- **Pillar**: the pillar name from `10-content-pillars`.
- **Topic / Headline**: a specific working title ("How we helped a Kampala salon double bookings", not "Case study").
- **2-Sentence Content Brief**: sentence 1, what the post covers; sentence 2, what the audience should feel or do after seeing it.
- **Notes**: observances, campaign windows, cross-platform tie-ins, or anything needing advance preparation.

## Ugandan and East African observances

When an observance falls in the 90-day window, add a content row for the relevant platform(s) in that week, mark it `[OBSERVANCE]` in Notes, and write a brief suited to the occasion. Do not insert a generic post and claim it is culturally relevant.

Fixed dates:

- 1 January: New Year's Day.
- 14 February: Valentine's Day (consumer brands; use with restraint for B2B).
- 8 March: International Women's Day. Amplify women's voices; do not reduce it to a single post if the client works in sectors affecting women.
- 3 June: Uganda Martyrs Day. Tone: solemn; most brands acknowledge rather than promote.
- 9 June: Uganda Heroes Day. Tone: celebratory, patriotic.
- 9 October: Uganda Independence Day. Tone: pride, national identity; strong for Ugandan consumer brands.
- 25 December: Christmas Day.

Variable dates (confirm the current year's date before generating):

- **Eid al-Fitr**: end of Ramadan; lunar date.
- **Eid al-Adha**: about 70 days after Eid al-Fitr.
- **Ramadan**: mark the start and end weeks. For Muslim-majority or mixed audiences, reduce overt promotion, increase value-led and community content, and avoid daytime food imagery where relevant to the brand.

## Industry seasonal hooks

Ask the consultant: "Name the three periods in the year when your client's business earns most revenue or has highest customer demand. Give each a name and an approximate date range." Mark those windows `[HIGH SEASON]` in Notes, raise posting frequency by 20–30 % in those weeks, and brief at least two seasonal posts per platform in each window.

## Campaign windows

Reserve the weeks of any campaign dates from `09-campaign-strategy` as `[CAMPAIGN]`. Reduce standard pillar content in campaign weeks so the campaign leads, and suggest one supporting organic post per platform per campaign week that reinforces the campaign message without duplicating paid content.

## Cross-platform consistency

Under each monthly table, show how the same weekly theme appears across platforms:

**Week [N] Cross-Platform Theme: [Theme title]**
- Facebook: [format and brief, Monday or Tuesday]
- Instagram: [format and brief, Wednesday or Thursday]
- WhatsApp: [broadcast brief, Friday afternoon]
- LinkedIn *(if in scope)*: [article or post brief, Monday]
- TikTok *(if in scope)*: [reel brief, any high-engagement day]

## Weekly rhythm template

Place this before Month 1. The team uses it as the default week unless a campaign or observance changes it; adjust it to the client's platforms and frequency.

| Day | Platform | Content Type | Pillar (rotate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Facebook | Value post or article share | [Pillar A] |
| Tuesday | Instagram | Carousel or single image | [Pillar B] |
| Wednesday | Facebook + Instagram | Repost or UGC or community content | [Pillar C] |
| Thursday | LinkedIn *(if in scope)* | Thought leadership post | [Pillar A or B] |
| Friday | WhatsApp broadcast | Weekly update or offer reminder | [Promotional pillar] |
| Friday | Instagram Stories | Behind-the-scenes or poll | [Pillar C] |
| Weekend | TikTok *(if in scope)* | Entertainment or trend content | [Pillar B or C] |

## Three monthly tables

Produce Month 1, 2 and 3 in sequence (about 4–5 weeks each). Before each, write one paragraph on the month's strategic focus: main themes, observances or campaigns, and tone. Apply the 10-4-1 rule (Bodnar and Cohen, 2012) per month: about 10 of every 15 rows are value or sharing content, 4 original brand content and 1 promotional. Note the ratio at the end of each table.

## Pre-publish QC: stratified sampling

A 90-day calendar typically holds 60+ rows. Full review is costly and the wrong tool: the failure that matters at this size is systematic (the same wrong observance date, broken 10-4-1 ratio or missing tie-in repeated across dozens of rows). Use stratified batch sampling instead (adapted from the ECC `santa-method` skill's Pattern C; it cuts verification cost to roughly 15–20 % of a full review while catching over 90 % of systematic issues):

1. **Sample** a random 10–15 % of rows, minimum 5, spread across all three months and all platforms in scope; never sample only Month 1.
2. **Verify each sampled row**: the topic is specific, not a placeholder ("TBC", "educational post"); the pillar matches the brief; observance, awareness-day, campaign or high-season tagging is correct for the date; the week's cross-platform theme is differentiated per platform, not pasted copy; date range and day of week match the weekly rhythm.
3. **Classify failures by type** (wrong-date tagging, generic topic, broken cross-platform differentiation, pillar mismatch, ratio drift), not as one-offs.
4. **A type seen more than once is systematic**: batch-fix every matching row in the full calendar, not just the sampled rows.
5. **Re-sample** a fresh 10–15 % (minimum 5) and repeat until a clean sample shows no systematic failure type.
6. **Record the pass** in the decision/source register: sample size, rows checked, failure types found and batch-fixed, and the round that converged. A calendar that reaches round 3 without converging is escalated to the client lead, not shipped on a partial pass.

This check runs after all three tables are drafted and before client review; it is a release gate, not a drafting aid.

## Consultant guidance note

The calendar is a planning tool, not a locked schedule. Review it every Friday for the following week: (1) Is there breaking news or a local event that needs a reactive post? (2) Did last week's top post suggest a follow-up? (3) Are approval timelines on track? Adjust before the next week's production begins, and flag major changes to monthly themes to the client before execution.
