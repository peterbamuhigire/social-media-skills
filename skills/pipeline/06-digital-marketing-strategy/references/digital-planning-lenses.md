# Digital Planning Lenses

Use these lenses while drafting the strategy's diagnosis and channel sections. Read this when the client's plan treats every channel as an acquisition tool, when budget is tight, when the environment is uncertain, or when a B2B client needs a demand-generation sequence. Each lens is a question to answer in the deliverable, not a heading to fill.

## 1. Customer lifecycle coverage

Map every recommended channel and asset to the lifecycle stage it serves:

| Stage | Question the plan must answer | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | How do new, qualified people find the client? | Search, paid media, PR, partners, creators |
| Conversion | What turns interest into a first purchase or enquiry? | Landing page, WhatsApp flow, offer, proof |
| Retention | Why do customers come back? | Onboarding, service follow-up, owned lists, community |
| Value growth | How does each customer become worth more (repeat, upgrade, referral)? | Cross-sell, loyalty, referral loop, account reviews |

Decision rule: if more than three-quarters of the budget or effort sits in acquisition while retention and value growth have no named asset, rebalance or state why the imbalance is deliberate. Source: Zahay, D. et al. (2024) *Digital Marketing: Foundations and Strategy* (5th ed.), Cengage — lifecycle framing, paraphrased.

## 2. Impact before budget (lean clients)

For small budgets, rank each candidate investment by strategic impact — how directly it moves the stated objective per shilling and per hour — before asking what the budget allows. Tie every channel to a named business destination (the enquiry, booking or sale it should produce). Drop channels that cannot name their destination. Source: Westergaard, N. (2016) *Get Scrappy*, AMACOM — "brains before budget" and destination-led channel choice, paraphrased.

## 3. Scenario planning under uncertainty

Use when regulation, platform access, currency or competition could change the plan materially:

1. List the key uncertainties (for example, platform availability, data-protection enforcement, ad-cost movement, a competitor's entry).
2. Rank them by impact on the objective and by how uncertain they are.
3. Combine the two highest-ranked uncertainties at their extremes into three or four concrete scenarios.
4. Write a short, plausible narrative for each and name the early signal that would show it is happening and the plan change it triggers.

Keep scenarios to decisions the client can act on; this is not forecasting. Source: Wind, J. and Mahajan, V. (2001) *Digital Marketing: Global Strategies from the World's Leading Experts*, Wiley — scenario steps, paraphrased.

## 4. B2B demand-generation sequence

For formal-sector B2B clients, lay the programme out as an ordered chain and give each link an owner and a measure:

content creation → lead capture → prospect identification → nurture → scoring and qualification → marketing-qualified hand-over → sales acceptance.

Route scoring to `meta-lead-scoring`, hand-over rules to `meta-sales-marketing-alignment`, and account-level programmes to `strategy/strategy-b2b-customer-community`. Source: Zahay, D. et al. (2024), B2B demand-generation process, paraphrased; any statistic about when buyers contact sales must come from a current, dated source.

## 5. Annual channel-mix review

Platforms rise, decline and change terms, and East African adoption differs from global trends. Put a dated channel-mix review in the plan at least once a year and whenever a platform's access, policy or cost changes materially; re-run channel choice with `strategy/traction-channel-bullseye` if growth has flattened. Source: Pidsley, M. (2023) *Digital Marketing in 2023*, platform review principle, paraphrased.
