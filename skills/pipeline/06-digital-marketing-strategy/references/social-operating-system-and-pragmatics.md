# Social operating system and conversation reference

Use this reference with `06-digital-marketing-strategy`, `05-social-media-strategy`, `strategy-channel-architecture` and `playbook-community-management`. It is an operating lens from the August 2026 book synthesis, not a replacement for P.O.S.T., POEM, RACE or the Kaizen learning loop.

## 1. SOCIAL operating cycle

Use the cycle to move from evidence to learning. Do not treat it as a promise that every campaign needs equal time in every stage.

| Stage | Required decision | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Scan** | What are people, customers, competitors and staff saying or doing? | Listening log, supplied research or clearly labelled gap |
| **Objective** | What business or relationship outcome matters, for whom and by when? | Named objective, audience, baseline, capacity and approval boundary |
| **Campaign** | What team, content, offer, channel role and risk controls are needed? | Brief, channel cards, content plan, consent/rights and escalation owner |
| **Implement** | What will be published, tested, answered, routed or held? | Approved assets, response rules, schedule and stop conditions |
| **Analyse** | What changed, for whom, at what cost and with what unintended effect? | Metric denominator, attribution notes, qualitative context and guardrail |
| **Leverage** | What should be reused, improved, referred, retained or stopped? | Learning record, owner, standardisation decision and next baseline |

## 2. Audience-affordance card

Complete one card per priority channel or channel role:

```text
Audience segment and job:
Primary motive: consume / control / connect / compete / create
Platform affordance: what this channel makes easy or visible
Social job: identity / conversation / sharing / presence / relationships / reputation / groups
Audience design: who can see, reply, forward or act?
Public/private boundary: what must stay public, move to an official private route, or remain confidential?
Desired action and conversion hub:
Consent, safeguarding, rights or dignity risk:
Primary metric and denominator:
Trust/attention guardrail and stop rule:
Channel-native adaptation needed:
```

Do not infer audience motive from age alone. Validate it with supplied audience evidence, listening, observed behaviour or a bounded pilot.

## 3. Conversation response ladder

Before replying, classify the interaction and record the audience and risk context:

1. **Acknowledge** the person or signal without conceding an unverified fact.
2. **Answer** the actual question briefly, using an approved source where the answer is factual.
3. **Repair or clarify** when the message, thread, tone or topic has been misunderstood.
4. **Redirect or close** when the exchange has reached its useful public limit; give the official next route.
5. **Escalate** when there is a credible safety, legal, safeguarding, privacy, threat or crisis concern.

Classify before selecting a template:

| Interaction | Default treatment |
|---|---|
| Good-faith disagreement | Answer or clarify; do not label it trolling |
| Specific complaint | Acknowledge, take the case to the official route, record and close the loop |
| Topic drift | State the thread's purpose, answer if useful, then redirect or start a separate thread |
| Abuse or harassment | Preserve evidence, apply the moderation rule and avoid a prolonged argument |
| Credible threat or safety concern | Preserve evidence and escalate under the relevant crisis, safeguarding or legal route |

Humour, emoji, memes and short replies are context-dependent. When the meaning is ambiguous and the stakes are material, ask a clarifying question or use a neutral reply.

## 4. Public-to-private handoff

Use this sequence for a service issue, personal-data question or conversion that should not be completed in public:

1. Acknowledge publicly without repeating personal or sensitive information.
2. Name the official private channel and expected response time.
3. Ask for permission before moving the conversation or requesting personal details.
4. Record only the minimum necessary case information in the approved system.
5. Resolve or route the issue, then close the public thread with a factual status update where safe and appropriate.
6. Invite an honest review or advocacy action only after the underlying experience has been addressed; never condition service on a positive review.

## 5. Attention and truth guardrail

Do not use outrage, humiliation, fear, compulsive posting or misleading omission as the default growth mechanism. When a metric moves, interpret it with its denominator, audience context, content type, channel affordance, time window and likely business meaning. Record uncertainty instead of turning a raw number into a conclusion.

## 6. Measure value, not visible activity

Social activity includes listening, conversation, sharing, reviews, ratings, groups, messaging, advocacy and service, not only publishing. Close the loop from social activity to enquiries, sales, retention and advocacy where evidence allows. Engagement is a signal, not proof of business value: high activity can coexist with distrust, complaints or harmful attention. Every experiment carries a primary metric, a trust or safety guardrail (for example unresolved complaints, opt-outs, safety incidents, response time), a stop rule and a learning record. If a guardrail cannot be measured, mark it `not assessed` and use a bounded qualitative check.

## 7. Pilots and capability ladders

Start with a small listening-and-publishing pilot, learn by doing, then standardise only what has evidence. Do not recommend every platform. For creator, career and monetisation work, separate entry tasks from strategy, analytics, creative production, community operations and safety work. Any claim about income, vacancies, eligibility or payment must be sourced, dated and market-specific; reject guaranteed earnings and pay-to-access schemes.

## 8. Deliberately not imported from older social-media books

| Excluded item | Reason |
|---|---|
| Historical follower counts, adoption rates, engagement benchmarks, CPMs, platform features | Time-sensitive; use the source register or client evidence |
| Church, US or India market conclusions presented as East African facts | Context-specific; keep only transferable operating principles |
| Guaranteed social-media income claims | Promotional and unsupported; retained only as a risk signal |
| A critical book's strongest causal or political conclusions as settled fact | Useful questions about incentives and power, not neutral measurement |
| Legal, privacy or consent conclusions as certification | Current jurisdiction and counsel review remain mandatory |
| Identical cross-posting, platform-first selection, vanity-metric success claims | Conflict with the engine's audience, strategy and measurement contracts |
