# Free Audit as a Lead Offer

Merged from skills/business-development/biz-dev-social-media-audit-offer on 2026-09-29 at cda737c (S04 tree); preservation map: [biz-dev-social-media-audit-offer.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/biz-dev-social-media-audit-offer.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the consultant wants to use a short, free social media audit to win a new client: a prospect has not signed, there is no approved client brief yet, and the audit's job is to open a paid engagement. It is consultant-facing guidance, not a client-facing deliverable in itself. The free audit shows expertise the prospect does not have, surfaces the right problems and creates a natural path to paid work.

This is a lighter, pre-sale variant of the SKILL.md audit. Once the prospect signs and `01-client-brief` has produced an approved brief, run the full SKILL.md audit (per-platform tables, benchmarking, ten quick wins, six-slide deck). For per-element profile fixes after either audit, use [profile-optimisation-fixes](profile-optimisation-fixes.md).

Working basis: Johnson, J. (2023) *How to Become a Social Media Manager* (publisher not recorded in the source). Persuasion frameworks: Sant, T. (2012) *Persuasive Business Proposals*, 3rd edn, and Hatton, A. (2007) *The Definitive Business Pitch* (publishers not recorded in the source); summarised in [proposal-frameworks](proposal-frameworks.md).

## Inputs

Ask for the following before generating:

| Input | What to capture | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect business name and industry | The business being audited | Stop; the audit cannot be specific. |
| Active platforms to audit | Only platforms the prospect currently uses; do not audit dormant accounts | Ask; do not guess handles. |
| Presentation format | In-person meeting, WhatsApp call or written email report | Default to WhatsApp or in person (see EA delivery notes). |
| Consultant's primary service | The core service to position after the audit (for example monthly content management, social media strategy, community management) | Ask; the transition script needs it. |
| Commercial brief, target buyer, offer, proof and requested next step | Requester or approved brief | Stop and request the missing decision context. |
| Brand voice, offer facts, constraints and approvals | Client source pack or authorised owner | State assumptions; do not invent names, prices, results or approvals. |
| Performance, platform or research evidence used for claims | Traceable export, URL, document or named source | Issue a qualified finding and name the evidence needed. |

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Deciding whether to offer a free audit at all | Apply Hatton's Go/No-Go test first: do we want this business, can we win it, can we deliver it? Offer the audit only when all three are yes. | Spending unpaid time on wrong-fit prospects. |
| Buyer problem, proof strength and commercial objective align | Choose the offer and proof sequence that supports the buying decision being asked for. | A generic sales asset with unsupported claims or the wrong ask. |
| Tempted to charge for the audit | Do not charge. Charging adds friction and removes the low-barrier entry point that makes the technique work. | A lost lead at the first step. |
| A finding could be solved in full within the audit | Name the gap and the direction of the fix only; keep the detail for the paid engagement. | Giving away the engagement for free. |
| Competitor data is available | Present it as factual market context, with real figures and no editorial comment. | Offending a small, connected business community. |
| A required fact or approval is missing | Stop that claim or action; request it or use an explicit placeholder. | Fabricated facts, implied consent or unauthorised publication. |
| Evidence is partial but a useful draft is possible | Deliver a qualified draft with gaps and the next verification step. | Treating an unassessed requirement as passed. |
| The prospect defers the decision | Close with a 30-day check-back; keep the lead open. | Treating a deferred decision as a lost lead. |

## Procedure

### 1. The business logic

The free audit works because it does two things at once:

1. **It shows expertise** — the consultant surfaces findings the prospect has not noticed. Every specific observation builds credibility.
2. **It sets up problem → solution** — every finding is a gap, and every gap is a potential service line.

The audit must be specific enough to be of real value, but not so complete that it removes the need to hire the consultant. Surface the right problems; do not solve them for free.

It costs the consultant 45 minutes: 30 minutes to conduct and 15 minutes to prepare findings. If the prospect converts to a paid client, that time is recovered within the first month's retainer.

### 2. Conduct the audit — five areas, 30 minutes

Conduct it before the discovery meeting, never live in front of the prospect.

| Area | Time | What to check | Score and note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Profile completeness | 5 min | Across all active platforms: profile photo quality and consistency; bio completeness (clear call to action, a link, relevant keywords); contact information (WhatsApp number, email, physical location where applicable); pinned posts, story highlights and cover photos current and purposeful; link-in-bio destination works and leads to a relevant, converting page | 0–10; note the 2 most critical gaps |
| 2. Content quality | 10 min | Last 30 posts across all active platforms: content pillars evident or posting random and reactive; brand voice consistent or shifting in tone and register; posts formatted for the algorithm (video, carousel) or single static images by habit; copy opens with a hook or buries the lead; promotional posts carry a call to action; AI-generated content that sounds generic or off-brand | 0–10; note the most common failure pattern — the single issue that appears most often across the 30 posts |
| 3. Engagement and reach | 5 min | Native analytics (Meta Business Suite, LinkedIn Analytics, TikTok Studio): average engagement rate against an industry benchmark (EA working benchmark: 2–4% is healthy for most SMEs; below 1% signals a content or frequency problem — verify before stating, no register record); post frequency against the recommended cadence for each platform, noting the gap; best- and worst-performing post type (video, carousel, static image, story); follower growth over the last 90 days (growing, flat or declining) | 0–10; note the single biggest engagement opportunity |
| 4. Competitor snapshot | 5 min | Two local competitors (same city or same sector): follower count, posting frequency and content types against the prospect; most engaged post (post type, approximate engagement numbers, what made it work); one specific thing the competitor does better | No score; comparative reference for the findings presentation |
| 5. Monetisation and conversion | 5 min | Clear path from social post to purchase or enquiry; link-in-bio leads to a landing page, a WhatsApp number or just the homepage; lead magnet or opt-in offer visible on any platform; paid posts run, and whether boosted posts or properly targeted campaigns; social commerce integration (Facebook Shop, WhatsApp Business Catalogue, Instagram Shopping — verify current availability before stating, no register record) | 0–10; note the single clearest missed conversion opportunity |

The competitor snapshot creates urgency without the consultant manufacturing it: present the data factually and let the numbers carry the weight. **EA note:** refer to competitor findings with care. East African professional and business circles are small and word travels quickly, so present the data as market context, not as criticism of named businesses.

### 3. Present the findings

Structure the findings as a one-page document or a five-slide mini-deck. Deliver verbally and leave a printed one-pager, or send a WhatsApp voice-note summary followed by the document. Adapt to the prospect's preferred communication style. Use the five sections in the template below, in order: summary score, top three wins, top three gaps, the competitor gap, recommended next steps.

- **Summary score** — overall score out of 50 (see the scoring note below). Colour-code: **0–20 Critical** (foundational gaps across several areas; significant opportunity); **21–35 Developing** (inconsistent performance; clear areas for improvement); **36–50 Healthy** (strong baseline; refinement and growth strategy needed). Do not present it as a grade; frame it as a starting point: "Here's where things stand today, and here's what's possible."
- **Top 3 wins** — lead with three real positives, not faint praise. This shows careful attention, not a hunt for problems, and builds trust before the gaps.
- **Top 3 gaps** — the three most important problems, each expressed as a business impact, not a technical deficiency. Not "Your captions don't have hooks", but "Your posts aren't stopping the scroll, which means your organic reach isn't growing even when you post consistently." Frame each as **symptom → business consequence → fix (headline level only)**. Name the direction; keep the detail for the paid engagement.
- **The competitor gap** — one concrete example of a local competitor outperforming the prospect, with real figures (follower counts, engagement numbers, post frequency). Avoid speculation; one well-chosen example beats a general statement about the market.
- **Recommended next steps** — three actions tiered by effort and investment:
  1. **Quick win** — doable this week at no cost (for example update the link-in-bio, pin a strong post, add a WhatsApp number to the Facebook profile). It shows goodwill.
  2. **Medium-term action** — achievable within a month at low cost (for example set three content pillars, commit to a posting schedule, set up Meta Business Suite).
  3. **Strategic recommendation** — needs professional help and is the natural entry point to paid work (for example a 90-day content strategy, a conversion-optimised profile suite, a community management programme). It opens the conversation about paid work.

Scoring note: the method sets a /50 overall score and bands, but scores only four areas at 0–10 (the competitor snapshot is unscored), so the raw maximum is 40. Either scale the raw total to 50 before applying the bands, or report it out of 40 with proportionally adjusted bands, and say which in the one-pager.

### 4. The transition to paid

Deliver this pivot after presenting the findings, verbatim or closely adapted:

> "Based on what I've found, there are three areas where I could make an immediate difference for your business. I typically work with businesses like yours on a monthly retainer that covers [describe the core service]. Before I put together a formal proposal, can I ask — what's your priority right now: more content, better reach, or more enquiries from social?" (house script from the original skill; not a quotation from Johnson 2023)

The question opens a conversation about the prospect's real goal rather than the consultant's service menu, avoids quoting a price before priorities are understood, and creates a natural path to the written proposal. After the prospect responds, move to the proposal stage with [`biz-dev-proposal`](../../../business-development/biz-dev-proposal/SKILL.md) for the formal proposal and Statement of Work. Where the task is the consultant's own positioning rather than the audit, use [`biz-dev-positioning`](../../../business-development/biz-dev-positioning/SKILL.md).

### 5. East Africa delivery notes

- Run the initial audit discovery on WhatsApp or in person, not email. Conversion for professional-services engagements in the East African market is higher through direct, personal communication (working observation; no register record).
- Deliver findings verbally with a printed one-pager, or by WhatsApp voice-note summary followed by a shared document. Match the prospect's communication habits.
- If the prospect is not ready, close with: "Can I check back with you in 30 days?" Recurring touchpoints are how East African professional-services firms build pipeline. A deferred decision is not a lost lead.
- On WhatsApp, send the one-pager as a PDF, not as a series of text messages. A document signals professionalism and is easier to share internally.

### 6. Persuasion frameworks for the audit offer

Apply [proposal-frameworks](proposal-frameworks.md) when writing the audit offer and findings. Key principles:

- Open with the problem the audit will reveal, not the audit method — Sant's Primacy Principle: the client cares about the insight, not the process.
- Position the audit as a diagnostic gift, not a sales tool: intelligence the prospect needs whether or not they engage (Hatton's Empathy Model).
- Apply Hatton's Go/No-Go criteria before offering a free audit; invest time only in prospects that meet all three.
- Treat the audit findings as the N (Need) in Sant's NOSE; the proposal that follows supplies the O, S and E.
- The audit document is Physical Evidence (Hatton's Competitive Advantage Equation): its quality and specificity show the rigour of the agency's analysis before the engagement begins.

## Templates

**Findings one-pager / five-slide mini-deck**

| # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Summary score | Total and band (Critical / Developing / Healthy), framed as a starting point |
| 2 | Top 3 wins | Three specific positives |
| 3 | Top 3 gaps | Symptom → business consequence → headline fix, for each |
| 4 | The competitor gap | One local competitor, real figures |
| 5 | Recommended next steps | Quick win (free, this week) · medium-term action (low cost, one month) · strategic recommendation (paid entry point) |

**Outputs and evidence**

| Artefact | Consumer | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-backed audit report (one-pager or mini-deck) | Prospect and consultant | Addresses the named prospect and objective, records assumptions, and passes the checklist below without invented facts. |
| Decision and gap note | Consultant or next workflow (`biz-dev-proposal`) | Names the chosen route, evidence used, unresolved inputs and any action needing authority. |
| Finding-to-source register and unassessed-check list | Reviewer | Every material claim, decision and unavailable check is traceable. |

## Checklist

- [ ] Go/No-Go test passed before any time was spent.
- [ ] Every scored area (1, 2, 3, 5) has a numeric 0–10 score; no area is described only qualitatively.
- [ ] Findings lead with wins before gaps.
- [ ] The competitor gap uses real, specific data (follower counts, post types, engagement figures), not hypothetical or generalised comparisons, and names no business critically.
- [ ] The transition to paid is scripted word for word, not described in the abstract.
- [ ] The audit took no more than 30 minutes to conduct and 15 minutes to prepare.
- [ ] Delivery format is specific to the EA context: WhatsApp, in person and printed one-pager are named.
- [ ] Next steps follow the three tiers: one free quick win, one low-cost medium-term action, one professional-help strategic recommendation.
- [ ] Gap statements are framed as business consequences, not technical observations.
- [ ] Benchmarks and platform features are verified or labelled; missing access is marked `not assessed`, never passed.
- [ ] Nothing is published, sent, spent or changed on a live account from drafting authority alone.
