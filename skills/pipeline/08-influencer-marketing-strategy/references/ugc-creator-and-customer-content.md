# UGC: Creator and Customer Content

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client wants customers, not only paid creators, to produce content about the brand: a user-generated content (UGC) programme with an audit of what already exists, a tiered collection system, a permissions log, a curation and republishing workflow, and East African handling of WhatsApp testimonials, language and cultural sensitivity. It sits beside the parent skill's paid-creator work: paid micro-UGC creators (Tier 3 below) bridge the two. Produce an operating playbook with named roles, ordered actions, controls and measures; a worked example must use a labelled scenario, never invented client evidence.

Related skills: [playbook-community-management](../../../playbooks/playbook-community-management/SKILL.md) for managing the community that makes and engages with UGC; [meta-social-listening](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-social-listening/SKILL.md) for tracking brand mentions and finding organic UGC; [playbook-reputation-management](../../../playbooks/playbook-reputation-management/SKILL.md) for negative UGC or reviews found during the audit; [11-content-calendar](../../11-content-calendar/SKILL.md) for scheduling UGC beside brand content; [legal, privacy and market release gate](../../../../docs/quality-gates/legal-market-release-gate.md); [anti-ai-slop](../../../ai-marketing/anti-ai-slop/SKILL.md); [east-african-english](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md).

## Inputs

Ask for all of the following before producing the playbook:

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name and industry | For example "Kampala Coffee House — food and beverage" |
| Country / city | Default Uganda / East Africa |
| Platforms where customers already post about the brand | For example Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp; or "none observed yet" |
| Type of UGC targeted | Reviews, photos, videos, testimonials, unboxings, event photos, or a mix |
| Current volume of organic UGC | Do customers already post unprompted? High / moderate / low / none |
| Incentive budget | Prizes, discounts and recognition (approximate UGX), or confirm a zero-budget, recognition-only approach |
| Brand guidelines for visual content | Colours, aesthetic, and imagery the UGC must match or avoid |
| Roles, timing and approval limits | Delivery owner; without them produce a draft only and do not schedule, spend or publish |

## Decision rules

| Condition | Action | Failure or risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Consent, usage duration or compensation is unclear | Do not reuse the contribution until the terms are recorded in the permissions log | Rights disputes and contributor exploitation |
| A customer tagged the brand publicly but has not replied to a permission request | Do not reshare; tagging is not permission | Republishing without consent |
| A piece of UGC has no "Y" entry in the permissions log | Do not publish it | Personal-data processing without documented consent (Uganda DPPA 2019) |
| Budget is zero | Run Tier 1 and Tier 2 collection only | Promising prizes the client cannot fund |
| Budget allows prizes or fees | Add Tier 3, with competition terms from the contests reference | Transactional, low-authenticity entries or unenforceable competitions |
| Content fails any curation-filter test | Do not publish; when in doubt, do not publish | Embarrassment to the customer, a third party or the brand |
| UGC would exceed about 30% of the feed | Rebalance towards brand content | A passive-looking feed that suggests the brand is not investing in its own presence |
| Action publishes, spends, messages people or changes production state | Require explicit approval first | Unauthorised external impact |

## Procedure

### 1. Set the case: why UGC works in East Africa

In Uganda and East Africa, peer recommendation is the strongest commercial signal available. A customer's photo of a meal, a WhatsApp screenshot from a satisfied buyer or a Facebook post by a happy client carries more persuasive weight than any brand-produced advertisement. The research is consistent worldwide (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022), but where brand advertising is less trusted and word of mouth is the default way people discover things, UGC is the highest-converting content type available (the original skill's claim; no data source). In the POEM model (Paid / Owned / Earned), UGC sits firmly in Earned: the most credible channel and the hardest to manufacture.

UGC also solves the production problem for clients with small budgets. Turning customers into content producers yields a continuing stream of authentic material at near-zero cost and builds the community and advocacy that grow social presence without matching ad spend. For Ugandan small and medium enterprises, where production budgets are tight but smartphone penetration is high, UGC is a primary content channel, not a supplementary tactic.

### 2. Audit existing UGC (before anything else)

1. Search the brand name and key product names on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and X/Twitter.
2. Check Google Business Profile reviews and customer-uploaded photos.
3. Search relevant Facebook Groups for organic mentions.
4. Ask the client whether they have received and saved WhatsApp messages from customers; screenshots of these (with permission) count as UGC.
5. Record findings in the audit table.

| Platform | UGC volume (monthly est.) | Quality (High/Med/Low) | Common content type | Most shareable examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | | | | |
| Facebook | | | | |
| TikTok | | | | |
| GBP reviews | | | | |
| WhatsApp screenshots | | | | |

Summarise the audit in two or three sentences: total estimated monthly UGC volume, strongest platform, and the single best existing piece of UGC.

### 3. Build the three-tier collection system

Choose tiers by budget and capacity.

**Tier 1 — Passive (no effort, no budget)**

- **Branded hashtag:** create one simple, memorable hashtag the brand owns, for example `#BrandNameUG`. It must be specific enough to belong to the brand and not be shared with unrelated content. Promote it in-store, on packaging, in post captions, in the Google Business Profile description and in email footers. For hashtag selection and tiers, see [caption-writer](../../../content-writing/caption-writer/SKILL.md).
- **Check-in prompt:** display a sign at the premises: "Tag us on Facebook when you visit — we love sharing your photos!" Place it at the point of highest satisfaction: beside the product display, at the service counter or at the exit.
- **Review request:** send a WhatsApp message after every confirmed positive interaction:

  > "We are so glad you enjoyed your experience! Would you mind sharing a quick Google review? It takes under a minute and means a great deal to us. Here is the link: [GBP link]"

**Tier 2 — Active (effort, low budget)**

- **Feature campaign:** run "Feature Friday": every Friday the best customer photo tagged with the branded hashtag is featured on the main feed and Stories, with a public shout-out and name credit. No purchase required to enter. Announce it in the bio, in Stories and as a pinned post.
- **Testimonial request:** send a WhatsApp message 48–72 hours after purchase:

  > "Hi [Name], how did you enjoy [product/service]? We would love to hear from you — even a quick voice note works perfectly. Your feedback helps other customers find us."

  Voice notes, text replies and typed messages all qualify as testimonial UGC.
- **Photo prompt:** train staff to ask customers to photograph the product or experience at the moment of highest satisfaction: in a restaurant when the meal arrives, in a salon after the final look, in a shop at product handover. Staff do not take the photo themselves; the prompt is "You should get a photo of this — it looks great!"

**Tier 3 — Incentivised (with budget)**

- **Monthly UGC contest:** "Post a photo or video using [product] with #BrandNameUG and win [prize]." Keep the prize between UGX 50,000 and UGX 200,000 equivalent in products or services: enough to motivate, modest enough not to attract purely transactional entries that lack authenticity. State the competition terms clearly (step 4) and design the mechanic with [contests-promotions-and-gaming-rules.md](../../09-campaign-strategy/references/contests-promotions-and-gaming-rules.md).
- **Paid micro-UGC creators:** find individual customers with 200–2,000 followers (authentic everyday customers, not macro-influencers) and commission one piece of content a month at UGX 20,000–50,000 per piece. Higher authenticity than macro-influencer content, lower cost, and an audience that overlaps with the brand's real customers. Use the parent skill's brief and usage-rights guidance.
- **Community content session:** propose a content session with a relevant local group (a church congregation, sports club, women's savings group or campus association). Offer hosting or catering in exchange for the session being photographed and shared. The result is a large batch of authentic UGC plus goodwill in the community.

### 4. Secure permission and log it

Never republish UGC without documented permission. Apply these rules without exception.

- **Public posts that tag the brand:** even then, send a direct message before resharing, and wait for an explicit yes. Tagging is not permission.

  > "Hi [Name]! We love your photo of [product]. Would you mind if we shared it on our page? We will credit you!"

- **WhatsApp messages:** never share a screenshot of a WhatsApp conversation without the customer's explicit written consent:

  > "Would you mind if we shared this as a testimonial on our Facebook Page? Your name will be included — or we can keep you anonymous if you prefer."

  A reply of "Yes you can share" sent on WhatsApp counts as documented consent.
- **Competition entries:** put this in all competition terms: "By entering this competition, you grant [Brand Name] permission to use your content for marketing and promotional purposes, including social media posts. Your name/handle will be credited."
- **Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019:** publishing an individual's image or personal content without consent counts as processing personal data under the Act; obtain consent in writing before publishing. A WhatsApp "yes you can share" is documented consent provided the conversation is saved (verify at use; see register UG-DPPA-2019; opt-out handling in lists and giveaways: UG-DPPA-S26-DIRECT-MARKETING-2026). For other markets, open the data rows of the disclosure register in [influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md](influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md). Not legal advice; confirm with counsel.

**Permissions log** (a shared spreadsheet such as Google Sheets):

| Date | Platform | Content description | Creator handle/name | Permission obtained (Y/N) | Date of permission | Where published |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Review the log monthly. Publish nothing that lacks a matching "Y" entry.

### 5. Curation and republishing workflow

Not all UGC should be published. Run every piece through this workflow before it is scheduled. This is the workflow that listening-led discovery (see [meta-social-listening](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-social-listening/SKILL.md)) hands UGC into.

1. **Capture:** save the post, screenshot or voice note with its source link, creator handle or name, and date found.
2. **Permission:** confirm a "Y" entry in the permissions log (step 4). No entry, no further steps.
3. **Curation filter — approve only if every test passes:**
   - **Photo or video quality:** resolution adequate for the target platform; not blurry or badly underlit. Mobile-phone quality is fine when the subject is clear; do not reject UGC because it was shot on a mid-range Android phone.
   - **Brand alignment:** reflects the brand's values and visual style. A competitor's product or logo in the background means do not use.
   - **Sentiment:** positive or neutral only; never republish content with an ambiguous tone.
   - **Context:** would it embarrass the customer, a third party or the brand if seen at scale? Use common sense and err on the side of caution.
   - **Cultural and religious sensitivity:** check for religious imagery, political symbols or divisive cultural references (step 6).
4. **Prepare:** credit the creator ("@CreatorHandle" in the caption, or "Photo: [First Name]" if there is no public handle); add a brand caption that gives context or a call to action (never publish UGC with no brand copy); keep the original language and add a translation where needed (step 6).
5. **Publish:** for Instagram and Facebook Stories, use the native "Add Post to Story" function (platform feature; verify at use, no register record) so the link to the original post and the attribution survive. Schedule UGC through the same content calendar as all other posts ([11-content-calendar](../../11-content-calendar/SKILL.md)).
6. **Balance:** keep roughly **30% UGC / 70% brand content** in the feed. An all-UGC feed looks passive and suggests the brand is not investing in its own presence; UGC amplifies brand content, it does not replace it.
7. **Record:** fill the "Where published" column of the permissions log.

Negative UGC found at any step goes to [playbook-reputation-management](../../../playbooks/playbook-reputation-management/SKILL.md), not into the publishing queue.

### 6. Apply East African considerations

- **WhatsApp testimonials as a primary UGC format:** in Uganda, customers often express satisfaction directly on WhatsApp instead of posting publicly. These private messages are valuable social proof. Collect them with permission using a dedicated WhatsApp Business label such as "Testimonials — with permission" so they are easy to retrieve.
- **Camera-phone quality:** most Ugandan customers shoot on mid-range Android smartphones. Quality is adequate for every social platform when the subject is well lit and in focus. Do not reject UGC for device quality alone.
- **Language:** Ugandan customers may post in English, Luganda, Swahili or a natural mix of the three. Republish in the original language where possible, since it reflects the authentic community voice. Where the original is not English, add a short English translation in the caption or a bracketed note: "[Translation: This chicken is unbelievable!]"
- **Religious and cultural sensitivity:** Uganda has large Muslim and Christian communities, and norms around gender, public behaviour and hospitality are specific. Review all UGC for religious imagery, political symbols or culturally loaded content before publishing. When in doubt, do not publish.
- **Peer recommendation as trust infrastructure:** in East African markets, peer recommendation is the main way people discover new products and services (Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2022). Every published piece of UGC is a trust signal, not just content; curate accordingly, putting quality and authenticity ahead of volume.

## Release checklist

- [ ] The UGC audit table is completed with realistic estimates across all five platforms before the strategy is built.
- [ ] A branded hashtag following the `#BrandNameUG` convention is created and documented with a distribution plan across all touchpoints.
- [ ] All three collection tiers are applied with specific, actionable instructions fitted to the client's industry and budget.
- [ ] A permissions log is set up with reference to the Uganda Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019, and every piece of UGC is logged before publication.
- [ ] The curation filter (quality, brand alignment, sentiment, context, cultural sensitivity) is applied before publishing.
- [ ] Every published piece credits the original creator by handle or name in the caption.
- [ ] WhatsApp testimonials are named as a primary UGC format in the East African context, with a collection system in place.
- [ ] Every action has an owner, timing and acceptance condition; volatile platform or legal details are verified or qualified; publishing, spending and messaging wait for explicit authority.
