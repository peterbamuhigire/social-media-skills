# Creator Due Diligence, Typology and Pricing

**When to read:** when classifying, shortlisting, vetting or budgeting creators for a brand programme. Parent skill: [08-influencer-marketing-strategy](../SKILL.md). The scorecard and fee method are the engine's own, informed by buyer-side casting practice (including Hennessy, B. (2018) *Influencer*, Citadel Press) and the fake-influence rules in register AD-09. No fee figures, follower thresholds or engagement benchmarks from books are used; platform features and metric definitions are checks against the current help centre.

## Inputs

| Input | Source | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Brief: objective, audience, formats, usage and exclusivity needs | Brand or campaign brief | Stop; these drive both selection and price |
| Creator long-list with profile links | Research, rosters, inbound pitches | Build from the discovery steps in the parent skill |
| Dated audience-insight screenshots | Each creator | Score audience fit `not assessed` |
| Budget and payment method | Brand | Draft ranges only |

## 1. Decide what you are buying

| Need | Buy | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Produced assets to a brief (tutorials, shoots, explainer videos) | **Makers** | Bloggers, video creators, credentialed experts, specialist animal or object accounts |
| Reach through someone's life or presence (appearances, event amplification) | **Carriers** | Special talents (chef, comedian, dancer), entrepreneurs, models, celebrities, public figures, everyday people with a following |

Price appearances and usage separately from content production. Celebrities offer reach from off-line fame but weaker perceived authenticity.

**East African classes to add:** radio and TV presenters and comedians; faith and community leaders (high trust, high sensitivity); professional experts such as doctors, lawyers, agronomists and accountants (check professional-body rules on endorsement); WhatsApp community and channel admins; local-language creators (Luganda, Swahili, Runyankole, Luo and others); diaspora creators for property and remittance-linked offers.

## 2. Due-diligence scorecard (0–3 per line; the engine's own grouping)

| Area | Check | Good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Findability | Name search | Their own site, profiles and interviews on page one |
| | Handle and links | Same handle everywhere; links go to the right live profiles |
| | Profile and bio | Clear photo; bio states what they create, town, contact email, real name |
| Relevance | Category recency | Recent posts in the category being cast, not just any posts |
| | Audience fit | Age, sex and location match the brief (dated screenshots) |
| | Competitor adjacency | No recent praise of a direct competitor in organic content |
| Craft | Format range | Can deliver the formats needed (self, scenes, flat lays, video, to-camera) |
| | On-camera skill | Speaks clearly to camera; puts talking points in their own words |
| | Appearance honesty | Filtered images match the real person (matters for shoots) |
| Audience health | Engagement | Rate on recent posts; sponsored posts perform at or near organic ones |
| | Growth pattern | No sudden follower jumps with flat engagement |
| | Organic to sponsored balance | Mostly organic content; heavy ad loads tire audiences |
| Professionalism | Responsiveness | Replies to a soft offer within a day or two |
| | Track record | Delivery, conduct and agent behaviour checked with past buyers where possible |
| East Africa | Local fit | Language fit; WhatsApp Status or Channel view screenshots; radio/TV crossover; political or religious content exposure (especially near elections); regulator complaints; mobile-money payee name matches the contract |

**Red lines (exclude regardless of score):** bought or automated audience; undisclosed past advertising; hateful, harassing or dangerous content; safeguarding risk; buying or selling fake indicators of influence (prohibited in the US by 16 CFR Part 465 — register AD-09, checked 2026-09-23).

**Engagement calculation:** (likes + comments) ÷ followers at the time of posting, averaged over recent posts, computed on the subset that matters (only videos, only the relevant category). Add saves, shares, watch time and link clicks where the platform reports them; confirm current definitions in each platform's analytics help.

## 3. Budget: two fees plus explicit extras

1. **Talent fee** — production costs (photographer or editor, location, props, wardrobe, make-up, transport, data bundles) + the creator's hours (brief, research, scouting, mood board, shoot, edit).
2. **Distribution fee** — the value of placement, judged on audience size, engagement (branded at or above organic is a strong signal), content quality (higher grades support wider usage), recognition and special skills, and audience precision.
3. **Extras, priced separately:**
   - *Exclusivity* beyond a short, narrow window (for example a few weeks against named direct competitors); category-wide or peak-season exclusivity costs more. Where few brands buy creators in a category, exclusivity can remove the creator's biggest income source — price accordingly.
   - *Usage* beyond the brand's own channels with credit: paid social, pre-roll, print, in-store, point of sale, third-party native.
   - *Season*: Christmas and New Year, Easter, Eid, school-term openings, Valentine's Day, graduation season, festivals, corporate year-end.
4. **Terms levers** when the fee cannot move: deposit, split or faster payment. Payments to individuals may carry withholding tax — check with `chwezi-accounting-doctrine`.

**Worked method (illustrative, Kampala):** a mid-sized creator is booked for a one-day shoot, one short video and three Stories. Build the talent fee from the actual quotes (photographer, café hire, wardrobe, transport) plus agreed hours; set the distribution fee from the factors above; add an agreed percentage for three months of paid-social usage and another for festive-season category exclusivity; quote with the breakdown. Every number comes from real quotes and current local evidence.

## 4. Programme design

- One post is an advertisement; natural, repeated integration over several weeks builds endorsement — plan three or four touchpoints where budget allows.
- Reward over-delivery (mood boards, extra images, extra organic Stories, thank-you notes) with first refusal on the next campaign.
- Offer longer ambassadorships within a few days of a successful campaign, while the result is fresh.
- Build ambassadorships around seasons and life events: introduction ceremonies and weddings, a new baby, building a house (cement, roofing, tiles, finance), starting university, relocating to Kampala or Nairobi, a first car.
- Keep a monthly rising-talent watchlist and a do-not-book register with reasons.

## 5. Soft offer (brand to creator)

"Hi [First name], we're planning a [theme] campaign for [Brand] and your [specific strength] fits. Scope: [deliverables] on [dates] in [city]. Budget for this scope: [UGX amount]. Usage: [own channels only / paid social for X months]. Exclusivity: [category or named competitors, duration]. Are you interested and available? We're confirming by [date and time]."

Put every requirement — travel, usage, exclusivity, approvals — on the table before agreeing the fee.
