# AI-Assisted Influencer Discovery and Virtual Creators

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the influencer programme uses AI or data tools to find and vet creators, when fraudulent engagement must be screened out before budget is committed, or when the client asks whether to use a human creator or a virtual (CGI) influencer. It adds tool choice by budget, a fraud-signal table with rejection thresholds, the human-versus-virtual decision and the Parasocial Interaction Scale to the parent skill's tier definitions, scorecard and brief. For the full due-diligence scorecard and pricing, use [creator-due-diligence-and-pricing.md](creator-due-diligence-and-pricing.md); for disclosure law by market, use [influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md](influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md). For integrating creator content into the wider proof plan, see [meta-social-proof-system](../../../meta-analytics-ops/meta-social-proof-system/SKILL.md). If the client really wants a judgement on AI maturity or automation level rather than a creator plan, route to [ai-readiness-diagnostic](../../../ai-marketing/ai-readiness-diagnostic/SKILL.md).

Principal source: Ltifi, M. (Ed.) (2024) *Advances in Digital Marketing in the Era of Artificial Intelligence*, CRC Press.

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|

| Primary goal | For example product launch awareness, brand credibility, Gen Z reach, community building |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign budget is at or below UGX 5,000,000 | Use Meta Audience Insights for Facebook and Instagram candidates and the manual audit below for vetting; do not buy paid tools | Spending the creator budget on software |
| Campaign budget is above UGX 5,000,000 | Paid discovery and vetting tools (HypeAuditor, Modash, Heepsy) are justified | Missing fraud that free methods cannot see |
| Creator has 100K+ followers and engagement below 1% | Reject | Paying for a purchased audience |
| Less than 60% of the creator's followers are in the client's target market | Reject | Paying for reach outside the market |
| Any AI or manual fraud signal in the table below is present | Hold the creator and complete the manual vetting checklist before any offer | Committing budget before fraud is ruled out |
| Client proposes a virtual influencer | Score the character on the Parasocial Interaction Scale with a 10–20 person panel before full production | Uncanny-valley rejection or a "corporate pretence" backlash |
| EA client with no strong case for virtual | Recommend human, local, micro and nano creators (1K–100K followers) | Weak trust in markets where parasocial credibility for virtual characters is not yet established |
| Creator content will be reused as paid social advertising | Agree the right in writing and refer the contract to a lawyer | Rights disputes over boosted content |

## Procedure

### 1. Build the longlist with AI identification

AI tools can scan millions of creator accounts and match audience demographics, engagement patterns, content-topic affinity and posting behaviour to the client's target profile. Use AI identification to build a longlist of 20–50 candidates; the final selection is a human judgement.

**Tools by access level** (costs and coverage change; verify at use, no register record):

| Tool | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Meta Audience Insights | Free (within Meta Business Suite) | Facebook and Instagram influencer research |
| HypeAuditor | Paid; free limited reports | Full audience demographics and fraud score |
| Modash | Paid | Discovery and vetting; EA creator coverage |
| Heepsy | Paid | Discovery with an engagement-quality filter |
| TikTok Creator Marketplace | Free (brand account required) | TikTok influencer discovery and analytics |
| Manual audit + Phlanx | Free engagement calculator | Micro and nano influencer vetting in EA |

**Manual longlist process** (for EA clients on limited budgets):

1. Search the relevant hashtags for the client's category in the target city or country.
2. Review the top 20–30 posts by engagement, not just by views.
3. Pick out creators who appear consistently, not those with one viral post.
4. Note follower count, engagement rate, content style and the quality of audience comments.
5. Add each to the longlist for formal vetting.

### 2. Screen for fraudulent engagement

Purchased followers, bot activity and fake comments are widespread in every market, East Africa included. Detect them with AI audit tools and manual signals before committing any campaign budget.

**Red flags detectable with AI tools:**

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Irregular follower-growth spikes | A sudden gain of 10,000+ followers in 24–48 hours points to purchased followers |
| Engagement-rate anomaly | 100,000 followers with a 0.1–0.5% engagement rate means a large purchased follower base |
| Comment-quality index | Generic comments ("Great post!", emoji-only) at high volume indicate bot activity |
| Follower-geography mismatch | A creator claiming a Ugandan audience while 80%+ of followers sit in South Asia or Eastern Europe |
| Sudden engagement spikes on old posts | Engagement-pod activity or purchased likes on particular posts |
| Like-to-comment ratio | Authentic content typically draws 10–20 comments per 100 likes; below 1 comment per 100 likes suggests artificial inflation |

**Manual vetting checklist:**

- Read the 20 most recent comments: specific and genuine, or generic?
- Check follower geography with a free tool (HypeAuditor free report, Social Blade): does it match the claimed audience?
- Click through 10 random followers: do they have real profiles, their own content and their own followers?
- Check posting consistency over the past 6 months: regular, or gaps followed by bursts?

**Rejection threshold:** do not proceed with any creator whose engagement rate is below 1% at the 100K+ follower level, or whose follower geography matches the client's target market by less than 60%.

### 3. Decide human or virtual

Address the choice explicitly; never assume it.

| | Human influencers | Virtual / CGI influencers (for example Lil Miquela, Shudu) |
|---|---|---|
| Strengths | Authenticity: audiences hold parasocial relationships with human creators and follow a person, not a brand proposition. Community connection: the creator carries existing trust. Local credibility: EA audiences are relationship-oriented, so a trusted local voice outperforms an anonymous brand message. | Full message control over every word, image and position taken. No scandal risk: no personal controversies, off-brand behaviour or fatigue. Always available: no scheduling conflicts, production delays or negotiation friction. |
| Risks | Personal lives, opinions and crises create brand risk; off-brand behaviour, controversies or public disagreements damage the client by association; the message cannot be fully controlled because the creator's voice shapes delivery. | **Uncanny valley:** an almost-but-not-quite-human character is rejected as unsettling (Ltifi, 2024); the character must be either fully realistic or clearly stylised, because the middle ground fails. **Sarcasm and cynicism:** audiences increasingly read virtual influencers as corporate pretence, particularly Gen X and Boomer audiences. Higher production cost: quality CGI needs significant creative investment. |
| Best for | Trust-building, community connection, local market penetration, EA campaigns of all types | Tech-forward brands, fashion and beauty, Gen Z audiences with high tolerance for digital innovation, international campaigns that need strict brand consistency |

**Decision matrix:**

| Factor | Choose a human influencer | Choose a virtual influencer |
|---|---|---|
| Brand risk tolerance | Low | High |
| Message-control requirement | Low to medium | High |
| Target audience age | 35+ | Under 30 |
| Brand positioning | Authentic, community-first | Innovative, tech-forward |
| Production budget | Lower | Higher (CGI cost) |
| Market maturity | Developing markets (EA) | Mature digital markets |
| Campaign geography | EA-focused | International, or urban EA only |

**EA default recommendation:** for the great majority of EA clients, human influencers, particularly local micro and nano creators (1K–100K followers), produce stronger results than virtual ones. EA audiences are relationship-oriented, digital trust is still maturing, and virtual influencers have not yet established parasocial credibility in most EA markets. State this rationale in the deliverable.

### 4. Test a virtual character with the Parasocial Interaction Scale (Ltifi, 2024)

Audiences judge virtual influencers on four dimensions. Assess each before recommending a virtual-influencer campaign:

| Dimension | What it measures | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Admiration | Does the audience find the virtual influencer aspirational? | High |
| Identification | Does the audience see themselves in the virtual influencer? | High |
| Uncanny-valley rejection | Does the almost-human appearance cause discomfort? | Low |
| Sarcasm | Does the audience see the virtual influencer as a corporate pretence? | Low |

A virtual-influencer campaign succeeds when admiration and identification are high and uncanny-valley rejection and sarcasm are low. Test with a small panel of 10–20 members of the target demographic before committing to full production.

### 5. Apply the campaign design principles

Once the creator is selected and vetted:

1. **Brief, do not script.** Give a creative brief that defines the message, the required claim accuracy, the mandatory disclosures and what is off-limits. Do not write the creator's caption or dictate delivery style. Use the brief template in the parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) § 4.
2. **Integrate authentically.** The most effective creator content fits the brand into the creator's natural content style instead of interrupting it: a food blogger filming a weekly meal prep that naturally features the client's product outperforms a scripted testimonial.
3. **Disclose clearly.** No influencer-specific disclosure statute was found for Uganda, Kenya or Tanzania (register UG-01, KE-01, TZ-01, 2026-09-24; see register UG-KE-INFLUENCER-DISCLOSURE-2026 and UG-INFLUENCER-CONSUMER-2026), but misleading-representation and code rules apply indirectly. Run the jurisdiction register and pre-campaign checklist in [influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md](influencer-term-sheet-and-disclosure.md) (not legal advice; confirm with counsel) and apply the FTC/ASA standard as best practice. Require "#ad", "#sponsored" or the equivalent in the creator's primary language, in the caption, not buried among hashtags.
4. **Settle content rights.** Agree in writing whether the client may repurpose creator content as paid social advertising. This is contractual: refer it to a lawyer.
5. **Track performance.** Require UTM links on all creator content that links to the client's website. Track reach, engagement, link clicks and conversions. Set up UTMs with [measurement-tracking-plan](../../../meta-analytics-ops/measurement-tracking-plan/SKILL.md).

- [ ] The human-versus-virtual decision is addressed explicitly, not assumed.
- [ ] The Parasocial Interaction Scale (Ltifi, 2024) is applied to any virtual-influencer option.
- [ ] The EA default recommendation (human, local, micro/nano) is stated with its rationale.

- [ ] Influencer contracts and payments are flagged as out of scope, with referral to a lawyer.
