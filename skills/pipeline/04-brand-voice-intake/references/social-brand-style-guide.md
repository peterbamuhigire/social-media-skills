# Social Brand Style Guide

Merged from skills/playbooks/playbook-social-media-brand-style-guide on 2026-09-29 at cda737c (S04 tree); preservation map: [playbook-social-media-brand-style-guide.md](../../../../docs/kaizen/consolidation-2026-09-29/preservation/playbook-social-media-brand-style-guide.md)

## When to use this reference

Read this reference when the client needs an operational social media brand style guide — the finished document handed to whoever runs the accounts — rather than only the brand voice guide and visual identity brief the parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) produces. It adds what an operator needs day to day: "we are / we are not" voice pairings, brand-specific terminology and competitor-naming rules, emoji limits and no-emoji situations, aspect ratios, watermark, filter and video-length standards, hashtag counts and research, the content approval workflow, the breaking-news content pause protocol, and house language and grammar rules.

Produce one complete, formatted guide as a client-ready deliverable. It is not advice or a framework: every section is filled with client-specific content and no placeholder text appears in the final output. Apply British English throughout, follow the `east-african-english` skill for prose register, and default to the Uganda/East Africa market unless the client names another.

## Inputs

If the brand voice intake (parent SKILL.md) has already been completed for this client, request that document and extract the inputs from it; do not ask the client to repeat information already captured. Otherwise collect:

| Input | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Client business name | Exact spelling as it must appear in all communications |
| Industry and primary services or products | Sector and main offers |
| Country and city | Defaults to Kampala, Uganda |
| Primary goal for social media | Awareness, leads, sales, community building or retention |
| Three brand tone adjectives | The client's own words. Mandatory: do not proceed without them |
| Active social media platforms | Every current and planned platform |
| Brand colours | Hex codes if available; descriptive if not |
| Approved product/service names | Exact spelling for every product or service |
| Competitor names | How, or whether, to refer to competitors in content |
| Content approver | Name and role of the person who must approve posts before publishing |
| Content drafter | Name and role of the person who creates content |
| Turnaround time expectation | Hours between draft and approval deadline |

## Decision rules

| Situation | Action | Failure avoided |
|---|---|---|
| The three tone adjectives are missing | Stop and ask for them. | A voice built on guesses. |
| A platform is neither active nor planned | Omit it from every platform table. | Rules nobody will use. |
| A post names a competitor, makes a price, statistic or performance claim, features a named individual (other than own staff in approved formats), touches a current news event or sensitive national topic, or announces a collaboration or partnership | Get explicit approval **before drafting begins**, not only before publishing. | Wasted drafts and unapproved claims. |
| Urgent content gets no approver response within the set window | Hold it; do not publish. Verbal approval is never enough — approval is confirmed in writing (WhatsApp message or email). | Publishing without a traceable approval. |
| A pause trigger in Section 8 occurs | Pause all scheduled content at once and follow the pause procedure. | Tone-deaf commercial posts during a national event. |
| A crisis, holding, formal (regulatory, legal, compliance), condolence or tragedy-related post, or a LinkedIn post on a sensitive professional topic | Use no emojis. | Trivialising a serious message. |
| A new hashtag is proposed | Run the Section 6.5 research process before adding it to the rotation. | Sharing a tag with unrelated or harmful content. |
| A longer video than the Section 5.6 maximum is wanted | Get client approval before production. | Unapproved production spend. |

## Procedure

1. Collect or extract the inputs; confirm the approver, drafter and turnaround in hours.
2. Generate the guide as one complete, headed document using the section titles below, in this order.
3. Replace every bracketed prompt with client-specific content; delete any platform the client does not use.
4. Check the guide against the release checklist and the repository anti-slop gate; confirm volatile platform figures at use.

Section order: SECTION 1: BRAND VOICE; SECTION 2: PLATFORM-BY-PLATFORM TONE GUIDE; SECTION 3: VOCABULARY STANDARDS; SECTION 4: EMOJI AND STICKER POLICY; SECTION 5: IMAGE AND VIDEO STANDARDS; SECTION 6: HASHTAG USAGE RULES; SECTION 7: CONTENT APPROVAL WORKFLOW; SECTION 8: BREAKING-NEWS CONTENT PAUSE PROTOCOL; SECTION 9: LANGUAGE AND GRAMMAR RULES.

## Section templates

### SECTION 1: BRAND VOICE

**1.1 Voice definition.** State the three client-supplied adjectives. For each, give a "we are X, not Y" pairing that shows where the brand sits on a spectrum (this complements the parent SKILL.md's 4–6 tone attributes with Right/Wrong examples; the pairings are the operator's quick test):

| We are… | We are not… |
|---|---|
| [Adjective 1 — e.g. Warm] | [Its shadow side — e.g. Familiar to the point of being unprofessional] |
| [Adjective 2] | [Its shadow side] |
| [Adjective 3] | [Its shadow side] |

Follow with a tone-scale statement, marking the brand's position with **[HERE]**, and one sentence on why that position suits this brand and audience:

```
Formal ←————————[HERE]————————→ Informal
```

**1.2 Brand voice summary.** One paragraph (5–7 sentences) every new team member reads before writing a word for this client. Describe the voice as a person: who they are, what they say, what they never say. It must be impossible to apply to a different brand without rewriting. (Same requirement as the parent SKILL.md "Brand Voice Summary Paragraph"; reuse that paragraph if it exists.)

### SECTION 2: PLATFORM-BY-PLATFORM TONE GUIDE

For each platform in scope:

```
[PLATFORM NAME]
Tone in one sentence: [How the brand sounds on this platform]
Rules:
1. [Specific rule — formatting, length, opening style, etc.]
2. [Specific rule]
3. [Specific rule]
Example post: [Worked example using this client's actual product or service — not a generic placeholder]
```

Default tone per platform (three rules each in the guide, against two in the parent SKILL.md Section 2):

| Platform | Default tone and rules |
|---|---|
| Facebook | Conversational, community-focused. Lead with a question or an invitation. Short paragraphs. The brand acts as a neighbour, not a broadcaster. |
| Instagram | Visual-first; aspirational or relatable depending on the personas. The caption supports the image. The first line must work without the "more" button. Hashtags go at the end. |
| LinkedIn | Professional, credibility-driven. Lead with an industry observation or data point. Full sentences. One strong idea per post. No more than one emoji, if any. |
| WhatsApp | Personal, direct, value-led. Treat every message as a one-to-one conversation, not a broadcast. Lead with context and value. One clear call to action. Maximum three sentences. |
| TikTok | Energetic, entertaining, hook-first. The first word of the caption and the first second of video must earn attention. Use the vocabulary the audience actually uses. |
| X / Twitter | Concise, opinionated, topical. One strong idea per post. Cut every word that is not working. A take or a question — never a press release. |

### SECTION 6: HASHTAG USAGE RULES

For a full client hashtag strategy (tiered niche, community and awareness sets, tags to avoid, monthly review) use [hashtag and keyword tagging](../../../content-writing/caption-writer/references/hashtag-and-keyword-tagging.md); this section sets the house rules the guide must state.

**6.1 Branded hashtags** — 1–3 unique to the client. For each:

- **#[Hashtag]**
- **Purpose:** [All organic content / Campaign-specific / Community and UGC]
- **Platforms:** [Where to use it]
- **Usage rule:** [Every post / Campaigns only / Specific content types]
- **Growing adoption:** [One-line instruction — e.g. "Ask followers to use this hashtag when sharing their experience"]

Advise the client to search each hashtag on Instagram and TikTok before first use to confirm another brand is not already using it widely.

**6.2 Campaign hashtags** — protocol: create a fresh tag for each campaign and retire it afterwards; [approver name/role] approves it before use; format [e.g. #[ClientName][CampaignYear] or #[CampaignTheme][Location]].

**6.3 Community and discovery hashtags** — supplementary, unbranded, rotating:

- **Industry hashtags:** [3–5 for the client's sector — e.g. #UgandaFinance #EastAfricaBusiness]
- **Location hashtags:** [3–5 — e.g. #Kampala #Uganda #EastAfrica #KampalaBusinesses]
- **Review frequency:** the content team reviews and refreshes community hashtags monthly.

Branded hashtags are permanent. Community hashtags are refreshed monthly. Campaign hashtags are retired at campaign end.

**6.4 Maximum hashtag count by platform** (source house figures; verify before stating, no register record):

| Platform | Recommended | Maximum | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 3–5 | 5 | End of caption; cap per INSTAGRAM-HASHTAG-LIMIT-2025 (partial), confirm live; the original skill's figures were 5–10 recommended / 30 maximum |
| Facebook | 2–3 | 5 | Fewer hashtags perform better on Facebook |
| LinkedIn | 3–5 | 5 | Industry and professional topic hashtags only |
| TikTok | 3–5 | 8 | Include at least one trending hashtag where relevant |
| X / Twitter | 1–2 | 2 | Embedded in the sentence where possible |
| WhatsApp | 0 | 0 | No hashtags in WhatsApp messages |

Currency warning: Instagram said in December 2025 that it would gradually cap captions on posts and Reels at five hashtags (Social Media Today, 18 Dec 2025, reporting an Instagram @creators statement; register INSTAGRAM-HASHTAG-LIMIT-2025, partial). Until the primary statement is confirmed, set Instagram to a maximum of 5 in the client's guide and confirm on the live platform before release.

**6.5 Hashtag research process** — before adding a new hashtag to the rotation:

1. Search it on Instagram and TikTok; check post volume and the type of recent content.
2. Confirm recent posts are brand-safe and the tag is not shared with unrelated or harmful content.
3. Check the tag is active — at least 1,000 posts on Instagram.
4. Add approved tags to the master hashtag list [stored in: e.g. Google Drive / Notion].
5. Review and retire underperforming hashtags monthly.

### SECTION 7: CONTENT APPROVAL WORKFLOW

**7.1 Roles**

| Role | Name | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Content Drafter | [Name] | Writes and designs all content; creates the draft for review |
| Content Reviewer | [Name or "not applicable"] | Reviews for brand voice, accuracy and grammar before approval |
| Content Approver | [Name] | Final sign-off before any post is scheduled or published |

**7.2 Standard approval process**

1. **Drafter** creates content and submits it to the Reviewer (or the Approver if there is no separate reviewer) via [channel — e.g. WhatsApp, Google Drive, Trello].
2. **Reviewer** checks it against this style guide and returns feedback or approves within [X hours].
3. **Approver** gives final sign-off within [X hours of receiving the reviewed draft].
4. **Drafter** schedules the post only on confirmation of approval — not before.
5. **Turnaround deadline:** all content is submitted for approval at least [X hours/days] before the intended publish time.

**7.3 Urgent content** (breaking-news responses, event announcements, same-day promotions):

- The drafter alerts the approver by WhatsApp immediately, with the draft.
- The approver has [X hours] to respond; with no response, the content is held, not published.
- Verbal approval is not enough; approval is confirmed in writing (WhatsApp message or email).

**7.4 Content needing additional approval** — explicit approval from [Approver name/role] before drafting begins, not just before publishing:

- Posts referring to competitor products or services.
- Posts making any claim involving prices, statistics or performance figures.
- Posts featuring a named individual (other than own staff in approved formats).
- Any content related to a current news event or sensitive national topic.
- Collaboration or partnership announcements.

### SECTION 8: BREAKING-NEWS CONTENT PAUSE PROTOCOL

When a significant national or regional event occurs — a national tragedy, a major disaster, a public figure's death, civil unrest, or any event dominating national conversation — pause all scheduled content immediately.

**8.1 Pause triggers** — pause all scheduled and planned content when:

- The Government of Uganda (or the relevant national government) declares a national mourning period.
- Two or more credible national news outlets report a major disaster, attack or tragedy.
- A public figure's death is announced and dominates social media conversation.
- A significant political or civil event makes any commercial content look tone-deaf. (In Uganda this includes election-period network restrictions, such as the January 2026 mobile internet suspension; see registers UG-SOCIAL-RESTORATION-2026 and UG-INTERNET-SHUTDOWN-2026.)

**8.2 Pause procedure**

1. [Content Drafter / Social Media Manager] identifies the trigger event.
2. Pause all scheduled posts immediately in [scheduling tool — e.g. Buffer, Hootsuite, Meta Business Suite].
3. WhatsApp [Approver name] within 30 minutes: "Content pause triggered. [One-sentence summary of event]. Awaiting your direction."
4. Post nothing — condolences included — without approver sign-off.
5. Do not resume normal content until the approver gives an explicit instruction.

**8.3 During the pause**

- Monitor the situation closely; check credible news sources every two hours.
- If the brand wishes to post a condolence or acknowledgement, draft it and submit it for approval before posting.
- Condolence posts: plain text, no emojis, no branded hashtags, no calls to action.
- Never use a tragedy as a content opportunity.

**8.4 Resuming content** — only when the immediate crisis or mourning period has passed, the approver gives explicit written approval to resume, and the first post back is neutral or informational, not promotional.

### SECTION 9: LANGUAGE AND GRAMMAR RULES

Apply consistently across all platforms and content types.

**9.1 English standard**

- **Primary standard:** British English — organisation, colour, programme, behaviour, analyse, recognise, centre, enquiry, favour, licence (noun), license (verb).
- **Local register:** use East African professional English where appropriate; follow the `east-african-english` skill.
- **Spell-checker:** set to English (United Kingdom), not English (United States).

**9.2 Number formatting**

| Type | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers 1–9 | Spell out | "three locations", "seven days" |
| Numbers 10 and above | Numerals | "15 products", "100 customers" |
| Large numbers | Numeral + word | "1.2 million", "50,000 customers" |
| Percentages | Numeral + % (no space) | "25%", not "25 percent", in social copy |
| Telephone numbers | Local format | +256 700 000 000 |

**9.3 Currency formatting**

- **Primary currency:** UGX (Uganda Shillings), written UGX 50,000 — not USh, and not UGX50,000 without a space.
- **Thousands separator:** comma — UGX 1,500,000.
- **Abbreviations:** avoid unless space is a hard constraint (for example Story text); then write 1.5M.
- **USD:** write USD [amount], only where the product or service is priced in dollars.

**9.4 Date and time formatting**

- **Date format:** DD/MM/YYYY — e.g. 19/03/2026, never MM/DD/YYYY.
- **Written dates in copy:** 19 March 2026 — no ordinal suffix (not "19th March").
- **Time:** 12-hour clock with am/pm and no space — 10:00am, 2:30pm.
- **Day ranges:** Monday–Friday, with an en dash, not a hyphen.

**9.5 Capitalisation**

- **Brand name:** always as set in Section 3.3; check every post.
- **Post copy:** sentence case only, not Title Case For Every Word.
- **Platform names:** Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, WhatsApp, X (formerly Twitter), YouTube — exactly as shown.
- **Job titles:** capitalise only before a name — "Managing Director Jane Akello" but "the managing director".
- **Acronyms:** spell out in full at first use — e.g. "small and medium enterprises (SMEs)".

**9.6 Punctuation**

- **Contractions (apostrophes):** acceptable on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp; avoid on LinkedIn.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per post; never two in a row; none in formal announcements or on LinkedIn.
- **Oxford comma:** use in every list of three or more items — "strategy, content, and reporting".
- **Ellipsis:** sparingly, only for genuine suspense or continuation; not as a trailing sentence ender in formal posts.
- **Em dash (—):** for parenthetical asides and emphasis; not a hyphen.

## Release checklist

The guide is ready for the client when all of the following hold:

- [ ] The "we are X, not Y" pairings are specific and actionable: a new team member can make a real writing decision with them, not just grasp an abstract principle.
- [ ] The platform tone guide shows a genuine, audible tonal shift between platforms using the client's own product or service — the worked examples are not the same sentence with different punctuation.
- [ ] Vocabulary tables contain at least three terms specific to the client's industry; generic East African professional vocabulary does not fill the list.
- [ ] The emoji policy can be acted on without further discussion; entries such as "use occasionally" or "as appropriate" are not acceptable.
- [ ] Image and video standards contain the client's actual colour codes and approved terminology — not a template with placeholders.
- [ ] The approval workflow names specific roles and states turnaround times in hours — no "promptly" or "in due course".
- [ ] The breaking-news pause protocol names the specific approver and communication channel, not generic role descriptions.
- [ ] The language and grammar section applies UGX formatting, DD/MM/YYYY dates and British English throughout; inconsistency between sections is a failure.
- [ ] Volatile platform figures (aspect ratios, hashtag caps, video maximums) are confirmed on the live platform or carry a verification note.
