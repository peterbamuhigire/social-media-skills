# Launch Channel Sequencing

Use this reference when channel architecture must support a timed campaign, launch, enrolment window, or event.

Primary source:

- Jeff Walker, *Launch*

Supporting strategic lens:

- Dan Kennedy, *Magnetic Marketing*

## Core rule

Assign channels by sequence role, not only by platform popularity.

## Sequence roles

- teaser channel
- education channel
- proof channel
- conversion channel
- retention channel
- close-out channel

One platform can serve more than one stage across time, but its role at each stage must be explicit.

## Typical mapping

- discovery platforms: teaser and reach
- owned or direct channels: conversion and close
- community channels: proof and objection handling

## Market-message-media check

Before locking the sequence, confirm:

1. market: which segment is this launch actually for?
2. message: what promise or problem frame will repeat across channels?
3. media: why is each channel in the sequence?
4. offer: what does each stage ask the audience to do next?

If the same message and CTA are copied unchanged across all channels, the sequence is too shallow.

## Retention and referral after close

The launch does not end at purchase or sign-up.
Plan the post-conversion path:

- onboarding or welcome sequence
- proof capture and testimonial request
- referral ask or share prompt where appropriate
- reactivation path for non-buyers who engaged but did not convert

## Output standard

A strong channel architecture should show:

- where the first attention comes from
- where trust is built
- where the actual ask happens
- where reminders and follow-up happen
- where retention and referral happen after the first conversion
