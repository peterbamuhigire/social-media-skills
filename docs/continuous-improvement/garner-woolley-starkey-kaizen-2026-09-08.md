## Trigger and baseline

Three supplied local Markdown books were requested for critical study:

- Rob Garner, *Search and Social: The Definitive Guide to Real-Time Content
  Marketing* (2012/13 context);
- Itamar Blauer and Andy Woolley, *Keywords for SEO: Actionable Knowledge Bombs
  to Help you Rank on Google in 2021*; and
- Wendy Bishop and David Starkey, *Keywords in Creative Writing* (2006).

The social engine already had current AI-search, anti-slop, rights, and
measurement controls. The gap was an explicit, reusable bridge from listening
and customer vocabulary to verified native content, distinctive human voice,
approval/moderation, canonical destinations, and learning.

## Standardisation decision

Standardise the bridge as qualified guidance. Do not standardise any book-
specific platform roster, interface instruction, social-signal/ranking claim,
fixed keyword rule, algorithm assertion, statistic, reach promise, or analytics-
product workflow. Those remain historical, stale, or `NOT_ASSESSED` until a
current primary source supports the exact claim.

## Pilot and release gates

Pilot one approved content slice. Record audience, market, source and locator,
intent, voice intention, rights/consent, accessibility, native variants,
canonical destination, CTA/failure path, moderation/correction path, baseline,
guardrail, owner, and review date. Separate reach and interaction from referral,
qualified conversation, action, and revenue.

Release is blocked by fabricated proof, privacy or rights breach, manufactured
mentions, spam/link-dropping, unsupported current claims, inaccessible content,
or an unclear audience job.
