# Kaizen Wave — Human English for Social Content

Date: 2026-08-27
Scope: captions, posts, scripts, emails, comments, campaigns, calendars, reports, training assets, and client messages.

## Baseline

This language-craft screening scored 58/100; it is not a campaign-performance or publication certification. Strengths were channel and platform coverage, native-language routing, AI-content governance, and anti-slop controls. Gaps were the absence of a local synthesis of the supplied books and no single overlay for audience, genre, grammar, lexical precision, rhythm, rights, and proof.

## Root cause

Social guidance correctly valued channel fit, but channel formulas can become content-factory cadence. The engine needed a shared test for specific observation, honest voice, grammatical control, and useful action.

## Improvement implemented

- Added `book-extractions/human-english-craft-synthesis-2026.md` with source limits, five passes, social register, grammar/lexical checks, and original anti-slop tests.
- Routed it through `AGENTS.md`, content-writing, AI content humaniser, and language-standards.
- Preserved rights, cultural, platform, disclosure, and approval gates; “human” never means careless or deceptive.

## Engine-level experiment and evidence

Experiment: verify that the local reference exists and is linked from the router and three anchor content/language gates.
Success criterion: links resolve without loosening rights, safety, or platform controls.
Result: PASS — the local reference and three anchor content/language links resolve. No performance or audience improvement is claimed until human review and product testing.

## Guardrails and rollback

If validation fails, remove only the new links. Do not add typos, fabricated lived experience, forced slang, or unsupported performance claims to make copy appear human.
